# Clustered forward main pass with 4x MSAA

The opaque main pass is clustered forward: a 4x MSAA reverse-Z depth pre-pass, then a lit pass at depth equal that loops over its Froxel's lights and traces shadow rays inline. Geometric edges get 4x MSAA (the Apple9 maximum), with alpha-to-coverage for masked materials. This is option A of `docs/research/visibility-and-gpu-driven-main-pass.md`: the forward shapes are the only ones whose MSAA costs no more memory than its 4x depth, 58,982,400 bytes at 1440p, which the pre-pass stores for the Hi-Z pyramid and the lit pass, while 4x colour needs none when the lit and transparent passes share one render pass and resolve in it (SMAA 1x cannot reconstruct sub-pixel geometry), and A writes nothing that no decided pass reads. Accepted 2026-10-12 ([#22](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/22)). Planned; today the renderer issues one `draw_indexed` per Primitive per Instance in one forward pass with reverse-Z `Depth32Float` cleared to 0 (ADR 0015), no MSAA, a directional light and up to four point lights.

## Considered options

- Forward with a visibility pre-pass (option B): A plus a 32-bit Instance-and-triangle ID per sample (59 MB at 4x and 1440p), and `primitive_index` made the ID pass 19 to 24% slower on the M4 Pro. Nothing decided reads the IDs; it is A plus one attachment, so it is taken when a compute pass needs per-pixel geometry.
- Deferred G-buffer (option C) and visibility buffer with compute materials (option D): MSAA only with per-sample storage or per-sample shading at edges, otherwise SMAA 1x. Lighting every sample at 4x cost deferred 1.2 to 3.7 times forward 4x on the M4 Pro.
- Virtual geometry beside A (option E): no MSAA in Nanite or Bevy, Nanite hides its LOD switches with TAA, and wgpu 30 cannot build an acceleration structure that follows the cut, so shadow rays against proxy geometry self-shadow. Reopens only when wgpu can build or refit one that follows the cut and a non-temporal method keeps sub-pixel switches stable. Dense static meshes use discrete LOD.
- Per-pixel class-c lighting (ReSTIR, Lumen, MegaLights), which needs a G-buffer or visibility buffer, motion vectors and a temporal denoiser: excluded by ADR 0008. Lighting is ADR 0020 ([#65](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/65)), which shades from position and normal.
- Tiled 2.5D light culling and Z-binning: 2.5D culling bounds each tile's lights by the opaque depth, so transparent surfaces in front of it need lists of their own, and Z-binning intersects tile and depth bins per pixel at shading time. Froxels, each with its own list built once against its bounds, serve the opaque and transparent passes alike.

## Consequences

- One compute culling pass composes each Instance's camera-relative transform (ADR 0014), tests the frustum and a two-phase Hi-Z pyramid of minimum reverse-Z depth (ADR 0015), and writes a compacted index buffer drawn with one `draw_indexed_indirect` per pipeline, one native call on every backend. No count draws: Metal has none, and runs `multi_draw_indirect` as a CPU loop.
- The depth pre-pass writes 4x MSAA depth only; it seeds the Hi-Z pyramid and the lit pass's depth-equal test, and restores front-to-back cost when the culling pass writes draws out of order.
- Lights are culled into Froxels, each with its own light list built against the Froxel's bounds; today's up to four point lights become a light buffer.
- Transparency is a sorted forward pass after the opaque pass, sharing the Froxel lists, MSAA and the opaque depth. Order-independent transparency waits for content that per-Instance sorting cannot fix.
- Render pipelines are vertex pipelines. Metal forbids ray queries in mesh-shaded pipelines, so the lit pass cannot be one; a mesh-shaded pre-pass returns with Meshlet culling, which needs the cooked format (ADR 0011).
- Reopen when either holds: on a Reference scene at 1440p with 4x MSAA, the opaque main pass (culling, pre-pass and lit pass) takes more than 6 ms of GPU, half of ADR 0013's 12 ms; then B and D are prototyped on that scene before anything changes. Or lighting (ADR 0020) comes to need per-pixel G-buffer inputs. The 6 ms is provisional, as ADR 0013's budgets are.
