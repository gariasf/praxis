# Coverage in the main pass is a written `sample_mask`, and alpha-to-coverage stays off

A fragment of the main pass that stands for part of a pixel says so by writing `sample_mask`, the samples it keeps of the pixel's four, and alpha-to-coverage stays off in every pipeline of the main pass. Geometry that writes the mask draws through pipelines of its own, so no other geometry pays for it. Accepted 2026-11-14 ([decision](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/797#issuecomment-6874904284), items 2 and 12; kept by both prototypes, [the LOD switch's](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/800#issuecomment-6875787005) for its cost and [the widening's](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/801#issuecomment-6876086730) for its look). Implemented for Thin Primitives ([#360](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/360)) and small Instances ([#821](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/821)). Not yet: M7's masked Materials.

## Decision

- **The carrier.** Partial coverage is a `sample_mask` output of the fragment stage. No pipeline of the main pass enables alpha-to-coverage.
- **Who writes it.** The depth passes write the mask, so only the kept samples take the fragment's depth. The lit pass draws the same geometry at depth equal and writes none: it meets the depths the depth passes kept. A fragment of its twin that keeps no sample is discarded there, since another fragment's depth can equal its own (amended 2026-11-16, [#892](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/892)).
- **Five levels, rounded.** 4x MSAA holds five coverage levels. A fragment keeps a count of the four samples equal to its coverage times four, rounded to the nearest, and none below an eighth. The samples kept follow from the count alone, sample 0, then 3, 1 and 2, so every fragment of a pixel keeps the same ones and a tube's faces share them. No dither, no pattern across pixels, no state across frames.
- **Pipelines of their own.** Each pipeline of the main pass has a twin whose fragment stage writes the mask, or for the lit pass meets it, and the culling pass lists the geometry drawn through the twins apart, in slot order within each phase. The twins draw every frame, since any Instance can be small.
- **A Thin Primitive's coverage.** Its vertices move out from its tube's centre line until the tube draws a pixel wide, and its coverage is the tube's true width over the width it draws. It reads the camera and the Instance alone. With culling on, the culling pass drops an Instance of a Mesh that is all Thin Primitives once its widest tube keeps no sample at the bounds' nearest depth ([#892](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/892)). A Far proxy holds no Thin Primitive, so the rule ends at the Load radius.
- **A small Instance's coverage.** An Instance whose Mesh bounds cover under a pixel's area keeps the larger of the area they truly cover over a pixel's and the square of the sphere about them over four pixels, so a long sliver's samples follow its length. It is scaled about their centre, across the view, each vertex keeping its depth, until the area they draw times that coverage is the area they truly cover, a pixel's but for a sliver, at any angle; it is scaled no further than the sphere about its bounds drawing four pixels across, so a long sliver is not stretched, and keeps more samples where that stops it (amended 2026-11-24, [#1026](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/1026): the scale had restored that area on the view axis only, and the coverage had been written as the area over a pixel's). With culling on, the culling pass drops it once its coverage rounds to no sample, under an eighth. A Mesh with a Thin Primitive keeps that rule alone. It reads the camera and the Instance alone.

## Considered options

- **Alpha-to-coverage.** Which samples a given alpha keeps is the platform's choice, so two machines would draw two images, and a pipeline that enables it may not also write `sample_mask` [ref:webgpu-alpha-to-coverage-sample-mask], which the same passes need.
- **A dither between the levels.** The right mean coverage at any width, as a stipple that crawls under a moving camera. The owner flew `town` on both and chose rounding ([decision](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/801#issuecomment-6876086730), item 1).
- **Alpha blending**, as phone-wire antialiasing fades the line it widens. Continuous at any distance, but it needs M7's sorted transparent pass.
- **Accumulating coverage over frames.** Excluded by ADR 0008.
- **The mask in the pipelines every Instance draws through.** On the M4 Pro at 2560×1440, a `sample_mask` output in both depth passes and the lit pass cost `town`'s GPU frame 0.73 ms with shadow rays on and its gate 0.56 ms, as much as a `discard` there; the cost followed the share of indices drawn through the stages that write it, and a second pipeline and draw a pass cost nothing ([#800](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/800)). Measured on the M4 Pro alone.

## Consequences

- ADR 0019's "alpha-to-coverage for masked materials" no longer holds: a masked Material derives its mask from alpha in the shader and writes it as `sample_mask`, through the twins.
- Five levels are all 4x MSAA holds: the contrast along a Thin Primitive steps by a quarter as it narrows, and it ends at an eighth of coverage. A tube of few faces draws wider than a pixel so that its narrowest view spans one, a third wider for three faces, so `town`'s wire ends where it is a sixth of a pixel wide. Every tube takes three faces' share, 0.75 of its diameter, whatever its faces ([#820](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/820)): the samples a tube keeps are its true width over the width it draws at any count of faces, so its own share would move where it starts to widen and where it ends, `town`'s six-faced post from 65 m to 75 m and from 521 m to 602 m at 2560×1440, not the coverage it sums to, and a wire of six faces would end at 361 m for twice its triangles through the twins. A tapered tube keeps its taper as it widens, so the coverage at its ends is off by up to the quarter the cook tolerates.
- The culling pass's compute stage already binds nine storage buffers, as many as the Capability floor allowed then (ADR 0021; ten since 2026-11-28, for the shadow mask pass), so the twins' packed indices share the frame's draw's buffer: each phase's fill its end, back from the phases' before, in slot order.
- A fragment stage that writes the mask reads a flat input, so the M4 Pro merges none of its fragments (ADR 0019).
- A small Instance keeps its mean coverage whatever its Mesh and wherever it is in the view, to first order in its bounds' size over their distance, since the samples it keeps fall by the area it gains. How steadily it keeps them follows how much of its bounds its Mesh fills: on `town`'s far flocks at 2560×1440, 81% of the dots a frame shows are there in the next, against 64% unscaled, on the M4 Pro (measured 2026-11-16, [#891](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/pull/891), when the scale restored a pixel on the view axis only).
- The culling pass tests a small Instance's bounds as it draws them, scaled, against the view frustum and the depth pyramid, so whether it is drawn does not follow the frame before; it works out every Instance's coverage for that, culled or not.
- A pixel whose samples a mask splits between two surfaces is listed for the shadow mask pass's second surface, as any edge pixel is.
