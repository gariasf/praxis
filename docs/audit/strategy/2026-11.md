# Strategy review, November 2026

## Summary

The ladder, the ADRs and the way of working hold up against the owner's goals: nothing built has to be undone, and 19 of the 24 ADRs fit as written. Three things carry a cost that grows if left: every technique is judged on the slower of the two GPUs owned while the target is hardware not yet owned; the far field and global illumination are being decided on flat, generated worlds; and the dependencies have no rule for when they move. The largest recurring cost is process that runs on every PR whatever the PR changes. The owner is asked for the ten decisions in the Decision queue; the first two bind M5's exit criteria, which are open now.

- **Milestones**: M5's exit criteria (`town`'s GPU frame, a terrain row in the scalability arithmetic); M6's charting (the probe Budget's adapter, the Reference scene's interior and moving sun, the interim authoring path); M8's charting (a per-Tick CPU Budget, `bevy_ecs`).

- **The owner's recurring work**: 17 of 25 `ready-for-human` tickets concern the RTX 2070 SUPER, 11 of them to re-record its Baselines or Goldens.

## The goals it was judged against

The owner's statement of 2026-11-14, joined with `ROADMAP.md` and ADRs 0008, 0010, 0013 and 0021.

- **G1, the game.** The owner's own big-world, lore-heavy, single-player RPG of the Skyrim or RDR class. It may take ten years. No date for a first playable, none for shipping.
- **G2, before the game.** Many experiments on the engine first. Clouds, water, oceans, weather, texture blending and smoothing, physics and everything between are solved before a game is started.
- **G3, hardware.** The target is next-generation hardware the owner does not own yet. Current-generation hardware may be skipped for now. Lower settings for lesser devices come later. A Steam Deck is the lowest bar anyone would consider.
- **G4, distribution.** The owner's games, later, on Windows, Linux and macOS at a stated level of performance and quality. The engine is not a product.
- **G5, the team.** One owner plus Claude sessions. The scarce resources are the owner's attention, tokens and context. Development is on an Apple M4 Pro, the Budget reference, with an RTX 2070 SUPER beside it.
- **G6, quality posture.** Image quality and correctness first. Temporal accumulation only where it is both good and necessary. Evidence beats assertion.

Findings rank by cost of delay: what has to be undone or rebuilt later, and the milestone at which it bites. Calendar time is not a cost (G1). The owner's attention and tokens are (G5).

## Method

The pipeline of `docs/agents/audit.md`, one level above the code. Six read-only readers at high effort, one per lens, shared one brief and the frozen commit `d4ac21eb8ca9df5927cb74c50504f57478298efb`; each filed at most 12 Findings with evidence. Two adversarial verifiers (lenses 1 to 3, lenses 4 to 6) reopened every Finding's evidence, re-derived its numbers, looked for the decision that already answers it, and tested its cost of delay. Nobody ran a build or a bench: every figure is read from a file or computed from figures that are. `map body:n` and `map comments:n` are line numbers in a snapshot of the ladder's map issue taken on 2026-11-14, and `M5 map:n` in one of M5's map. The Holds are the readers'; the verifiers corrected the ones they found false and did not audit the rest. Web sources were opened in the session that cites them.

A Finding's row gives the claim as the verifier left it. `placement` is filled at the triage.

## Decision queue

Each item is the owner's to decide. The recommendation is the session's.

1. **Which hardware does a Budget verdict speak for?** (S2-01, S3-01, S4-01, S4-02; S2-05, S3-02 and S3-04 are instances.) At one engine source, the M4 Pro's ray lines read 2.7 to 4.0 times the RTX 2070 SUPER's and its raster lines 1.2 to 1.8 times. `town`'s GPU frame is 20.0 ms on the M4 Pro and 9.7 ms on the RTX, which meets both the 12 ms Budget and the 6 ms gate. ADR 0013 once said the Windows machine "gets its own ADR"; that sentence was removed and nothing replaced it. Options: (a) the M4 Pro stays the only judge, and its role as a stand-in is written down; (b) the M4 Pro gates regressions, and a technique rejection or a reopen trigger needs the figure on both adapters before it is final; (c) the Budgets go provisional until the target is named. **Recommended: (b)**, plus a clause in ADR 0013 that owning target-class hardware re-records the Baselines and re-decides the Budgets. Binds at M5's exit criteria and at M6's probe Budget. It adds runs on the RTX, which item 5 has to make cheap first.
2. **What does M5's exit ask of `town`'s GPU frame?** (S3-03.) LOD returns 1.73 ms of an 8.01 ms gap, 22%. Options: (a) hold 12 ms on the M4 Pro at `town`'s density, which needs about 6 ms out of shadow rays; (b) judge the M4 Pro at a stated lower density and keep `town` as a stress scene; (c) state the criterion on both adapters: the RTX meets it, and the M4 Pro's breach stays a ticketed offender. **Recommended: (c)**, following item 1's (b). The Mac is still a platform the games ship on (G4), so the offender stays open.
3. **Does "good and necessary" admit screen-space history for a named term, and when is the volumetrics survey run?** (S2-06, S2-03, S1-04.) ADR 0008 already admits world-space accumulation; it is stricter than G6 only if G6 means screen-space history. The survey "volumetrics without screen-space history" was promised at a boundary the ladder has since renumbered, and no line on the map carries it. Options: (a) a line on the map now, the survey run before world content is charted; (b) the survey run before M6 is charted. **Recommended: (b).** Clouds and fog are the first things G2 names and the one technical block the pre-mortem left open; the survey costs one research ticket. The verifier found no rework shown either way, so (a) is defensible.
4. **When does a pinned dependency move?** (S5-01, S5-02, S2-04, S5-03, S5-04, S5-05.) wgpu publishes a breaking release about every three months; the one bump so far was made when the repo had 8 tests, and today 1,276 lines in 34 files name `wgpu::`. `bevy_ecs` is at 0.18.1 against 0.20.1. ADR 0007 makes wgpu permanent with no reopen trigger and no exit. Recommended, as four parts: a bump review at every milestone exit, in the pass that already re-records Baselines (take the releases, or record a dated reason to skip); the `bevy_ecs` choice (track it, freeze and own it, or replace it) made at M8's charting, before gameplay sits on it; ADR 0007 gains a measurable trigger and names the exit already in use in small, an owned fork; and wgpu, wgpu-hal, wgpu-types and naga move to 30.0.2 now. The last is a version bump, so the owner's: wgpu-hal 30.0.1 sets no colour space on the Metal layer, which 30.0.2 fixes; whether the M4 Pro's panel shows the difference is one windowed frame beside its capture.
5. **Who keeps the RTX machine current?** (S4-05, S6-03, S4-06.) Options: (a) agent sessions get a path to that machine that needs no owner; (b) per-change refreshes stop, and its nightly sweep re-records its counter Baselines and opens the PR, the owner judging only a Golden that moved where Metal's did not; (c) as today. For DX12, which has timing Baselines for 12 scenes and no Goldens: record its Goldens, or stop refreshing it by hand until the Windows backend is chosen. **Recommended: (b)**, with DX12 left to the sweep. The sweep there last published on 2026-11-09, so (b) starts by finding out why.
6. **Three per-PR rituals take a lighter form?** (S6-01, S6-02, S6-08.) Recommended, each separately: a field guide page is marked stale by a test when its mechanism's files change after its *Describes* commit, and stale pages are rewritten once at the milestone's exit, not in every PR; gallery images are required only when the gallery reports a difference, a Golden moves or a `.wgsl` file changes, and otherwise one line states the count; the Sources axis stays per PR for what code or an ADR cites, and a survey's rows are checked once, adversarially, when its decision is taken.
7. **Is timing judged once or twice?** (S6-05, S6-06.) Hand-recorded Baselines and the Bench history both judge timing. Options: (a) Baselines in git keep counters and memory, and timings are judged against the Series' recent median; (b) both stay, and CI commits lavapipe's files so the designed first red run goes; (c) as today. **Recommended: (b)**, and no further tickets on the Suspect and Regression layer until it files a regression nobody had measured. The history holds five days of results; moving the gate onto it is premature. Revisit at M5's exit.
8. **Where do the rules live, and what does a session load?** (S6-04, S6-09, S6-10, S1-09, S2-08, S1-06.) Recommended as one docs ticket and one check ticket: Session start orients on the ticket and its milestone's map, not the whole map issue; `CLAUDE.md`'s Project shape shrinks to a line per directory; rules a session must follow move from the sessions' private memory into `docs/agents/`; `workflow.md` points at the current ladder and names the prototype ticket as the unit of an experiment; `ROADMAP.md`'s two stale clauses are rewritten; the PR form check reads the PR's title and body as the squash commit they become; the 400-line ceiling is either enforced or restated at what practice holds.
9. **What is M6 judged on, and how is its content placed?** (S1-01, S1-02, S1-03, S1-05, S3-08.) Recommended: a terrain row in M5's scalability arithmetic before its exit criteria are firm (arithmetic only); and three notes for M6's charting: its Reference scene has an interior and a moving sun; the interim authoring path is named (a Blender scene through glTF nodes, whose line on the map reads "likely M7" today); one opaque authored scene is judged before probes are tuned.
10. **Does the repo stay public under MIT?** (S2-07.) The decision lives in a map comment, and ADR 0024 already turned down self-hosted GPU runners because of it. Options: (a) stay public and record it as an ADR with what it rules out; (b) take new work private. No cost of delay was shown. **Recommended: (a)**; it is the state today and costs one ADR.

Two answers only the owner has, which several Findings wait on: what next-generation hardware means in numbers (resolution, refresh rate, a class of GPU), and what "a stated level of performance and quality" is for players (G4).

## Holds

Checked and found sound. The next review does not re-find these.

**The ladder**

- Streaming, then the far field, then GI is the right order: probe rays trace the same TLAS, Far proxies included, and M5's scalability check found M4's capacities hold.
- No first playable before M8 is consistent with G1 and G2.
- Skinned crowds do not reopen ADR 0020: both forms were measured inside their Budget before M3 closed.
- The coverage carrier decided at M5 does not foreclose M7's foliage.
- Physics at M8 does not change the frame's shape; the step model was decided early (ADR 0022).
- Consolidation has no fixed cadence and is judged at each exit.

**The decisions**

- ADR 0010 matches G4 word for word.
- Memory can tier by data: every capacity derives from one Budgets value (`src/render/renderer.rs:263-265`, `src/assets/pools.rs:82-84`).
- ADR 0008 leaves a render-scale mode open; only its history clause is in question.
- ADR 0019's reopen trigger worked as designed: it fired on `town`, two options were prototyped, and the decision stood on measurements. The RTX's gate also read over 6 ms at the time, so the clause would have fired there too.
- ADR 0012 has cost nothing yet: no `mlua` in `src/`, so M8 re-judges it freely.

**Budgets**

- The streamed world meets every Budget on the M4 Pro: CPU p99 39%, GPU p99 60%, gate p99 44%.
- `town`'s CPU frame is 31% of its Budget at p99 on the M4 Pro.
- GPU memory peaks at 12.5% of 8 GB on the traversal.
- The M4 Pro is the stricter judge of the GPU frame: the RTX's GPU frame is 0.415 to 0.693 of it on all twelve scenes. This is true of the GPU frame only; CPU is not established (S3-07).
- Budgets are checked by `praxis compare` on both machines and move up only by decision.

**Hardware**

- On paper the Capability floor's ray queries do not exclude the Steam Deck's driver. The rest of the floor there is unchecked.
- Rays in a compute pass, not the fragment shader, agrees with the published desktop guidance opened.
- Real hardware beside the reference pays: nine defects were found only on the RTX.
- The floor is proven on software Vulkan in every CI run.

**Shipping and dependencies**

- Dependency licences are permissive only, and an unlisted expression fails `cargo test`.
- Every committed asset is CC0 1.0.
- A build without the cooker exists and CI renders every Golden from it.
- Cook-side crates have a cheap exit: the Cooked cache is disposable.
- Deferrable at no cost, none creating rework by waiting: packaging and installers; code signing and notarisation; pipeline caching; DX12's compiler; crash and log collection; display settings; a quality setting; an install-relative data root; archive packaging; save format and versioning; third-party licence notices. Only four of these have a line on the map.

**The way of working**

- The agent's own three-axis review pays: across the 250 PR bodies that carry counts, 2,850 found and 1,841 fixed.
- The owner's gate is narrow: 46 of 300 PRs carry a Decide item, 41 with a recommendation.
- The exit's visual audit at native resolution finds what the 640×360 Goldens pass.
- The shipping-build CI job catches what a local verify cannot: 6 of 25 failed CI runs.
- Exact counter Baselines catch real behaviour: one extra heap allocation was found by one.
- Consolidation lanes 1, 4 and 7 earned their place: 26 and 38 fixes, and the list of M3 choices M4 undid.
- Research and prototypes change decisions cheaply.
- The calendar cost of process is small: the Consolidation milestone took four days for 60 tickets.

## Could not determine

1. What next-generation hardware means in numbers, and what level of performance and quality players are promised (G3, G4). Only the owner can say.
2. Whether the Windows machine meets the 6 ms CPU Budget on today's engine, and whether `world`'s 110 ms CPU maximum there is gone. One bench of `main` on that machine answers both.
3. Whether the shadow mask's adapter ratio holds for probe rays, which are longer and less coherent. M6's first probe measurement, run on both adapters.
4. Whether terrain forces a rebuild of M5's far field and TLAS reach or only an addition beside them. A terrain survey plus the scalability lane against M7's scope.
5. Which decisions of M3 to M5 authored content would overturn. Only loading an authored scene answers it; none is shown to fail.
6. What probe GI costs with alpha-tested foliage in the TLAS.
7. Whether a world-space or sky-space cache is enough for volumetric clouds and fog. Queue item 3's survey.
8. Any cost, on any hardware, for terrain, vegetation, atmosphere, weather, particles, transparency or UI. No research note covers them yet.
9. Whether a Steam Deck meets the whole Capability floor and at what frame time; whether inline ray queries are slower than ray pipelines on AMD in this engine's use. An AMD adapter.
10. Why DX12 traces shadow rays faster than Vulkan on one GPU.
11. Whether S5-04's defect is visible on the M4 Pro's panel. One windowed frame before and after 30.0.2, beside its capture.
12. What pipeline and shader creation cost at start-up on each backend. No figure is recorded.
13. Tokens per ritual, and the owner's time per RTX ticket. No session or usage record was in reach; rituals are costed in lines, bytes and runs.
14. Whether the hand-recorded timing Baselines have ever caught a regression at PR time. Local `praxis compare` failures leave no record.
15. One outside-view source: the developer's own account of a six-year solo engine project's cancellation could not be opened, so that comparison rests on the released source's README and one news report.
