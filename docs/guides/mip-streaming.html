Mip streaming

The mechanism that brings a Texture's mips finer than its Resident tail into GPU memory as the camera nears: each frame it finds the finest mip any drawn Placement of a loaded Chunk needs, from its distance and its Primitive's UV texel density, requests the missing mips from the IO pool finest-needed first, and moves each Texture whose mips arrive to the Texture class of its new finest mip, first evicting the finest mip of the lowest-priority Texture when its Channel has no room for that class; the mips a frame needs and lacks are its late mips.

Describes main at 92643f28 (2026-11-08), with mip streaming as its change leaves it. A change to mip streaming rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: Texture cooking for the Blob, the Resident tail and the Texture classes a load writes into; The Chunk streamer for the IO pool and Settled frames.

Interface

Runs
Once a frame, in ChunkStreaming::update after the frame's Chunk loads; with a Settled wait, again until no mip request is in flight. Off when the residency Render switch holds every Texture to its tail. The IO pool's threads read and decode the mips beside the frame.
Reads
The camera's World position; the World's LoadedChunks, each Placement's World transform, Mesh and Material; each Mesh's bounds, Primitive texel densities and Materials, from LoadedMeshes; each Material's Textures and each Texture's shape and finest Resident mip, from the Material pool; for an eviction, each candidate's need and the last plan that needed its finest Resident mip.
Writes
Requests to the IO pool, one per Texture, for the mips from the finest needed to the finest Resident. For each Texture whose mips arrive, through the load staging: the mips into a slice of the class of its mips from the finest, its Resident mips copied after them on the GPU, and each loaded Material that samples it with its new Layer. For each eviction, the evicted Texture's chain copied from its mip 1 on into a slice one mip coarser, or its Layer pointed back at its tail, and its Materials written again.
Counted
Frame stats' late_mips, which the bench's Counters sum over their frames; the Blob bytes each request read, in bytes_read; the bytes its moves write into the load staging, and those their copies move, in uploaded_bytes; its time within the CPU stage stream_chunks.
Where in the code
MipPriority, MipPriority::eviction_rank, MipRequest and finest_mip_needed in src/streaming/mips.rs; MipStreaming, IoRequest and decode_mips in src/streaming/io.rs; MaterialPool::stream_mips, evict_for, evictable, evict_finest_mip, full_class_for_mips, coarsest_evictable_needs, EvictionRank, texture_residency and textures_of in src/assets/material.rs; CookedTexture::resident_tail_from_blob, mips_from_blob and resident_tail_level in src/assets/cooked.rs.

1 · decode_chunk · resident_tail_from_blob

The tail first

A Texture a Chunk names reaches the GPU as its Resident tail alone: the IO pool decodes only the tail's levels from its Blob, and the load writes them into a slice of the tail's own Texture class. Its finer mips come later, when a Placement needs them.

let texture = CookedTexture::resident_tail_from_blob(&read(texture_id)?)
    .with_context(|| format!("reading Texture {texture_id}"))?;

decode_chunk, src/streaming/io.rs.

The tail is the mips from the first of 64 KiB or less, as resident_tail_level finds it from the Blob's format, size and mip count; a CookedTexture holds its mips from first_level on, here the tail's first. ChannelTextures::push writes whatever mips it is given: the tail into the tail's class, then any finer ones into the class of its mips from its finest, with the tail copied after them. So a Texture read whole, as a spawn reads it from the Cooked cache, still loads every mip at once, and one read tail first stops at its tail. A Texture whose mip 0 is 64 KiB or less is all tail and never streams.

2 · finest_mip_needed · MipPriority::plan

The finest mip a Placement needs

A surface needs the mip whose texels are no larger than a pixel's footprint on it. The footprint is taken at the narrowest pixel of a 16:9 view 1,440 pixels tall, at the nearest point of the sphere around the Placement's Mesh, so neither trilinear filtering nor anisotropic filtering with an unrounded ratio samples a finer mip than the one requested there.

fn finest_pixel_metres_per_metre() -> f64 {
    let half_height = (f64::from(FIELD_OF_VIEW) / 2.0).tan();
    let corner_squared = half_height.powi(2) * (1.0 + PRIORITY_VIEW_ASPECT_RATIO.powi(2));
    2.0 * half_height / PRIORITY_VIEW_HEIGHT_PIXELS / (1.0 + corner_squared)
}

fn finest_mip_needed(texels_per_metre: f64, distance_metres: f64) -> u32 {
    let texels_per_pixel = texels_per_metre * distance_metres * finest_pixel_metres_per_metre();
    texels_per_pixel.max(1.0).log2().floor() as u32
}

src/streaming/mips.rs.

A pixel on the view axis spans 2 tan(θ/2) / 1440 radians, 0.0005753 at the 45° field of view: that many metres per metre of distance. A pixel off the axis spans less, by the square of the cosine of its angle off it, and the corners are farthest off: there tan² of that angle is tan²(θ/2) (1 + (16/9)²), so the corner pixel spans 1.7138 times less. Times the distance that is the pixel's footprint on a surface facing it, which is also the shorter axis of its footprint on a surface at any slant. The bound rests on Vulkan's LOD formula: λbase = log2(ρmax/η), with η = min(ρmax/ρmin, maxAniso), ρmax and ρmin the footprint's longer and shorter axes in texels. Within the anisotropy cap that is log2(ρmin), the shorter axis; past it, and with no anisotropy, coarser. So no filter reads finer than the face-on footprint gives, and trilinear reads the mip of the floor of λ, and the one after it.

Texels per metre come from the Primitive's UV texel density, UV units per metre of the Mesh, times the larger side of the Texture's mip 0, over the Placement's smallest scale. The distance is to the nearest point of the sphere around the Mesh's bounds, placed and scaled as the Placement is, and none when the camera is inside it:

for texture_use in uses.iter() {
    let distance = (camera.distance(texture_use.centre) - texture_use.radius).max(0.0);
    let level = finest_mip_needed(texture_use.texels_per_metre, distance);
    let needed = self.needed.entry(texture_use.texture).or_insert(level);
    *needed = (*needed).min(level);
}

MipPriority::plan, src/streaming/mips.rs.

The uses are built once per loaded Chunk, the first frame that sees it: for each Placement, each Primitive's Material, or the Placement's own, and each Texture of it that has mips finer than those Resident. A Placement whose Materials have nothing to stream costs no transform. Every drawn Placement counts, in front of the camera or behind it; a held Chunk keeps its uses but adds no need, and an unloaded one's are dropped. A Texture's need is the finest any of its uses gives.

3 · MipPriority::plan · decode_mips

Requests

A Texture whose need is finer than its finest Resident mip is requested once, for exactly the mips between the two, when its move would find room, and the frame's requests go to the IO pool finest-needed first, then by Asset ID. A Texture with a request in flight is not requested again.

for (&texture, &needed) in needed_by_texture {
    let Some((_, resident)) = materials.texture_residency(texture) else {
        continue;
    };
    if needed >= resident {
        continue;
    }
    late_mips += resident - needed;
    if self.in_flight.contains(&texture) || self.refused.contains(&texture) {
        continue;
    }
    if let Some(class) = materials.full_class_for_mips(texture, needed) {
        let needs = evictable_needs
            .get_or_insert_with(|| materials.coarsest_evictable_needs(&eviction_rank));
        if needs.get(&class).is_none_or(|&coarsest| coarsest <= needed) {
            continue;
        }
    }
    self.requests.push(MipRequest {
        texture,
        levels: needed..resident,
    });
}
self.requests
    .sort_unstable_by_key(|request| (request.levels.start, request.texture.0));

MipPriority::plan, src/streaming/mips.rs.

The IO pool's threads serve these requests from the same channel as the Chunk streamer's, as an IoRequest::Mips. decode_mips reads the Texture's Blob from the Cooked cache and decompresses only the requested levels through CookedTexture::mips_from_blob; the Blob's bytes count in bytes_read. A need that grows finer while a request is in flight waits for that request to be applied, then asks for the rest. A move whose Channel has no room for its class is requested only when some Texture whose chain lies in that class could be evicted for it, by the rules of Eviction; the first such move in a plan has the Material pool list, once, the coarsest evictable need per Channel and class. A Texture whose move would find no room is counted late and asked about again next frame, so a Texture behind the camera whose need coarsens lets it in without any read spent on it meanwhile. A Texture whose read failed is not requested again until the Material pool next frees an entry.

4 · MaterialPool::stream_mips

The move

A Texture whose mips arrive moves to the Texture class of its mips from the finest: the new mips are written into a slice there, its Resident mips are copied after them on the GPU, and every loaded Material that samples it is written again with the new Layer. The tail's slice stays taken; an earlier chain's slice is given back.

let Some(target) = channel.place(device, target_shape) else {
    return StreamedMips::Refused;
};
self.bind_group_is_stale |= channel.views.len() != classes_before;
let target_array = &channel.textures[target.class as usize];
for (level, bytes) in levels.iter().enumerate() {
    staging.write_texture(target_array, level as u32, target.slice, bytes);
}
// …
let source = loaded.placement.chain.unwrap_or(loaded.placement.tail);
let source_array = &channel.textures[source.class as usize];
staging.copy_texture(
    source_array,
    source.slice,
    0,
    target_array,
    target.slice,
    levels.len() as u32,
);
if let Some(chain) = loaded.placement.chain.replace(target) {
    channel.table.release(chain);
}
loaded.resident_level = first_level;
self.write_layer(staging, id, texture_channel, target.layer());

MaterialPool::stream_mips, src/assets/material.rs.

Arrivals are applied finest-needed first, then in Asset ID order, each only while the frame's writes into the load staging stay within its load bytes, the share of the staging Budget a frame's Chunk loads also take; the rest wait for a later frame. Arrivals whose Texture is gone, or whose finest Resident mip is no longer the one they end before, are dropped as stale. The writes, copies and Material writes go on the load's encoder, which the frame submits before its passes, so the frame that applies a move draws with it. A class the move opens marks the material bind group stale, and the frame rebuilds it once before it draws.

Keeping the tail's slice charges the tail twice in resident_tails: 87,408 bytes for a 2,048² BC7 or BC5 Texture, 1.6% of its whole chain. In return a Texture always has its tail in a slice of its own, which an eviction falls back to by rewriting its Layer, without a copy.

5 · MaterialPool::evict_for · MipPriority::eviction_rank

Eviction

A move whose Channel has no room for its class first evicts one finest mip: of the Textures whose chain lies in that very class, the one with the coarsest need goes, the least recently used breaking ties, so the slice it gives back is the one the move takes. A finest mip stays until its Texture's need coarsens by two mips, a Texture never loses a mip it needs, and a Resident tail never leaves.

let finest_mip_needed = needed.get(&texture).copied().unwrap_or(u32::MAX);
let evictable =
    finest_mip_needed > first_level && finest_mip_needed >= resident_level.saturating_add(2);
evictable.then(|| EvictionRank {
    finest_mip_needed,
    last_used_plan: last_used_plan.get(&texture).copied().unwrap_or(0),
})

rank, behind MipPriority::eviction_rank, src/streaming/mips.rs.

The capacity is the Channel's Texture classes, which PoolCapacities::from_budgets sets from the texture pool's Budget, and a class keeps its slices once it opens. So a move finds room only in a class of its own shape: a released slice, a free one in the newest class of that shape, or a new class while the Channel holds fewer than its capacity. When it finds none, the candidates are the Textures of the same Channel whose chain, their mips from the finest Resident one, has the move's shape; a 1,024² Texture at mip 0 and a 2,048² one at mip 1 of the same format share it. Each is ranked by the finest mip it needed at the last plan, u32::MAX when no drawn Placement samples it, as for a Texture whose Chunks unloaded while its Material stays loaded, and by the last plan whose need reached its finest Resident mip: when that mip was last used. The coarsest need goes first, then the least recently used, then the lowest Asset ID, so a Settled frame evicts the same Texture every run.

A candidate is ranked only when its need is coarser than the mip the move brings, so a move never evicts a Texture that needs as fine a mip as its own, and only when its need is at least two mips coarser than its finest Resident one. A mip loads when it is needed, so a Texture whose finest Resident mip is p loaded it at a need of mip p; it is evicted only once the need is mip p + 2 or coarser, which in priority, where a coarser mip ranks lower, is the ADR's "below p − 1": a Placement swaying across one mip's boundary never evicts and reloads it. And it is taken only when it keeps every mip it needs: when the Channel has room for the class of its mips from the next, or when it needs nothing finer than its Resident tail (MaterialPool::evictable).

let coarser_chain = if next_level < tail_level && channel.table.has_room(&[next_shape]) {
    channel.place(device, next_shape)
} else {
    None
};
self.bind_group_is_stale |= channel.views.len() != classes_before;
if let Some(target) = coarser_chain {
    staging.copy_texture(
        &channel.textures[chain.class as usize],
        chain.slice,
        1,
        &channel.textures[target.class as usize],
        target.slice,
        0,
    );
}
channel.table.release(chain);
loaded.placement.chain = coarser_chain;

MaterialPool::evict_finest_mip, src/assets/material.rs.

The evicted Texture drops exactly its finest mip when the Channel has room for the class of its mips from the next: its chain is copied from its mip 1 on into a slice there, on the GPU. When that next mip starts its Resident tail, or there is no room and it needs nothing finer than its tail, its Layer points back at its tail's slice, which it never gave up, with nothing copied. Either way its chain's slice is given back, then the move writes into it on the same encoder, after the copy out of it, and each loaded Material that samples the evicted Texture is written again with its Layer. A move that finds no candidate after all, as when an earlier move of the same frame took the slice, is refused and logged, and asked about again next frame.

6 · MipStreaming::stream

Settled frames and late mips

A Settled frame waits until no mip request is in flight, and while settling applies arrivals only once nothing at all is in flight, in that same order, so which slices and classes Textures take never depends on the IO pool's timing. The late mips a frame counts are, per Texture, how many mips its need is finer than its finest Resident one.

if !self.settle {
    self.io.receive();
    self.apply(streamed);
} else if self.io.in_flight == 0 {
    self.apply(streamed);
}

MipStreaming::stream, src/streaming/io.rs. While settling, the Chunk streamer's own take of the IO pool's finished reads, earlier in the same pass, is the one this reads.

The Settled wait loops until the Chunk streamer reports the frame Settled and no mip request is in flight, waiting on the IO pool between passes:

let mips_settled = mip_streaming.stream(world, &mut streamed);
if !settle || (frame.settled && mips_settled) {
    return Ok(streamed);
}

ChunkStreaming::update, src/streaming/io.rs.

A Settled frame's Textures therefore hold every mip its Placements need from the camera, by a footprint no larger than any pixel's of a 1440p view, and a capture smaller than that samples no finer: the Goldens render bit-exact against images drawn with every mip loaded. A window never waits, nor the bench from its counter frames on, so a mip that is late shows as its coarser mips, magnified, until it arrives.

8 · praxis bench

What it costs

No Baseline measures a streaming frame: the bench settles its frames before the counter frames, so every Reference scene's Textures have finished streaming by then, and every counter equals its Baseline on every scene. A frame with nothing to stream costs a pass over the loaded Chunks' uses.

before mip streaming264.1269.1280.5238.5
with mip streaming257.2260.9266.6236.5

Release builds on the Apple M4 Pro at 2560×1440, 1,800 measured frames each, the two builds interleaved over four rounds while the one-minute load average stood between 2.8 and 3.8. The GPU frame median was 2,299 to 2,304 µs in both. texture-wall's Textures are 4×4 and wholly Resident tail, so it measures only the per-frame planning over its four Chunks; the difference between the builds lies within the spread of the rounds.

The per-frame work is one distance, one logarithm and one map entry per use, a use per Placement and Texture that had mips to stream when its Chunk loaded; its map and request list are cleared each frame, not dropped, so a frame with nothing to request allocates nothing. Building a Chunk's uses visits each of its Placements once, on the first frame that sees it.

9 · ADR 0017

Why this way

A mip at a time, not tail or whole. ADR 0017 streams Textures a whole mip at a time. A Texture held to its finest needed mip rather than its whole chain keeps about a quarter of the chain's bytes when it needs mip 1, as mip 0 is three quarters of it, and each nearer step adds one class's move.

On the CPU, from distance and texel density. ADR 0017 computes priority on the CPU from distance and the Primitive's UV texel density against the 1440p pixel footprint, with no GPU feedback until a measured miss rate asks for it. Taken at the narrowest pixel and the nearest point, the estimate errs fine; the Limits below name where a GPU can still sample finer.

Every drawn Placement, frustum aside. A need that followed the view would drop and return as the camera turned, and a turn would show late mips; counting every drawn Placement of the loaded Chunks spends memory instead, which eviction, under the texture pool's Budget, bounds.

Exactly the missing mips, once. A request reads only the mips between the finest needed and the finest Resident, and the Resident ones move by a copy on the GPU, never read or written through the belt again. One request per Texture in flight keeps a need that grows while it travels from reading the same mips twice.

The tail's slice kept. Releasing the tail's slice after a move would save 1.6% of a chain; keeping it gives eviction a fallback that needs no copy, and every Texture a slice it is never without.

Room known before the read. A request is made only when its move would find room, so a full Channel costs no Blob reads that end refused, and a Texture waiting for room is let in the frame an eviction becomes possible, not when the Material pool next frees.

One mip down, then the tail. ADR 0017 evicts the finest mip of the lowest-priority Texture, least recently used breaking ties, and loads a mip at priority p to evict it only below p − 1. Dropping one mip keeps the evicted Texture as sharp as the room allows; dropping it to its tail when the next class is full costs no copy and still frees the slice the move needs. A victim never loses a mip it needs, so it asks for nothing back and evictions do not chase one another; one that would have to fall back past its need is passed over instead.

10

Limits

Chunks only. Renderables outside Chunks give no priority: a spawn loads its Textures whole, and a Texture a Chunk loaded first and a spawn also samples streams by the Chunks' Placements alone.

Room by shape. Classes never close, so an eviction frees room only for a move of its victim's chain's shape; a move of a shape no chain has is refused while the Channel holds its capacity of classes, however many lower-priority mips are Resident.

Every loaded Texture per eviction. Finding the candidates looks through every loaded Texture, at each eviction and once per plan while a move waits for room in a full class, and a candidate that needs a mip finer than its tail costs a look through its Channel's classes.

A failed read waits for a free. A Texture whose read fails is not requested again until the Material pool next frees an entry.

The whole Blob per request. A request reads the Texture's whole Blob from the Cooked cache and decompresses only the levels it asked for; the Blob keeps each mip a byte range of its own, which a ranged read would take alone.

Every Material per move. A move looks through every loaded Material for those that sample its Texture.

A mean density. The texel density is the Primitive's mean over its triangles, and the sphere and the larger side treat its mapping as even: where its UVs are denser than their mean, or stretched along one axis, the GPU can sample finer than the mip requested, and shows the finest Resident one magnified there.

A rounded ratio. A GPU may round the ratio of anisotropy up before it divides by it, as Vulkan allows an implementation to use its sample count ⌈η⌉ for η. Within the cap that can pick up to one mip finer than the one requested, where the finest Resident mip then shows magnified.

Unbounded requests. Every Texture whose need grows is requested in the same frame, ahead of Chunks requested after it on the same channel.

History

Textures first loaded every mip at once, then their Resident tails first, moved to their whole chains' classes in the same load, with a Render switch that held every Texture to its tail. The Mesh Blob gained each Primitive's UV texel density for a priority not yet computed. Mip streaming then split the two steps across frames: a Chunk's Textures arrive as their tails, and the mips their Placements need follow by priority, with the late mips counted. Eviction followed, so that a move into a full Channel takes a finest mip from a Texture that needs it less. This page describes the mechanism as it is.
