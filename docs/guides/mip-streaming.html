Mip streaming

The mechanism that brings a Texture's mips finer than its Resident tail into GPU memory as the camera nears: each frame it finds the finest mip any Placement of a drawn or held Chunk or Far proxy needs, from its distance a lead ahead of the camera and its Primitive's UV texel density, requests the missing mips from the IO pool finest-needed first, and moves each Texture whose mips arrive to the Texture class of its new finest mip, first evicting the finest mip of the lowest-priority Texture when its Channel has no room for that class. A Chunk's Textures arrive as their tails, a Far proxy's down to the mip its distance needed when it was requested; the mips a drawn Placement needs from the camera and lacks are the frame's late mips.

Describes main at 62052fe8 (2026-11-21). A change to mip streaming rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: Texture cooking for the Blob, the Resident tail and the Texture classes a load writes into; The Chunk streamer for the IO pool, the Chunks and Far proxies it holds, and Settled frames.

Interface

Runs
Once a frame, in ChunkStreaming::update after the frame's Chunk and Far proxy loads; with a Settled wait, again until no mip request is in flight. Off when the residency Render switch holds every Texture to its tail, and a Far proxy is then read as its tails too. The IO pool's threads read and decode the mips beside the frame, after every Chunk read queued and before any Far proxy read.
Reads
The camera's World position, where it was at the last plan and whether it teleported; the World's LoadedChunks and ResidentFarProxies, each drawn or held, and the coordinates of the Chunks drawn; each Placement's World transform, Mesh and Material; each Mesh's bounds, Primitive texel densities and Materials, from LoadedMeshes; each Material's Textures and each Texture's shape and finest Resident mip, from the Material pool; for an eviction, each candidate's need and the last plan that needed its finest Resident mip. For a Far proxy's read, the camera's position when it was requested.
Writes
Requests to the IO pool, one per Texture, for the mips from the finest needed to the finest Resident. For each Texture whose mips arrive, through the load staging: the mips into a slice of the class of its mips from the finest, its Resident mips copied after them on the GPU, and each loaded Material that samples it with its new Layer. For each eviction, the evicted Texture's chain copied from its mip 1 on into a slice one mip coarser, or its Layer pointed back at its tail, and its Materials written again.
Counted
Frame stats' late_mips, which the bench's Counters sum over their frames; the Blob bytes each request read, in bytes_read; the bytes its moves write into the load staging, and those their copies move, in uploaded_bytes; its time within the CPU stage stream_chunks.

1 · decode_chunk · down_to_from_blob

The tail first, a Far proxy's to its need

A Texture a Chunk names reaches the GPU as its Resident tail alone: the IO pool decodes only the tail's levels from its Blob, and the load writes them into a slice of the tail's own Texture class. A Far proxy's atlas, read for the camera's position at its request, arrives with its mips down to the one its distance from there needs, never coarser than its tail. Finer mips come later, when a Placement needs them.

let far_proxy_camera = camera.filter(|_| load == Load::FarProxy);
for texture_id in textures {
    if claim(texture_id) {
        let blob = read(texture_id)?;
        let texture = match (far_proxy_camera, densest_uses.get(&texture_id)) {
            (
                Some(camera),
                Some(&DensestUse {
                    texel_density,
                    bounds,
                }),
            ) => {
                let transform = far_proxy_transform(coordinates);
                CookedTexture::down_to_from_blob(&blob, |side| {
                    placement_finest_mip_needed(camera, &transform, bounds, texel_density, side)
                })
            }
            _ => CookedTexture::resident_tail_from_blob(&blob),
        };
        let texture = texture.with_context(|| format!("reading Texture {texture_id}"))?;
        assets.textures.insert(texture_id, texture);
    }
}

decode_chunk, src/streaming/io.rs.

The tail is the mips from the first of 64 KiB or less, as resident_tail_level finds it from the Blob's format, size and mip count; a CookedTexture holds its mips from first_level on. ChannelTextures::push writes whatever mips it is given: the tail into the tail's class, then any finer ones into the class of its mips from its finest, with the tail copied after them. So a Texture read whole, as a spawn reads it from the Cooked cache, loads every mip at once, a Chunk's stops at its tail, and a Far proxy's at its need. A Chunk load that reads a Texture on the main thread, because a read still in flight claimed it, reads its tail alone too (DecodedReader), so a Chunk never brings a Texture whole. A Texture whose mip 0 is 64 KiB or less is all tail and never streams.

A Far proxy's need is the one priority gives a drawn use (placement_finest_mip_needed, section 2): from the camera's position when the proxy was requested, scene-local, to the sphere around its Mesh's bounds placed at far_proxy_transform, with the densest texel density of any of its Primitives whose Material samples the Texture, and the Texture's side. The Chunk streamer's FrameIo passes that position for a Far proxy alone, and only while mips stream. A proxy that loads where it is drawn, as when the Far field fills after a Fast travel, is drawn from the mips it needs on its first frame, without a request; one that takes over from its Chunk at the Load radius holds the mips it needs there. most_written_bytes sizes the load from the decoded Textures, so a frame's load bytes count the mips a proxy brings.

2 · finest_mip_needed · MipPriority::plan

The finest mip a Placement needs

A surface needs the mip whose texels are no larger than a pixel's footprint on it. The footprint is taken at the narrowest pixel of a 16:9 view 1,440 pixels tall, at the nearest point of the sphere around the Placement's Mesh, so neither trilinear filtering nor anisotropic filtering with an unrounded ratio samples a finer mip than the one requested there. Requests judge it from where the camera will be a lead ahead; late mips from where it is.

fn finest_pixel_metres_per_metre() -> f64 {
    let half_height = (f64::from(FIELD_OF_VIEW) / 2.0).tan();
    let corner_squared = half_height.powi(2) * (1.0 + PRIORITY_VIEW_ASPECT_RATIO.powi(2));
    2.0 * half_height / PRIORITY_VIEW_HEIGHT_PIXELS / (1.0 + corner_squared)
}

fn finest_mip_needed(texels_per_metre: f64, distance_metres: f64) -> u32 {
    let texels_per_pixel = texels_per_metre * distance_metres * finest_pixel_metres_per_metre();
    texels_per_pixel.max(1.0).log2().floor() as u32
}

src/streaming/mips.rs.

A pixel on the view axis spans 2 tan(θ/2) / 1440 radians, 0.0005753 at the 45° field of view: that many metres per metre of distance. A pixel off the axis spans less, by the square of the cosine of its angle off it, and the corners are farthest off: there tan² of that angle is tan²(θ/2) (1 + (16/9)²), so the corner pixel spans 1.7138 times less. Times the distance that is the pixel's footprint on a surface facing it, which is also the shorter axis of its footprint on a surface at any slant. The bound rests on Vulkan's LOD formula: λbase = log2(ρmax/η), with η = min(ρmax/ρmin, maxAniso), ρmax and ρmin the footprint's longer and shorter axes in texels. Within the anisotropy cap that is log2(ρmin), the shorter axis; past it, and with no anisotropy, coarser. So no filter reads finer than the face-on footprint gives, and trilinear reads the mip of the floor of λ, and the one after it.

Texels per metre come from the Primitive's UV texel density, UV units per metre of the Mesh, times the larger side of the Texture's mip 0, over the Placement's smallest scale. The distance is to the nearest point of the sphere around the Mesh's bounds, placed and scaled as the Placement is, and none when the camera is inside it. Each use gives two needs:

for texture_use in uses.iter() {
    let distance_from = |position: DVec3| {
        distance_to_sphere(position, texture_use.centre, texture_use.radius)
    };
    let distance = distance_from(camera);
    let closing = camera_before.map_or(0.0, |before| distance_from(before) - distance);
    let lead_metres = (closing.max(0.0) * LEAD_FRAMES).min(CHUNK_EDGE_METRES);
    let distance_ahead = (distance - lead_metres).max(0.0);
    let texels_per_metre = texture_use.texels_per_metre;
    let ahead = finest_mip_needed(texels_per_metre, distance_ahead);
    let drawn = match sight {
        Sight::Drawn => finest_mip_needed(texels_per_metre, distance),
        Sight::Ahead | Sight::Replaced => u32::MAX,
    };
    let need = self.needed.entry(texture_use.texture).or_insert(Need {
        ahead: u32::MAX,
        drawn: u32::MAX,
    });
    need.ahead = need.ahead.min(ahead);
    need.drawn = need.drawn.min(drawn);
}

MipPriority::plan, src/streaming/mips.rs.

The lead is how far the camera closed on the use's sphere since the last plan, times 16 frames, the Chunk streamer's least lead (LEAST_LEAD_FRAMES), and at most a Chunk edge, 256 m, as the Chunk streamer requests nothing further than one past its radius; a camera moving away gets none, nor one that teleported this frame. The need from there, needed, is what requests and eviction read. A drawn use also gives its need from the camera itself, drawn, which only the late mips read. A Texture's need of each kind is the finest any of its uses gives.

The uses are built once per loaded Chunk or Resident Far proxy, the first frame that sees it: for each Placement, each Primitive's Material, or the Placement's own, and each Texture of it that has mips finer than those Resident. A Far proxy is a Chunk of one Placement. A Placement whose Materials have nothing to stream costs no transform, and an unloaded Chunk's or proxy's uses are dropped. How each is seen this frame, its Sight, decides what its uses give: a drawn one (Sight::Drawn) both needs; one held past its radius, a Chunk past the Load radius or a Far proxy past the View distance, where the camera may come to draw it (Sight::Ahead), its need from a lead ahead alone, so its mips are requested and never late; and a Far proxy held while its Chunk is drawn in its place (Sight::Replaced) nothing. Which Chunks and proxies are held, and why, is on The Chunk streamer. Every one counts, in front of the camera or behind it.

3 · MipPriority::plan · decode_mips

Requests

A Texture whose need is finer than its finest Resident mip is requested once, for exactly the mips between the two, when its move would find room, and the frame's requests go to the IO pool finest-needed first, then by Asset ID. A Texture with a request in flight is not requested again. The IO pool takes them after every Chunk read queued and before any Far proxy read.

for (&texture, &needed) in needed_by_texture {
    let Some((_, resident)) = materials.texture_residency(texture) else {
        continue;
    };
    if needed >= resident {
        continue;
    }
    if self.in_flight.contains(&texture) || self.refused.contains(&texture) {
        continue;
    }
    if let Some(class) = materials.full_class_for_mips(texture, needed) {
        let needs = evictable_needs
            .get_or_insert_with(|| materials.coarsest_evictable_needs(&eviction_rank));
        if needs.get(&class).is_none_or(|&coarsest| coarsest <= needed) {
            continue;
        }
    }
    self.requests.push(MipRequest {
        texture,
        levels: needed..resident,
    });
}
self.requests
    .sort_unstable_by_key(|request| (request.levels.start, request.texture.0));

MipPriority::plan, src/streaming/mips.rs.

The IO pool's threads serve these requests from the same queue as the Chunk streamer's, as an IoRequest::Mips, in a lane of their own: a thread takes every queued Chunk read first, then mips, then Far proxy reads, each lane in the order requested (Lane), so a drawn Chunk's mips wait behind no Far proxy's decode. decode_mips reads the Texture's Blob from the Cooked cache and decompresses only the requested levels through CookedTexture::mips_from_blob; the Blob's bytes count in bytes_read. A need that grows finer while a request is in flight waits for that request to be applied, then asks for the rest. A move whose Channel has no room for its class is requested only when some Texture whose chain lies in that class could be evicted for it, by the rules of Eviction; the first such move in a plan has the Material pool list, once, the coarsest evictable need per Channel and class. A Texture whose move would find no room is asked about again next frame, so a Texture behind the camera whose need coarsens lets it in without any read spent on it meanwhile. A Texture whose read failed is not requested again until the Material pool next frees an entry.

4 · MaterialPool::stream_mips

The move

A Texture whose mips arrive moves to the Texture class of its mips from the finest: the new mips are written into a slice there, its Resident mips are copied after them on the GPU, and every loaded Material that samples it is written again with the new Layer. The tail's slice stays taken; an earlier chain's slice is given back.

let Some(target) = channel.place(device, target_shape) else {
    return StreamedMips::Refused;
};
self.bind_group_is_stale |= channel.views.len() != classes_before;
let target_array = &channel.textures[target.class as usize];
for (level, bytes) in levels.iter().enumerate() {
    staging.write_texture(target_array, level as u32, target.slice, bytes);
}
// …
let source = loaded.placement.chain.unwrap_or(loaded.placement.tail);
let source_array = &channel.textures[source.class as usize];
staging.copy_texture(
    source_array,
    source.slice,
    0,
    target_array,
    target.slice,
    levels.len() as u32,
);
if let Some(chain) = loaded.placement.chain.replace(target) {
    channel.table.release(chain);
}
loaded.resident_level = first_level;
self.write_layer(staging, id, texture_channel, target.layer());

MaterialPool::stream_mips, src/assets/material.rs.

Arrivals are applied finest-needed first, then in Asset ID order, each while the frame's writes into the load staging, its Chunk and Far proxy loads' among them, stay within its load bytes, or while nothing has been written yet; the rest wait for a later frame. A Settled wait applies them all. Arrivals whose Texture is gone, or whose finest Resident mip is no longer the one they end before, are dropped as stale. The writes, copies and Material writes go on the load's encoder, which the frame submits before its passes, so the frame that applies a move draws with it. A move whose class the table has just opened creates that class's texture array, the only time an array is created; a released slice reuses its class's. The new class marks the material bind group stale, and the frame rebuilds it once before it draws.

Keeping the tail's slice charges the tail twice in resident_tails: 87,408 bytes for a 2,048² BC7 or BC5 Texture, 1.6% of its whole chain. In return a Texture always has its tail in a slice of its own, which an eviction falls back to by rewriting its Layer, without a copy.

5 · MaterialPool::evict_for · MipPriority::eviction_rank

Eviction

A move whose Channel has no room for its class first evicts one finest mip: of the Textures whose chain lies in that very class, the one with the coarsest need goes, the least recently used breaking ties, so the slice it gives back is the one the move takes. A finest mip stays until its Texture's need coarsens by two mips, a Texture never loses a mip it needs, and a Resident tail never leaves.

let finest_mip_needed = needed.get(&texture).map_or(u32::MAX, |need| need.ahead);
let evictable =
    finest_mip_needed > first_level && finest_mip_needed >= resident_level.saturating_add(2);
evictable.then(|| EvictionRank {
    finest_mip_needed,
    last_used_plan: last_used_plan.get(&texture).copied().unwrap_or(0),
})

rank, behind MipPriority::eviction_rank, src/streaming/mips.rs.

The capacity is the Channel's Texture classes, which PoolCapacities::from_budgets sets from the texture pool's Budget, and a class keeps its slices once it opens. So a move finds room only in a class of its own shape: a released slice, a free one in the newest class of that shape, or a new class while the Channel holds fewer than its capacity. When it finds none, the candidates are the Textures of the same Channel whose chain, their mips from the finest Resident one, has the move's shape; a 1,024² Texture at mip 0 and a 2,048² one at mip 1 of the same format share it, and so do a Chunk's Texture and a Far proxy's atlas of one shape: a proxy's Material takes an entry of the Material table's own count for Far proxies, but its Textures share the Channel's classes. Each is ranked by its need from a lead ahead at the last plan, u32::MAX when nothing drawn or held ahead samples it, as for a Texture whose Chunks unloaded while its Material stays loaded, and by the last plan whose need reached its finest Resident mip: when that mip was last used. The coarsest need goes first, then the least recently used, then the lowest Asset ID, so a Settled frame evicts the same Texture every run.

A candidate is ranked only when its need is coarser than the mip the move brings, so a move never evicts a Texture that needs as fine a mip as its own, and only when its need is at least two mips coarser than its finest Resident one. A mip loads when it is needed, so a Texture whose finest Resident mip is p loaded it at a need of mip p; it is evicted only once the need is mip p + 2 or coarser, which in priority, where a coarser mip ranks lower, is the ADR's "below p − 1": a Placement swaying across one mip's boundary never evicts and reloads it. And it is taken only when it keeps every mip it needs: when the Channel has room for the class of its mips from the next, or when it needs nothing finer than its Resident tail (MaterialPool::evictable).

let coarser_chain = if next_level < tail_level && channel.table.has_room(&[next_shape]) {
    channel.place(device, next_shape)
} else {
    None
};
self.bind_group_is_stale |= channel.views.len() != classes_before;

MaterialPool::evict_finest_mip, src/assets/material.rs.

The evicted Texture drops exactly its finest mip when the Channel has room for the class of its mips from the next: its chain is copied from its mip 1 on into a slice there, on the GPU. When that next mip starts its Resident tail, or there is no room and it needs nothing finer than its tail, its Layer points back at its tail's slice, which it never gave up, with nothing copied. Either way its chain's slice is given back, then the move writes into it on the same encoder, after the copy out of it, and each loaded Material that samples the evicted Texture is written again with its Layer. A move that finds no candidate after all, as when an earlier move of the same frame took the slice, is refused and logged, and asked about again next frame.

6 · MipStreaming::stream

Settled frames and late mips

A Settled frame waits until no mip request is in flight, and while settling applies arrivals only once nothing at all is in flight, in that same order, so which slices and classes Textures take never depends on the IO pool's timing. The late mips a frame counts are, per Texture its drawn uses sample, how many mips their need from the camera is finer than its finest Resident one.

if !self.settle {
    self.io.receive();
    self.apply(streamed);
} else if self.io.in_flight == 0 {
    self.apply(streamed);
}

MipStreaming::stream, src/streaming/io.rs. While settling, the streamers' own take of the IO pool's finished reads, earlier in the same pass, is the one this reads.

The Settled wait loops until the Chunk streamer and the Far proxies' report the frame Settled and no mip request is in flight, waiting on the IO pool between passes:

let mips_settled = mip_streaming.stream(world, &mut streamed);
if !settle || (settled && mips_settled) {
    return Ok(streamed);
}

ChunkStreaming::update, src/streaming/io.rs.

The late mips read only the drawn needs, whatever was requested ahead:

for (&texture, need) in &self.needed {
    let Some((_, resident)) = materials.texture_residency(texture) else {
        continue;
    };
    late_mips += resident.saturating_sub(need.drawn);

MipPriority::plan, src/streaming/mips.rs.

A Settled frame's Textures therefore hold every mip its drawn Placements need from the camera, by a footprint no larger than any pixel's of a 1440p view, wherever their Channels had room, and a capture smaller than that samples no finer: the Goldens render bit-exact against images drawn with every mip loaded. A window never waits, nor the bench after its counter frames, so a mip that is late shows as its coarser mips, magnified, until it arrives. A mip requested ahead for a held Chunk or proxy is never late; a drawn one's need finer than what a lead ahead brought, as when the camera speeds up, is.

9 · ADRs 0016, 0017 and 0026

Why this way

A mip at a time, not tail or whole. ADR 0017 streams Textures a whole mip at a time. A Texture held to its finest needed mip rather than its whole chain keeps about a quarter of the chain's bytes when it needs mip 1, as mip 0 is three quarters of it, and each nearer step adds one class's move.

On the CPU, from distance and texel density. ADR 0017 computes priority on the CPU from distance and the Primitive's UV texel density against the 1440p pixel footprint, with no GPU feedback until a measured miss rate asks for it. Taken at the narrowest pixel and the nearest point, the estimate errs fine; the Limits below name where a GPU can still sample finer.

Every drawn or held Placement, frustum aside. A need that followed the view would drop and return as the camera turned, and a turn would show late mips; counting every Placement of the drawn and held Chunks and Far proxies spends memory instead, which eviction, under the texture pool's Budget, bounds.

Held ahead, at the full need. Unreal's streamer prestreams the textures of components not yet visible, one mip coarser than they need. Here a held Chunk or Far proxy asks its full need: a proxy held past the View distance needs, from there, the mip it needs once drawn at the View distance, so one mip coarser would leave each late by a mip on the frame it is drawn (docs/audit/deviations.md, ADR 0017). The late mips read only the drawn uses, so what is fetched ahead never counts as late.

A lead ahead, at most a Chunk edge. A request judged from where the camera is lands a read's time after the camera needs it; judged from where the camera, closing at its last frame's rate, will be the Chunk streamer's least lead later, it lands before, by the rule the Chunk streamer loads Chunks by. The cap keeps a camera that jumped without a teleport, as across frames run without streaming, from asking every surface on its path for mip 0, and eviction ranks by the same need, so a mip fetched ahead is not evicted as unneeded (ADR 0017).

A Far proxy read to its need. Read as its tails, a proxy that loads and is drawn on one frame, as the Far field fills after a Fast travel, is drawn from its tail for the frame its own request takes. Reading its atlas down to the mip its distance needs makes the read larger instead, which the IO pool's lanes keep off the Chunk reads (ADR 0026).

Chunk reads, then mips, then Far proxies. A Far proxy read decodes its atlas's chain, 5.3 MiB for a 2,048² BC7 atlas, which would otherwise hold back the Chunks a Fast travel waits for and a drawn Chunk's mips; so the IO pool takes Chunk reads first, then mips, then Far proxy reads, a Far proxy's only while no Chunk read is taken and on all threads but one (ADR 0016).

Exactly the missing mips, once. A request reads only the mips between the finest needed and the finest Resident, and the Resident ones move by a copy on the GPU, never read or written through the belt again. One request per Texture in flight keeps a need that grows while it travels from reading the same mips twice.

The tail's slice kept. Releasing the tail's slice after a move would save 1.6% of a chain; keeping it gives eviction a fallback that needs no copy, and every Texture a slice it is never without.

Room known before the read. A request is made only when its move would find room, so a full Channel costs no Blob reads that end refused, and a Texture waiting for room is let in the frame an eviction becomes possible, not when the Material pool next frees.

One mip down, then the tail. ADR 0017 evicts the finest mip of the lowest-priority Texture, least recently used breaking ties, and loads a mip at priority p to evict it only below p − 1. Dropping one mip keeps the evicted Texture as sharp as the room allows; dropping it to its tail when the next class is full costs no copy and still frees the slice the move needs. A victim never loses a mip it needs, so it asks for nothing back and evictions do not chase one another; one that would have to fall back past its need is passed over instead.

Limits

Chunks and Far proxies only. Renderables outside them give no priority: a spawn loads its Textures whole, and a Texture a Chunk loaded first and a spawn also samples streams by the Chunks' Placements alone.

Uses fixed at first sight. A Chunk's or Far proxy's uses are built once, from the Textures that then have mips finer than those Resident. A Texture already Resident to mip 0 then, as a Far proxy's atlas read to mip 0 is, gives that Chunk or proxy no use: nothing it draws counts as sampling it, so eviction ranks it with the coarsest need, and once evicted its mips are not asked back while that Chunk or proxy stays loaded.

A Far proxy's need from its request. A proxy's atlas is read for where the camera was when the proxy was requested, at the densest texel density any of its Primitives samples it at and the sphere around its whole Mesh. A camera that closed on it since sees coarser mips until priority's request lands, and a Channel without room for the chain loads the tail alone.

A lead from one frame's rate. The lead extrapolates the last frame's closing speed: a camera that speeds up asks too late, and its drawn Placements count the difference as late mips until the reads land.

Room by shape. Classes never close, so an eviction frees room only for a move of its victim's chain's shape; a move of a shape no chain has is refused while the Channel holds its capacity of classes, however many lower-priority mips are Resident.

Every loaded Texture per eviction. Finding the candidates looks through every loaded Texture, at each eviction and once per plan while a move waits for room in a full class, and a candidate that needs a mip finer than its tail costs a look through its Channel's classes.

A failed read waits for a free. A Texture whose read fails is not requested again until the Material pool next frees an entry.

The whole Blob per request. A request reads the Texture's whole Blob from the Cooked cache and decompresses only the levels it asked for; the Blob keeps each mip a byte range of its own, which a ranged read would take alone.

Every Material per move. A move looks through every loaded Material for those that sample its Texture.

A mean density. The texel density is the Primitive's mean over its triangles, and the sphere and the larger side treat its mapping as even: where its UVs are denser than their mean, or stretched along one axis, the GPU can sample finer than the mip requested, and shows the finest Resident one magnified there.

A rounded ratio. A GPU may round the ratio of anisotropy up before it divides by it, as Vulkan allows an implementation to use its sample count ⌈η⌉ for η. Within the cap that can pick up to one mip finer than the one requested, where the finest Resident mip then shows magnified.

Rays at the Resident tail. A Material rays reach off screen holds the mips its distance from the camera needs, and at least its Resident tail, the floor ADR 0017 sets for rays. Shadow rays sample no Texture, and the tails of the Textures the Reference scenes stream span 10.4 to 15.6 mm a texel, no finer than a probe ray needs from 7 cm of its probe on at 256 rays a probe. An alpha-tested shadow ray would be the first to sample a Texture nearer its origin, and may need a finer floor.

Unbounded requests. Every Texture whose need grows is requested in the same frame, and each waits behind every Chunk read queued, those requested after it included.

History

Textures first loaded every mip at once, then their Resident tails first, moved to their whole chains' classes in the same load, with a Render switch that held every Texture to its tail. The Mesh Blob gained each Primitive's UV texel density for a priority not yet computed. On 2026-11-08 mip streaming split the two steps across frames: a Chunk's Textures arrive as their tails, and the mips their Placements need follow by priority, with the late mips counted; eviction followed the same day, so that a move into a full Channel takes a finest mip from a Texture that needs it less. From 2026-11-10 a Chunk's Texture read on the main thread is its tail too. Far proxies, whose Materials took a count of their own in the Material table on 2026-11-14 while their Textures share the Channels', streamed from 2026-11-16 and were drawn from 2026-11-18, their atlases' mips read by priority like a Chunk's. On 2026-11-20 held Chunks' and Far proxies' mips came to be requested ahead, each request judged a lead ahead of the camera, and a Far proxy's atlas came to be read with its load down to the mip its distance needs, the IO pool then taking Chunk reads, mips and Far proxy reads in that order. This page describes the mechanism as it is.
