The Reference scenes

The eleven Reference scenes are the engine's fixed inputs: each a Scene that code spawns the same way every time, a camera that is a function of the frame number, a frame delta that is a constant, and lights in photometric units, so that a capture, a Golden, a Baseline and a Cycles render all see the same frames.

Describes main at e2732b97 (2026-11-07), with every scene's static content loaded from its Chunks as their change leaves it. A change to a scene's content, lights, camera path or frame delta, or to the counts the tests pin, rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: Light units for the lux and candela the scenes' lights are given in, The bend pass for what bends the crowd, The dynamic TLAS for where the orrery's, the crowd's and the town's Moving Instances go.

Interface

Built
When a State is created for a scene: its spawn function fills the ECS World with what moves, from the Cooked cache through an AssetLoader; then load_chunks loads the Chunks chunk_coordinates lists, which hold what does not move and the point lights, into the LoadedChunks resource; then the State adds the scene's environment and its sun, and shifts every root, Placement and Chunk light by the capture's offset. Its Meshes, Materials and Textures were cooked from the generators in the same two files, under Asset IDs named by the scene.
Scenes
helmet, pbr-sweep, uv-grid, normal-tangent, far-coplanar, orrery, minification, texture-wall, shadows, crowd and town: ReferenceScene::ALL, in that order. Two load a fetched or committed glTF; nine are generated.
Camera
camera_at(frame, shift): a Camera from the frame number alone, moved by the shift, at the scene's EV100. Eight scenes hold still, pbr-sweep and uv-grid orbit the origin a quarter degree per frame, town walks a street, rises and circles.
Time
frame_delta(): 1/60 s per frame, the orrery's 1/144 s. The fixed schedule runs the Ticks those deltas complete; spin and sway move the orrery, the flocks and the crowd there, from the Tick count.
Lights
One white sun of 100,000 lux in every scene, with an ambient of a tenth of it and a blue background; four coloured point lights of 900,000 cd in six scenes, one white in shadows, one of 30,000,000 cd in minification, 1,024 lamps of 2,000,000 cd in town, none in texture-wall and crowd.
Used by
praxis capture and capture, which render 60 frames and read the last back; the Goldens test, which compares that frame with the committed image at 640×360; the bench (The bench), which times 1,800 frames at 2560×1440 after 248 of warm-up; the gallery, scripts/gallery.sh, which captures every scene at 2560×1440 for the visual audit; and the Cycles reference, scripts/cycles-reference/, which rebuilds what praxis export-scene writes of a scene at a frame and renders it in Blender.
Where in the code
src/scenes/mod.rs: the enum, the camera paths, the lights, each scene's Chunks and static_content, the spawns of what moves, the generators, the Spin and Sway systems. src/scenes/town.rs: the town. src/state.rs: the helmet scene's spawn and the dispatch, which loads each scene's Chunks after its spawn. src/capture/mod.rs: the capture.

What a Reference scene is

Nothing in a Reference scene reads the wall clock, the window or the machine. Its content comes from constants and a hash, its camera from the frame number, its time from a constant delta, so two captures of the same scene on the same adapter give the same bytes, and a Golden, a Baseline and a Cycles render all describe one frame.

ReferenceScene is an enum of eleven variants, listed in ALL; name gives each its command-line name and from_name finds a variant by it, which is how praxis capture, praxis bench, praxis export-scene and the gallery select a scene. The State that a capture, a bench run or the window creates spawns what moves in the scene by its spawn function, loads what does not move from the scene's Chunks, then adds what every scene shares: an Environment and a sun.

A scene's static content, each Renderable that does not move and each point light, is cooked into Chunks (Chunk cooking) from static_content, which gives each as a Placement or a light in World coordinates: placed makes a Placement unturned and unscaled, and cooked_lights turns a scene's light constants into Chunk lights. No entity holds them: load_chunks loads the Chunks chunk_coordinates names, in coordinate order, and prepare gives each Chunk its own range of Instance slots (Prepare). Each scene's Chunks are those its content lies in, which a test checks against the cook's split.

A Chunk is 256 m on a side, its origin at a multiple of 256 m on X and Z, so content around the World's origin falls into the four Chunks about it, or into one where all of it lies at or past the origin on both axes.

src/scenes/mod.rs
/// A camera on a circle of `radius` at `height` around the origin, looking at
/// it, starting a little left of +Z and moving a quarter degree per frame.
fn orbit(frame: u32, radius: f32, height: f32) -> Camera {
    let angle = (-7.5 + 0.25 * frame as f32).to_radians();
    let position = glam::vec3(radius * angle.sin(), height, radius * angle.cos());
    Camera::looking_at(position.as_dvec3(), glam::DVec3::ZERO)
}

The Golden is frame 59, so an orbiting scene's Golden sees its content from 7.25° right of +Z, the 59 steps to the Golden turning 14.75° in all. The capture injects time as well as the camera: every frame advances the State by the scene's frame_delta, 1/60 s for ten scenes, and for the orrery 6,944,444 ns, 1/144 s, which the code's comment intends to land each frame at a different point between Ticks, the last two thirds of the way from one Tick to the next; in whole nanoseconds the weights repeat every 9 frames (section 16). The fixed schedule runs the Ticks those deltas complete, and spin and sway move what moves (section 13).

Every scene exposes the same way: ev100 is ev100_for_exposure(1.0 / SUN_ILLUMINANCE), which scales the sun's 100,000 lux to 1 before tonemapping, EV100 16.35. The sun is white, of that illuminance, shining along (0.5, −1, −0.3) in every scene but the town, whose sun shines along (0.25, −0.55, −0.8), 33° up in the south-southwest behind the camera's street. The environment is the same everywhere: an ambient of 10,000 lux from all around, and a background of (0.1, 0.2, 0.3) × 100,000 cd/m². The point lights differ per scene, and all but the town's are literal constants.

2 · helmet_placements · State::spawn_helmet_scene, src/state.rs

helmet: the fetched model and a white sweep

The only scene whose source is fetched rather than committed: Khronos's SciFiHelmet, drawn once with its own textured Material and ten times more with a white factor sweep, so a real glTF model with four 2048² Textures runs through the cook and the loader. It has no Golden, since CI never has the model.

helmet_placements places the Mesh named SciFiHelmet, from the manifest source SCI_FI_HELMET by sci_fi_helmet_mesh_id, once for each of the sweep's ten Materials, metallic 0 and 1 by SWEEP_METALLIC against roughness 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9 by SWEEP_ROUGHNESS, with the white base colour HELMET_SWEEP_BASE_COLOR, each scaled to 0.75, in two rows 2.6 m above and below the origin, columns 2 m apart from −4 to 4 m, all 8 m along −Z from the origin, 10 m ahead of the camera. The eleventh helmet, at (0, 0, −8), draws with the Material its Primitive names, the textured one. The model is 2.9 units tall, which is why the copies shrink. The camera is Camera::new, at (0, 0, 2) looking along −Z, and does not move; the lights are the four coloured ones. Each helmet draws 23,358 triangles, so the frame draws 11 Instances and 256,938 triangles.

3 · pbr_sweep_placements

pbr-sweep: metallic against roughness

Ten spheres of one Mesh in two rows: a dielectric row and a metal row, roughness growing left to right, all in gold's base colour, so the two rows differ in where the colour goes, into the diffuse or into the highlight.

The sphere is scene_sphere(SPHERE_RADIUS), a UV sphere of radius 0.5 m with 48 segments around and 24 from pole to pole, 2,208 triangles, since each pole's ring has one triangle per quad. The ten Materials are the sweep over SWEEP_METALLIC and SWEEP_ROUGHNESS with SWEEP_BASE_COLOR, (1.0, 0.71, 0.29): gold's reflectance at normal incidence in linear sRGB from Hoffman's 2013 course notes, which the Sources name. pbr_sweep_placements places column c at (c − 2) × 1.3 m along X and the rows at ±0.65 m, by SPHERE_SPACING, the dielectric row above. Each sphere is its own Placement, whose Material overrides the Mesh's; the two columns left of the origin lie in one Chunk and the other three in the next.

pub(crate) fn spawn_orrery(world: &mut World, loader: &mut dyn AssetLoader) -> anyhow::Result<()> {
    let sphere = loader.mesh(ReferenceScene::Orrery.mesh_id("sphere"))?;
    let body = |radians_per_second| {

            Spin { radians_per_second },
            PreviousWorldTransform::default(),

        .spawn((Transform::IDENTITY, body(ORRERY_SPIN_RATES[0])))
        .id();

        };
        parent = world

            .id();
    }
    Ok(())

The Mesh is the same 2,208-triangle sphere as the sweep's, with the grid Texture on its one Primitive at roughness 0.5, so each sphere's spin shows. 8 · static_content · checker_texture · bump_texture · gloss_texture

minification: a glossy floor to the horizon

A 100 m checker floor seen from 1.2 m up at a grazing angle, with a bump normal map, a gloss map and one raking point light whose reflection lies 16 m out in the minified distance: the scene that shows mips, anisotropic filtering and Toksvig's widening of roughness, and fails without any of them.

Far away one pixel covers many texels, and reading one per pixel turns fine checks into moiré; mips, averaged in linear light by the cook (Texture cooking), give each pixel the average of what it covers. A pixel's footprint on a floor seen edge-on is long and thin, so the sampler reads the mip of the short side and takes samples along the long one. And mips that average a normal map flatten its bumps: the averaged normals point straight up, and a flat glossy floor is a mirror whose highlight is a hard line. The averaged normal's shortened length measures how much the normals disagreed, and the cook widens each mip of the roughness Texture by it, Toksvig's correction, so the distance scatters the light as the bumps would.

9 · spawn_texture_wall · texture_wall_placements · wall_texture

texture-wall: 10,000 Textures in one Channel

10,000 Textures of 4 × 4 texels resident in the albedo Channel, and a 10 × 10 grid of quads each with its own Material, sampling Layers across the whole range, so a Layer read one off shows the wrong colour and the frame pays what keeping 10,000 Textures bound costs.

A shader reads only the Textures bound before the draw; the engine binds each Channel as one array of Layers, so a Material stores Layer numbers and no draw swaps a texture. town: 254,319 Instances the same way every time

A procedural town of 1 × 1 km on a street grid at ADR 0013's target scale: 244,319 static Instances and 10,000 birds, the dynamic TLAS's capacity, with every random choice hashed from where it stands and what it decides, so every launch places the same town and a change to one choice moves only what that choice decides.

The hash

Every choice in the town, from whether a block is a park to what stands on each cell of a roof, is a value in [0, 1) hashed from four parts: the block's column and row, a Choice naming what the value decides, and an item number for which building, cell, side or car it is about. The same four parts always give the same value, whatever else is decided and in whatever order.

src/scenes/town.rs
/// A well-mixed 64-bit value of `seed`: SplitMix64's output function
/// (Steele, Lea and Flood, "Fast Splittable Pseudorandom Number
/// Generators", OOPSLA 2014), its constants from Sebastiano Vigna's
/// splitmix64.c, which is in the public domain.
fn mix(seed: u64) -> u64 {
    let mut value = seed.wrapping_add(0x9e37_79b9_7f4a_7c15);
    value = (value ^ (value >> 30)).wrapping_mul(0xbf58_476d_1ce4_e5b9);
    value = (value ^ (value >> 27)).wrapping_mul(0x94d0_49bb_1331_11eb);
    value ^ (value >> 31)
}

/// A value in [0, 1) from `parts`, the same for the same parts.
fn random(parts: [u64; 4]) -> f64 {
    let hash = parts.into_iter().fold(0, |hash, part| mix(hash ^ part));
    (hash >> 11) as f64 / (1u64 << 53) as f64
}

/// What a random value decides, so that no two values for one place share
/// a seed: each choice numbers its items apart.
#[derive(Clone, Copy)]
enum Choice {
    Park,
    Storeys,
    Plaster,
    RoofPlant,
    PlantYaw,
    StreetTreeYaw,
    ParkTreeYaw,
    Car,
    Bollards,
    Bench,
    Bin,
    Lamp,
    Flock,
    Bird,
}

mix is one step of SplitMix64 used as a hash: the golden-gamma add, two rounds of xor-shift and multiply, and a final xor-shift, so nearby seeds give values that look unrelated. random folds the four parts through it one at a time and keeps the hash's top 53 bits, as many as an f64 holds exactly, as a fraction of 1. A Block hashes with its own column and row, a flock with its column and row on the flocks' grid. The alternative, one stream of random numbers consumed in placement order, makes every value depend on how many were drawn before it: one more bench in the first block, or a loop visiting a block's sides in another order, would shift every value after it, and the town beyond the change would come out different, with new Goldens and Baselines for a change that touched one thing. Hashed from its own parts, a value depends on nothing else, so adding a kind of Renderable moves nothing already placed, and the town could be placed in parallel and come out the same. The one order that matters is Choice's: a value hashes its choice by its discriminant, so a new choice joins the end of the enum.

const RISE_FRAMES: u32 = 360;
/// In the avenue's right-hand lane, at eye height, and moving at 4 m/s.
const STREET_START: glam::DVec3 = glam::DVec3::new(2.0, 1.7, 445.0);
const STREET_SPEED: f64 = 4.0 / 60.0;
const ORBIT_RADIUS: f64 = 480.0;
const ORBIT_HEIGHT: f64 = 260.0;
/// Radians a frame round the circle, reached over `ORBIT_EASE_FRAMES`.
const ORBIT_SPEED: f64 = 0.0006;
const ORBIT_EASE_FRAMES: f64 = 120.0;

/// The camera at `frame` of its path.
pub(crate) fn camera_at(frame: u32) -> Camera {
    let frame = f64::from(frame);
    let street_end = STREET_START - glam::DVec3::Z * (STREET_SPEED * f64::from(STREET_FRAMES));
    let orbit_frame = frame - f64::from(STREET_FRAMES + RISE_FRAMES);
    if frame < f64::from(STREET_FRAMES) {
        let position = STREET_START - glam::DVec3::Z * (STREET_SPEED * frame);
        Camera::looking_at(position, position - glam::DVec3::Z)
    } else if orbit_frame < 0.0 {
        // A cubic from the street's end, leaving at the street's speed, to
        // the circle's start, arriving at rest; the gaze turns from far up
        // the avenue to the town's middle.
        let progress = (frame - f64::from(STREET_FRAMES)) / f64::from(RISE_FRAMES);
        let orbit_start = glam::dvec3(0.0, ORBIT_HEIGHT, ORBIT_RADIUS);
        let leaving = -glam::DVec3::Z * (STREET_SPEED * f64::from(RISE_FRAMES));
        let squared = progress * progress;
        let cubed = squared * progress;
        let arrived = 3.0 * squared - 2.0 * cubed;
        let position = street_end * (1.0 - arrived)
            + leaving * (cubed - 2.0 * squared + progress)
            + orbit_start * arrived;
        let up_the_avenue = street_end - glam::DVec3::Z * TOWN_HALF_SIZE;
        Camera::looking_at(position, up_the_avenue * (1.0 - arrived))
    } else {
        // At rest on arrival, then at `ORBIT_SPEED`, with no jolt between.
        let eased = if orbit_frame < ORBIT_EASE_FRAMES {
            orbit_frame * orbit_frame / (2.0 * ORBIT_EASE_FRAMES)
        } else {
            orbit_frame - ORBIT_EASE_FRAMES / 2.0
        };
        let angle = ORBIT_SPEED * eased;
        let position = glam::dvec3(
            ORBIT_RADIUS * angle.sin(),
            ORBIT_HEIGHT,
            ORBIT_RADIUS * angle.cos(),
        );
        Camera::looking_at(position, glam::DVec3::ZERO)
    }

13 · spin · sway · FixedTime

Motion without a clock

A Reference scene moves in the fixed schedule, from the Tick count, never from the wall clock: the orrery's bodies and the town's flock centres turn by Spin, the crowd's figures bend by Sway, and each sets its state for the end of the running Tick from the Tick's index, so no rounding accumulates and the same frame always shows the same state.

src/scenes/mod.rs
/// Sets each spinning entity's rotation for the end of the running Tick, from
/// the Tick count, so no rounding accumulates over Ticks.
pub(crate) fn spin(fixed_time: Res<FixedTime>, mut spinning: Query<(&Spin, &mut Transform)>) {
    let seconds = (fixed_time.tick + 1) as f64 * TICK_SECONDS;
    for (spin, mut transform) in &mut spinning {
        let angle = (spin.radians_per_second * seconds).rem_euclid(std::f64::consts::TAU);
        transform.rotation = glam::Quat::from_rotation_y(angle as f32);
    }
}

pub(crate) fn sway(
    fixed_time: Res<FixedTime>,
    mut figures: Query<(&Sway, &mut Bend, Option<&Children>)>,
    mut bones: Query<(&Bone, &mut Transform)>,
) {
    let seconds = (fixed_time.tick + 1) as f64 * TICK_SECONDS;
    for (sway, mut bend, children) in &mut figures {
        bend.curvature = sway.curvature(seconds);
        for &child in children.into_iter().flatten() {
            if let Ok((bone, mut transform)) = bones.get_mut(child) {
                *transform = bone_transform(bone.0, &bend);
            }
        }
    }
}

A capture advances the State by the scene's frame delta; FixedTime accumulates it and runs the whole Ticks it completes, 1/64 s each by TICK, at most four a frame, keeping the remainder as the frame's blend weight. Both systems run in the fixed schedule between record_previous_world_transforms, which keeps the Tick's starting state for the blend, and propagate_world_transforms, which composes each child's World transform from its parent's; the schedule refuses to build with any two conflicting systems left unordered, so the order is fixed, and nothing in it reads Time. A frame then draws every Moving Instance blended between the two newest Ticks by the weight, and the bend pass bends each figure by the curvature blended the same way. In whole nanoseconds, at 1/60 s a frame the blend weight steps by 1/15 and repeats every 15 frames; at the orrery's 1/144 s it steps by 4/9 and repeats every 9.

Why this way

Generated, not loaded. The test-asset policy in docs/research/test-assets.md puts the CI-gated Reference scenes in its first tier: procedural and in-repo content, with no licence, deterministic and tiny. Nine scenes build their Meshes and Textures by code at cook time and need no file; the two glTF scenes take the second tier, Khronos sample models under CC0, one committed at 1.8 MB and one of about 30 MB fetched, which is why the helmet has no Golden. The engine's real content will arrive as Placements in a streamed Game world; every scene's static content already loads as Placements from Chunks it cooks, through the path that world will stream, and the dense scene is generated, since the measurements could not wait for authored content.

A scene at target scale. ADR 0013 made its Budgets provisional until the first Baseline of a Reference scene at 100k visible Instances and about 5M visible triangles, and named the third milestone's dense scene as that scene. The town was built toward those two figures: enough blocks, windows, railing posts and roof plant that the Baseline's median counters round the circle clear both after culling, and the whole town with culling off, which sized the frame's draw at 225 packed indices. At the milestone's exit the Budgets were re-decided against its Baseline and kept.

A hash per placement, not a stream. A sequential generator makes every value depend on how many were drawn before it, so any change moves everything after it and a Golden cannot tell the change's own effect from the shuffle. Hashing each value from its place and its choice makes each placement independent: a new kind of Renderable moves nothing, and the town could be placed Chunk by Chunk. SplitMix64's output function is the mixer because it is a few multiplies and shifts with published constants and a public-domain reference.

A street pass, then an orbit, with no cuts. A Reference scene's figures are medians over its camera path, so the path decides what they measure. The street gives a Golden at eye level, where the lamps' pools, the windows and the wires are seen as a player would see them; the circle gives the bench a frame that sees most of the town, so the median frame measures the engine at scale rather than down one street. The cubic between them keeps position and speed continuous, so no frame of the bench is a cut, where culling phase one would find nothing of the frame before to draw.

Photometric lights, one exposure. ADR 0023 gives lights glTF's units: the sun in lux, the point lights in candela, the background in cd/m². The scenes give every other light against the sun's 100,000 lux, each lamp or lantern stated as the distance at which it matches the sun, and share one EV100 that exposes the sun to 1, so every Golden was lit as it was before the lights took units and no scene is tuned on its own.

The orrery's frame delta. ADR 0022's fixed 64 Hz simulation draws each frame blended between Ticks. The code's comment states the intent: at 144 Hz the orrery's frames land at a different point between Ticks each time, and the last lands two thirds of the way from one Tick to the next. What FixedTime::advance gives in whole nanoseconds is narrower than the first half of that: at 16,666,667 ns a frame the blend weight steps by 1/15 and repeats every 15 frames, reaching 0.933 at frame 13; at 6,944,444 ns it steps by 4/9 and repeats every 9 frames, so a 60-frame capture sees nine distinct weights, and its last frame does land two thirds of the way across a Tick.

The crowd's proxies. ADR 0020 traces shadows against BLASes, and a BLAS of a bent figure's rest Mesh would shadow the wrong shape. Rigid proxies per bone, Moving Instances in the dynamic TLAS, stand in until skinning arrives; the refit and rebuild forms exist to prove them right, and the default form is what ships.

Reversed depth and camera-relative positions. far-coplanar is ADR 0015's demonstration and would fail under standard depth; the 100 km shift in the Goldens test is ADR 0014's, in whole 64 m anchor cells so the shifted scene composes bit for bit as at the origin.

Limits

No Golden for the helmet. Its source is fetched, so CI never has it; the Goldens test runs its equivalence checks only where it is present.

No Cycles reference for the crowd. praxis export-scene refuses it, since its figures are bent on the GPU and the export writes source data.

Counters only on CI. CI benches every scene with CI_COUNTERS, at 640×360 over the 64 counter frames alone, since its software Vulkan is too slow for the town's full run; its Baselines judge counters only.

Goldens per backend. A Golden is recorded on one backend's key, Metal on the M4 Pro and software Vulkan on CI; an adapter without a Golden directory skips the comparison.

One exposure, one sun. Every scene shares the sun's illuminance and the EV100 that exposes it to 1; a scene lit by its point lights alone is not exposed for them.

The hash's order. A choice hashes by its discriminant, so inserting a Choice before the last renumbers every choice after it and moves what those choices placed; a new choice goes at the end.

Paths by frame number. A capture or bench at another frame delta sees the same camera positions at the same frame numbers but another simulated time, so its Moving Instances are elsewhere.

Rigid flocks. The birds keep their places about a centre that turns; the town measures 10,000 Moving Instances, not a flocking simulation.

History

The scenes arrived one mechanism at a time, each made to pin the mechanism it tests: the sweep, the grid and the helmet with the first lit pass; the far planes with reversed depth; the orrery with the fixed schedule; the floor and the wall in the second milestone, the floor for mips, anisotropic filtering and Toksvig, whose safeguards were each switched off to show the Golden fails without them, and the wall for the binding arrays' cost at 10,000 Textures, which gated texture streaming on them; the shadows scene with the shadow rays; the crowd with the bend pass and its shadow proxies. The volumes that narrated the floor, the wall and the town, with their measurements of the day, are absorbed into this page; Texture cooking describes the Toksvig widening that keeps the floor's highlight from sparkling.
