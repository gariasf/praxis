The Chunk streamer

The module that decides, each frame, which Chunks of a Game world to request, keep and unload around the camera, from the camera's World position and the loads its IO finished, and reports its Late Chunks, its hold after a teleport and whether the frame is Settled; with the IO pool that reads and decodes Chunks off the main thread, it keeps the World's loaded Chunks.

Describes main at 25ab340f (2026-11-08), with the Chunk streamer driven by the IO pool as its change leaves it. A change to the Chunk streamer rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: Chunk cooking for the Chunks it streams and the Asset IDs their Placements name; Prepare for the slot ranges loaded Chunks take.

Interface

Runs
Once a frame, in State::update after the Ticks and before prepare, through ChunkStreaming::update; with a Settled wait, which capture and the Goldens take, again until the frame is Settled. The streamer itself reads no GPU state, records no GPU work and reads no clock; the IO pool's four threads read and decode beside the frame.
Reads
The camera's World position, whether it teleported, and each finished load from its ChunkIo: the Chunk's coordinates and the Mesh and Material Asset IDs its Placements name. Which Chunks exist and the Load radius are given at creation, from the scene's chunk_coordinates and load_radius_metres.
Writes
Its requests, nearest first, to its ChunkIo, and a ChunkFrame: the Chunks requested, loaded, held and unloaded this frame, the Assets whose last user unloaded, the Late Chunks, whether it holds and whether the frame is Settled. Through ChunkStreaming, the Chunks loaded into the Mesh and Material pools, and the World's LoadedChunks: loads join it, unloads leave it and give back their pool users, held Chunks are marked.
Counted
Frame stats' late_chunks, chunks_loaded, chunks_unloaded and bytes_read, which the bench's Counters sum over their frames; the CPU stage stream_chunks.
Where in the code
ChunkStreamer, ChunkIo, FinishedLoad and ChunkFrame in src/streaming/mod.rs; ChunkStreaming, the IO pool, decode_chunk and DecodedReader in src/streaming/io.rs; LoadedChunks in src/streaming/chunks.rs; the Chunk's edge, CHUNK_EDGE_METRES, in src/assets/cooked.rs.

1 · distance_metres · chunks_within

Distance to a Chunk

Every radius is measured from the camera to the nearest point of a Chunk's 256 m square, across x and z only: a Chunk is within a radius when any of its column is. At a Load radius of 256 m that is 7 to 9 Chunks.

Along each axis the camera is either over the Chunk, at no distance, or past its lowest or highest edge by the difference; the two axes combine as a hypotenuse. Height plays no part, as a Chunk is a column at every height.

let along = |position: f64, coordinate: i32| {
    let lowest = coordinate as f64 * CHUNK_EDGE_METRES;
    (lowest - position)
        .max(position - lowest - CHUNK_EDGE_METRES)
        .max(0.0)
};
along(camera.x, x).hypot(along(camera.z, z))

distance_metres, src/streaming/mod.rs.

Over the middle of a Chunk, the camera has its own Chunk, the four beside it at 128 m and the four at its corners at 181 m within 256 m: nine. Near the middle of an edge it has the two Chunks the edge divides, the four beside those and one of the two beyond: seven. Near a corner it has the four that share the corner and four of those around them: eight. chunks_within lists the Chunks that overlap the square around the camera of the radius's half-size, and each is kept only if it is in the Game world, which the streamer was given at creation, and within the radius.

2 · update

Requests and the lead

A Chunk is requested once it is within the Load radius plus the lead: the distance the camera covered in its last frame, times one frame more than the latest load took. Requests go out nearest first, and a Chunk already requested is not requested again.

let lead_metres = metres_per_frame * (self.load_frames + 1) as f64;
let request_radius_metres = (load_radius_metres + lead_metres).min(hold_radius_metres);

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

Speed and load time are both counted in frames: the camera's horizontal move since the frame before, and the frames from the request of the latest load that finished to the frame the streamer saw it finish. By ChunkIo's contract a load finishes once its Chunk's content is applied and the Assets it names are Resident, so the lead covers all of that. Their product is how far the camera goes while a load like the last one is under way. A load is seen on the frame after IO completes it, so a load as long as the last one can be seen a frame later; the extra frame covers that. The camera then cannot come within the Load radius of a requested Chunk before it loads, unless it speeds up or a load takes longer. On a teleport, and on the first frame, the speed is zero and the request radius is the Load radius.

The request radius never passes the hold radius, so nothing is requested that would be unloaded on arrival. Requests are sorted by distance, ties by coordinates, so a run of the same camera path and the same IO requests the same Chunks in the same order.

3 · update · ChunkFrame

Held and unloaded

A loaded Chunk stays loaded to the hold radius, one Chunk (256 m) beyond the Load radius, and unloads past it. Between the two it is held: kept with its Assets but neither drawn nor traced, so the camera can cross the Load radius back and forth without a reload.

At a Load radius of 256 m the hold radius is 512 m, which holds 20 to 23 Chunks. ChunkFrame::held lists every held Chunk each frame, by coordinates; a held Chunk the camera comes back to leaves the list and is drawn again without IO. A load that finishes for a Chunk the camera has since left past the hold radius is dropped, never loaded, and a load in flight is never requested twice.

4 · update

Late Chunks, the hold and Settled

A Late Chunk is counted on the frame the camera comes within the Load radius of a Chunk not yet loaded, once per load. A teleport is an arrival, not travel: its Chunks are not Late, and the streamer holds until every Chunk within the Load radius has loaded. A frame is Settled when every Chunk within its request radius has loaded.

let entered = !self.within_load_radius_before.contains(&coordinates);
if !arrived && !loaded && entered && self.counted_late.insert(coordinates) {
    frame.late_chunks += 1;
}

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

The streamer keeps the set of Chunks within the Load radius on the frame before, so a Chunk counts on entering, not on every frame it is late, and the Chunks it has counted until their loads finish, so a camera crossing a late Chunk's border back and forth counts it once. The first frame arrives as a teleport does, since the camera did not travel to it. The hold starts on an arrival and ends on the frame the last Chunk within the Load radius loads:

frame.holding = (frame.holding || arrived) && !every_chunk_within_load_radius_loaded;

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

Settled reaches further than the hold: it waits for the lead's Chunks too, so a frame of travel whose lead has a request in flight is not Settled though it does not hold. A Settled frame is one a capture may render; the hold is what a fast travel waits on.

5 · update · FinishedLoad

Last users

The streamer counts how many times the loaded Chunks name each Asset ID. An unload that brings a count to zero reports that Asset as having lost its last user, in a fixed order.

A finished load carries the Mesh and Material Asset IDs its Placements name, in Placement order; an Asset named twice is counted twice, and so reaches zero once, at its last name. Held Chunks keep their counts. Chunks unload in coordinate order, and within each, its Assets in its own order:

for asset in assets.iter() {
    let Some(count) = users.get_mut(asset) else {
        continue;
    };
    *count -= 1;
    if *count == 0 {
        users.remove(asset);
        frame.last_users.push(*asset);
    }
}

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

What becomes of an Asset with no user is the pools' decision, not the streamer's: it reports the Asset and frees nothing.

6 · IoPool · decode_chunk

The IO pool

Four std threads take the streamer's requests from one channel. Each reads a Chunk's Blob from the Cooked cache and every Mesh, Material and Texture Blob it names, through its Meshes' Materials to their Textures, and decodes each where it read it, into CPU memory. An Asset another load already claimed is skipped, so each is read and decoded once.

The claimed set holds every Asset loaded into a pool, by any load, and every Asset decoded and not yet loaded. A thread claims an Asset before it reads it:

let mut seen = HashSet::new();
let mut claim = |asset: AssetId| {
    seen.insert(asset)
        && claimed
            .lock()
            .unwrap_or_else(PoisonError::into_inner)
            .insert(asset)
};

decode_chunk, src/streaming/io.rs.

The decoded Assets reach the main thread with their Chunk, and join one shared table there as soon as their Chunk's read comes back, before any Chunk loads. The main thread loads each finished Chunk into the pools through DecodedReader, which hands the pools a decoded Asset when the table holds it, and otherwise reads it from the Cooked cache on the main thread: the case, outside a Settled wait, of an Asset another Chunk claimed whose read has not come back yet. That Chunk's decoded copy is then dropped when it comes. Every Asset the pools read, the spawn's included, joins the claimed set. Nothing is freed yet, so nothing leaves it.

The threads touch no GPU object. The main thread writes what they decoded through the load's staging, as every load does (ADR 0017), and only it submits (ADR 0016).

7 · ChunkStreaming::update

Settled frames and the loaded Chunks

Each frame, the finished reads are loaded into the pools in coordinate order, x then z, the streamer decides, and the World's LoadedChunks follows its decision. A capture's Settled wait loads nothing until every read in flight has come back, then all of them at once, in coordinate order, so slot ranges and pool entries never depend on the threads' timing.

fn take_finished(&mut self) -> Option<FinishedLoad> {
    self.io.receive();
    for decoded in self.io.ready.values_mut().flatten() {
        self.decoded.extend(std::mem::take(&mut decoded.assets));
    }
    if self.settle && self.io.in_flight > 0 {
        return None;
    }

FrameIo::take_finished, src/streaming/io.rs. Finished reads wait in a map ordered by coordinates.

let frame = streamer.update(camera, false, &mut frame_io);
streamed.late_chunks += frame.late_chunks;
streamed.chunks_loaded += frame.loaded.len() as u32;
streamed.chunks_unloaded += frame.unloaded.len() as u32;
keep_loaded_chunks(world, frame, finished, renderer);
if !settle || frame.settled {
    return Ok(streamed);
}
if let Some(error) = error.take() {
    return Err(error);
}
io.receive_all()?;

ChunkStreaming::update. The bench and a window never settle: they draw what has loaded.

A load the streamer drops, as the camera left its hold radius before it finished, gives back the pool users it counted. An unloaded Chunk leaves LoadedChunks and gives back its users too: one per Mesh and per Material its Placements name, the entries kept whatever their count. Every loaded Chunk is then marked held or not from the streamer's held list:

for chunk in chunks.iter_mut() {
    let held = frame.held.binary_search(&chunk.coordinates).is_ok();
    changed |= chunk.held != held;
    chunk.held = held;
}

keep_loaded_chunks. LoadedChunks is marked changed only when something changed, which is what prepare checks before it visits the Chunks.

Each Reference scene sets its Load radius. Every scene but the world holds all its Chunks from each frame of its camera path: 256 m for most, 1,024 m for town and 4,352 m for far_coplanar, whose farthest Chunks lie 842 m and 4,238 m from the camera. Their first frame requests every Chunk, and a capture's first Settled frame loads them all, in coordinate order, as their spawn did before they streamed.

A load that fails is logged. While settling it fails the frame and names the sources the launch's cook found absent; otherwise the Chunk stays requested and is not asked for again.

What it costs

No Baseline measures streaming: every Reference scene loads all its Chunks in its first frames, before a bench measures. The update's cost grows with the Chunks it examines: the square around its request radius and the Chunks it has loaded. A first Settled frame costs the reads, decodes and pool loads of every Chunk.

Flying diagonally at 50 m/s, 3,600 framesMedian, µsp99, µs
Load radius 256 m over 40 × 40 Chunks0.29–0.330.58–0.71

Release build on the Apple M4 Pro: the update alone, in a test against the scripted IO with loads of 6 frames, five rounds while other sessions held the one-minute load average near 26, so the ranges may run high.

The work is the Chunks overlapping the request radius's square, clamped to the lowest and highest coordinates of the Game world's Chunks, each looked up in the Game world's set, plus every loaded Chunk for the hold radius and the held list. At 256 m that is 9 to 25 Chunks examined, and the loaded ones, at most the 20 to 23 within the hold radius. Its lists and sets are cleared each frame, not dropped, so they keep their capacity.

10

Why this way

The nearest point. The Load radius was chosen from a table that counts the Chunks whose nearest point lies within each radius, 7 to 9 at 256 m against 20 to 23 at 512 m, so the streamer measures the same way. Measured to a Chunk's middle instead, a Load radius of 256 m would leave unloaded a Chunk whose corner is 75 m from the camera.

A hold radius one Chunk out. OGRE's terrain paging holds pages to a larger radius than it loads them to, to stop thrashing when the camera moves back and forth across the loading border. The streamed world takes that form with the hold one Chunk, 256 m, beyond the Load radius; held Chunks are not drawn or traced, as at town's density the static TLAS could not hold them.

A lead the same in every direction. Funkhouser bounds where an observer can be in N frames by a sphere of radius N times its top speed, and suggests weighting it toward the direction of travel. The streamer takes the bound alone: one request radius around the camera. The extra Chunks it requests beside and behind the camera lie well within the hold radius at any lead a few frames long, so they cost a request each and no churn.

Frames, not seconds. Speed and load time are both counted in frames, so their product is a distance whatever the frame rate, and the streamer reads no clock: the same camera path and the same IO give the same requests, which the tests and Settled captures both need.

Decoding on the IO threads, four of them. The survey left open whether the IO threads decode or hand each Blob to the compute pool. Run as a compute-pool task the IO thread waits on, decoding settled town, far_coplanar and texture-wall within 2% of the time two IO threads took, with a hop more per Blob, so it stays on the threads that read. Four threads settled them in 22 to 38% less time than two, as the survey's four-thread reads and decodes outran two; most of a small Blob's cost is its open, which threads overlap.

Loads in coordinate order while settling. Slot ranges and pool entries follow the order Chunks load in, and the order Instances reach the GPU can change an image on the M4 Pro, so a Settled frame loads its Chunks only once all have come back, sorted. That is also the order the scenes loaded their Chunks in before they streamed, so their Goldens pass bit-exact.

Arrivals hold. A teleport has no lead to cover it: Sunset Overdrive's streaming had to load all seven zones around a teleport's destination at once. The streamer holds instead, and does not count the arrival's Chunks as Late, so Late Chunks measure travel alone.

11

Limits

No scene streams yet. Every Reference scene holds all its Chunks from every frame of its path, so only the flight test loads, holds and unloads a Chunk.

No cap on a frame's loads. A frame loads every Chunk whose reads came back, however many bytes that writes through the load's staging.

Nothing freed. A Chunk's unload gives back its pool users but frees no entry, and the claimed set never shrinks.

Main-thread reads. Outside a Settled wait, an Asset claimed by a Chunk whose read is still in flight is read and decoded on the main thread by the Chunk that loads first, and bytes_read does not count it.

The latest load only. The lead takes the latest load's time, so a fast load after a slow one shortens it at once; a camera that speeds up during a load can make a Chunk late.

No cancelled reads. A request whose Chunk the camera leaves stays in flight until it finishes and is dropped.

Wide radii examine many Chunks. The square around a radius that holds a whole scene, clamped to the Game world's bounds, is examined every frame, every Chunk of it looked up, whether or not anything moves.

History

The streamer was written for the streamed world after its survey set the Load radius at 256 m and the hold radius one Chunk beyond it, before any scene loaded its Chunks through it. The IO pool then drove it: every scene's Chunks loaded through it, each scene with a Load radius of its own, and captures waited for Settled frames. This page describes the mechanism as it is.
