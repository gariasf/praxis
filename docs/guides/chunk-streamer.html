Praxis · Chunks · src/streaming/

The Chunk streamer

The module that decides, each frame, which Chunks of a Game world to request, keep and unload around the camera, from the camera's World position and the loads its IO finished, and reports its Late Chunks, its hold after a teleport and whether the frame is Settled; with the IO pool that reads and decodes Chunks, and the mips mip streaming requests, off the main thread, it keeps the World's loaded Chunks.

Describes main at 2e6ba738 (2026-11-09), with the lead as its change leaves it. A change to the Chunk streamer rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: Chunk cooking for the Chunks it streams and the Asset IDs their Placements name; Prepare for the slot ranges loaded Chunks take.

Interface

Runs
Once a frame, in State::update after the Ticks and before prepare, through ChunkStreaming::update; with a Settled wait, which capture, the Goldens and the bench's frames up to the end of its counter frames take, again until the frame is Settled; mip streaming runs after its loads in each pass (Mip streaming). The streamer itself reads no GPU state, records no GPU work and reads no clock; ChunkStreaming reads one, to time a Fast travel's hold; the IO pool's four threads read and decode beside the frame.
Reads
The camera's World position, whether it teleported, from the Camera resource's teleported, which ChunkStreaming clears, and each finished load from its ChunkIo: the Chunk's coordinates and the Mesh and Material Asset IDs its Placements name, or none when the load failed. Which Chunks exist and the Load radius are given at creation, from the scene's chunk_coordinates and load_radius_metres.
Writes
Its requests, nearest first, to its ChunkIo, and a ChunkFrame: the Chunks requested, loaded, not loaded, held and unloaded this frame, the Assets whose last user unloaded, the Late Chunks, whether it holds and whether the frame is Settled. Through ChunkStreaming, the frame's StreamedFrame, which reports whether the camera still holds and, on the frame a Fast travel's hold ends, its time from the jump; the Chunks loaded into the Mesh and Material pools, nearest the camera first and up to the frame's load bytes, and the World's LoadedChunks: loads join it, unloads leave it and give back their pool users, held Chunks are marked.
Counted
Frame stats' late_chunks, chunks_loaded, chunks_unloaded, bytes_read, which counts mip streaming's reads too, and pool_entries_freed, which the bench's Counters sum over their frames; chunks_not_loaded, and the pools' refusals since the frame before, meshes_refused and materials_refused, which the traversal sums; the bytes its loads write into the load staging, in uploaded_bytes; holding and fast_travel_hold, which the bench reduces to a Fast travel's hold; the CPU stage stream_chunks.
Where in the code
ChunkStreamer, ChunkIo, FinishedLoad, ChunkFrame and ChunkStreamer::within_hold_radius in src/streaming/mod.rs; ChunkStreaming with cook_ahead, StreamedFrame, the IO pool, IoRequest, decode_chunk and DecodedReader in src/streaming/io.rs; LoadedChunks in src/streaming/chunks.rs; the Chunk's edge, CHUNK_EDGE_METRES, in src/assets/cooked.rs.

1 · distance_metres · chunks_within

Distance to a Chunk

Every radius is measured from the camera to the nearest point of a Chunk's 256 m square, across x and z only: a Chunk is within a radius when any of its column is. At a Load radius of 256 m that is 7 to 9 Chunks.

Along each axis the camera is either over the Chunk, at no distance, or past its lowest or highest edge by the difference; the two axes combine as a hypotenuse. Height plays no part, as a Chunk is a column at every height.

let along = |position: f64, coordinate: i32| {
    let lowest = coordinate as f64 * CHUNK_EDGE_METRES;
    (lowest - position)
        .max(position - lowest - CHUNK_EDGE_METRES)
        .max(0.0)
};
along(camera.x, x).hypot(along(camera.z, z))

distance_metres, src/streaming/mod.rs.

Over the middle of a Chunk, the camera has its own Chunk, the four beside it at 128 m and the four at its corners at 181 m within 256 m: nine. Near the middle of an edge it has the two Chunks the edge divides, the four beside those and one of the two beyond: seven. Near a corner it has the four that share the corner and four of those around them: eight. chunks_within lists the Chunks that overlap the square around the camera of the radius's half-size, and each is kept only if it is in the Game world, which the streamer was given at creation, and within the radius.

2 · update

Requests and the lead

A Chunk is requested once the camera, closing on it at the rate its distance fell over the last frame, would come within the Load radius in the lead: one frame more than the slowest load so far took, and never fewer than LEAST_LEAD_FRAMES, 16. Requests go out nearest first, and a Chunk already requested is not requested again.

self.lead_frames = self.lead_frames.max(self.frame_number - requested_on + 1);
// ...
let closing_metres = match self.camera_before {
    Some(before) if !arrived => distance_metres(before, coordinates) - metres,
    _ => 0.0,
};
let lead_metres = closing_metres.max(0.0) * lead_frames;
if metres > (load_radius_metres + lead_metres).min(hold_radius_metres) {
    continue;
}

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

Speed and load time are both counted in frames: how far each Chunk's nearest point came closer since the frame before, and, for each load that finishes, the frames from its request to the frame the streamer saw it finish. By ChunkIo's contract a load finishes once its Chunk's content is applied and the Assets it names are Resident, so the lead covers all of that. A load is seen on the frame after IO completes it, so one as long as the slowest can be seen a frame later; the extra frame covers that.

The distance to a Chunk, a square, from a camera flying a straight line at a steady speed falls ever more slowly as the camera passes it, so the last frame's closing rate never makes the Chunk look farther off than it is: a Chunk the camera will reach within the lead is requested at least the lead ahead, and a load as slow as any so far lands first. The camera can still come within the Load radius of a Chunk before it loads if it turns or speeds up, or if a load takes longer than every one before it and the floor. A Chunk the camera passes beside, outside the Load radius, closes ever more slowly and is never requested, nor are Chunks behind it, which only grow farther.

The floor covers a slow load that no earlier load foretold. Most loads take a frame; a Chunk naming Assets the pools lack, such as a settlement's first facade Materials, takes several, and the first of them follows nothing but one-frame loads, so no lead built from past loads alone could cover it. Sixteen frames is 13.3 m at 50 m/s. Above the floor the lead follows the slowest load, and it never shrinks. On a teleport, and on the first frame, nothing is closing and only Chunks within the Load radius are requested.

No Chunk's request radius passes the hold radius, so nothing is requested that would be unloaded on arrival. The square chunks_within lists spans the widest request radius, the camera's whole move times the lead, which no Chunk's closing rate exceeds. Requests are sorted by distance, ties by coordinates, so a run of the same camera path and the same IO requests the same Chunks in the same order.

3 · update · ChunkFrame

Held and unloaded

A loaded Chunk stays loaded to the hold radius, one Chunk (256 m) beyond the Load radius, and unloads past it. Between the two it is held: kept with its Assets but neither drawn nor traced, so the camera can cross the Load radius back and forth without a reload.

At a Load radius of 256 m the hold radius is 512 m, which holds 20 to 23 Chunks. ChunkFrame::held lists every held Chunk each frame, by coordinates; a held Chunk the camera comes back to leaves the list and is drawn again without IO. A load that finishes for a Chunk the camera has since left past the hold radius is dropped, never loaded, and a load in flight is never requested twice.

4 · update

Late Chunks, the hold and Settled

A Late Chunk is counted on the frame the camera comes within the Load radius of a Chunk not yet loaded, once per load. A teleport is an arrival, not travel: its Chunks are not Late, and the streamer holds until every Chunk within the Load radius has loaded. A frame is Settled when every Chunk it requests, now or on an earlier frame, has loaded.

let entered = !self.within_load_radius_before.contains(&coordinates);
if !arrived && !loaded && entered && self.counted_late.insert(coordinates) {
    frame.late_chunks += 1;
}

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

The streamer keeps the set of Chunks within the Load radius on the frame before, so a Chunk counts on entering, not on every frame it is late, and the Chunks it has counted until their loads finish, so a camera crossing a late Chunk's border back and forth counts it once. The first frame arrives as a teleport does, since the camera did not travel to it. The hold starts on an arrival and ends on the frame the last Chunk within the Load radius loads:

frame.holding = (frame.holding || arrived) && !every_chunk_within_load_radius_loaded;

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

Settled reaches further than the hold: it waits for the lead's Chunks too, so a frame of travel whose lead has a request in flight is not Settled though it does not hold. A Settled frame is one a capture may render; the hold is what a Fast travel waits on, and while it lasts a capture's or the bench's camera path pauses (The bench).

5 · update · FinishedLoad

Last users

The streamer counts how many times the loaded Chunks name each Asset ID. An unload that brings a count to zero reports that Asset as having lost its last user, in a fixed order.

A finished load carries the Mesh and Material Asset IDs its Placements name, in Placement order; an Asset named twice is counted twice, and so reaches zero once, at its last name. Held Chunks keep their counts. Chunks unload in coordinate order, and within each, its Assets in its own order:

for asset in assets.iter() {
    let Some(count) = users.get_mut(asset) else {
        continue;
    };
    *count -= 1;
    if *count == 0 {
        users.remove(asset);
        frame.last_users.push(*asset);
    }
}

update, src/streaming/mod.rs.

What becomes of an Asset with no user is the pools' decision, not the streamer's: it reports the Asset and frees nothing.

6 · IoPool · decode_chunk

The IO pool

Four std threads take requests from one channel, each an IoRequest: the streamer's Chunks, and mip streaming's mips. For a Chunk, a thread reads its Blob from the Cooked cache and every Mesh, Material and Texture Blob it names, through its Meshes' Materials to their Textures, and decodes each where it read it, into CPU memory, a Texture's Resident tail alone. An Asset another load already claimed is skipped, so each is read and decoded once.

The claimed set holds every Asset loaded into a pool, by any load, and not freed since, and every Asset decoded and not yet loaded. A thread claims an Asset before it reads it:

let mut seen = HashSet::new();
let mut claim = |asset: AssetId| {
    seen.insert(asset)
        && claimed
            .lock()
            .unwrap_or_else(PoisonError::into_inner)
            .insert(asset)
};

decode_chunk, src/streaming/io.rs.

The decoded Assets reach the main thread with their Chunk, and join one shared table there as soon as their Chunk's read comes back, before any Chunk loads. The main thread loads each finished Chunk into the pools through DecodedReader, which hands the pools a decoded Asset when the table holds it, and otherwise reads it from the Cooked cache on the main thread: the case, outside a Settled wait, of an Asset another Chunk claimed whose read has not come back yet. That Chunk's decoded copy is then dropped when it comes. Every Asset the pools read, the spawn's included, joins the claimed set. One the pools free leaves it as it is freed, through DecodedReader::forget, before anything reads it again, so the next Chunk to name it has it read on the IO pool once more.

A Texture's finer mips are read later, when a Placement needs them: mip streaming requests them on the same channel, and the threads read and decode just those levels. The threads touch no GPU object. The main thread writes what they decoded through the load's staging, as every load does (ADR 0017), and only it submits (ADR 0016).

7 · ChunkStreaming::update

Settled frames and the loaded Chunks

Each frame, the finished reads are loaded into the pools nearest the camera first, until the next would write more than the frame's load bytes into the load staging; the rest wait for a later frame. The streamer decides, and the World's LoadedChunks follows its decision. A capture's Settled wait loads nothing until every read in flight has come back, then all of them at once, in coordinate order and past the cap, so slot ranges and pool entries never depend on the threads' timing.

fn take_finished(&mut self) -> Option<FinishedLoad> {
    self.io.receive();
    for decoded in self.io.ready.values_mut().flatten() {
        self.decoded.extend(std::mem::take(&mut decoded.assets));
    }
    if self.settle && self.io.in_flight > 0 {
        return None;
    }

FrameIo::take_finished, src/streaming/io.rs. Finished reads wait in a map ordered by coordinates.

Each call takes the ready read nearest the camera, by the same distance the streamer measures, the lowest coordinates first among equals; a Settled wait takes the lowest coordinates. Before it loads, the Chunk must fit:

fn fits(&mut self, chunk: &CookedChunk) -> anyhow::Result<bool> {
    if self.settle || *self.written_bytes == 0 {
        return Ok(true);
    }
    let bytes = self.most_written_bytes(chunk)?;
    Ok(*self.written_bytes + bytes <= self.load_bytes_per_frame)
}

FrameIo::fits. A Chunk that does not fit goes back to the ready map, and the frame loads nothing more.

most_written_bytes walks the Chunk's Placements as the load will: each Mesh the Mesh pool lacks, its Primitives' Materials and the Placements' own that the Material pool lacks, and their Textures it has not placed, each counted once at the bytes the pools write for it: MeshPool::written_bytes for a Mesh drawn and traced, MaterialPool::WRITTEN_BYTES for a Material, and texture_written_bytes, every mip the Texture holds, its Resident tail alone for one the IO pool decoded, with its rows padded to 256 bytes, for a Texture. An Asset the IO pool did not decode is read from the Cooked cache and left among the decoded ones, so the load reads it once. The sum is exact for a Chunk's Assets, and an upper bound where the Material pool holds back a Texture's finer mips or has no room for them; a debug build asserts that no load writes more. The cap is RenderCapacities::load_bytes_per_frame, what the frame staging's slots at their largest leave of the staging Budget, over the frames in flight and the one being written: 22,937,393 bytes from the checked-in Budgets.

let frame = streamer.update(camera, teleported, &mut frame_io);

    io,
    priority: mips,

io.receive_all()?;

ChunkStreaming::update. After the Chunk loads, mip streaming applies the mips that arrived and requests those the loaded Chunks need; a Settled frame waits for its mip requests too. A window never settles, nor the bench after its counter frames: they draw what has loaded.

Around that pass, ChunkStreaming::update takes the camera's teleported and clears it, so a jump is an arrival for one frame alone, though one that render_frames placed on a frame it ran undrawn stays set until streaming next runs, and times a Fast travel's hold with the wall clock, from the jump to the frame the streamer stops holding:

let teleported = std::mem::take(&mut world.resource_mut::<Camera>().teleported);
if teleported {
    self.fast_travel_started = Some(Instant::now());
}
let streamed = self.stream(world, renderer, settle, teleported);
…

    streamed.fast_travel_hold = self.fast_travel_started.take().map(|jump| jump.elapsed());

ChunkStreaming::update. The State copies holding and fast_travel_hold into the frame's Frame stats; a jump whose Chunks are all loaded already reports its hold on its own frame.

A load the streamer drops, as the camera left its hold radius before it finished, gives back the pool users it counted. An unloaded Chunk leaves LoadedChunks and gives back its users too: one per Mesh and per Material its Placements name. An entry left with no user stays loaded until a load the pools refuse for want of room frees it, least recently used first, and a Material only once no loaded Mesh names it. Every loaded Chunk is then marked held or not from the streamer's held list:

for chunk in chunks.iter_mut() {
    let held = frame.held.binary_search(&chunk.coordinates).is_ok();
    changed |= chunk.held != held;
    chunk.held = held;
}

keep_loaded_chunks. LoadedChunks is marked changed only when something changed, which is what prepare checks before it visits the Chunks.

Each Reference scene sets its Load radius. Every scene but the world holds all its Chunks from each frame of its camera path: 256 m for most, 1,024 m for town and 4,352 m for far_coplanar, whose farthest Chunks lie 842 m and 4,238 m from the camera. Their first frame requests every Chunk, and a capture's first Settled frame loads them all, in coordinate order, as their spawn did before they streamed.

In a dev launch, ChunkStreaming runs the launch's cook on each Chunk it requests, on the main thread, before the IO pool reads it. render_frames has it run that cook first, through cook_ahead, on every Chunk the streamer's within_hold_radius lists for the camera of each frame not drawn Settled, so the frames that stream find each Chunk checked already.

A load that fails is logged, and load_cooked_chunk gives back the pool users it counted of the Meshes and Materials it loaded before the failure, so the pools are as they were. While settling it fails the frame and names the sources the launch's cook found absent. Otherwise its FinishedLoad carries no Asset IDs, and the streamer lists the Chunk as not loaded and asks for it again a frame later, then, after each further failure in a row, twice as late, up to LONGEST_RETRY_FRAMES, 1,024 frames:

let Some(assets) = load.assets else {
    let (failures, retry_on) = self.failed.entry(load.coordinates).or_default();
    *failures += 1;
    let wait_frames = 2u64.saturating_pow(*failures - 1);
    *retry_on = self.frame_number + wait_frames.min(LONGEST_RETRY_FRAMES);
    frame.not_loaded.push(load.coordinates);
    continue;
};

ChunkStreamer::update, src/streaming/mod.rs. A load that succeeds clears the Chunk's failures.

9 · update

What it costs

One Baseline times streaming, the world's on the M4 Pro: every Reference scene but the world loads all its Chunks in its first frames, before a bench measures, while the world's 3,352 measured frames load 42 Chunks, two of them late, and unload 41, inside a CPU frame no field splits by stage. The update's cost grows with the Chunks it examines: the square around its request radius and the Chunks it has loaded. A first Settled frame costs the reads, decodes and pool loads of every Chunk.

Release build on the Apple M4 Pro: the update alone, in a test against the scripted IO with loads of 6 frames, five rounds while other sessions held the one-minute load average near 26, so the ranges may run high.

The work is the Chunks overlapping the widest request radius's square, clamped to the lowest and highest coordinates of the Game world's Chunks, each looked up in the Game world's set, plus every loaded Chunk for the hold radius and the held list. At 256 m that is 9 to 25 Chunks examined, and the loaded ones, at most the 20 to 23 within the hold radius. Its lists and sets are cleared each frame, not dropped, so they keep their capacity.

10

Why this way

The nearest point. The Load radius was chosen from a table that counts the Chunks whose nearest point lies within each radius, 7 to 9 at 256 m against 20 to 23 at 512 m, so the streamer measures the same way. Measured to a Chunk's middle instead, a Load radius of 256 m would leave unloaded a Chunk whose corner is 75 m from the camera.

A hold radius one Chunk out. OGRE's terrain paging holds pages to a larger radius than it loads them to, to stop thrashing when the camera moves back and forth across the loading border. The streamed world takes that form with the hold one Chunk, 256 m, beyond the Load radius; held Chunks are not drawn or traced, as at town's density the static TLAS could not hold them.

A lead toward each Chunk. Funkhouser bounds where an observer can be in N frames by a sphere of radius N times its top speed, and suggests weighting it toward the direction of travel. The streamer weights it all the way: each Chunk's lead is its own closing rate times the lead's frames. With one request radius around the camera, the sphere alone, a 30-frame lead loaded four Chunks whose corners the world's diagonal passes outside the Load radius, and the world's bench 47 Chunks instead of 42; toward each Chunk, only those the camera reaches load. The sphere stays the better bound for a camera that turns: what a turn can miss is a Chunk within a few metres of the Load radius in the new direction, which a sphere a few frames wide would also have missed.

A floor under the slowest load. Funkhouser's later system prefetched up to 16 frames ahead, the floor's count, and OpenMW preloads the cells at the player's position predicted a second ahead by default: fixed lookaheads, not measured ones. A lead taken from the latest load alone dropped to two frames after every one-frame load, and the slow loads that settlements bring then landed after the camera arrived. A lead from past loads alone, the slowest or a high percentile, still misses the first slow load, which follows only fast ones. So the lead is a fixed count of frames, like Funkhouser's, that grows to the slowest load seen when loads outlast it, on slower IO or heavier content. Sixteen frames is over twice the slowest load the world's traversal has met on the M4 Pro, 7 frames at the bench's pace, and kept the traversal's peak GPU memory within the 1,020 to 1,177 MB the latest-load lead left. A 30-frame floor peaked at 1,356 MB in most traversals: Chunks ahead loaded while those behind were not yet past the hold radius, so more Textures were Resident at once, and Texture classes, created whole with every slice counted, opened one or two more of 88 MB. Predicting each Chunk's load from what it must read would need its Asset IDs before its read, which only the read gives.

Frames, not seconds. Speed and load time are both counted in frames, so their product is a distance whatever the frame rate, and the streamer reads no clock: the same camera path and the same IO give the same requests, which the tests and Settled captures both need.

Decoding on the IO threads, four of them. The survey left open whether the IO threads decode or hand each Blob to the compute pool. Run as a compute-pool task the IO thread waits on, decoding settled town, far_coplanar and texture-wall within 2% of the time two IO threads took, with a hop more per Blob, so it stays on the threads that read. Four threads settled them in 22 to 38% less time than two, as the survey's four-thread reads and decodes outran two; most of a small Blob's cost is its open, which threads overlap.

Loads in coordinate order while settling. Slot ranges and pool entries follow the order Chunks load in, and the order Instances reach the GPU can change an image on the M4 Pro, so a Settled frame loads its Chunks only once all have come back, sorted. That is also the order the scenes loaded their Chunks in before they streamed, so their Goldens pass bit-exact.

A share of the staging Budget per frame. ADR 0017 caps the bytes a frame writes into the load's StagingBelt, whose chunk size is only its allocation unit, at the staging Budget. A frame's belt lives until the GPU has run its copies, so the frames in flight and the one being written can hold three belts at once; a third of the Budget each keeps them within it together. The Reference scenes' Chunks write far less: at most 5,595,688 bytes in one frame, uv-grid's first, so no scene's loads wait.

Sized before, not measured after. A load cannot be undone once written into the belt, so a frame that only stopped after crossing the cap would cross it. Sizing each Chunk from its decoded Assets first keeps every frame under the cap but one: a Chunk that alone writes more than the cap loads with no other writes in its frame, rather than never.

Arrivals hold. A teleport has no lead to cover it: Sunset Overdrive's streaming had to load all seven zones around a teleport's destination at once. The streamer holds instead, and does not count the arrival's Chunks as Late, so Late Chunks measure travel alone, and the hold is timed from the jump, so the wait an arrival costs is measured rather than lost among the frames after it.

11

Limits

Only the world streams. Every other Reference scene holds all its Chunks from every frame of its path. The world's camera flies at 50 m/s and Fast travels once, so its captures and its bench load the Chunks ahead of it and hold, undrawn, those it leaves past the Load radius, until they pass the hold radius and unload.

A Chunk past the cap loads alone. A Chunk is not sized while the frame's loads have written nothing, so one that writes more than the frame's load bytes loads without other writes in its frame and crosses the cap.

Settled waits are uncapped. A Settled frame, a capture's or one of the bench's up to the end of its counter frames, loads every Chunk it waited for in one frame, whatever their bytes.

Waiting reads count against no one. A Chunk that waits is sized again each frame, and one the camera has left still takes its turn before it is dropped.

Freed only for room, a few a frame. An entry left with no user is freed only when a load is refused, and each pool frees at most ENTRIES_FREED_PER_FRAME a frame for Arena and Texture room; a full Material table frees one unused Material for each Material it loads, past that, as each free makes the one entry the load needs. A load still refused fails its Chunk, which is asked for again later.

Main-thread reads. Outside a Settled wait, an Asset claimed by a Chunk whose read is still in flight is read and decoded on the main thread by the Chunk that loads first, and bytes_read does not count it.

The slowest load stays. The lead never shrinks, so one stalled load widens every request radius for the rest of the run, up to the hold radius, where requests lose the margin that stops thrashing. A load slower than the floor and every load before it still makes its Chunk late, as does a camera that turns toward a Chunk near the Load radius or speeds up during a load.

No cancelled reads. A request whose Chunk the camera leaves stays in flight until it finishes and is dropped.

Wide radii examine many Chunks. The square around a radius that holds a whole scene, clamped to the Game world's bounds, is examined every frame, every Chunk of it looked up, whether or not anything moves.

History

The streamer was written for the streamed world after its survey set the Load radius at 256 m and the hold radius one Chunk beyond it, before any scene loaded its Chunks through it. The IO pool then drove it: every scene's Chunks loaded through it, each scene with a Load radius of its own, and captures waited for Settled frames. Each frame's loads were then capped by the staging Budget, nearest the camera first, and the pools began freeing the entries no user held, for room, with the claimed set following their frees. Mip streaming then shared the IO pool, whose threads came to decode a Chunk's Textures to their Resident tails alone and read their finer mips on request. Then the camera's teleport flag reached the streamer, which had been told of none, so the world's Fast travel holds and its hold is timed. Last, the lead, which had followed the latest load around the camera, took the slowest load so far with a floor of 16 frames, toward each Chunk at the rate the camera closed on it, after the world's traversal found the first slow loads of each settlement late. This page describes the mechanism as it is.
