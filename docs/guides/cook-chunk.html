Chunk cooking

The cook of a Reference scene's static content into Chunks: every Renderable that does not move becomes a Placement, and every point light a light, each in the 256 m Chunk that holds its pivot and translated in f64 from that Chunk's origin; each Chunk's bounds grow to hold its Placements' Meshes, and each Chunk is stored as one Blob, a field at a time, under an Asset ID made from its scene's name and its coordinates.

Describes main at dd5ffd53 (2026-11-08), with a dev launch cooking each Chunk it loads on a miss, praxis cook leaving world out, and each Chunk generated as it cooks, as their changes leave them; town's costs were measured at 075919da, world's launch at this page's commit. A change to the Chunk cook rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: The Reference scenes for what town places, Camera-relative placement for the Anchors and a vertex's 128 m from its pivot, Texture cooking for the Cook key and the Cooked cache.

Interface

Runs
Before the frame, never during it: in praxis cook for every Reference scene but world, in praxis cook <scene> for one, and in a dev launch for each Chunk it loads, as the load finds its Blob missing or stale, writing only a Chunk whose Cook key changed. Every scene loads its Chunks as it spawns.
Reads
One Chunk's Placements and lights in World coordinates: ReferenceScene::chunk_content for world, which generates that Chunk alone, and ReferenceScene::static_content once for every other scene, whose Chunks are cut from it. The source bounds of every Mesh the same cook reads, through source_bounds.
Writes
One Blob per Chunk into the Cooked cache, under ReferenceScene::chunk_id; its Cook key; and to the cook's report, each Mesh or Material a Placement names that the cook does not make.
Counted
Not in Frame stats: the cook runs outside the frame. The cook's cook complete log counts Chunks among the Assets cooked and unchanged.
Where in the code
chunks and cook_key in src/cook/chunk.rs; read_sources, Sources::chunk and cook_sources in src/cook/mod.rs; a dev launch's cook in CookingLoader, src/cook/launch.rs; the Blob in CookedChunk, CookedPlacement and CookedLight, src/assets/cooked.rs; each scene's content in static_content and chunk_content, src/scenes/mod.rs, town's in src/scenes/town.rs, world's in static_content_reaching, src/scenes/world.rs.

1 · static_content

Static content

A scene's Chunks cook from its generator, and the scene loads what does not move from them, with each Mesh and Material named by Asset ID instead of a loaded Handle: town's placements, and every other scene's Placements of what does not move and its point lights. What moves or bends, such as the town's flocks, the orrery's bodies and the crowd's figures, the Moving Instances, stay out: scene systems spawn them at runtime.

town's placements returns every Renderable it places, block by block, then its lamps, poles and wires, and the position of each lamp's light. static_content maps each to a CookedPlacement in World coordinates: the Mesh's Asset ID from mesh_id, the overriding Material's from material_id where the Renderable has one, as the boxes do, and its Transform's translation, rotation and scale. Each light takes LAMP_LIGHT's colour, intensity in candela and range. Every other scene's arm of static_content in src/scenes/mod.rs places its Meshes with placed and turns its light constants into lights with cooked_lights.

let placements = placed
    .into_iter()
    .map(|renderable| CookedPlacement {
        mesh: scene.mesh_id(renderable.mesh.name()),
        material: renderable
            .material
            .map(|material| scene.material_id(material.name())),
        translation: renderable.transform.translation,
        rotation: renderable.transform.rotation,
        scale: renderable.transform.scale,
    })
    .collect();

static_content, src/scenes/town.rs.

A cook holds one Chunk at a time. read_sources lists each scene's Chunks by chunk_coordinates and generates none; Sources::chunk makes one as cook_sources reaches it, or as a dev launch's load first asks for it. world's 16,214,556 Placements are generated a Chunk at a time: chunk_content calls static_content_reaching, which walks the same ground tiles, settlements, lots and farms in the same order as static_content but places only those whose square reaches the Chunk, each widened by how far its Placements' pivots stray from its centre, then keeps the Placements whose pivots the Chunk holds. Each Placement is placed from a hash of where it stands, so skipping a lot changes nothing else, and the Chunk holds what its cut from the whole scene holds, in the same order. Every other scene's generator places block by block, not Chunk by Chunk, so its whole content is generated once per cook and cut, and each Chunk is taken from that cut.

let reaches = |centre: glam::DVec3, reach_metres: f64| {
    let near = centre - lowest + reach_metres;
    let far = highest - centre + reach_metres;
    near.x >= 0.0 && near.z >= 0.0 && far.x >= 0.0 && far.z >= 0.0
};

static_content_reaching, src/scenes/world.rs: a lot reaches 6 m, half its pitch, a farm's ground tile 64 m, a settlement its half size.

2 · chunks · chunk_holding

The Chunk over a pivot

A Placement or light belongs to the Chunk whose column holds its translation: x and z divided by 256 m and floored. Its translation is stored from that Chunk's origin, in f64, so a Mesh may reach past the Chunk's edge and still have one owner.

A Chunk at integer coordinates (x, z) has its origin at (256x, 0, 256z) and holds the column from there to 256 m further along x and z, at every height. chunk_holding floors, so a pivot at x = −0.5 m is in Chunk −1 and one at x = 256 m in Chunk 1. chunks keeps the Chunks in a BTreeMap by coordinates, so they and their contents come out in the same order every cook.

[pivot.x, pivot.z].map(|metres| (metres / CHUNK_EDGE_METRES).floor() as i32)

chunk_holding, src/assets/cooked.rs, which chunks and chunk_content both use.

pub(crate) fn chunk_origin([x, z]: [i32; 2]) -> glam::DVec3 {
    glam::DVec3::new(f64::from(x), 0.0, f64::from(z)) * CHUNK_EDGE_METRES
}

chunk_origin, src/assets/cooked.rs, which load_chunk adds each translation back to.

The subtraction is not always exact. When the translation from the origin has a larger exponent than the World translation, a pivot at x = −10.1 m becoming 245.9 m in Chunk −1 say, it is rounded to f64's step at that size, at most 2−45 m, about 3 × 10−14 m, for any translation under 256 m. Adding the origin back can then differ from the World translation in its last bits, by at most 2−46 m, about 1.4 × 10−14 m. town's 16 Chunks hold 244,319 Placements and 1,024 lights: its 20 × 20 blocks span 4 × 4 Chunks, and its ground's 8 × 8 tiles of 128 m lie four to a Chunk.

3 · chunks · source_bounds

Bounds

A Chunk's bounds are the box, from its origin, that holds the eight corners of every Placement's Mesh box, each scaled, rotated and translated as the Placement places it. They may reach past the Chunk's edge; lights do not grow them.

Each Mesh's box is source_bounds of the Primitives the same cook reads: every source vertex and every box a Primitive adds, which holds the box its cooked Blob stores, as welding moves no vertex. The corners are placed in f64, the rotation's f32 quaternion widened first. A Chunk with no Placement keeps its empty box, its lowest corner at +∞ and its highest at −∞. town's wires reach up to 18.5 m past their Chunk's edge, and the bounds of the Chunks at x = 1 start up to 1.48 m before theirs.

for corner in 0..8 {
    let is_highest = BVec3::new(corner & 1 != 0, corner & 2 != 0, corner & 4 != 0);
    let local = Vec3::select(is_highest, highest, lowest) * placement.scale;
    let placed =
        placement.translation + placement.rotation.as_dquat() * local.as_dvec3();
    *bounds = [bounds[0].min(placed), bounds[1].max(placed)];
}

4 · CookedChunk · cook_key

The Blob

A Chunk Blob is the magic PXCH, its format version and one zstd frame at level 9 holding the counts, the bounds, then each field for every Placement in turn and each field for every light: 84 bytes per Placement and 44 per light before compression. Its Cook key hashes that payload, so a moved Placement or a Mesh whose box changes cooks the Chunk again.

FieldBytes eachEncoding
Placement count, light count4, 4u32, little-endian
bounds, lowest then highest6 × 8f64 from the Chunk's origin
Mesh Asset IDs16u128, big-endian, as a Material Blob stores one
Material Asset IDs16u128, big-endian; zero for none
translations' x, then y, then z3 × 8f64 from the Chunk's origin
rotations' x, y, z, w; scales' x, y, z7 × 4f32
lights' translations' x, then y, then z3 × 8f64 from the Chunk's origin
lights' red, green, blue, intensity, range5 × 4f32: colour, candela, metres

CookedChunk::from_blob refuses another magic or version with a re-cook message, and a payload whose length after the header is not its counts' Placements and lights. CookedCache::chunk reads one by Asset ID. The Chunk's Asset ID is ReferenceScene::chunk_id: the scene's name, the Chunk kind and the coordinates written as a name, -2 1 for Chunk (−2, 1).

The Cook key is SHA-256 over the magic, the version, zstd's library version, the level and the payload. The payload holds everything the Chunk cooks from, its Meshes' boxes included through its bounds, so an unchanged scene finds every Chunk up to date and writes none.

5 · dangling_references

Names that dangle

Every Mesh and Material a Placement names is checked against the Assets the same cook makes. One it does not make is warned of, naming the Chunk and the Asset ID, even when the Cooked cache still holds a stale Blob for it.

The Cooked cache never drops an index entry, so a renamed Mesh's old ID still loads its old Blob. cook_sources adds each Placement's Mesh and Material, with Referrer::Chunk and the scene and coordinates, to the references it already checks: each Material's Textures, each Mesh's Materials and what each scene's spawn loads before any Chunk. A Chunk of a scene with an absent source is let through as that scene's own references are, since an absent source keeps its Blobs. A dev launch checks each Mesh and Material a Chunk's Placements name the same way as it loads the Chunk, and a Chunk the cook keeps passes, as its Blob stays as it is.

7 · praxis cook

What it costs

The Chunk cook costs cook time and bytes, never frame time. town's 16 Chunks compress from 20.6 MB to 0.69 MB, and a launch whose Chunks are up to date still generates, cuts and hashes each it loads; world's launch generates only those, a Chunk at a time.

8 · ADRs 0014, 0018, 0023

Why this way

f64 from the Chunk's origin. ADR 0018 stores a Placement's translation in f64 because f32 heights fail the simulation bar above 1,024 m; ADR 0014 addresses a Chunk by integer (x, z) with its origin at the edge's multiple, so a Placement's translation stays small however far its Chunk lies.

The pivot decides. A Placement belongs to one Chunk, the one over its pivot, rather than to every Chunk its Mesh touches, so it loads once; the Chunk's bounds grow instead, and ADR 0014's 128 m pivot bound, which the Mesh cook enforces, keeps a Mesh at unit scale within 128 m of its pivot, though the corners of its rotated box, which the bounds hold, can reach about 222 m along an axis.

A Material per Placement. An Instance's Material overrides its Mesh's, and town's boxes rely on it: one unit cube drawn as slabs, walls, windows and rails in eight Materials. A Placement carries the same optional override, so the Chunks hold what the spawn places with no Mesh per Material.

A field at a time. Stored a Placement at a time, an Asset ID repeats every 84 bytes among unrelated floats; stored a field at a time, the Asset IDs, the translations along each axis and the unit scales each sit together, and zstd at level 9 compresses them to two thirds of what level 19 reaches the other way, about a hundred times faster.

A Chunk at a time. Generating a scene whole and holding every Chunk with its Cook key, as the cook once did, made each launch of world place 16,214,556 Instances before its first frame, for the hundred or so Chunks it loads. Its generator places from a hash of where each thing stands, so it can place one Chunk alone; the other scenes' generators place block by block, and their content is small enough to generate once and cut. Lowering world's density was ruled out: the static TLAS's capacity sets it.

Photometric lights. A light holds what its PointLight holds: a colour, an intensity in candela as ADR 0023 has it, and a range in metres as KHR_lights_punctual defines it.

9

Limits

Spawned, not streamed. Each scene loads all its Chunks as it spawns, town 16 and far-coplanar 258, each into a range of Instance slots with no entity per Placement or light.

The last bit of a translation. A World translation rebuilt from a Chunk's origin and a Placement's translation can differ from the generator's in f64's last bits, by at most 2−46 m.

Generated alone only in world. Every other scene's whole content is generated once per cook or launch, however few of its Chunks it loads: town's 244,319 Placements each time.

A Mesh the cook does not read. A Chunk a Placement of which names a Mesh no source of the same cook defines, its source absent or the Mesh renamed, is not cooked: its bounds would know only that Placement's pivot, so it keeps the Blob it has, as an absent source's Assets do, and is warned of; its Placements' references are still checked.

Lights outside the bounds. A light's range does not grow its Chunk's bounds.

No index of Chunks. Nothing lists which Chunks a scene has: a reader finds one by its coordinates.

Sources

Sources

The Chunk cook relies on no outside source beyond the zstd library the Cooked cache already uses; its rules are the ADRs named above.

History

Chunks were a line of ADR 0018, a list of Placements with no code behind it. They were first cooked for the streamed world, after the cooker began refusing a Mesh past 128 m from its pivot and town's ground was cut into 128 m tiles, with lights and an overriding Material added to the ADR's Placement. Once world placed sixteen million Instances, each Chunk came to be generated as it cooks. This page describes the mechanism as it is.
