Praxis · before the frame · src/cook/texture.rs

Texture cooking

The cook of one Texture: it takes a source image into linear light, reduces it by means of 2×2 boxes down to a single texel, widens a metallic-roughness Texture's roughness at each mip by how far its normal Texture's normals spread there, after Toksvig, encodes every mip in its Channel's BC format, and stores the chain as a KTX2 Blob, each level zstd-compressed on its own, smallest first.

Describes main at b3d764b4 (2026-11-04). A change to the Texture cook rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: Staging for the belt the loader writes each mip through.

Interface

Runs
Before the frame, never during it: in praxis cook, over every manifest source and every Reference scene, and in each dev launch, over the sources its scene references; in both, only for a Texture whose Cook key changed, one Texture per core at a time.
Reads
A TextureSource: a PNG or JPEG's bytes that a glTF Material samples, or a procedural scene's generated RGBA8 pixels; for a metallic-roughness Texture, also the normal Texture of the Material that samples it.
Writes
One Blob per Texture into the Cooked cache in cooked/, with the index entry that names it under its Asset ID and Cook key.
Formats
Albedo and emissive Bc7RgbaUnormSrgb; normal and metallic-roughness Bc5RgUnorm; occlusion Bc4RUnorm. Every mip, from the full size down to 1 texel on the longer side.
Counted
Not in Frame stats: the cook runs outside the frame. Each cook logs how many Assets it cooked and how many it found up to date (cook complete).
Where in the code
TextureSource::cook and its helpers in src/cook/texture.rs; the batches in cook_sources, src/cook/mod.rs; the Blob in CookedTexture, src/assets/cooked.rs; the load in ChannelTextures::push, src/assets/material.rs.

1 · cook_sources · cook_key

When it runs

A Texture is cooked only when its Cook key differs from the one the Cooked cache holds for its Asset ID. The stale Textures cook in batches of one per core: each filters on its own thread, and the cores left over split among the batch's encoders.

cook_sources first cooks the stale Materials and Meshes, then hashes every Texture. A Texture's Cook key is SHA-256 over the Blob format's key and version, the encoder and its profile (ENCODER, intel_tex_2 0.5.0 basic), the zstd level of CookSettings, the Channel, the Cook key of the normal Texture it is widened by, if any, tagged and ahead of the source so no source's bytes can stand in for it, and last the source's bytes: a file's encoded bytes, or a generated image's size and pixels. So a changed normal map cooks its own Texture again and the metallic-roughness Texture it widens, and a changed zstd level cooks every Texture and no Material.

// One Texture per core at a time: its filtering runs on its own thread,
// and memory holds at most that many Textures' working sets. The cores
// split among a batch's encoders.
let threads = std::thread::available_parallelism().map_or(1, |threads| threads.get());
for batch in stale.chunks(threads) {
    let encode_threads = threads.div_ceil(batch.len());
    let blobs: Vec<_> = std::thread::scope(|scope| {
        let cooks: Vec<_> = batch
            .iter()
            .map(|(texture, _)| scope.spawn(|| texture.cook(settings, encode_threads)))
            .collect();
        cooks
            .into_iter()
            .map(|cook| cook.join().expect("a Texture cook panicked"))
            .collect()
    });
    for ((texture, cook_key), blob) in batch.iter().zip(blobs) {
        cache.write(texture.id, cook_key, &blob?)?;
        report.cooked.push(texture.id);
    }
}

cook_sources, src/cook/mod.rs.

A batch holds as many Textures as available_parallelism reports cores, and its Textures' encoders each take that count divided by the batch's size, rounded up. Each Blob is written when its batch has joined, on the calling thread, one after another: CookedCache::write stores the Blob under the SHA-256 of its bytes, unless a Blob of those bytes is already there, then the index entry that points the Asset ID at it, each file written whole, synced to the drive and renamed into place, so a reader or a concurrent cook sees the old file or the new one.

Which Textures a cook reads is decided by its callers. read_gltf adds each image a glTF Material samples once per Channel, and pairs a metallic-roughness Texture with its Material's normal Texture through with_normal_texture; the first Material's, when several share the image. A procedural scene's textures pairs its own the same way; minification's gloss Texture is the only one paired so.

2 · TextureSource::cook · linearise

Into linear light

Every mean the cook takes, it takes in linear light: a colour Channel's sRGB values are decoded through a table of 256 entries, and a data Channel's bytes are scaled to the range they encode. Each Channel keeps only the components it stores.

The cook decodes the source to RGBA8 and refuses it, rather than resizing it, unless each side is a multiple of 4 and at most MAX_TEXTURE_SIDE, 8,192 texels. The Channel fixes the format, and the longer side the count of mips: the base-2 logarithm of that side, rounded down, plus one, so the chain ends where the longer side is 1 texel.

let format = match self.channel {
    MaterialChannel::Albedo | MaterialChannel::Emissive => {
        wgpu::TextureFormat::Bc7RgbaUnormSrgb
    }
    MaterialChannel::Normal | MaterialChannel::MetallicRoughness => {
        wgpu::TextureFormat::Bc5RgUnorm
    }
    MaterialChannel::Occlusion => wgpu::TextureFormat::Bc4RUnorm,
};
let mips = width.max(height).ilog2() as usize + 1;

TextureSource::cook, src/cook/texture.rs.

linearise turns the decoded texels into a Level, floats in linear light with a count of components per texel: albedo and emissive keep RGBA with the colour decoded and alpha scaled to 0 to 1; a normal keeps x, y and z, each mapped to −1 to 1; metallic-roughness keeps glTF's green and blue, roughness then metallic; occlusion keeps glTF's red.

let srgb_to_linear: Vec<f32> = (0..=255u8)
    .map(|value| {
        let encoded = value as f32 / 255.0;
        if encoded <= 0.04045 {
            encoded / 12.92
        } else {
            ((encoded + 0.055) / 1.055).powf(2.4)
        }
    })
    .collect();

linearise, src/cook/texture.rs: the sRGB curve's inverse, the one the specification gives, once per byte value.

Averaging the stored values of a fine black and white checker gives 128, which displays too dark; averaging the light gives half of it, which sRGB stores as about 188. A cook test checks for 188.

3 · Level::downsample · Level::reduce

The mip chain: means of 2×2 boxes

Each mip is the next one up reduced by the mean of each 2×2 box of texels, in linear light; a normal mip is then renormalised. Mip 0 of a colour Channel encodes the source's own bytes, so it never makes the round trip through linear light.

let mut levels = Vec::with_capacity(mips);
for mip in 0..mips {
    if mip > 0 {
        level = level.downsample();
    }
    if self.channel == MaterialChannel::Normal {
        level.renormalise();
    }
    // The normal Texture's level whose texels cover this level's; one
    // coarser than this level, or of another aspect ratio, widens
    // nothing here.
    let widened = spread.as_mut().and_then(|spread| {
        while spread.width > level.width || spread.height > level.height {
            *spread = spread.downsample_spread();
        }
        (spread.width == level.width && spread.height == level.height)
            .then(|| level.widened(spread))
    });
    let texels = match (mip, self.channel) {
        // The source's own bytes, without a round trip through
        // linear light.
        (0, MaterialChannel::Albedo | MaterialChannel::Emissive) => Texels {
            width,
            height,
            bytes: image.pixels.clone(),
        },
        _ => widened.as_ref().unwrap_or(&level).to_texels(self.channel),
    };
    let blocks = encode(self.channel, format, &texels, encode_threads);
    debug_assert_eq!(blocks.len(), level_bytes(format, width, height, mip));
    levels.push(blocks);
}

TextureSource::cook, src/cook/texture.rs.

The loop holds one Level at a time and reduces it in place of the last. Level::reduce halves each side, to no less than 1, and hands each 2×2 box to the function that combines it; where a side is already 1 the box repeats its row or column, and where a side is odd its last row or column falls outside every box. Level::downsample combines a box by the mean of each component.

fn downsample(&self) -> Level {
    self.reduce(|box_texels, values| {
        values.extend((0..self.components).map(|component| {
            box_texels.iter().map(|texel| texel[component]).sum::<f32>() / 4.0
        }));
    })
}

Level::downsample, src/cook/texture.rs.

A normal mip's mean vector is shorter than 1 where its box's normals disagree, so Level::renormalise scales each back to unit length before it is encoded, and a mean of zero length becomes +Z. The shortening is not lost: the spread of section 4 keeps it, from the normal Texture's own finest normals.

4 · Level::spread · Level::downsample_spread · widened_roughness

Toksvig's widening

At each mip, a metallic-roughness texel covers a patch of the normal Texture whose normals, averaged, point one way: flat and glossy where the bumps tilted every way. The cook measures how far those normals spread about their mean and widens the texel's roughness by it, so a distant patch of fine bumps shades as broad and dim as the bumps would make it.

The spread, carried per mip

Level::spread starts from the normal Texture's finest level: per texel its unit normal and a variance of zero. Each coarser level of the spread holds, per texel, the unnormalised mean of the finest normals it covers and their variance about that mean, carried from the level above:

fn downsample_spread(&self) -> Level {
    self.reduce(|box_texels, values| {
        let means = box_texels.map(glam::Vec3::from_slice);
        let mean = means.iter().sum::<glam::Vec3>() / 4.0;
        let variance = box_texels
            .iter()
            .zip(means)
            .map(|(texel, texel_mean)| texel[3] + texel_mean.distance_squared(mean))
            .sum::<f32>()
            / 4.0;
        values.extend(mean.to_array());
        values.push(variance);
    })
}

Level::downsample_spread, src/cook/texture.rs.

A box's variance is the mean of its texels' variances plus the mean squared distance of their means from the box's: the law of total variance. For unit normals it equals 1 − |m|², where m is the unnormalised mean of every finest normal the texel covers. It is carried rather than read off the mean's length so that normals that agree keep a variance of exactly zero, which a length rounded in f32 would not give.

Before each mip, the cook reduces the spread until neither of its sides exceeds the mip's, and widens the mip only where the two are then the same size. With a normal Texture the size of its metallic-roughness Texture, mip 0 is widened by zero, which leaves it as it is, and every coarser mip is widened. A larger normal Texture widens mip 0 too; a smaller one widens the mips from the first whose size it matches; one of another aspect ratio matches only the last mip, one texel, where both chains end.

The widening

Toksvig estimates the normals' angular variance from the mean's length, σ² = (1 − |m|) / |m|. The cook has the variance v = 1 − |m|², so |m| = √(1 − v), and the same quantity rewritten, (1 − |m|)(1 + |m|) / ((1 + |m|)|m|) = v / ((1 + |m|)|m|), takes no difference of two nearly equal numbers when v is small. GGX's α is roughness squared, and a lobe filtered by normals of slope variance σ² widens to α′² = α² + 2σ², after Kaplanyan and colleagues. So widened_roughness raises roughness to the fourth power for α², adds twice the angular variance, caps the sum at 1 and returns its fourth root, the roughness that stores it.

fn widened_roughness(roughness: f32, variance: f32) -> f32 {
    if variance <= 0.0 {
        return roughness;
    }
    // Toksvig, "Mipmapping Normal Maps" (NVIDIA, 2004; JGT 10(3), 2005),
    // eq. 1 [ref:toksvig-2004-variance]: the normals' angular variance from
    // their mean's length, (1 - |mean|) / |mean|, with |mean| =
    // sqrt(1 - variance) and the difference written so that it cancels
    // nothing.
    let mean_length = (1.0 - variance.min(1.0)).sqrt();
    let angular_variance = variance / ((1.0 + mean_length) * mean_length);
    // A Beckmann lobe filtered by normals of slope variance σ² adds 2σ² to
    // alpha²: Kaplanyan, Hill, Patney and Lefohn, "Filtering Distributions
    // of Normals for Shading Antialiasing" (HPG 2016), eqs. 14 and 15
    // [ref:kaplanyan-2016-ndf-filtering]; Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan, "Improved
    // Geometric Specular Antialiasing" (I3D 2019), section 2.2, apply the same
    // sum to GGX [ref:tokuyoshi-2019-roughness-matrix]. Toksvig's angular
    // variance stands in for the slope variance, as tan θ ≈ θ for small
    // angles. Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan keep Kaplanyan et al.'s cap of 0.18 on
    // the added term, against the error of screen-space derivatives; a
    // variance measured from the texels needs no such cap, so only alpha² is
    // capped, at 1. Equations only; no code copied.
    (roughness.powi(4) + 2.0 * angular_variance)
        .min(1.0)
        .powf(0.25)
}

widened_roughness, src/cook/texture.rs.

Level::widened applies it to every texel of the mip and keeps metallic as it is.

What each mip then holds

Each mip's widening covers the normals inside one of that mip's texels. On minification's floor, whose bumps repeat every 8 texels, a texel of mip 2 covers half a bump each way, and the slopes at the four finest texel centres it covers along each axis cancel, so from mip 2 on every normal is flat and the bumps' whole spread is in roughness. What decides the shading is then the mip the sampler reads. It picks the mip by the width of the pixel's footprint, so the widening covers the width; along a long footprint's length the anisotropic probes average the normals, and the shader rebuilds a unit normal from that average in tangent_space_normal, so the spread along the length reaches neither the normal nor the roughness. That is why the data Channels are sampled at 4x anisotropy (Box filtering, chapter 4).

The cooked widening covers detail smaller than a texel of the mip being sampled. It does nothing for the change of normal from pixel to pixel, the mesh's curvature or normal-map detail at a fine mip, which the lit pass's own widening covers (The lit pass).

5 · Level::to_texels · encode

BC7, BC5, BC4

Each mip goes back to the 8-bit texels the encoder reads, sRGB-encoded for the colour Channels, then to blocks of 4×4 texels in its Channel's BC format with intel_tex_2's basic profile and no rate-distortion optimisation, in strips of block rows on as many threads as the batch leaves it.

Level::to_texels pads each side to a multiple of 4 by repeating the last row and column, which a mip smaller than a block needs, and writes RGBA8 for albedo and emissive, two bytes for a normal's x and y, two for roughness and metallic, and one for occlusion. A normal's z is not stored: the shader rebuilds it from x and y. Roughness lands in BC5's red and metallic in its green.

// The sRGB encoding, ≤ at the knee [ref:khronos-dfs-srgb].
let linear_to_srgb = |linear: f32| {
    if linear <= 0.0031308 {
        linear * 12.92
    } else {
        1.055 * linear.powf(1.0 / 2.4) - 0.055
    }
};
let byte = |value: f32| (value.clamp(0.0, 1.0) * 255.0).round() as u8;

Level::to_texels, src/cook/texture.rs.

encode cuts the texels into strips of block rows, one per thread, and each thread encodes its strip into its share of the output, so the blocks concatenate in row order. BC7 takes the profile that keeps alpha only where a texel of the mip has alpha under 255.

6 · CookedTexture::to_blob · ChannelTextures::push

The Blob and its load

A Texture Blob is KTX2: a header, a level index, a data format descriptor, one key-value entry with the format version, then each level's blocks zstd-compressed on its own, the smallest mip first. The runtime refuses a Blob of another version, format, size or mip count, and creates the GPU texture with the whole chain.

The key-value entry is PXTXVERSION, its NUL and TEXTURE_FORMAT_VERSION, 2, as a little-endian u32. The key and its NUL are 12 bytes, a multiple of the value's 4, as KTX 2.0 requires of a key whose value starts with a multi-byte number. The levels follow in reverse, so the smallest mips are one contiguous byte range: the Resident tail that streaming will keep loaded, read in one span.

let data_offset = key_value_offset + key_value_entry.len();
for (level, texels) in self.levels.iter().enumerate().rev() {
    let compressed = zstd::bulk::compress(texels, zstd_level)?;
    level_index[level] = ktx2::LevelIndex {
        byte_offset: (data_offset + data.len()) as u64,
        byte_length: compressed.len() as u64,
        uncompressed_byte_length: texels.len() as u64,
    }
    .as_bytes();
    data.extend(compressed);
}

CookedTexture::to_blob, src/assets/cooked.rs. A supercompressed level needs no alignment, so the levels pack end to end.

Each level's uncompressed size is level_bytes: the level's side, halved per mip and no less than 1, in whole blocks of 4×4, times the format's block_copy_size. A level smaller than a block still takes a whole one. CookedTexture::from_blob checks every level against that size as it decompresses it, and the mip count against the one the cook makes.

At load, MaterialPool::load_texture reads the Blob by Asset ID once, and ChannelTextures::push creates the texture at the next free Layer of its Channel, with every mip, and writes each through the load's staging, which pitches the unpadded rows to a multiple of 256 bytes as it copies them into the belt. Streaming is not in yet: every mip is loaded with its Texture.

let gpu_texture = device.create_texture(&wgpu::TextureDescriptor {
    label: Some(self.label),
    size,
    mip_level_count: texture.levels.len() as u32,
    sample_count: 1,
    dimension: wgpu::TextureDimension::D2,
    format: texture.format,
    usage: wgpu::TextureUsages::TEXTURE_BINDING | wgpu::TextureUsages::COPY_DST,
    view_formats: &[],
});
for (level, bytes) in texture.levels.iter().enumerate() {
    staging.write_texture(&gpu_texture, level as u32, bytes);
}

8 · praxis cook

What it costs

The Texture cook costs cook time and bytes, never frame time: a launch whose Textures are up to date only hashes them. A whole cold cook is dominated in count by texture-wall, whose 10,000 Textures are 10,000 of the 10,193 Assets it cooks.

Two cold runs of praxis cook, each into an empty cooked/. In both, user plus system time is less than half the wall clock, so the cook spent much of its wall clock off the CPU; the runs do not say where, though the cook test's own comment puts texture-wall's 20,000 files, each flushed to the drive as it is written, at 84 s cold on the M4 Pro. The code filters and encodes in parallel, one Texture per core, and writes the Blobs and index entries one at a time on the calling thread, each synced before its rename; texture-wall's Textures are two files each.

9 · ADRs 0008, 0010, 0011, 0017, 0018

Why this way

Cooked ahead, not at load. ADR 0011 has the runtime load Blobs with mip chains and block compression, sized for streaming, and ADR 0018 has it load cooked Blobs only, by Asset ID, never a source path. The filtering, the widening and the encode run once per change of a Cook key.

BC per Channel, encoded directly. ADR 0018 fixes the formats: BC7 sRGB for colour, BC5 for the two two-component data Channels, BC4 for occlusion, with BC6H left for HDR. Basis UASTC transcoded at load measured 2.1 to 2.5 dB under a direct BC7 encode, for a portability a desktop-only engine (ADR 0010) does not need. The encoder runs at its basic profile without rate-distortion optimisation; its slow profile buys 0.1 to 0.7 dB for about 3.5 times the time.

A box in linear light. ADR 0018's mips are a 2×2 box in linear light with normals renormalised per mip. Averaging sRGB-encoded values would darken every mip of a high-contrast Texture.

Toksvig at cook time. ADR 0008 keeps screen-space history out of the image, so no temporal filter averages a distant normal map's sparkle away; widening each mip's roughness by its normals' spread is a cook-time, non-temporal answer. The added term is not capped: Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan cap it at 0.18 against the error of screen-space derivatives, and a variance measured from the texels has no such error, so only α′² is capped, at 1.

The whole chain, smallest first, each level on its own. ADR 0017 streams Textures a whole mip at a time with hardware trilinear and anisotropic filtering unchanged, keeping each Texture's Resident tail loaded. The Blob stores each mip as a separately loadable, separately compressed byte range, smallest first, so the tail is one read.

Refused, not resized. A source whose sides are not multiples of 4, or exceed 8,192, is refused (ADR 0018): every Texture a Blob holds is then one wgpu creates as BC, and no cook rescales an artist's image.

Keyed by content. The Cook key covers the source bytes, the normal Texture's key, the settings, the encoder and the format version (ADR 0018), so a cook redoes exactly what changed and deleting cooked/ is always safe.

10

Limits

The Material's factors. The widening reads the Texture's own roughness, as if its Material's roughness factor were 1; a factor f scales the widening's share of α² by f⁴. It reads none of the normal scale either: a Material that halves its bumps is widened as if they were whole.

One lobe, no direction. Toksvig widens one isotropic lobe. It gets the highlight's width about right, not its shape: bumps that make two peaks shade as one, and a spread along one axis widens both.

Along a long footprint. The widening covers a texel of the mip the sampler reads, which it picks by the footprint's width; the spread that anisotropic probes average along the length is lost.

A shared image. A metallic-roughness image that several Materials sample is widened by the first one's normal Texture only, and a normal Texture coarser than its metallic-roughness Texture, or of another aspect ratio, leaves the mips it does not match unwidened.

Odd sides. Below its multiple of 4, a side that is not a power of two halves to an odd one, whose last row or column no box covers.

Small Textures and small mips. Every level below 4×4 takes a whole block, which for a 4×4 Texture triples its bytes.

Images the cook skips. A glTF image given as a data URI is warned of and not cooked.

History

The Texture cook took its present form over two weeks of the third milestone: BC encoding with mips in linear light first, then the Toksvig widening of metallic-roughness mips and, the same day, the runtime reading cooked Textures and Materials; then the procedural scenes' Textures paired with their normal Textures, for the minification floor; a sources audit that tied each step of the widening to its paper and said where it departs from them; and the KTX2 key-value entry aligned as KTX 2.0 requires, which moved the format to version 2. Before the cook, Textures were uncompressed RGBA8 without mips. The narrative, with its images and measurements, is in the volume Texture cooking until a page absorbs its scenes; Box filtering explains what a mean of texels lets through and why a supersampled reference rewards blur; The lit pass describes what the widening leaves to the lit pass. This page describes the mechanism as it is; the volumes describe how it got here.
