The static TLAS

A TLAS over every static Instance, a drawn Far proxy's included, each in an entry of its own, tagged with its slot and masked by its Mesh's width class, placed in a frame whose origin is the corner of an Anchor that every build keeps until the camera strays more than a Chunk's edge from it, and then moves to the camera's; held in two TLASes, one bound for rays while a worker thread brings the other up to date and records its build, which the main thread submits and binds the frame after, it is the first structure every shadow ray queries.

Describes main at 61426980 (2026-11-25), with its capacity as the Budget split leaves it. A change to the static TLAS rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: A BLAS per Mesh for the structures its entries place, Instance Buffer Explained for the slots and writes it follows, Camera-relative placement for the Anchor and offset its entries compose from. The dynamic TLAS holds the Instances it leaves out; the shadow mask pass traces against both.

Interface

Runs
In every frame's Renderer::upload, after the frame's loads are submitted and the BLASes awaiting compaction are taken a step, and before the dynamic TLAS's fill: StaticTlas::update takes the frame's entry changes, binds the spare whose build the frame before submitted, submits a build the worker has finished, and hands the idle spare to a new worker when the spare lacks a change, the bound TLAS lacks one that starts a build or one FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES old, or the camera's Anchor is more than REANCHOR_CELLS from the bound TLAS's along an axis. The build runs on the worker thread, never in the frame's encoder.
Reads
The Frame data's instance_writes and the instances they cover; of each static Instance's Mesh, its BLAS (MeshPool::blas), the radius of its widest Thin Primitive if every Primitive is one (MeshPool::thin_radius_metres) and whether it is a Far proxy's (MeshPool::is_far_proxy); the camera's Anchor, every frame; the wait_for_static_tlas Render switch, which a capture sets.
Writes
The entry each static Instance's slot holds, with its mask, and each frame's entry changes, which both TLASes take; the Anchor of the bound TLAS's frame, which the camera uniform carries to the GPU as tlas_anchor and the dynamic TLAS shares; the static Instances it leaves out, by slot.
Builds
Both TLASes, empty, at creation; then one build_acceleration_structures of the spare alone, recorded and finished on the worker into a command buffer of its own, which the main thread submits before the frame's, when the bound TLAS lacks a change that starts a build, has waited FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES on a Far proxy's, or is to re-anchor.
Bind groups
Group 2 of the shadow mask pass, SHADOW_RAY_SCENE, as static_tlas: one group per TLAS, built once since each is rebuilt in place, and set by StaticTlas::bound_index.
Shader
Queried in first_intersection, src/render/shaders/shadow_mask.wgsl.
Counted
static_instances_left_out in counters; static_tlas_build_time, the worker's time on the build a frame submits, and frames_until_traced, in a frame that binds a new build that brings a change that starts a build; each filled entry's backend record in uploaded_bytes, in the frame that submits a build; both TLASes at full capacity in the acceleration structures' ledger, and so in gpu_memory_bytes; the main thread's share, the entry changes and the submit, in the static_tlas_update stage. No GPU pass time is taken of its build.

Where it sits in the frame

The static TLAS is updated in the upload, after the frame's loads are submitted and the BLASes awaiting compaction are taken a step, and before the dynamic TLAS's fill. It records nothing into the frame's encoder: a build lands on the GPU in a command buffer of its own, and rays trace it from the frame after its submit.

Renderer::new creates it with capacities.static_tlas_instances entries in each of its two TLASes, the capacity it derives from the Budgets (section 5), and builds both empty at once, so rays can query either from the first frame. Each frame's upload first submits the loads staged since the last frame, then MeshPool::compact_blases takes each BLAS awaiting compaction as far as the GPU has got (A BLAS per Mesh, Compaction). A streamed load's BLASes are deferred: nothing waits for them, and its Chunk's Instances are placed, and so written, only in the first frame that finds every BLAS they name built and compacted (MeshPool::blas_ready). A Settled frame's loads and a spawn's are not deferred, so that frame's upload waits on the main thread for the GPU to build and compact their BLASes, and its Instances are written the frame they load. Either way, no Instance the static TLAS takes names a BLAS the GPU has not finished, which StaticTlas::entry_changes asserts in debug builds; only then does StaticTlas::update take the frame's Instance writes. Its time on the main thread is the static_tlas_update stage, taken out of upload; the worker's time on the build the update submits goes into Frame stats as static_tlas_build_time, apart from the frame, and the frames a change waited to be traced as frames_until_traced. The bytes that build uploads, a record per filled entry, join the frame's uploaded_bytes (section 4). The update also hands Frame stats the count of Instances it left out. The bound TLAS's Anchor, read after the update, goes into the camera uniform, which shadow rays start from, and into the dynamic TLAS's fill, so both follow a TLAS newly bound at another Anchor in the frame that binds it.

An entry per static Instance

A static Instance whose Mesh has a BLAS takes an entry of its own, a freed one before a fresh one, tagged with its slot; a Moving Instance, a freed slot and a Mesh no ray traces empty its entry and free it. Each is an entry change, which both TLASes take in turn, and each but a Far proxy's starts a build. Its slot is only the key that finds the entry, so a slot past the capacity traces like any other.

/// The static TLAS (ADR 0020): each static Instance in an entry of its own,
/// tagged with its slot, in a frame whose origin is the corner of an Anchor:
/// the camera's when the first static Instance took an entry, or when a
/// build last re-anchored it (ADR 0014). Two TLASes hold it: rays
/// trace the bound one while a worker thread brings the spare up to date
/// and records its build, which the main thread submits (ADR 0016), and the
/// spare is bound from the frame after that submit. A frame that changes a
/// static Instance or frees one's slot starts a build once the spare is
/// idle, as does a frame whose camera's Anchor is more than
/// `REANCHOR_CELLS` from the bound TLAS's along an axis. A Far proxy's entry
/// change starts none of its own while frames pace themselves: it joins the
/// next build, and starts one once it is `FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES` old,
/// as Far proxies cross the View distance several times a second and every
/// build is of every entry. Both are built
/// empty at creation, so rays can always query them, and take the camera's
/// Anchor with no build until a static Instance takes an entry.
pub struct StaticTlas {
    bound: AnchoredTlas,
    /// Which of the two `bound` is: 0 for the one bound at creation.
    bound_index: usize,
    spare: Option<Spare>,
    /// The entry changes each TLAS's entries lack, by TLAS, oldest first;
    /// the bound one's are those its last build lacked.
    behind: [Vec<ChangeBatch>; 2],
    /// Frames it has taken.
    frame: u64,
    capacity: u32,
    /// The entry each slot's static Instance holds, by slot, up to the
    /// highest slot that held one.
    entry_by_slot: Vec<Option<u32>>,
    /// Entries emptied since they were filled, taken before fresh ones.
    free_entries: Vec<u32>,
    /// One past the highest entry filled.
    end: u32,
    /// Static Instances it had no free entry for, by slot, which take the
    /// entries still free after a later frame's writes, lowest slot first.
    left_out: BTreeMap<u32, StaticInstance>,
    /// Whether each slot's static Instance is a Far proxy's, by slot, up to
    /// the highest slot that held one.
    far_proxy_by_slot: Vec<bool>,
    /// Bytes each filled entry uploads per build.
    entry_upload_bytes: u64,
}
/// An empty static TLAS of `capacity` entries, both TLASes counted in
/// `memory` and built at once through `queue`.
pub fn new(
    device: &wgpu::Device,
    memory: &GpuMemory,
    queue: &wgpu::Queue,
    capacity: u32,
) -> Self {
    let create = |label| AnchoredTlas {
        tlas: memory.create_tlas(
            device,
            &wgpu::CreateTlasDescriptor {
                label: Some(label),
                max_instances: capacity,
                // Rebuilt only when its Instances change or it re-anchors,
                // traced every frame.
                flags: wgpu::AccelerationStructureFlags::PREFER_FAST_TRACE,
                update_mode: wgpu::AccelerationStructureUpdateMode::Build,
            },
        ),
        anchor: [0; 3],
        positions: Vec::new(),
    };
    let bound = create("Static TLAS 0");
    let spare = create("Static TLAS 1");
    let mut encoder = device.create_command_encoder(&wgpu::CommandEncoderDescriptor {
        label: Some("Static TLAS Encoder"),
    });
    encoder.build_acceleration_structures(std::iter::empty(), [&*bound.tlas, &*spare.tlas]);
    queue.submit(std::iter::once(encoder.finish()));
    Self {
        bound,
        bound_index: 0,
        spare: Some(Spare::Idle(spare)),
        behind: [Vec::new(), Vec::new()],
        frame: 0,
        capacity,
        entry_by_slot: Vec::new(),
        free_entries: Vec::new(),
        end: 0,
        left_out: BTreeMap::new(),
        far_proxy_by_slot: Vec::new(),
        entry_upload_bytes: tlas_entry_upload_bytes(device.adapter_info().backend),
    }
}

/// The entry changes of `frame`'s Instance writes, its entries taken and
/// freed as its static Instances need, and whether one of them is not a
/// Far proxy's.
fn entry_changes(
    &mut self,
    frame: &FrameData,
    mesh_pool: &MeshPool,
) -> (Vec<EntryChange>, bool) {
    let mut changes = Vec::new();
    let mut starts_build = false;
    let was_leaving_out = !self.left_out.is_empty();
    for write in &frame.instance_writes {
        let instances = &frame.instances[write.instances.clone()];
        for (slot, instance) in (write.first_slot..).zip(instances) {
            // A freed slot's Instance has no Mesh, so no BLAS.
            let blas = (!write.moving)
                .then(|| {
                    let handle = mesh_pool.handle_at(instance.mesh)?;
                    debug_assert!(
                        mesh_pool.blas_ready(handle),
                        "a static Instance placed before its Mesh's BLAS is ready"
                    );
                    Some((
                        mesh_pool.blas(handle)?,
                        mesh_pool.thin_radius_metres(handle),
                        mesh_pool.is_far_proxy(handle),
                    ))
                })
                .flatten();
            let held_far_proxy =
                (self.far_proxy_by_slot.get_mut(slot as usize)).is_some_and(std::mem::take);
            let Some((blas, thin_radius_metres, far_proxy)) = blas else {
                if let Some(entry) = self.release(slot) {
                    starts_build |= !held_far_proxy;
                    changes.push(EntryChange {
                        entry,
                        instance: None,
                    });
                }
                continue;
            };
            if far_proxy {
                let slot = slot as usize;
                if slot >= self.far_proxy_by_slot.len() {
                    self.far_proxy_by_slot.resize(slot + 1, false);
                }
                self.far_proxy_by_slot[slot] = true;
            }
            let instance = StaticInstance {
                tlas_instance: tlas_instance(
                    blas,
                    unplaced_transform(instance),
                    slot,
                    thin_radius_metres,
                ),
                position: InstancePosition::of(instance),
            };
            match self.entry_of(slot) {
                Some(entry) => {
                    starts_build |= !far_proxy;
                    changes.push(EntryChange {
                        entry,
                        instance: Some(instance),
                    });
                }
                None => {
                    self.left_out.insert(slot, instance);
                }
            }
        }
    }
    while !self.left_out.is_empty()
        && let Some(entry) = self.take_entry()
    {
        let (slot, instance) = self.left_out.pop_first().expect("not empty");
        self.hold(slot, entry);
        starts_build |= self.far_proxy_by_slot.get(slot as usize) != Some(&true);
        changes.push(EntryChange {
            entry,
            instance: Some(instance),
        });
    }
    if !was_leaving_out && !self.left_out.is_empty() {
        tracing::warn!(
            left_out = self.left_out.len(),
            capacity = self.capacity,
            "Static Instances left out of the static TLAS: it is full, so they cast no shadow"
        );
    }
    (changes, starts_build)
}

/// The entry of `slot`'s static Instance, taking one if it has none;
/// None when it is full.
fn entry_of(&mut self, slot: u32) -> Option<u32> {
    self.left_out.remove(&slot);
    if let Some(entry) = self.entry_by_slot.get(slot as usize).copied().flatten() {
        return Some(entry);
    }
    let entry = self.take_entry()?;
    self.hold(slot, entry);
    Some(entry)
}

/// Frees `slot`'s entry, if it has one, and returns it; drops its
/// Instance if left out.
fn release(&mut self, slot: u32) -> Option<u32> {
    self.left_out.remove(&slot);
    let entry = self
        .entry_by_slot
        .get_mut(slot as usize)
        .and_then(Option::take)?;
    self.free_entries.push(entry);
    Some(entry)
}

fn hold(&mut self, slot: u32, entry: u32) {
    let slot = slot as usize;
    if slot >= self.entry_by_slot.len() {
        self.entry_by_slot.resize(slot + 1, None);
    }
    self.entry_by_slot[slot] = Some(entry);
}

/// A freed entry, else a fresh one, else None when it is full.
fn take_entry(&mut self) -> Option<u32> {
    self.free_entries.pop().or_else(|| {
        let fresh = (self.end < self.capacity).then_some(self.end)?;
        self.end += 1;
        Some(fresh)
    })
}

StaticTlas::entry_of, StaticTlas::release, StaticTlas::hold and StaticTlas::take_entry, src/render/acceleration_structures.rs.

Prepare writes Instances in runs of consecutive slots, each run an InstanceWrite flagged moving where its Instances are Moving Instances, and splits a run where the flag changes. The update walks every written slot. A static Instance gets its Mesh's BLAS, found through the Handle of the Mesh at the index the Instance names (MeshPool::handle_at), with the Mesh's thin radius, which sets the entry's mask (below), and whether it is a Far proxy's; a Moving Instance gets none, so a static Instance that starts to move empties its entry the frame it does. A freed slot is written an Instance whose Mesh is NO_MESH, which no Mesh is loaded at, and a Mesh loaded MeshUse::Drawn has no BLAS, so both empty the entry.

A written static Instance asks entry_of for its slot's entry: the one the slot already holds, else the last entry freed, else the next fresh one below the capacity, and when neither is left its Instance waits in left_out, keyed by slot. The entry's new Instance becomes an EntryChange, pushed into the frame's list: a StaticInstance, whose TLAS Instance holds the rotation and scale rows and no translation yet (unplaced_transform), beside the Instance's position, from which each TLAS composes the translation in its own frame (section 3); a frame that changes nothing static allocates no list. A write with no BLAS calls release, which frees the slot's entry into free_entries, drops it from entry_by_slot, a table indexed by slot, and returns the entry to empty. After the writes, the waiting Instances take whatever entries are still free, lowest slot first, each a change. So prepare's slots never limit what it holds: a Chunk's range, which comes past the highest slot taken, traces whether it lies under the capacity or not. Entries are taken in write order, and wgpu-core builds from the filled entries in entry order, skipping empty ones, so a scene whose first frame writes its static Instances in slot order builds from the list it built from when entries were slots.

A Far proxy is a static Instance like any other. Its Mesh, loaded as MeshUse::FarProxy, is traced, so it has a BLAS of its LOD 0, and prepare draws each Resident Far proxy as a Chunk of one Placement, in a slot after the loaded Chunks' ranges: it writes the proxy's Instance while the proxy is drawn, and an Instance with no Mesh while its Chunk is drawn instead or once it is released. Its entry so joins and leaves through the same writes, and a swap at the Load radius is one frame that releases one side's entries and fills the other's. What differs is whether the change starts a build. entry_changes marks each slot whose Instance is a Far proxy's in far_proxy_by_slot, and returns, beside the changes, whether any change is not a Far proxy's: a filled entry whose Mesh is not one, an emptied entry whose slot did not hold one, or a left-out Instance admitted that is not one. A frame of Far proxy changes alone hands them to both TLASes like any other but starts no build for them (section 4). A Chunk's Placements, which a swap writes in the same frame, start one, so a swap waits no longer than any other change.

/// How many of the narrowest width class's lower bound, 3.125 mm, a metre
/// holds; each class's bound is twice the one before.
pub(crate) const NARROWEST_SHADOW_CLASS_BOUNDS_PER_METRE: f32 = 320.0;

/// The width classes, an instance-mask bit each above the ordinary bit.
pub(crate) const SHADOW_WIDTH_CLASSES: u32 = 7;

/// An Instance's entry in a TLAS: `blas`, placed by `transform`
/// (`anchored_transform`) and tagged with its `slot`.
fn tlas_instance(
    blas: &wgpu::Blas,
    transform: [f32; 12],
    slot: u32,
    thin_radius_metres: Option<f32>,
) -> wgpu::TlasInstance {
    let mask = instance_mask(thin_radius_metres, &transform);
    wgpu::TlasInstance::new(blas, transform, slot, mask)
}

/// A ray meets an entry whose mask shares a bit with its own
/// [ref:wgpu-30-tlas-instance-fields], so `shadow_ray_mask` reads these bits.
fn instance_mask(thin_radius_metres: Option<f32>, transform: &[f32; 12]) -> u8 {
    const ORDINARY_MASK: u8 = 1;
    let Some(radius_metres) = thin_radius_metres else {
        return ORDINARY_MASK;
    };
    let rows: [[f32; 4]; 3] = bytemuck::cast(*transform);
    let mut axis_scales = [0, 1, 2]
        .map(|column| glam::vec3(rows[0][column], rows[1][column], rows[2][column]).length());
    axis_scales.sort_by(f32::total_cmp);
    // The middle scale is the tube's radial one, whichever axis it runs along.
    let width_metres = 2.0 * radius_metres * axis_scales[1];
    let narrowest_bounds = (width_metres * NARROWEST_SHADOW_CLASS_BOUNDS_PER_METRE).max(1.0);
    let class = (narrowest_bounds as u32).ilog2();
    if class < SHADOW_WIDTH_CLASSES {
        2 << class
    } else {
        ORDINARY_MASK
    }
}

NARROWEST_SHADOW_CLASS_BOUNDS_PER_METRE, SHADOW_WIDTH_CLASSES, tlas_instance and instance_mask, src/render/acceleration_structures.rs; the dynamic TLAS's entries share tlas_instance, and the two constants reach WGSL through the shader modules.

Each entry is a wgpu::TlasInstance of the BLAS, a transform, custom data and a mask. The custom data is the slot, which a hit returns to the shader as instance_custom_data, so a hit can name the Instance it struck; the shadow mask pass's rays need only whether they hit, and read it nowhere. wgpu-core rejects custom data of 224 or more at build time, and every slot under the capacity fits, as a test holds the checked-in Budgets' Instance slots and static TLAS entries to it.

The mask is the entry's width class. A ray's 8-bit cull mask counts an entry only where the two share a bit, so the mask is how a ray leaves out entries in traversal, before it meets a triangle. Bit 0 is the ordinary bit, which every shadow ray carries. A Mesh takes it unless every Primitive of the LOD 0 its BLAS holds is a Thin Primitive: then MeshPool::thin_radius_metres gives its widest Primitive's radius, and the Instance's width is twice that radius times the middle of its transform's three axis scales, the tube's radial scale whichever axis it runs along. The classes double from a lower bound of 3.125 mm, so bits 1 to 7 take widths from under 6.25 mm (a width under 3.125 mm included) up to 400 mm, and a width of 400 mm or more takes the ordinary bit. The mask is set once, when the entry's change is made from the rotation and scale rows, before any translation, so a re-anchor leaves it as it was. Which classes a ray carries, by the width of its receiver's pixel, is shadow_ray_mask's, on the shadow mask pass's page: a class's shadow ends where it would fall between a pixel and half a pixel wide.

3 · AnchoredTlas · place · REANCHOR_CELLS

A frame anchored at the camera

An entry's transform is twelve f32, so each TLAS owns a frame of its own, as ADR 0014 requires of any subsystem that computes in f32: its origin is the corner of an Anchor, and each entry's translation is composed from that corner in f64 and rounded to f32 once. A build keeps the bound TLAS's Anchor, so the frame rays trace in does not depend on which frame last built; a camera that strays more than a Chunk's edge from it starts a build that moves the frame to the camera's Anchor, with no change needed, so a shadow ray composes and lifts 10 km from where it started as at the origin.

/// `instance`'s transform as rows of the 3×4 matrix a TLAS Instance holds,
/// its translation from `anchor`'s corner (`place`).
fn anchored_transform(instance: &InstanceData, anchor: [i32; 3]) -> [f32; 12] {
    let mut transform = unplaced_transform(instance);
    place(&mut transform, InstancePosition::of(instance), anchor);
    transform
}

/// `instance`'s transform as rows of the 3×4 matrix a TLAS Instance holds,
/// with no translation.
fn unplaced_transform(instance: &InstanceData) -> [f32; 12] {
    let [x_axis, y_axis, z_axis] = instance.rotation_scale;
    bytemuck::cast([0, 1, 2].map(|axis| [x_axis[axis], y_axis[axis], z_axis[axis], 0.0]))
}

/// Sets `transform`'s translation to `position` from `anchor`'s corner,
/// composed in f64, so only the f32 result rounds (ADR 0014).
fn place(transform: &mut [f32; 12], position: InstancePosition, anchor: [i32; 3]) {
    for axis in 0..3 {
        let cells = i64::from(position.anchor[axis]) - i64::from(anchor[axis]);
        let translation = cells as f64 * ANCHOR_CELL_SIZE + f64::from(position.offset[axis]);
        transform[axis * 4 + 3] = translation as f32;
    }
}

translation = (Instance Anchor − TLAS Anchor) × 64 m + Instance offset

The difference of the two Anchors is exact in integers, and the Instance's offset from its own cell's corner is under 64 m, so the only rounding that grows with distance is the final one, to f32, of the distance from the TLAS's origin. The rows come out as the 3×4 matrix [R | t], row-major, as wgpu takes them: each row is one axis's components of the rotation and scale columns, then that axis's translation. The rows of an entry change carry no translation: each of the two TLASes keeps its own Anchor and, by entry, the position of the Instance it holds, so the TLAS that takes a change composes the translation in its own frame, whichever Anchor that is.

impl AnchoredTlas {
    /// Takes `change`, composing its Instance's translation in its frame.
    fn apply(&mut self, change: &EntryChange) {
        let entry = change.entry as usize;
        let instance = change.instance.as_ref().map(|instance| {
            if entry >= self.positions.len() {
                self.positions
                    .resize(entry + 1, InstancePosition::default());
            }
            self.positions[entry] = instance.position;
            let mut tlas_instance = instance.tlas_instance.clone();
            place(&mut tlas_instance.transform, instance.position, self.anchor);
            tlas_instance
        });
        *self
            .tlas
            .get_mut_single(entry)
            .expect("entries are taken under its capacity") = instance;
    }

    /// Composes every entry's translation anew in its frame.
    fn place_every_entry(&mut self) {
        let anchor = self.anchor;
        let entries = self
            .tlas
            .get_mut_slice(0..self.positions.len())
            .expect("positions are held for entries under its capacity");
        for (entry, &position) in entries.iter_mut().zip(&self.positions) {
            if let Some(instance) = entry {
                place(&mut instance.transform, position, anchor);
            }
        }
    }
}

Two TLASes and a worker

wgpu 30 cannot update a TLAS in place, so every change is a full rebuild, whose CPU cost a worker thread takes: rays trace the bound TLAS while the worker applies the changes the other lacks and records its build, the main thread submits it, and the frame after binds it. Each frame's changes are one shared batch that both TLASes take, so neither ever copies the other.

/// Takes `frame`'s Instance writes, each static one's BLAS from
/// `mesh_pool`; binds the spare if its build was submitted in the
/// frame before; collects the worker's result, submitting its build
/// through `queue`; and hands the idle spare to a new worker on
/// `device` when either TLAS lacks a change or the bound one is to
/// re-anchor at `frame`'s camera. With `wait`, it waits for that worker,
/// and a Far proxy's entry change starts a build as any other does, so
/// no frame depends on the worker's timing or on the frames before it.
pub fn update(
    &mut self,
    frame: &FrameData,
    mesh_pool: &MeshPool,
    device: &wgpu::Device,
    queue: &wgpu::Queue,
    wait: bool,
) -> StaticTlasUpdate {
    self.frame += 1;
    self.follow_the_camera_while_empty(frame.camera.anchor);
    let (changes, starts_build) = self.entry_changes(frame, mesh_pool);
    if !changes.is_empty() {
        let changes = Arc::new(changes);
        for behind in &mut self.behind {
            behind.push(ChangeBatch {
                frame: self.frame,
                changes: changes.clone(),
                starts_build: starts_build || wait,
            });
        }
    }
    let mut update = StaticTlasUpdate::default();
    if let Some(Spare::Submitted { .. }) = self.spare {
        let Some(Spare::Submitted { tlas, built_from }) = self.spare.take() else {
            unreachable!("matched")
        };
        let unbound = std::mem::replace(&mut self.bound, tlas);
        self.bound_index = 1 - self.bound_index;
        self.spare = Some(Spare::Idle(unbound));
        update.frames_until_traced =
            built_from.map(|built_from| (self.frame - built_from) as u32);
    }
    loop {
        self.collect(queue, wait, &mut update);
        if !self.start(device, frame.camera.anchor) || !wait {
            break;
        }
    }
    update
}

/// Moves both TLASes to `camera_anchor` while no static Instance has
/// taken an entry, which needs no build.

        && let Some(Spare::Idle(spare)) = &mut self.spare
    {
        self.bound.anchor = camera_anchor;

/// Hands the idle spare to a worker on `device` when its entries lack a
/// change, the bound TLAS's build lacks one that starts a build or one
/// `FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES` old, or the bound TLAS's Anchor is
/// more than `REANCHOR_CELLS` from `camera_anchor` along an axis. The
/// worker records its build in the latter two cases, and re-anchors it
/// at `camera_anchor` in the last alone. Returns whether it did.
fn start(&mut self, device: &wgpu::Device, camera_anchor: [i32; 3]) -> bool {
    let spare_index = 1 - self.bound_index;
    let lacking = &self.behind[self.bound_index];
    let built_from = (lacking.iter())
        .find(|batch| batch.starts_build)
        .map(|batch| batch.frame);
    let waited = lacking
        .first()
        .is_some_and(|batch| self.frame - batch.frame >= FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES);
    let camera_strayed = (0..3).any(|axis| {
        (i64::from(camera_anchor[axis]) - i64::from(self.bound.anchor[axis])).abs()
            > i64::from(REANCHOR_CELLS)
    });
    let records_build = built_from.is_some() || waited || camera_strayed;
    let build_anchor = if camera_strayed {
        camera_anchor
    } else {
        self.bound.anchor
    };
    if !matches!(self.spare, Some(Spare::Idle(_)))
        || (self.behind[spare_index].is_empty() && !records_build)
    {
        return false;
    }
    let Some(Spare::Idle(mut tlas)) = self.spare.take() else {
        unreachable!("matched")
    };
    let batches = std::mem::take(&mut self.behind[spare_index]);
    let filled_entries = u64::from(self.end) - self.free_entries.len() as u64;
    let uploaded_bytes = filled_entries * self.entry_upload_bytes;
    let device = records_build.then(|| device.clone());
    let worker = std::thread::Builder::new()
        .name("Static TLAS Worker".to_string())
        .spawn(move || {
            let started = Instant::now();
            let moved = device.is_some()
                && std::mem::replace(&mut tlas.anchor, build_anchor) != build_anchor;
            for change in batches.iter().flat_map(|batch| batch.changes.iter()) {
                tlas.apply(change);
            }
            drop(batches);
            if moved {
                tlas.place_every_entry();
            }
            let build = device.map(|device| {
                let mut encoder =
                    device.create_command_encoder(&wgpu::CommandEncoderDescriptor {
                        label: Some("Static TLAS Encoder"),
                    });
                encoder.build_acceleration_structures(
                    std::iter::empty(),
                    std::iter::once(&*tlas.tlas),
                );
                encoder.finish()
            });
            Work {
                tlas,
                build,
                time: started.elapsed(),
            }
        })
        .expect("the OS starts a thread");
    self.spare = Some(Spare::Working {
        worker,
        built_from,
        uploaded_bytes,
    });
    true
}
/// Takes the worker's result, with `wait` waiting for it, else once it
/// is done, and submits its build through `queue`.
fn collect(&mut self, queue: &wgpu::Queue, wait: bool, update: &mut StaticTlasUpdate) {
    let Some(Spare::Working { worker, .. }) = &self.spare else {
        return;
    };
    if !wait && !worker.is_finished() {
        return;
    }
    let Some(Spare::Working {
        worker,
        built_from,
        uploaded_bytes,
    }) = self.spare.take()
    else {
        unreachable!("matched")
    };
    let work = worker
        .join()
        .unwrap_or_else(|panic| std::panic::resume_unwind(panic));
    self.spare = Some(match work.build {
        Some(build) => {
            queue.submit(std::iter::once(build));
            update.build_time = Some(work.time);
            update.uploaded_bytes = uploaded_bytes;
            Spare::Submitted {
                tlas: work.tlas,
                built_from,
            }
        }
        None => Spare::Idle(work.tlas),
    });
}

A job starts only on an idle spare, when its entries lack a change, the bound TLAS lacks a change that starts a build, its oldest lacking batch is FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES, 120, frames old, or the camera's Anchor is more than REANCHOR_CELLS from the bound TLAS's along an axis. The worker, a thread spawned for the job, applies every batch the spare lacks, oldest first, so an entry changed twice ends as its last change says, and drops its share of the batches. It builds in the latter three cases: it first moves the spare's Anchor to build_anchor, the camera's when the camera strayed and the bound TLAS's otherwise, takes the batches in that frame, and, if the Anchor moved, composes every entry anew (section 3); the job's Work then carries a finished command buffer. A Far proxy's change alone so waits on the bound TLAS's list until another change starts a build, the camera strays, or it is 120 frames old; any build brings every change the spare lacks, the waiting ones included. built_from, the frame of the earliest change the bound TLAS lacks that starts a build, is set only when it lacks one, so a build that only re-anchors, or only brings Far proxies' changes, reports no frames until traced, and a build that brings both counts from the change that started it. CommandEncoder and Tlas are Send, so the worker records and finishes, and only the main thread submits, as ADR 0016 rules. Once the worker is done, or at once with wait, collect joins it, submits a build through the queue and reports the worker's time and the bytes the build uploads; a job without one, a catch-up, returns the spare to idle and reports neither. A panic on the worker resumes on the main thread.

A build uploads as well as records. wgpu-core converts each filled entry into its backend's own instance record on the CPU and stages it for the build, every build, whether the entry changed or not: 72 bytes on Metal and 64 on Vulkan and DX12, as tlas_entry_upload_bytes gives them, which the dynamic TLAS counts its own by (the dynamic TLAS, The fill). start counts the build's bytes on the main thread as it hands the spare over: the entries taken less the entries freed, which are the entries the spare holds once the worker has applied the batches it lacks, at the backend's bytes each. The count waits in Spare::Working beside built_from, and collect hands it to the update when it submits the build, so the frame that submits a build adds it to its uploaded_bytes. 9 · ADRs 0013, 0014, 0016, 0020

Why this way

Two TLAS, split by motion. ADR 0020 splits the near field into a static TLAS, rebuilt only when its static Instances change, and a dynamic TLAS of Moving Instances, rebuilt each frame and capped at 10,000 Instances. One TLAS over everything would rebuild over every static Instance whenever anything moved, since wgpu-core 30 has no TLAS update, only rebuilds.

A worker records, the main thread submits. Rebuilding town's 254,256 Instances took 17.5 to 18.1 ms of CPU on the M4 Pro under wgpu 30.0.1, and an empty TLAS of 262,144 entries 4.3 to 6.9 ms (docs/research/acceleration-structures-for-streamed-worlds.md). A worker can fill, record and finish, 13 to 14 ms of it, and hand the command buffer over, as ADR 0016 lets workers do; the main thread keeps the submit, which the research measured at 4.3 to 4.5 ms at that size alone and 5.6 to 7.1 ms beside a frame's when wgpu-core 30.0.1 walked one dependency per entry; the vendored wgpu-core, which walks one per distinct BLAS, has since taken that walk out of the submit's cost (section 6), and the worker keeps the part that grows with the capacity. Recording the build in a command buffer of its own on the main thread (the research's option A) overlaps it on the GPU but leaves the whole CPU cost in the frame; several smaller static TLASes, by Chunk group or ring (option C), make each rebuild cheap, but every ray then queries each of them, at a cost not yet measured, and on Metal each is a binding of its own.

A spare, swapped in. A bind group holds its TLAS from creation, so two TLASes take two groups, and the worker builds the one no frame binds; rebuilding the bound TLAS in place (S2) would put the build in the frame that traces it and leave ordering its build after the passes that read it to Metal's hazard tracking. The spare costs a second TLAS's memory, about 76.3 MB at 264,720 entries on the M4 Pro. Ize, Wald and Parker switch a BVH rebuilt over several frames in "with two pointer swaps"; the research found no shipped engine that double-buffers its TLAS. Sharing each frame's changes between the two keeps the main thread's work in proportion to the changes, not to the capacity, and the catch-up keeps the second build a change would otherwise cost.

Captures wait, frames do not. Which frame finds the worker done depends on the machine, so a capture, whose image must not, waits for the worker in the frame that starts it and binds the build in the next, and has every change start a build, a Far proxy's included, so which frame traces a change never depends on how many frames came before it. Frames in a window and the bench never wait.

Tagged with the slot, indexed by none. Indexing entries by slot would need no map, as an Instance keeps its slot while it is one, but it would cap slot numbers rather than static Instances: Chunks take their ranges past the highest slot taken, and the Instance buffer holds nearly twice the static TLAS's capacity, so a Chunk's static Instances could fall past the capacity with entries to spare. A table of entries by slot keeps the old cost, one array read per Instance written, Moving ones included, for 8 bytes of CPU memory per slot up to the highest that held a static Instance; a hash map would cost a hash per Moving Instance each frame, 10,000 of them in town. The tag stays the slot: the frame's shaders do not read it, and a test's query reads it back to name the Instance a ray hit.

An Anchor kept until the camera strays. ADR 0014 forbids f32 World positions on the GPU and names ray-tracing acceleration structures among the subsystems that convert into a local frame they own. In a frame fixed at load, a camera 10 km from where it started would see its shadow rays start 153 mm off their surfaces, with 0.49 mm of rounding. Unreal 5 and Unity's HDRP write their TLAS entries relative to the camera, but rebuild every frame, so moving the origin costs them nothing (docs/research/acceleration-structures-for-streamed-worlds.md, option 8). Re-anchoring every frame here would cost a rebuild a frame, which is ruled out. Re-anchoring at every build a Chunk's change already starts was free, but it made the frame rays trace in depend on which frame last built, and a ray's offset grows with its distance from the Anchor: world drawn through every frame and drawn at a few frames alone traced the same frame in different cells, and a ray that grazed an edge answered differently. An Anchor computed from the frame's camera alone, the nearest corner of a Chunk-wide grid, would have fixed that, but it moved the Goldens of six scenes and builds at every crossing of a grid line. So a build keeps the bound TLAS's Anchor and only a stray camera moves it (ADR 0020), and the dynamic TLAS, rebuilt each frame in the bound TLAS's frame, follows in the frame a new one is bound. Composing each TLAS's translations from its own positions, rather than shifting the old ones, keeps a moved entry rounding as a fresh one does; it costs 24 bytes of CPU memory per entry per TLAS.

A Chunk's edge away. A camera can travel without a Chunk change, straight up or within the Load radius of a scene that loads everything, and with builds no longer moving the frame, only a stray camera does: a build that moves the frame runs once the camera's Anchor is more than four cells from the bound TLAS's along an axis. Four cells, a Chunk's edge, keeps what the camera adds to a ray's offset under 4.9 mm, for the price of a full rebuild per just over 256 to 320 m of travel. world, whose camera flies, pays it about every 320 m of its flight; no other Reference scene's camera strays that far.

Far proxies join the next build. Every build is of every entry, about 250,000 in world, and at its camera's 50 m a second Far proxies cross the View distance several times a second. With each crossing starting a build, the worker would rebuild almost without pause and frames would sit on a build's submit. A Far proxy's change alone therefore waits for the next build another change or a re-anchor starts, or for FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES, 120 frames, so a proxy that appears where nothing else changes is still traced. A swap at the Load radius writes the Chunk's Placements in the same frame, which start a build, so the frames the draw and the shadows disagree there stay as few as for any load. frames_until_traced counts from the earliest change that starts a build, so a waiting Far proxy does not enter it.

A width class in the mask. A Thin Primitive is drawn a pixel wide where it would be narrower, so its shadow, traced at its true width, breaks into dots where it falls under a pixel. The one per-entry field a ray filters on in traversal is the 8-bit mask, so the width goes there, binned: bit 0 for everything ordinary, and seven classes that double from 3.125 mm, past which a tube is wide enough to cast at any pixel that sees it. The ray side then leaves out the classes too narrow for its receiver's pixel at no cost per triangle (the shadow mask pass). One entry holds one mask, so a Mesh that mixes thin and ordinary Primitives keeps the ordinary bit and casts everywhere, as before, and a Mesh of several Thin Primitives takes its widest one's class.

A capacity sized from its Budget. Every rebuild walks the full capacity, filled or not, so a larger one costs every static spawn in every scene. ADR 0013 splits the 8 GB GPU memory Budget into a Budget per kind, and the static TLAS's two TLASes take 57% of the acceleration structures', at 289 bytes an entry, the most a driver sizes one on the adapters benched, which gives 264,720 entries, enough for town and for world's densest view with its Far proxies.

A streamed load waits for its BLASes, not the frame. Nothing on the GPU orders the build after the compaction copies of the BLASes it reads: wgpu-core 30.0.2 records no barrier after a compaction copy, and Metal has no acceleration-structure barrier. Waiting on the main thread, which alone submits and polls under ADR 0016, cost every frame that loaded a Mesh a GPU round trip. A streamed load's Instances are instead placed only once a poll finds each BLAS they name built and compacted, a few frames later, and no frame waits; a Settled frame and a spawn keep the wait, so a capture's frames, its Goldens and the bench's counter frames place what they load the frame they load it. The per-frame refit, which could not wait either way, orders its writes on the GPU instead (the BLAS refit).

10

Limits

A streamed Chunk is traced frames after it loads. Its Instances are placed, drawn and handed to the static TLAS only once every BLAS they name is built and compacted, and then wait for a build like any change. A Settled frame and a spawn instead wait on the main thread for the GPU to build and compact their BLASes, a GPU round trip in that frame. Both hold until a wgpu release orders a TLAS build after a compaction copy on every backend, a version change that is the owner's call.

A build lands a frame or more after its change. A change is traced from the frame after the build that brings it is submitted, and a change made while a build or a catch-up is on the worker waits for it to come back first. frames_until_traced counts the wait.

A Far proxy can wait 120 frames. A Far proxy's change in a frame that changes nothing else static, and whose camera has not strayed, is traced only from the next build another change starts, or after FAR_PROXY_BUILD_WAIT_FRAMES; meanwhile a proxy that came into view casts no shadow and one that left still casts its own. frames_until_traced does not count that wait.

A rebuild walks every entry. Spawning or despawning one static Instance costs a full rebuild at 264,720 entries, on the worker, and uploads every filled entry's record again; only the submit's dependency walk is by distinct BLAS.

The unbound TLAS is rebuilt after frames that read it. Frames still on the GPU may trace the TLAS just unbound when its next build is submitted; queue order and Metal's hazard tracking order the two, as they ordered an in-place rebuild before.

Changes held for the catch-up. The TLAS just unbound keeps its share of the change batches until its catch-up has taken them, so a load's changes stay in CPU memory for a frame or more after its build.

A lock shared with the main thread. finish holds the device's snatch lock for reading, which a buffer or texture destroy, a poll that frees what one left, and a surface's present or discard take for writing, so such a call on the main thread can wait for the worker's whole finish.

Left out by write order. Past the capacity, which static Instances cast shadows is settled by when they were written, the left-out ones admitted lowest slot first, not by their distance from the camera.

Memory at full capacity, twice. The count, and wgpu-core's allocation, are for every entry of both TLASes, so the smallest scene pays what town does.

The old frame until the new one is bound. A camera that strays, or jumps, keeps tracing in the bound TLAS's frame, with its larger offset and coarser rounding, until a build re-anchored at it is bound: a frame or more after the move, longer while the worker is busy with an earlier job. Within a Chunk's edge the frame stays behind a travelling camera, so its rays' offsets sit nearer the 4.9 mm bound than at the Anchor.

The Anchor follows the camera's path. Where a build re-anchors depends on where the camera was in the frame it strayed, so a capture that draws only some frames of a path re-anchors where it next draws, and matches a run through every frame only while the camera stays within a Chunk's edge of the Anchor. A scene whose first static Instance is written after its first frame differs the same way.

A move rebuilds every entry. A build that only re-anchors costs what a Chunk change's build does: every entry composed anew on the worker and a full rebuild at 264,720 entries.

One mask per entry. A Mesh that mixes Thin Primitives with ordinary ones casts at every distance, and one of several Thin Primitives casts as far as its widest one; an Instance scaled unevenly across its tube takes its middle scale's width.

Entries far from the camera. The frame follows the camera, so an entry far from it rounds to f32 more coarsely: from 2,048 m out its step is 0.244 mm.

A pinned wgpu. No update in place and no barrier after compaction are wgpu 30's; each holds while the pin does. The submit's walk by distinct BLAS is the vendored wgpu-core's, carried under vendor/ until a wgpu release carries it (vendor/README.md); without it, every submit that builds or binds the static TLAS walks each of its entries.

History

The static TLAS arrived on 2026-10-20, together with the BLAS per Mesh, anchored at load and rebuilt only when a static Instance changes; its first build in every scene, empty or not, came when the orrery, whose bodies all move, showed it could stay unbuilt. Shadow rays then came to read it, and the dynamic TLAS took the Moving Instances beside it. On 2026-10-23 a test that sometimes missed a triangle showed the build could read a BLAS whose compaction had not finished, and the upload has waited for the copies since. On 2026-11-07 its entries stopped being the slots below its capacity, once Chunks took ranges of slots past the highest taken. On 2026-11-08 its build moved off the frame's critical path: a second TLAS, built on a worker thread and bound the frame after the main thread submits it, while rays trace the first; captures wait for the worker so their images do not depend on its timing. The same day its frame started following the camera: each build re-anchored it at the camera's Anchor, and a camera a Chunk's edge from it started a build of its own, so shadows 10 km out match those at the origin. On 2026-11-10 a vendored wgpu-core took each submit's walk of its dependencies from its entries to its distinct BLASes. On 2026-11-15 streamed loads stopped waiting for their BLASes: their Instances are placed once the BLASes are ready. On 2026-11-16 builds stopped re-anchoring it, once world showed that the frame rays trace in then depended on which frame last built, leaving the stray camera alone to move it; the same day its entries took a mask bit by width class, so a Thin Primitive's shadow ends where it would fall under a pixel.
