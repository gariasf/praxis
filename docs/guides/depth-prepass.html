The depth pre-pass

The render pass that draws phase one of the culling pass's frame's draw into the 4x MSAA depth target, cleared to 0 and kept by Greater under reverse Z, before anything is shaded, so the depth pyramid can reduce it and the lit pass can shade only what it kept; it draws Thin Primitives and Instances smaller than a pixel a pixel wide, through pipelines of their own that write the samples their coverage keeps as sample_mask; with shadow rays on it writes each sample's surface normals beside its depth, and phase two's depth pass draws phase two's Instances the same way once the pyramid has tested them.

Describes main at 62052fe8 (2026-11-21). A change to the pass rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: MSAA for samples and fragments, The culling pass for the frame's draw the GPU builds, its two phases, and which Instances it lists for the pipelines of their own or leaves out.

Interface

Runs
Every frame: after phase one of the culling pass and the Froxel pass, before the depth pyramid pass. Phase two's depth pass runs with the shadow_rays Render switch on, as Praxis ships: after phase two of the culling pass, before the shadow mask pass. With it off, the lit pass draws phase two's depth.
Reads
The frame's draw: its packed indices as the index buffer, and the indirect arguments of the phase's draw and of the phase's draw of its Thin Primitives and small Instances; the Meshlet records, the Instances, the Meshes' bounds, the Meshlets with each one's Thin Primitive radius, the Meshlet vertex Arena and the vertex Arena, from which the vertex shader pulls each vertex; the camera, with what a pixel spans a metre from it.
Writes
The depth target, Depth32Float at 4x MSAA: the pre-pass clears it to 0 and stores it, phase two's depth pass loads and stores it. With shadow rays on, the surface normals, Rgba8Unorm at 4x MSAA, cleared and loaded alike. A fragment of a Thin Primitive or a small Instance writes both only at the samples its coverage keeps.
Draws
Two draw_indexed_indirect per pass: the phase's draw, at its offset in the frame's draw, then the phase's Thin Primitives and small Instances, at PHASE_ONE_THIN_DRAW_OFFSET or PHASE_TWO_THIN_DRAW_OFFSET.
Bind groups
The groups its vertex stage sees: group 0, the frame, for the camera; group 2, the scene geometry; group 3, the main pass inputs, for the Meshlet records. Its fragment stages read no bind group.
Shader
src/render/shaders/main.wgsl, in the module MAIN, which wgpu compiles without bounding its loops: vs_depth_prepass with no fragment stage, or with shadow rays on vs_depth_normals and fs_surface_normals; for Thin Primitives and small Instances, vs_thin with fs_thin_depth, or with shadow rays on fs_thin_surface_normals.
Counted
depth_prepass and phase_two_depth_pass in GPU time, each with both its draws; the pre-pass inside ADR 0019's gate, which is measured with shadow rays off, when phase two's depth is the lit pass's; their draws in draw_calls; the depth target and the surface normals in GPU memory, under render_targets.

1 · MainPass::record_depth_pass

Where it sits in the frame

Depth comes before shading. The pre-pass gives the depth pyramid the surfaces phase two's occlusion test needs, gives the lit pass the one depth per sample it shades at, and, with shadow rays on, gives the shadow mask pass the surfaces it traces from.

The frame's passes with shadow rays on, in the order the GPU receives them

  1. dynamic TLAS build
  2. bend pass
  3. culling, phase one
  4. Froxel pass
  5. depth pre-pass
  6. depth pyramid pass
  7. culling, phase two
  8. phase two's depth pass
  9. shadow mask pass
  10. lit pass
  11. tonemapping pass

Phase one of the culling pass draws the Instances in view that were visible the frame before. The pre-pass draws them into depth; the depth pyramid pass reduces that depth to the farthest over each share of the screen; phase two tests every other Instance in view against the pyramid and draws what it does not hide. One function records both depth passes, differing in the attachment's load, the draws' offsets and whether the surface normals are cleared. Each pass draws twice: the phase's ordinary geometry, then its Thin Primitives and small Instances through a pipeline of their own.

MainPass::record_depth_pass, src/render/main_pass.rs.

The depth pipelines' layout keeps only the bind groups their vertex stage sees, since their fragment stages read none: the Materials' group, which the lit pass's fragment stage alone reads, is left out of the layout and not set. The second draw sets only its pipeline: both pipelines share the layout, so the groups and the index buffer set for the first stay bound. With shadow rays off the ordinary depth pipeline has no fragment stage at all.

Every pipeline of the main pass comes from one shader module, MAIN, which MainPass::new compiles with create_with_unbounded_loops: every runtime check wgpu inserts stays on but the one that bounds each loop.

let runtime_checks = wgpu::ShaderRuntimeChecks {

    ..wgpu::ShaderRuntimeChecks::checked()
};

The depth passes' stages hold no loop, so for them nothing changes; the loops of the module are the lit pass's. The function refuses a module outside LOOPS_UNBOUNDED, whose parts a test reads loop by loop.

2 · vertex_output · src/render/shaders/main.wgsl

One draw, its vertices pulled

The draw has no vertex buffers. Each index the culling pass wrote packs a Meshlet record above its low bits and a vertex of that Meshlet below them; the vertex shader reads the record for the Instance, the Meshlet and the Material, and pulls the vertex from the vertex Arena.

fn vertex_output(packed_index: u32) -> VertexOutput {
    var out: VertexOutput;
    // The frame's draw has no base vertex, so the vertex index is the packed
    // index: a Meshlet record, which names the Instance, the Meshlet and the
    // Material, and a vertex of that Meshlet (ADR 0019).
    let index = unpack_index(packed_index);
    let record = meshlet_records[index.record];
    let instance = instances[record.instance_slot];
    let first_vertex = meshlets[record.meshlet].first_vertex;
    let vertex = decode_vertex(vertices[meshlet_vertices[first_vertex + index.vertex]]);
    let position = camera_relative_position(
        instance.anchor,
        instance.offset,
        camera.anchor,
        camera.offset,
        instance.rotation_scale * vertex.position
    );
    out.clip_position = camera.view_proj * vec4<f32>(position, 1.0);

vertex_output, main.wgsl, to the clip position; the rest of it fills what the lit pass reads.

unpack_index splits the index at MESHLET_VERTEX_BITS, which Rust declares from the cooker's limit of 64 vertices a Meshlet. Since the record is in every index, triangles of different Meshlets or Instances never share an index, even where their positions coincide. The position is composed relative to the camera from the Instance's Anchor and offset and the camera's (ADR 0014), so no World position reaches the GPU in f32, and the view-projection matrix holds no translation.

Every vertex entry point of the main pass calls this one function and declares the clip position @invariant, so the depth passes and the lit pass compute each vertex's depth by the same expressions and the lit pass's Equal test meets exactly the depth kept here. vs_depth_prepass returns only that position; vs_depth_normals also passes on the position, normal and Material ID the fragment stage of section 7 reads; vs_thin, below, moves the vertex before it returns it.

3 · vs_thin · ADR 0027

A pixel wide

Geometry narrower than a pixel falls between the samples of 4x MSAA and breaks into dots and dashes. A Thin Primitive and a small Instance are instead moved out in the vertex stage until they draw a pixel wide, and their true size is kept as a coverage that decides how many samples each fragment keeps.

The culling pass lists apart, in each phase, every Meshlet of a Thin Primitive and every Meshlet of an Instance whose Mesh bounds cover under a pixel's area, a small Instance; a Mesh with a Thin Primitive is never small, and keeps that rule alone. Which it lists, and in what order, is on The culling pass. Each pipeline of the main pass has a twin that draws them, the depth pipelines' with the vertex stage vs_thin:

let thin_depth_prepass_pipeline = if switches.shadow_rays {

    )
} else {
    depth_pipeline(
        "Thin Depth Pre-pass Pipeline",
        "vs_thin",
        depth_fragment("fs_thin_depth", &[]),

MainPass::new, src/render/main_pass.rs. The two lit pass pipelines' twins run vs_thin too, with fs_thin_depth and fs_thin_main.

Every twin runs the same vertex stage, so the lit pass meets at depth equal the depths the depth passes kept, as for ordinary geometry. It starts from vertex_output and adds the inputs the fragment stages need:

A Thin Primitive: out from the centre line

Each Meshlet carries its Primitive's Thin Primitive radius in the Meshlet table, 0 for any other Primitive. A vertex of a tube lies the radius out from its centre line along its normal, so the centre lies the radius back along it. What a pixel spans there, pixel_size_per_metre times the view depth, sets the radius the tube draws at, and the vertex moves out along the same line to it:

@vertex
fn vs_thin(@builtin(vertex_index) packed_index: u32) -> ThinVertexOutput {
    var out = vertex_output(packed_index);
    let index = unpack_index(packed_index);
    let record = meshlet_records[index.record];
    let meshlet = meshlets[record.meshlet];
    let instance = instances[record.instance_slot];
    var radius_metres = 0.0;
    var pixel_metres = 0.0;
    var instance_coverage = 1.0;
    if meshlet.thin_radius_metres > 0.0 {
        let normal =
            decode_vertex(vertices[meshlet_vertices[meshlet.first_vertex + index.vertex]]).tangent_frame.normal;
        let radial = instance.rotation_scale * (normal * meshlet.thin_radius_metres);
        radius_metres = length(radial);
        let centre = out.camera_relative_position - radial;
        pixel_metres = camera.pixel_size_per_metre * (camera.view_proj * vec4<f32>(centre, 1.0)).w;
        // At or behind the camera's plane a pixel spans nothing: the vertex stays.
        if pixel_metres > 0.0 && radius_metres > 0.0 {
            let drawn_radius_metres = thin_drawn_radius(radius_metres, pixel_metres);
            out.camera_relative_position = centre + radial * (drawn_radius_metres / radius_metres);
            out.clip_position = camera.view_proj * vec4<f32>(out.camera_relative_position, 1.0);
        }
// A tube of three faces seen along a face is this share of its diameter wide.
const THIN_NARROWEST_VIEW: f32 = 0.75;

fn thin_drawn_radius(radius_metres: f32, pixel_metres: f32) -> f32 {
    return max(radius_metres, 0.5 * pixel_metres / THIN_NARROWEST_VIEW);
}

vs_thin, main.wgsl, its first branch; thin_drawn_radius, common.wgsl.

A tube of three faces seen along a face spans 0.75 of its diameter, so a tube drawn at half a pixel over 0.75 is a pixel wide in its narrowest view and a third wider than a pixel across. Every tube takes that share whatever its faces, so a tube of six faces draws a little wider than it needs. A tube already wider than that keeps its radius and does not move. A vertex at or behind the camera's plane, where a pixel spans nothing, stays where it is.

A small Instance: out from its bounds' centre

Any other vertex is of a Mesh with no Thin Primitive. Its Instance's coverage is the share of a pixel's area its Mesh bounds cover, and its vertices move out from the bounds' centre, across the view only, by the scale that brings that share to a whole pixel:

} else {
    let stored_min = meshes[instance.mesh].bounds_min;
    let stored_max = meshes[instance.mesh].bounds_max;
    let bounds_min = vec3<f32>(stored_min[0], stored_min[1], stored_min[2]);
    let bounds_max = vec3<f32>(stored_max[0], stored_max[1], stored_max[2]);
    let bounds_centre = camera_relative_position(
        instance.anchor,
        instance.offset,
        camera.anchor,
        camera.offset,
        instance.rotation_scale * (0.5 * (bounds_min + bounds_max))
    );
    instance_coverage = small_instance_coverage(
        bounds_centre,
        instance.rotation_scale,
        0.5 * (bounds_max - bounds_min),
        (camera.view_proj * vec4<f32>(bounds_centre, 1.0)).w,
        camera.pixel_size_per_metre
    );
    // Across the view only: each vertex keeps its depth, as the culling pass's box does.
    let view_axis = vec3<f32>(camera.view_proj[0].w, camera.view_proj[1].w, camera.view_proj[2].w);
    let from_centre = out.camera_relative_position - bounds_centre;
    let along_view = view_axis * (dot(from_centre, view_axis) / max(dot(view_axis, view_axis), 1e-30));
    out.camera_relative_position = bounds_centre + along_view
        + (from_centre - along_view) * small_instance_scale(instance_coverage);
    out.clip_position = camera.view_proj * vec4<f32>(out.camera_relative_position, 1.0);
}
// Under an eighth of coverage no sample is kept, so the scale stops there.
fn small_instance_scale(coverage: f32) -> f32 {
    return inverseSqrt(clamp(coverage, 0.125, 1.0));
}

vs_thin, main.wgsl, its second branch; small_instance_scale, common.wgsl.

small_instance_coverage sums the areas the bounds' faces present to the camera, projected one metre along the view axis, over a pixel's area there, first order in the bounds' size over their distance. It takes the larger of that and the square of the bounds' sphere's diameter over a square four pixels wide, SMALL_INSTANCE_WIDEST_PIXELS, so an Instance whose bounds' sphere draws four pixels wide counts as covering a pixel and a long sliver is not stretched. Coverage of a whole pixel or more, or bounds at or behind the camera's plane, read as 1, and the vertex keeps its place: the scale is then 1. The scale is the inverse square root of the coverage, since it widens the bounds along both axes across the view, and it stops at an eighth of coverage, under which no sample is kept. Each vertex keeps its distance along the view axis, as the culling pass's box does when it tests the widened bounds.

The coverage leaves the vertex stage as instance_coverage, flat, the same for every fragment of the Instance; a Thin Primitive's leaves as its radius, flat, and what a pixel spans at its centre line, interpolated.

4 · src/render/targets.rs · ADR 0015

Reverse Z: cleared to 0, nearer is greater

Depth is reversed with an infinite far plane: the far plane is 0, the near plane 1, and a sample keeps the greatest depth. A float's precision is densest near 0, which reverse Z puts at the distance, where a perspective projection needs it.

pub fn depth_stencil_state() -> wgpu::DepthStencilState {
    wgpu::DepthStencilState {
        format: DEPTH_FORMAT,
        depth_write_enabled: Some(true),
        depth_compare: Some(wgpu::CompareFunction::Greater),
        stencil: wgpu::StencilState::default(),
        bias: wgpu::DepthBiasState::default(),
    }
}
pub fn depth_attachment(&self) -> wgpu::RenderPassDepthStencilAttachment<'_> {
    wgpu::RenderPassDepthStencilAttachment {
        view: &self.depth,
        depth_ops: Some(wgpu::Operations {
            load: wgpu::LoadOp::Clear(0.0),
            store: wgpu::StoreOp::Store,
        }),
        stencil_ops: None,
    }
}

depth_stencil_state and RenderTargets::depth_attachment, src/render/targets.rs.

The projection puts the near distance in clip z and the view depth in clip w, so a stored depth is the near distance over the view depth: 1 at the near plane, falling toward 0 with distance, never reaching it. With a 0.1 m near plane in Depth32Float, standard depth resolves 2.4 m at 2 km and reversed depth with no far plane 0.15 mm (ADR 0015). A sample nothing covers keeps the clear value, 0, which every later pass reads as background. The same direction runs through the frame: the depth pyramid keeps the minimum, the farthest; the shadow mask pass takes a pixel's nearest sample as its greatest depth. Every depth pipeline, the twins included, uses this state, so a widened vertex is tested and kept like any other.

5 · fs_thin_depth · fs_thin_surface_normals · ADR 0027

The samples coverage keeps

A fragment drawn wider than its geometry keeps only as many of the pixel's four samples as its coverage, rounded to the nearest, and says so by writing sample_mask: the depth passes' twins write it, so only the kept samples take the fragment's depth, and the lit pass meets those depths. The samples kept follow from the count alone, so every fragment of a pixel keeps the same ones.

// The mask depends on the count alone: every fragment of a pixel must keep the same samples.
fn thin_kept_samples(coverage: f32) -> u32 {
    let count = u32(saturate(coverage) * 4.0 + 0.5);
    return (0xfb910u >> (4u * count)) & 15u;
}

thin_kept_samples, common.wgsl.

4x MSAA holds five coverage levels. The count is the coverage times four, rounded to the nearest, so none below an eighth, one from an eighth, two from three eighths, three from five eighths and four from seven eighths. The constant 0xfb910u holds a mask per count, four bits each: none; sample 0; samples 0 and 3; samples 0, 1 and 3; all four. Sample 0 comes first, then 3, 1 and 2. No dither, no pattern across pixels and no state across frames choose them, so two faces of one tube over a pixel keep the same samples, and a still camera keeps the same image.

// The samples of a pixel's four that a fragment of a Thin Primitive keeps:
// the tube's true width over the width it draws, of them, to the nearest;
// any other fragment keeps its Instance's coverage of them. The depth passes
// write them, and the lit pass shades what covers them. The radius is a flat
// input, which keeps the M4 Pro from merging the fragments of triangles that
// share indices (ADR 0019).
fn thin_fragment_samples(in: ThinVertexOutput) -> u32 {
    let width_share = in.radius_metres / thin_drawn_radius(in.radius_metres, max(in.pixel_metres, 1e-9));
    return thin_kept_samples(select(in.instance_coverage, width_share, in.radius_metres > 0.0));
}

@fragment
fn fs_thin_depth(in: ThinVertexOutput) -> @builtin(sample_mask) u32 {
    return thin_fragment_samples(in);
}

struct ThinSurfaceNormals {
    @location(0) normals: vec4<f32>,
    @builtin(sample_mask) kept_samples: u32,
}

@fragment

    return ThinSurfaceNormals(

        thin_fragment_samples(in)

}

thin_fragment_samples, fs_thin_depth and fs_thin_surface_normals, main.wgsl.

A Thin Primitive's coverage is the tube's true width over the width it draws: its radius over the drawn radius, worked out again in the fragment from the radius and what a pixel spans. A tube wide enough not to be widened has coverage 1 and keeps every sample it covers. A small Instance's coverage is its Instance's. The written mask is ANDed with the samples the triangle covers, so a fragment keeps a sample only where it is both covered and chosen.

What a pixel spans, pixel_metres, is interpolated at the centroid, a point inside the triangle's covered samples. Read at the pixel's centre, which can lie outside the triangle, it could fall below every vertex's value, and a tube just past the distance where it keeps no sample would keep one.

With shadow rays off the thin depth pipeline runs fs_thin_depth, which writes only the mask; with them on, fs_thin_surface_normals writes the mask and the surface normals of section 7 together, so the normals land in the kept samples alone. No pipeline of the main pass enables alpha-to-coverage: a pipeline that writes sample_mask may not, and the one multisample state every pipeline shares sets alpha_to_coverage_enabled to false. How the lit pass draws the same fragments at depth equal and shades the samples they keep is on The lit pass.

6 · thin_kept_samples · ADR 0027

No sample, no draw

Coverage under an eighth rounds to no sample. A fragment that keeps none writes no depth and no normals, and with culling on the culling pass leaves an Instance that would keep none out of the frame's draw altogether, so the depth passes never rasterise it.

In the depth passes the rule needs no branch: thin_kept_samples returns 0, the written mask keeps no sample, and the fragment leaves the depth target and the surface normals as they were. So a tube under a sixth of a pixel wide, whose drawn width is a third more than a pixel, and a small Instance under an eighth of a pixel's area draw nothing into depth, whether or not they are drawn.

With the culling Render switch on, as Praxis ships, the culling pass works out the same coverage from the camera and the Instance and leaves out of the frame's draw a small Instance whose coverage rounds to no sample, and an Instance of a Mesh that is all Thin Primitives once its widest tube keeps no sample at the bounds' nearest depth. The tests and where they sit among the culling pass's others are on The culling pass. With it off, every such Instance is drawn and writes nothing.

The lit pass's twin draws at depth equal, and another fragment's depth can equal a fragment's own at a sample it does not keep, so there a fragment that keeps no sample is discarded and writes no colour.

7 · surface_normals

The surface normals

The depth passes already rasterise every triangle the shadow mask pass will trace from. With shadow rays on they also write, for each sample, the triangle's own normal and the normal interpolated over it, so the mask knows what the triangle was rather than guessing it from neighbouring depths.

fn surface_normals(camera_relative_position: vec3<f32>, normal: vec3<f32>, material_id: u32) -> vec4<f32> {
    let crossed = cross(dpdx(camera_relative_position), dpdy(camera_relative_position));
    let crossed_length_squared = dot(crossed, crossed);
    let toward_camera = -normalize(camera_relative_position);
    let unit_crossed = crossed * inverseSqrt(crossed_length_squared);
    let triangle_normal = select(
        toward_camera,
        select(unit_crossed, -unit_crossed, dot(unit_crossed, toward_camera) < 0.0),
        crossed_length_squared > 0.0
    );
    let normal_length_squared = dot(normal, normal);
    let interpolated_normal = select(
        triangle_normal,
        normal * inverseSqrt(normal_length_squared),
        normal_length_squared > 0.0
    );
    let encoded_normals = vec4<f32>(octahedral_encode(triangle_normal), octahedral_encode(interpolated_normal))
        * 0.5 + 0.5;
    // Reads the flat input, so the stage has one the compiler cannot drop,
    // to no effect: Material IDs index the Material table, far below
    // NO_MATERIAL - 1.
    return select(encoded_normals, vec4<f32>(0.0), material_id == NO_MATERIAL - 1u);
}

@fragment
fn fs_surface_normals(in: DepthNormalsOutput) -> @location(0) vec4<f32> {
    return surface_normals(in.camera_relative_position, in.normal, in.material_id);
}

surface_normals and fs_surface_normals, main.wgsl; fs_thin_surface_normals calls the same function.

The triangle's normal. Across a triangle the camera-relative position changes linearly in the triangle's plane, so its screen-space derivatives are two vectors in that plane and their cross product is its normal. Where a 2×2 quad overhangs the triangle's edge, the pixels outside run as helper invocations of the same triangle, so even at an edge the derivatives belong to it. The cross product's sign depends on how the triangle lies on screen, so it is flipped to face the camera, the side the sample shows; a triangle seen exactly edge-on spans no plane on screen and takes the direction toward the camera. Neither case gives a NaN. For a widened triangle the position is the widened one, so the normal is that of the triangle as drawn.

The interpolated normal. The vertex normals, turned by the Instance's normal matrix and interpolated over the triangle, then normalised: the lit pass's normal before its normal map. One that interpolates to zero length takes the triangle's. The shadow mask reads no Textures, so normal maps play no part in it.

Octahedral, 8 bits a coordinate. A unit vector has two degrees of freedom. Projected onto the octahedron |x| + |y| + |z| = 1, with the lower half folded over the upper, every direction lands in the square [−1, 1]² with nearly even precision:

fn octahedral_encode(direction: vec3<f32>) -> vec2<f32> {
    let projected = direction.xy / (abs(direction.x) + abs(direction.y) + abs(direction.z));
    let sign = select(vec2<f32>(-1.0), vec2<f32>(1.0), projected >= vec2<f32>(0.0));
    return select((1.0 - abs(projected.yx)) * sign, projected, direction.z >= 0.0);
}

octahedral_encode, common.wgsl; the shadow mask pass decodes with octahedral_decode beside it.

Each coordinate is mapped from [−1, 1] to [0, 1] for the Rgba8Unorm target: the triangle normal in red and green, the interpolated one in blue and alpha, 4 bytes a sample. Decoded, each lies within 0.95° of the normal it encodes where the store rounds to the nearest step, as Metal's does; Vulkan only recommends that rounding and may round to either neighbour, which about doubles the error, and the shadow mask pass's margins are sized for both.

Under 4x MSAA the fragment shader runs once per pixel per triangle and its output lands in every sample that triangle covers, or for a twin every sample it keeps, so both normals are values at the pixel's centre, credited to each such sample. The pre-pass clears the target, phase two's depth pass loads it, and both store it for the shadow mask pass. With shadow rays off the depth pipelines write depth alone and the target is not written, though the targets module creates it either way.

8 · DepthNormalsOutput · ADR 0019

The flat input: each sample credited to its own triangle

On the Apple M4 Pro, a fragment stage that reads no flat input can have the fragments of neighbouring triangles that share indices merged in a pixel, one triangle's inputs written into both's samples. The depth passes and the lit pass must agree on which triangle each sample shows, so every fragment stage of them reads a flat input.

struct DepthNormalsOutput {
    @builtin(position) @invariant clip_position: vec4<f32>,
    @location(0) camera_relative_position: vec3<f32>,
    @location(1) normal: vec3<f32>,
    @location(2) @interpolate(flat) material_id: u32,
}

main.wgsl.

The lit pass reads its flat Material ID to find its Material, so its fragments are never merged. If the depth passes read none, theirs could be: at a sample beside a shared edge, the normals could come from one triangle while the lit pass shaded the other there, at the same depth, so it passed the Equal test. Across a crease the first may turn from a light the second faces, and the mask would hide a light the shading calls lit. Which fragments merge depends on what the draw holds, so culling on and off would give town images that differ at a few pixels.

An input a shader never reads can be dropped by the compiler, and the stage then reads no flat input after all. So surface_normals compares the Material ID with NO_MATERIAL − 1, the largest u32 but one: Material IDs index the Material table, far smaller, so the comparison never holds and the normals pass through unchanged, but the compiler cannot bound a value read from a vertex output and keeps the read. The twins need no such comparison: thin_fragment_samples reads the flat radius, and the flat coverage, for every fragment, and a fragment stage that writes sample_mask reads a flat input as ADR 0027 requires. What merging is, and how it was measured, is on Metal quirks.

9 · PHASE_TWO_DEPTH_PASS · fs_depth

Phase two's depth

Phase two's Instances are missing from the depth the pre-pass kept, and the lit pass shades only at depth equal. With shadow rays on they are drawn into depth in a pass of their own, so the shadow mask pass sees every surface; with them off, the lit pass draws them into depth just before it shades.

With shadow rays on, phase two's depth pass is the same function and the same pipelines as the pre-pass, recorded at PHASE_TWO_DRAW_OFFSET and PHASE_TWO_THIN_DRAW_OFFSET in the frame's draw, loading the depth target and the surface normals and storing both for the shadow mask pass. It costs one more load and store of the 4x depth each frame, even when phase two draws nothing, as in a scene where nothing moves into view.

With shadow rays off, no compute pass reads depth before the lit pass, so the lit pass draws phase two's depth inside its own render pass first, with the pre-pass's vertex stages and fragment stages that write no colour:

if !self.shadow_rays {
    lit_pass.set_pipeline(&self.lit_pass_depth_pipeline);
    lit_pass.draw_indexed_indirect(&self.frame_draw_buffer, PHASE_TWO_DRAW_OFFSET);
    lit_pass.set_pipeline(&self.thin_lit_pass_depth_pipeline);
    lit_pass.draw_indexed_indirect(&self.frame_draw_buffer, PHASE_TWO_THIN_DRAW_OFFSET);
}

12 · ADRs 0008, 0014, 0015, 0019, 0021, 0027

Why this way

A depth pre-pass at all. ADR 0019's option A: the pre-pass seeds the Hi-Z pyramid phase two culls against and the lit pass's depth-equal test, so the lit pass shades each covered sample's visible triangle once and nothing behind it. The culling pass writes the frame's draw in slot order, not front to back, so without depth first the lit pass would shade surfaces later hidden; the pre-pass restores front-to-back cost at the price of rasterising the frame's triangles twice.

The GPU builds the draw. Recording a draw per Primitive of each Instance on the CPU grows with the Instances, and town holds about a quarter of a million. The culling pass writes one index buffer with each draw's indirect arguments beside it, drawn with one draw_indexed_indirect per pipeline and phase, one native call on every backend, since wgpu's Metal backend has no count draws and runs multi-draw as a CPU loop (ADR 0019).

Reverse Z with no far plane. The view reaches 2 km; reversed Depth32Float resolves sub-millimetre depth there where standard depth resolves metres, and needs no far plane at all (ADR 0015).

4x MSAA depth. Thin geometry, such as town's wires, needs coverage tested at more than one place per pixel in every frame, since ADR 0008 lets nothing average frames, and a post-process filter cannot rebuild what no sample saw; 4x is the Apple9 GPU family's maximum (ADR 0019, MSAA).

Coverage as a written sample_mask, not alpha-to-coverage. Which samples a given alpha keeps is the platform's choice, so two machines would draw two images, and a pipeline that enables alpha-to-coverage may not also write sample_mask, which the same passes need (ADR 0027).

Rounded, not dithered. A dither between the five levels gives the right mean coverage at any width, as a stipple that crawls under a moving camera; the owner flew town with both and chose rounding. Alpha blending, as phone-wire antialiasing fades the line it widens, would be continuous at any distance but needs a sorted transparent pass the engine does not have yet, and accumulating coverage over frames is excluded by ADR 0008 (ADR 0027).

Pipelines of their own. On the M4 Pro a sample_mask output in the pipelines every Instance draws through cost town's frame as much as a discard there, in proportion to the indices drawn through stages that write it, while a second pipeline and draw a pass cost nothing measurable; so only the geometry that needs the mask draws through stages that write it. The twins draw every frame, since any Instance can be small (ADR 0027).

Normals written here, not rebuilt from depth. The shadow mask pass runs before the lit pass, so the lit pass cannot write them, and depth alone gives at best each sample's plane, guessed from neighbours that at an edge belong to other triangles. Of the options measured on town, writing the triangle's normal and the interpolated one per sample, and starting a ray inside the surface where the triangle turns from a light the interpolated normal faces, was chosen over moving the ray's origin by the shading normals, which needs the triangle's vertices, and over an offset estimated from the depth's curvature, which is approximate. Two normals at 8 bits a coordinate fill the 4 bytes a sample one normal at 16 bits would have; ADR 0019's amendment records the target and its cost.

A flat read, not another draw. Of the ways to make the depth passes credit each sample to the lit pass's triangle, reading a flat input costs nothing measurable and matches the measurement that a fragment stage reading one is never merged. Drawing the normals in one draw of both phases at depth equal would cost about another geometry pass, and no specification promises identical fragments from different fragment shaders anyway; writing them in the lit pass's own draw is impossible, as the mask is traced before it; a triangle ID per sample, as a visibility buffer stores, is exact by construction and stays the fallback should another GPU merge regardless. WGSL's @invariant and Metal's preserved invariance cover the position alone, which already made depth equal (ADR 0019).

Phase two's depth in its own pass, with rays. The mask must see every surface the lit pass will shade before the lit pass runs, so phase two's depth cannot wait for it; with rays off nothing needs it sooner, and drawing it inside the lit pass saves a load and store of the 4x depth (ADR 0019).

Loops left unbounded. wgpu bounds every loop of a module unless told the module holds no infinite loop, and on town the bound cost ADR 0019's gate measurable time on the M4 Pro; the depth passes' stages hold no loop. The module keeps wgpu's other checks, bounds checks among them, and a test reads its loops so that none can run forever whatever its buffers hold.

13

Limits

Twice through the triangles. Every triangle of phase one is rasterised here and again in the lit pass. The lever every option shares is fewer triangles: LOD selection and the drops at zero coverage take many off town, and its gate is still over its Budget on the M4 Pro.

Five levels. 4x MSAA holds five coverage levels, so the contrast along a Thin Primitive steps by a quarter as it narrows and ends at an eighth of coverage; a tube ends where it is a sixth of a pixel wide. A tapered tube keeps its taper as it widens, so the coverage at its ends is off by up to the quarter the cook tolerates. Every tube takes three faces' share of its diameter, so one of more faces draws wider than it needs.

A small Instance's steadiness. It keeps its mean coverage whatever its Mesh, since the samples it keeps fall by the area it gains, but how steadily it keeps them from frame to frame follows how much of its bounds its Mesh fills (ADR 0027).

Merging measured on one GPU. Only the M4 Pro was measured. Another Apple GPU, or a later macOS, may merge under other conditions or regardless of a flat input; the culled-against-unculled check on Metal would show it, and a triangle ID per sample is the way out.

Normals at the pixel's centre. The normals written into a sample are the triangle's values at the pixel's centre, not at the sample, and quantised to within about a degree; the shadow mask pass's margins absorb both.

Edge-on triangles. A triangle seen exactly edge-on records the direction toward the camera as its normal, not its own.

The target with rays off. The surface normals are created and counted in GPU memory even when shadow rays are off and nothing writes them.

History

The pre-pass began as a depth-only pass over a draw the CPU recorded per Primitive; it came to draw the culling pass's frame's draw, then only phase one's, phase two's depth following first inside the lit pass and, once the shadow mask pass needed it, in a pass of its own; with shadow rays it gained the surface normals, and then the flat read that keeps them credited to the lit pass's triangle, which gave it its form in the third milestone. In the fifth it gained pipelines of their own for Thin Primitives and then small Instances, which draw them a pixel wide and write the samples their coverage keeps, and leave nothing in depth below an eighth of coverage; its shader module came to be compiled without wgpu's loop bounding. The merging, its measurement and the alternatives are on Metal quirks; this page describes the pass as it is.
