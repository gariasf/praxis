The BLAS refit

In the crowd scene's refit form, the step that brings each bent figure's BLAS to the shape the bend pass gave it, by refitting it in place through wgpu-hal on Metal, before the dynamic TLAS is built over it; its rebuild form does the same through wgpu as the oracle, and the shipping form, the shadow proxies, needs neither.

Describes main at fbae12f4 (2026-11-04). A change to the refit rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: Bend Pass for the shapes the refit follows, BLAS for how a Mesh's BLAS is built at load, Dynamic TLAS for the build that reads the refitted BLASes, Waiting on the GPU for why Metal needs an explicit order here.

Interface

Runs
With the crowd_form Render switch at CrowdForm::Refit, on Metal only, or CrowdForm::Rebuild, on any backend: in each frame that bent anything, in a submission of its own with the frame's commands up to the bend pass, before the dynamic TLAS's build. At the default, CrowdForm::Proxies, the Renderer has none.
Reads
Each MeshUse::Refitted Mesh's ranges of the vertex and index Arenas, as its MeshBlas records them.
Writes
Each refitted BLAS, in place; in the refit form, one scratch region per BLAS and the marker's 4-byte result.
Builds
Refit form: one update per refitted BLAS, all in one wgpu-hal call on a command buffer of its own, then a marker of one invocation, mark_blas_refit, in another. Rebuild form: one full build per BLAS through wgpu, recorded into the frame's head.
Bind groups
The marker's group 3, BLAS_REFIT_MARKERS: the refits' scratch, read-only, and the result. The refits themselves bind nothing.
Shader
src/render/shaders/blas_refit_marker.wgsl, the module BLAS_REFIT_MARKER, built on Apple targets and in tests.
Counted
blas_refit in GPU time, the refit form's on Metal; the CPU stage blas_refit, which dynamic_tlas_cpu_microseconds sums; the scratch and the marker's result in GPU memory; the refitted BLASes in the acceleration-structure estimate, at the uncompacted rate.

Why the BLAS must follow the bend

A BLAS holds its triangles where they were when it was built. When the bend pass rewrites a figure's vertices, a figure that traces its own BLAS would cast the shadow of its rest shape, so its BLAS must be updated in every frame: rebuilt from scratch, or refitted, keeping the tree and recomputing its boxes.

A rebuild makes the tree again from the triangles, as if they were new. A refit, which Vulkan and wgpu call an update, keeps which triangles share a leaf and how the leaves group, and recomputes only the boxes, from the leaves up, around the triangles where they now are. It costs less to make and gives up four things, each of which the code holds to:

The same triangles. A refit repeats its build's description: the same buffers, offsets, counts, stride and formats. Only the positions in those ranges may change.

Built to allow it, and never compacted. A refitted Mesh's BLAS is built with ALLOW_UPDATE, in the one constant both the build and every refit read, and skips the compaction every other BLAS takes after its build.

Its index stream kept. A bent Mesh loaded as MeshUse::Drawn keeps no index stream, since only a BLAS build reads one; a refitted one is traced, so it keeps its stream for every frame's refit.

A tree chosen for another shape. The grouping was picked for the shape the BLAS was built in, the rest shape. As the triangles drift from it the boxes grow and overlap, and a ray tests more of them.

Three crowd forms

Which way the crowd's figures reach the TLAS is a Render switch with three values: Proxies, the default and what ships, where the figures have no BLAS; Refit, through wgpu-hal on Metal alone; and Rebuild, the same setup rebuilt through wgpu on any backend, the shapes a refit must match.

    /// As `Refit`, each BLAS rebuilt through wgpu instead: what refit must

The frame, cut after the bend pass

The refit must sit between the bend pass, which writes the vertices it reads, and the dynamic TLAS's build, which reads the BLASes it writes. So in a frame that bent anything the frame's encoder is cut after the bend pass, and its head goes to the GPU with the updates, in a submission of its own, ahead of the build.

let bend_queries = timestamps
    .as_mut()
    .map(|timestamps| timestamps.pass(BEND_PASS));
let bent = self
    .bend_pass
    .record(&mut encoder, &frame.bends, bend_queries);
let encoding = started.elapsed();
// Their BLASes take them too, in a submission of their own, which
// the dynamic TLAS's build, recorded after it, follows.
if let Some(blas_refit) = self.blas_refit.as_mut().filter(|_| bent) {
    let started = Instant::now();
    let head = std::mem::replace(&mut encoder, create_frame_encoder(&self.device));
    let refit_queries = timestamps
        .as_mut()
        .map(|timestamps| timestamps.pass(BLAS_REFIT_PASS));
    blas_refit.submit(
        &self.device,
        &self.queue,
        head,
        &self.mesh_pool,
        refit_queries,
    );
    if let Some(timestamps) = &mut timestamps {
        timestamps.split_submission();
    }
    recorder.stage(BLAS_REFIT, started);
}

The head holds what upload recorded, the frame's copies through staging among it, the start of the frame's GPU stats and the bend pass. BlasRefit::submit does the rest:

/// Records the update of `mesh_pool`'s refitted BLASes, submits it with
/// `head`, the frame's commands up to its bend pass, through `queue`, and
/// orders what `queue` submits next after them. The rebuild form records
/// the update into `head` and submits that one command buffer; the refit
/// form, on Apple, records it after `head`, in a command buffer of its own
/// submitted beside it and followed, when given `queries`, by a marker that
/// ends it.
pub fn submit(
    &mut self,
    device: &wgpu::Device,
    queue: &wgpu::Queue,
    mut head: wgpu::CommandEncoder,
    mesh_pool: &MeshPool,
    queries: Option<PassQueries<'_, '_>>,
) {
    if self.form == CrowdForm::Rebuild {
        mesh_pool.rebuild_refitted_blases(&mut head);
    }
    let head = head.finish();
    #[cfg(target_vendor = "apple")]
    if let Some(order) = &mut self.order {
        order.signal_from_next_submit(queue);
    }
    #[cfg(target_vendor = "apple")]
    let submission = if self.form == CrowdForm::Refit {
        let refits = self.record_refits(device, mesh_pool);
        let marker = queries
            .zip(self.marker.as_mut())
            .zip(self.scratch.as_ref())
            .map(|((queries, marker), (scratch, _))| {
                marker.record(device, scratch, queries.marker())
            });
        queue.submit([head, refits].into_iter().chain(marker))
    } else {
        queue.submit(std::iter::once(head))
    };
    #[cfg(not(target_vendor = "apple"))]
    let submission = {
        let _ = queries;
        queue.submit(std::iter::once(head))
    };
    #[cfg(target_vendor = "apple")]
    if let Some(order) = &self.order {
        order.wait_in_next_submit(queue);
    }
    if self.waits_on_cpu {
        let wait = wgpu::PollType::Wait {
            submission_index: Some(submission),
            timeout: None,
        };
        if let Err(error) = device.poll(wait) {
            tracing::error!(%error, "waiting for BLAS updates failed");
        }
    }
}

4 · ADR 0007 · wgpu 30.0.1

Beneath wgpu-core

wgpu-core, the layer beneath wgpu's safe API that validates, tracks and records barriers, builds every BLAS in full; wgpu-hal's Metal backend, beneath it, can refit. So the refit records straight into a wgpu-hal encoder, on a command buffer wgpu-core never sees.

wgpu's API has an update mode, but wgpu-core 30.0.1 logs "only rebuild implemented" for a BLAS asked to update and hands wgpu-hal a full build, whatever was asked (wgpu-30-blas-rebuild-only). wgpu-hal's Metal backend implements both modes, and for an update calls Metal's refit, from a source structure into a destination, which here are the same BLAS (wgpu-30-metal-refit). ADR 0007 allows exactly this: wgpu-hal only for a subsystem past wgpu's safe API.

An encoder of its own. CommandEncoder::as_hal_mut lends a wgpu command encoder's wgpu-hal encoder to a closure, on terms its docs set: the raw encoder is not destroyed by hand, the command buffer is not ended there but by wgpu's finish, and the wgpu encoder takes no other call while wgpu-hal records into it (wgpu-30-as-hal-mut-safety). wgpu-core panics on an encoder that mixes the two (wgpu-30-mixed-encoding-panics). So the refits get an encoder labelled "BLAS Refit Encoder", which only wgpu-hal ever records into.

Guards and the snatch lock. A refit needs several wgpu-hal objects at once: both Arenas, the scratch and every refitted BLAS. Each wgpu resource lends its object through as_hal, whose guard holds a read of its device's snatch lock, the lock that keeps a resource's raw object from being destroyed while it is read; in debug builds wgpu-core panics when a thread takes it twice. So the refit cannot hold the guards together. unguarded reads the object's address through each guard, lets the guard go, and keeps the reference:

/// The wgpu-hal object `guard` reads, kept past the guard: each guard
/// holds its device's snatch lock, which a thread must not take twice,
/// and a refit needs several objects at once.
///
/// # Safety
///
/// The resource the object belongs to is neither destroyed nor dropped
/// while the reference lives.
pub(super) unsafe fn unguarded<'a, T>(
    guard: Option<impl std::ops::Deref<Target = T>>,
) -> Option<&'a T> {
    // SAFETY: wgpu-core boxes the object in the resource
    // [ref:wgpu-30-raw-boxed], so its address holds while the resource
    // lives undestroyed, as the caller ensures.
    guard.map(|guard| unsafe { &*std::ptr::from_ref(&*guard) })
}

metal::unguarded, src/render/blas_refit.rs. The lifetime 'a is the caller's to choose, which is why the function is unsafe: nothing in the types ties the reference to its resource.

The address holds because wgpu-core keeps each raw object boxed inside its resource (wgpu-30-raw-boxed), so it does not move while the resource lives undestroyed. Letting the guard go also drops the reference count it held, so the caller keeps every resource alive itself: the Mesh pool holds the Arenas, BlasRefit the scratch, and the refit clones every BLAS handle into a local list for the length of its recording.

5 · BlasRefit::record_refits

The refit, invariant by invariant

One unsafe block records one update per refitted BLAS, each the BLAS as its own source and destination with its own scratch region, in one call on the raw encoder. Its SAFETY comment lists every invariant it relies on; each is held by code or by a pinned wgpu source.

/// A command buffer of `device`'s, encoded through wgpu-hal alone, that
/// refits each of `mesh_pool`'s refitted BLASes in place from what its
/// Arena ranges hold.
#[cfg(target_vendor = "apple")]
fn record_refits(
    &mut self,
    device: &wgpu::Device,
    mesh_pool: &MeshPool,
) -> wgpu::CommandBuffer {
    use wgpu::hal::{self, CommandEncoder as _, api::Metal};

    use crate::assets::pools::REFITTED_BLAS_FLAGS;

    let blases = mesh_pool.refitted_blases();
    let mut encoder = device.create_command_encoder(&wgpu::CommandEncoderDescriptor {
        label: Some("BLAS Refit Encoder"),
    });
    let logged = &mut self.refusal_logged;
    let mut refuse = |refusal: &str| {
        if !std::mem::replace(logged, true) {
            tracing::error!(refusal, loaded = blases.len(), "BLASes not refitted");
        }
    };
    if self.scratch.is_none() {
        self.scratch = metal::scratch(device, mesh_pool);
    }
    let Some((scratch, offsets)) = &self.scratch else {
        refuse("no scratch: an Arena is not Metal's");
        return encoder.finish();
    };
    if blases.len() > offsets.len() {
        refuse("those loaded after the first refit have no scratch");
    }
    // SAFETY: wgpu-hal's refit is sound under these invariants, each
    // held here:
    // - Every handle is Metal's: `new` refuses the refit off Metal.
    // - Every wgpu-hal object outlives its use past its guard
    //   (`metal::unguarded`): the Mesh pool, this refit and `blases`
    //   hold their handles throughout, nothing destroys them, and only
    //   the main thread calls into wgpu meanwhile (ADR 0016).
    // - The encoder is used through wgpu-hal alone, never ended here,
    //   and finished by wgpu [ref:wgpu-30-as-hal-mut-safety]; wgpu-core
    //   panics on a mix of the two [ref:wgpu-30-mixed-encoding-panics].
    // - The GPU's use ends before any object is freed: wgpu-core never
    //   sees what this command buffer uses, but wgpu-hal's Metal command
    //   buffers retain every resource their commands name until they
    //   complete (its `retain_command_buffer_references`, on by
    //   default [ref:wgpu-30-metal-retain-references]).
    // - Each refit's descriptor is the one wgpu-core built the BLAS from
    //   (`MeshPool::load_for`), with its flags, which allow updates: the
    //   same buffers, offsets, counts, stride and formats.
    // - Each refit has its own scratch region, of the size Metal asks
    //   for its update, in a buffer any usage makes on Metal. The next
    //   frame's refits reuse the regions, and the frames before them may
    //   still run: Metal tracks hazards on wgpu-hal's buffers, scratch
    //   included, since Metal tracks a resource by default
    //   [ref:metal-hazard-tracking-default] and wgpu-hal never opts out
    //   [ref:wgpu-30-metal-buffer-tracked], so they wait.
    // - The bend pass's writes to the vertex Arena come first in the
    //   queue, and Metal tracks hazards on wgpu-hal's buffers, so each
    //   refit reads the bent shapes; the TLAS build that reads the
    //   BLASes waits for this command buffer through `metal::Order`, or
    //   on the CPU.
    // - wgpu-core never learns of the refit, and needs not: it changes no
    //   BLAS's handle or build index, so the TLAS built next still
    //   validates against it [ref:wgpu-30-tlas-blas-build-order], and
    //   wgpu-core's barriers are no-ops on Metal
    //   [ref:wgpu-30-metal-barriers-empty].
    unsafe {
        let (Some(vertices), Some(indices), Some(scratch)) = (
            metal::unguarded(mesh_pool.vertex_arena().as_hal::<Metal>()),
            metal::unguarded(mesh_pool.index_arena().as_hal::<Metal>()),
            metal::unguarded(scratch.as_hal::<Metal>()),
        ) else {
            refuse("an Arena or the scratch is not Metal's");
            return encoder.finish();
        };
        let mut handles: Vec<wgpu::Blas> =
            blases.iter().map(|blas| blas.blas.clone()).collect();
        let Some(raw_blases) = handles
            .iter_mut()
            .map(|blas| metal::unguarded(blas.as_hal::<Metal>()))
            .collect::<Option<Vec<_>>>()
        else {
            refuse("a BLAS is not Metal's");
            return encoder.finish();
        };
        let entries: Vec<_> = blases
            .iter()
            .map(|blas| metal::triangles(blas, vertices, indices))
            .collect();
        let descriptors: Vec<_> = entries
            .iter()
            .zip(&raw_blases)
            .zip(offsets.iter())
            .map(
                |((entries, raw_blas), &offset)| hal::BuildAccelerationStructureDescriptor {
                    entries,
                    mode: hal::AccelerationStructureBuildMode::Update,
                    flags: REFITTED_BLAS_FLAGS,
                    source_acceleration_structure: Some(*raw_blas),
                    destination_acceleration_structure: *raw_blas,
                    scratch_buffer: scratch,
                    scratch_buffer_offset: offset,
                },
            )
            .collect();
        encoder.as_hal_mut::<Metal, _, _>(|hal_encoder| match hal_encoder {
            Some(hal_encoder) => {
                hal_encoder.build_acceleration_structures(descriptors.len() as u32, descriptors)
            }
            None => refuse("the encoder is not Metal's"),
        });
    }
    encoder.finish()
}

BlasRefit::record_refits, src/render/blas_refit.rs, compiled on Apple targets alone. A refusal logs once, with its reason, and returns the encoder finished empty.

Every handle is Metal's. BlasRefit::new returns None for Refit off Metal, through unavailable_on, so the block never runs there; each as_hal::<Metal> would also return None for a foreign object, and the block refuses instead of recording.

Every object outlives its use past its guard. This is what unguarded asks of its caller. The Mesh pool, BlasRefit and the cloned handles hold every resource through the block, and nothing else destroys one meanwhile, since only the main thread calls into wgpu (ADR 0016).

The encoder is wgpu-hal's alone. It takes nothing through wgpu, and encoder.finish() ends it, as as_hal_mut's terms require.

The GPU's use ends before any object is freed. wgpu-core keeps a resource alive until the GPU has run every command buffer that uses it, but learns what a command buffer uses only from what wgpu recorded into it. Here Metal does it: wgpu-hal's Metal backend makes its command buffers with retained references by default (wgpu-30-metal-retain-references), and such a command buffer keeps every resource its commands name until it completes.

Each refit repeats its BLAS's build. metal::triangles rebuilds wgpu-hal's description of the triangles from the MeshBlas that load_for kept: the same two Arenas, first vertex, vertex count and format, the 28-byte CookedVertex stride, u32 indices from first_index, the geometry's flags, and REFITTED_BLAS_FLAGS, which allow updates.

Each refit has its own scratch region. All the refits are in one call and may run side by side, so none shares scratch. metal::scratch asks wgpu-hal for each BLAS's update scratch size, rounds it up to SCRATCH_ALIGNMENT, 256 bytes, a margin over the one byte wgpu-hal reports for Metal (wgpu-30-metal-scratch-alignment), and lays the regions end to end in one buffer, made at the first refit. The next frame's refits reuse the regions while the frame before may still run; Metal tracks hazards on a resource by default (metal-hazard-tracking-default), wgpu-hal never opts its buffers out (wgpu-30-metal-buffer-tracked), so the later refits wait.

The bend comes first; the build waits. The bend pass writes the vertex Arena earlier in the queue, and the refits name that buffer, so Metal's hazard tracking orders them after it. The TLAS build that reads the BLASes names them only by ID, so nothing tracked orders it after the refits: that half is section 6.

wgpu-core never learns of the refit, and need not. A TLAS validates against the build index of each BLAS it holds (wgpu-30-tlas-blas-build-order); a refit in place changes no BLAS's handle or build index, so the dynamic TLAS built next still validates. The barriers wgpu-core would have recorded around a write it knew of are empty functions on Metal (wgpu-30-metal-barriers-empty).

6 · metal::Order · gfx-rs/wgpu#9215 · ADR 0016

Ordering on Metal

Metal orders work that writes and reads the same resource, but only resources a command names, and a TLAS build names its BLASes by ID; nothing orders it after the refits (wgpu-issue-9215). So the refits' submission signals a Metal shared event as it completes, and the build waits for it on the GPU, under wgpu-hal's strict event sync.

/// Orders the GPU work of a submission after the one before it: the
/// earlier signals a shared event as it completes, and wgpu-hal's
/// strict event sync holds every command buffer encoded after it until
/// the event is signalled. Metal orders no TLAS build after the BLASes
/// it reads are written (gfx-rs/wgpu#9215), and waiting on the CPU
/// would stall the main thread on the GPU each frame.
pub(super) struct Order {
    device: wgpu::Device,
    /// wgpu-hal's fence, for its shared event; None once destroyed.
    fence: Option<hal::metal::Fence>,
    /// The value the last submission signals.
    signalled: u64,
}

impl Order {
    /// The order on `device`'s `queue`, whose strict event sync it
    /// turns on for the queue's lifetime; None, as logged, if Metal
    /// makes no shared event.
    pub(super) fn new(device: &wgpu::Device, queue: &wgpu::Queue) -> Option<Self> {
        // SAFETY: both handles are Metal's; the fence is destroyed by
        // the device that made it (`Drop`), and the queue lives as long
        // as wgpu's. Strict event sync then adds a wait to every command
        // buffer and a command buffer to every submission on the queue.
        let order = Self {
            device: device.clone(),
            fence: Some(unsafe { device.as_hal::<Metal>()?.create_fence().ok()? }),
            signalled: 0,
        };
        // Dropping `order` destroys the fence on the way out.
        order.fence.as_ref()?.raw_shared_event()?;
        let queue = unsafe { queue.as_hal::<Metal>()? };
        if let Err(error) = queue.enable_strict_event_sync() {
            tracing::error!(%error, "strict event sync not enabled");
            return None;
        }
        Some(order)
    }

    /// Has the submission `queue` makes next signal a new value as it
    /// completes: call it right before that submission.
    pub(super) fn signal_from_next_submit(&mut self, queue: &wgpu::Queue) {
        self.signalled += 1;
        self.stage(queue, true);
    }

    /// Has the submission `queue` makes next wait for the last value
    /// signalled, ahead of every command buffer encoded since the last
    /// submission: call it right after the submission that signals,
    /// before encoding what must follow it.
    pub(super) fn wait_in_next_submit(&self, queue: &wgpu::Queue) {
        self.stage(queue, false);
    }

    /// Stages a signal of the last value, or a wait for it, on `queue`.
    fn stage(&self, queue: &wgpu::Queue, signal: bool) {
        let Some(event) = self
            .fence
            .as_ref()
            .and_then(|fence| fence.raw_shared_event())
        else {
            return;
        };
        // SAFETY: the queue is Metal's. The submission right after the
        // staging takes it, whichever it is
        // [ref:wgpu-30-metal-event-staging]: only the main thread submits
        // (ADR 0016), and `Renderer::render` submits nothing between the
        // update's submission and the dynamic TLAS's build, nor calls
        // what makes wgpu-core submit on its own there, such as mapping
        // a buffer with pending writes [ref:wgpu-30-core-flush-submit].
        unsafe {
            let Some(queue) = queue.as_hal::<Metal>() else {
                return;
            };
            if signal {
                queue.add_signal_event(event.into(), self.signalled);
            } else {
                queue.add_wait_event(event.into(), self.signalled);
            }
        }
    }
}

metal::Order, src/render/blas_refit.rs; its Drop destroys the fence through the device that made it.

Staged for the next submit. wgpu-hal takes no event per command buffer: add_signal_event and add_wait_event stage one for the queue, and whichever submit runs next takes it (wgpu-30-metal-event-staging). So BlasRefit::submit stages the signal right before the head's submission and the wait right after it. Nothing else may submit in between: only the main thread submits (ADR 0016), Renderer::render submits nothing between the update's submission and the build's, and it calls nothing that makes wgpu-core submit on its own there, as mapping a buffer with pending writes would (wgpu-30-core-flush-submit). A staged signal rides on the submission's last command buffer, after its own completion signal.

Why strict event sync. A staged wait alone is not an order. By default wgpu-hal encodes it on an internal command buffer of its own, committed ahead of the submission's; Metal lets independent command buffers in a queue overlap, so that wait does not gate a command buffer that shares no tracked resource with it, and the build shares none. With strict event sync, every command buffer starts by waiting on an internal relay event, and each submit releases the relay only after the waits staged for it, so every command buffer encoded since the last submit, the build's among them, is held until the refits' value is signalled. Its price, as wgpu-hal documents it, is one extra wait per command buffer and one extra internal command buffer per submit, and once on, the queue stays strict for its lifetime. So BlasRefit::new makes an Order, and turns it on, only in the refit and rebuild forms on Metal.

The CPU wait. If Metal makes no shared event, Order::new returns None, BlasRefit::new warns once that each frame waits on the CPU for its BLAS updates, and submit polls the head's submission to completion before the build is recorded. It is correct and stalls the main thread on the GPU in every frame. Off Metal, the rebuild form adds neither.

Timing with a marker

The refits are recorded through wgpu-hal alone, so no wgpu timestamp can go in their command buffer. A marker in the next command buffer reads the refits' scratch, which Metal's hazard tracking makes it wait for, and the timestamp at its start ends the refits' time, which runs from the end of the bend pass.

blas_refit_marker.wgsl, whole. Its bindings are declared from BLAS_REFIT_MARKERS in src/render/bind_groups.rs.

/// A command buffer of `device`'s that reads `scratch`, timed into
/// `timestamp_writes`.
pub(super) fn record(
    &mut self,
    device: &wgpu::Device,
    scratch: &wgpu::Buffer,
    timestamp_writes: wgpu::ComputePassTimestampWrites<'_>,
) -> wgpu::CommandBuffer {
    let bind_group = self.bind_group.get_or_insert_with(|| {
        BLAS_REFIT_MARKERS.bind_group(
            device,
            &self.layout,
            [
                ("refit_scratch", scratch.as_entire_binding()),
                ("marker_result", self.result.as_entire_binding()),
            ],
        )
    });
    let mut encoder = device.create_command_encoder(&wgpu::CommandEncoderDescriptor {
        label: Some("BLAS Refit Marker Encoder"),
    });
    let mut pass = encoder.begin_compute_pass(&wgpu::ComputePassDescriptor {
        label: Some("BLAS Refit Marker"),
        timestamp_writes: Some(timestamp_writes),
    });
    pass.set_pipeline(&self.pipeline);
    pass.set_bind_group(BLAS_REFIT_MARKERS.index, &*bind_group, &[]);
    pass.dispatch_workgroups(1, 1, 1);
    drop(pass);
    encoder.finish()
}

RefitMarker::record, src/render/blas_refit.rs; its bind group is made once, for the scratch the first refit makes, which no later refit replaces.

On Metal, wgpu-hal takes a pass's timestamps as counter samples where its encoder starts and ends, so a marker must do real work, since a pass that does nothing writes no timestamp, and must name a resource the timed work writes, or Metal may run it beside that work rather than after it. Every refit names the scratch buffer, and the marker reads its first word, so it starts once the refits are done.

The marker writes only its start: PassQueries::marker gives it the refit's second query slot, the one a pass's end would take, and no end. No timestamp marks where the refits begin, so the GPU stats time blas_refit from the end of the bend pass, the last timed pass in the head, to the marker's start, a span that includes what Metal spends between command buffers. submit turns its queries into the marker's timestamp write only when it records the marker, which marks the refit run in the frame's pass log (Frame stats); a frame whose refit went untimed does not count the dynamic TLAS's build apart, since its span from the bend pass already holds the build's submission. The rebuild form times neither its rebuilds nor its build: a BLAS rebuilt through wgpu would fail the first marker's query of the dynamic TLAS, which wgpu-core checks against the BLASes of the last build.

On the CPU, the stage BLAS_REFIT times recording the refits and submitting them with the head; dynamic_tlas_cpu_microseconds sums it with the build's recording and submit. The head's submit is in it too, which the proxies form makes with the rest of the frame.

10 · ADRs 0007, 0013, 0016, 0020

Why this way

Proxies ship; the refit stays as the reference. ADR 0020 gives every shadow to a ray against the TLAS, so a deforming figure must reach the TLAS in its drawn shape, and the crowd's update, measured against its own Budget on the M4 Pro, decides how. Benched in four rounds alternating the forms (docs/performance.md, "The crowd's two forms"), the proxies' update, the dynamic TLAS's build over 1,600 proxies, fit both the GPU and the CPU halves of that Budget; in the refit form the refits alone took more than the whole GPU half, before the build. The proxies run on every backend through wgpu's safe API; the refit runs on Metal alone, through wgpu-hal and strict event sync. Their costs are the proxies' shapes, a little thinner than the figures', and 16 of the dynamic TLAS's entries per figure. ADR 0020 records the proxies as the default form, which skinning builds on, and the refit behind its Render switch, as the shapes' reference.

A refit, not a rebuild per frame. The world-design pre-mortem measured rebuilding a hundred 5,120-triangle BLASes at 65 to 78% of ADR 0013's whole GPU frame Budget. A refit recomputes only the boxes over a tree that is already built, which is why it was the form worth timing; the rebuild form stays as its oracle, since it reaches the same BLASes through the safe API.

wgpu-hal, for one subsystem. ADR 0007 keeps the engine on wgpu and allows wgpu-hal for a subsystem past its safe API. The refit is one: wgpu-core 30 never updates a BLAS. Keeping it to one unsafe block over one encoder, with its invariants listed, keeps the rest of the frame on the safe API.

A shared event, not a CPU wait. Waiting on the CPU for the refits, as the load path waits for its compactions, would stall the main thread on the GPU in every frame. A staged event is queue-wide, which makes it delicate where several threads submit; under ADR 0016 only the main thread submits, so the staging and the submit that takes it are ordered by the code. The CPU wait stays as the fallback where Metal makes no shared event.

A marker that reads the scratch. Metal times a pass at its encoder's ends, writes nothing for an empty pass, and runs a pass that names nothing the timed work writes beside it. The scratch is the one resource every refit names, so a marker that reads it is ordered after all of them, at the cost of one invocation (docs/performance.md, "Bracketing on Metal", found the same for the dynamic TLAS's markers).

11

Limits

Metal only, against wgpu 30.0.1. The refit form refuses every other backend. It relies on wgpu-hal's Metal internals at the pinned version: the update mode, retained command buffers, staged events and strict event sync; a wgpu bump re-checks each invariant against its sources.

The same triangles, a tree for the rest shape. A refit keeps its build's topology, and the tree was chosen for the upright rest shape; the further the triangles drift, the more boxes a ray tests. A refitted BLAS is never compacted.

The BLASes the first refit finds. The scratch is sized at the first refit for the refitted BLASes loaded then; one loaded later is not refitted, and the refusal is logged once.

Strict for the queue's lifetime. Once an Order turns strict event sync on, every later command buffer on the queue waits on the relay, and every submit adds one.

Nothing may submit in between. The order holds only while nothing submits between the update's submission and the build's; a change that makes wgpu-core submit there, such as mapping a buffer with pending writes, would hand the staged wait to the wrong submission.

Untimed oracle, no Baseline, no GPU Budget line. The rebuild form times neither its rebuilds nor its build. No committed Baseline holds the refit form, and no Budget field holds blas_refit's GPU time; its CPU stage counts toward the dynamic TLAS's CPU Budget.

History

The refit form arrived in the third milestone, in two steps after the crowd scene and its shadow proxies: first the switch, the refit through wgpu-hal, its order on Metal and the capture test against the rebuild; then the marker that times it and the bench flag for both forms. Benched on the M4 Pro, the proxies' update fit the crowd's Budget and the refits alone did not, and the owner chose the proxies as the form skinning builds on, keeping the refit behind its Render switch as the shapes' reference, as ADR 0020 records. The Metal ordering gap it closes was found at load, in BLAS builds and compaction, which Waiting on the GPU and Metal quirks describe; the crowd scene itself is on The Reference scenes. This page describes the mechanism as it is.
