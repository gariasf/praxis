Light units

Every light value in the engine is photometric, in the units glTF defines: a directional light's illuminance in lux, a point light's luminous intensity in candela, an emissive surface's and the background's luminance in cd/m², the ambient term's illuminance in lux; and the camera's exposure is an EV100, which scales the luminance the lit pass computes so that a scene's brightest ordinary surfaces land near display white before the HDR target stores them.

Describes main at a79fe156 (2026-11-05). A change to a light's units, to a scene's figures or to the exposure rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: The Froxel pass for the light buffer that carries the values, The lit pass for the shading that turns them into luminance.

Interface

Authored
DirectionalLight's intensity in lux and PointLight's in candela, each times a colour; the Environment's ambient_illuminance in lux and background_luminance in cd/m²; a Material's emissive in cd/m²; the Camera's ev100.
Carried
Each light's colour times its intensity in the light buffer's intensity; the ambient term in LightUniform; the exposure, as a scale, in CameraUniform; the background, already exposed, as the colour the lit pass clears to.
Shaded
In the lit pass, in f32: each light's illuminance at the surface, from lux as given or from candela over the squared distance, through the BRDF into luminance, plus the ambient term and the emissive; then times the exposure and clamped to fp16's largest value, 65504, before the HDR target stores it.
Set
Each Reference scene's EV100 by hand, from its sun's illuminance; a camera made without one starts at EV100 15.
Decided
ADR 0023.

Four units, three of them glTF's

Photometry measures visible light in four units; glTF uses three, and Praxis takes them as glTF defines them, so a glTF light imports with no scale and the ratios between sun, sky and lamps are real.

src/components.rs.

A point light of intensity I candela gives a surface facing it at distance d an illuminance of I / d² lux, inside its range window; the lit pass then reflects each light's illuminance through the surface's BRDF into luminance. A white diffuse surface facing a sun of 100,000 lx reflects 100,000 / π ≈ 31,831 cd/m², since an ideal diffuse surface's BRDF is its colour over π.

Direct sun is 100,000 lx and a moonless night about 0.0001 lx: 29.9 stops between them, and about 11 between a living room's 50 lx and the sun. Shading runs in f32, which holds that range; fp16, the HDR target's format, does not hold it unexposed (section 4).

For lamps the point lights look extreme. Their figures keep the ratios the scenes had before their lights had units, when the sun's illuminance was 1: a light that matched the sun 3 m away then is one that matches it 3 m away now. Each is in candela all the same, as glTF would give it.

3 · Camera::exposure

Exposure as an EV100

A camera does not show luminance as it is: its exposure decides which luminance comes out as white. Praxis sets it as photographers do, as an EV100, the exposure value at ISO 100, and the lit pass multiplies each pixel's luminance by the scale it gives.

exposure = 1 / (1.2 · 2EV100) EV100 = log₂(1 / (1.2 · exposure))
const SATURATING_LUMINANCE_AT_EV100_ZERO: f32 = 1.2;

pub fn ev100_for_exposure(exposure: f32) -> f32 {
    (1.0 / (SATURATING_LUMINANCE_AT_EV100_ZERO * exposure)).log2()
}

pub fn exposure(&self) -> f32 {
    1.0 / (SATURATING_LUMINANCE_AT_EV100_ZERO * self.ev100.exp2())
}

src/camera/mod.rs.

The 1.2 is a luminance: 1.2 cd/m² = 78 / (100 · 0.65), the luminance that saturates an ISO 100 sensor at EV100 0, with the lens and vignetting factor q = 0.65 of Lagarde and de Rousiers's course notes. At EV100 0, 1.2 cd/m² exposes to 1; each EV100 more halves the scale, so the luminance that exposes to 1 doubles.

The scenes' EV100 is ev100_for_exposure of one over SUN_ILLUMINANCE, so the sun's illuminance in lux exposes to 1, as it was before the lights had units: every value grew by 100,000 when they took units, and the exposure divides by 100,000. Exposed at 16.35, a white diffuse surface facing the sun is 0.318, and the background 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3.

4 · main.wgsl

Where the frame applies them

The units live in the CPU's components and the GPU's buffers unchanged; the lit pass computes luminance in f32 in real units and exposes it as its last step, so the fp16 HDR target stores values near 1 rather than near 105.

let luminance = ambient_luminance(surface, light.ambient_illuminance.rgb) + outgoing_luminance
    + surface.emissive;
// Pre-exposed in f32 and clamped to fp16's range before the HDR target
// stores it (ADR 0023), weighted for the resolve.
let exposed = min(luminance * camera.exposure, vec3<f32>(LARGEST_HALF));
return vec4<f32>(resolve_weighted(exposed), surface.opacity);

fs_main, main.wgsl; outgoing_luminance holds the directional and point lights' reflected luminance.

fp16 cannot hold the range unexposed: under 100,000 lx a dielectric's sun glint at roughness 0.04 peaks at 1.24×108 cd/m², 10.9 stops past fp16's largest value, 65504. Exposing in f32 first brings ordinary surfaces near 1, and the clamp keeps the rest finite on every backend: on overflow the Apple M4 Pro saturates, while Vulkan may store infinity. The background takes the same exposure on the CPU, as the colour the lit pass clears to. The tonemapping pass then maps the exposed values through GT7 to display colour (The tonemapping pass).

6

What it costs

Nothing a Baseline can see apart: the units are the values the frame already carries, and the exposure is one multiply and one clamp per fragment at the end of the lit pass, timed within lit_pass.

Real units cost range, not time: fp16 cannot store them unexposed, which is why the lit pass pre-exposes in f32, and why the HDR target's format, its memory and its resolve are what they are. Those are The HDR target's to account for, within the lit pass's and the tonemapping pass's own figures.

7 · ADRs 0008, 0020, 0023

Why this way

Real units, not relative ones. Relative units, a sun near 1 and unitless point lights, would mean fewer stops and an exposure near 1. But glTF lights would need a scale at import, values tuned in one scene would not carry to another, and an engine that moved its lighting to physical units after its content existed found converting it extremely hard. Units are hard to change once worlds are authored; ADR 0020's ray-traced shadows and the probe lighting planned beside them work on real ratios between sun, sky and interiors.

Candela, not lumens. Several engines author point lights in lumens, a bulb's familiar rating. glTF stores candela, so lumens would mean a conversion at every import, as one engine's glTF loader multiplies candela by 4π. Blender's glTF exporter, in its physically based mode, writes a point light's watts as candela and a sun's W/m² as lux, so lights authored there arrive in these units.

A manual EV100 per scene. Each Reference scene sets its exposure by hand: exact every frame, the Goldens exact, one multiply. Histogram adaptation, a pass that measures the frame and eases the exposure towards it, carries one number across frames, which ADR 0008 allows; local tone mapping exposes regions of the image apart. Both wait, by ADR 0023, for the first scene that joins an interior and sunlight or changes the time of day.

The 1.2 from a camera's sensor. The scale is that of a sensor at ISO 100 saturating, through the course notes' lens and vignetting factor, so an EV100 means what a photographer's means, and Sunny 16 lands near EV100 15.

8

Limits

Emissive is dim. Core glTF caps an emissive surface at 1 cd/m², its texture and factor each at most 1, which the scenes' EV100 maps to 10−5 of display white. Brighter emissives need KHR_materials_emissive_strength, which the cooker does not read.

One exposure per scene. Nothing adapts to the frame: a scene that moves from sunlight into an interior keeps the sun's exposure.

Point and directional lights only. Spot lights would take candela too, and area lights wait for soft shadows.

Headroom past white is fp16's. Past 65504 times the luminance that exposes to 1, a value is clamped; highlights beyond it saturate.

History

The lights took units in one step of the third milestone, with the HDR target and the tonemapping pass beside them: before it the sun's illuminance was 1, every other light a multiple of it, and the lit pass wrote display colour directly. The scenes' figures kept their ratios, and their EV100 of 16.35 divides by the 100,000 the values grew by, so no Golden moved by more than one 8-bit step, at no more than 110 pixels a scene. The volumes that narrated that step are retired: this page, The HDR target and The tonemapping pass describe the chain as it is.
