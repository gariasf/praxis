The bench

The command that renders a Reference scene along its camera path twice, with shadow rays on and off, and reduces every frame's Frame stats to one record of each time's median, p95, p99 and maximum and of counters; its two companions judge such a record against the scene's committed Baseline and the Budgets, and print it as the markdown tables a PR quotes.

Describes main at 88941ee2 (2026-11-08). A change to the bench, to what it compares or to how a record is kept rewrites this page and moves the commit; nothing is appended. Read first: The Reference scenes for the camera paths and injected time a run follows, Frame stats for the per-frame figures it reduces, The Chunk streamer for the Settled frames a run waits on and the hold a Fast travel waits on, Waiting on the GPU Explained for why the bench's main thread spins rather than blocks.

Interface

Runs
praxis bench <output directory> [--ci | --counters | --ci-counters] [--skip-aliasing] [--no-publish | --experiment <name>] [<scene>...], on a headless GPU context, every scene whose assets are present when none is named, world among them; then praxis compare <bench directory> [--ci] and praxis report <bench directory>, which run no GPU. The verify command runs the first two with --counters, --no-publish and --ci, on the scenes that have a Baseline; CI runs them with --ci-counters. praxis traverse, on its own, renders the world's traversal.
Reads
The scene through render_frames: its camera path and injected time at a 60 Hz frame delta, its frames up to the end of the counter frames as Settled frames, the path paused while the Chunk streamer holds after a Fast travel, with the default Render switches and again with shadow_rays off; every frame's Frame stats, GPU stats included; the checkout through git and the machine's load through preflight, once before the first scene; bench/budgets.json; the Baseline at bench/baselines/<adapter key>/<scene>.json.
Writes
One <scene>.json per scene into the output directory, a Measurements in pretty JSON, and one line of its figures to standard output; a crowd form other than the default writes crowd-<form>.json. compare prints a line per scene and per finding and exits non-zero when any metric failed; report and traverse print markdown tables.
Compared
Times by their median against the Baseline's, warning past 5% and failing past 10% and a noise floor; the CPU frame, GPU frame and gate against their Budgets at the median and p99, the CPU frame at its p95 too, and the two frames' maximum against one 60 Hz period; counters exactly, any increase failing but in the late mips and the TLAS build's allocations, the measured frames' where both runs measured as many frames and the Baseline streamed nothing in them, the counter frames' otherwise, with the measured frames' refusals judged too where the Baseline's streamed; GPU memory against its Budget in total and per kind; the two aliasing counters by the ratios with no floor. With --ci, counters, GPU memory and aliasing alone.
Published
Unless given --no-publish, the results join the Bench history as Bench records, under an Experiment's name with --experiment; a failed publish warns and fails no bench.

1 · Configuration · run · src/bench/mod.rs

A run: two passes along the camera path

A run renders the scene along its camera path with injected time, so every frame is the frame the Baseline measured, for a warm-up and a measured stretch whose lengths and size a Configuration fixes. It renders the whole stretch twice, with shadow rays on and then off, because two of its lines, the gate and shadow-ray time, need the frame without rays.

Four configurations exist, each with the size, the warm-up and the measured frames, and whether the run measures aliasing. The reference one is what praxis bench runs by default and what every Baseline on a hardware adapter holds.

src/bench/mod.rs

Frames per run is warm-up plus measured; a scene renders twice that, once per switch setting, and the configurations that measure aliasing capture 120 frames more (section 3). --skip-aliasing turns the measurement off in any configuration. A held frame, below, adds one to the frames rendered.

The world runs the reference configuration over its 60 s bench segment instead, warm-up included: a flight into a settlement and over the farmland beyond, then a Fast travel into another settlement (The Reference scenes). A configuration is the reference one when its size, warm-up and measured frames are, its aliasing aside, so --skip-aliasing still runs the segment. Every other configuration, and every other scene, runs as it is.

The wait on the oldest frame

render_frames keeps up to FRAMES_IN_FLIGHT frames, two, in flight, as a window's swapchain allows, so the GPU stays busy and the CPU never runs further ahead than a window would. Before it records a third, it waits for the oldest. How it waits is the FrameWait its caller passes: the Goldens' captures block, as a window does in get_current_texture; the bench polls the device with a zero timeout and spins until the frame is done.

pub(crate) enum FrameWait {
    /// Blocks, as a window does in `get_current_texture`.
    Block,
    /// Polls until the frame is done. Behind a GPU-bound frame, the M4 Pro
    /// runs a main thread that blocks markedly slower for the whole next
    /// frame (measured: `docs/performance.md`, Measurements behind the
    /// code), so its CPU times would measure the clock, not the work. Apple
    /// asks games not to busy-wait, which holds a P-core and spends thermal
    /// headroom [ref:apple-tech-talk-110147-busy-waits], so only the bench
    /// spins, on a hardware adapter.
    Spin,
}
frame_wait, src/bench/mod.rs
/// How a bench waits for the oldest frame in flight on `context`'s adapter:
/// a software rasterizer's threads would share the CPU with the spin.
fn frame_wait(context: &GpuContext) -> FrameWait {
    match context.adapter.get_info().device_type {
        wgpu::DeviceType::Cpu => FrameWait::Block,
        _ => FrameWait::Spin,
    }
}

The bench spins because the CPU Budgets price the main thread's work when it is on the critical path. Behind a GPU-bound frame it is not, and on the Apple M4 Pro a main thread that blocks there runs the whole next frame on a slower clock, so a blocked bench reads the clock rather than the work. Spinning holds a performance core for the minutes a bench lasts and spends the thermal headroom a game's GPU would want, which is why only the bench spins, and only on a hardware adapter: lavapipe rasterises on the CPU, where a spinning thread would take a core from it. The window keeps blocking. Waiting on the GPU tells how the clock was found.

A Fast travel's hold

While the Chunk streamer holds after the camera arrives, the camera path pauses: render_frames draws the same frame of the path again, the World's time still advancing by the frame delta, until the streamer stops holding. A path marks the frame that jumps with the camera's teleported, which ChunkStreaming::update takes and clears (The Chunk streamer); place_camera keeps the flag of a jump on a frame run undrawn until streaming runs. A hold past MAX_HELD_FRAMES, a minute at 60 Hz, fails the render, as a load that never finishes would hold for ever.

The counter frames

Every run, whatever its length, also records the counters of one fixed window of frames, so a run of 72 frames compares its counters with a Baseline of 2,048.

src/bench/mod.rs
/// The frames whose counters every run also records, whatever its length:
/// counters repeat exactly on every run, so runs of different lengths
/// compare on these. By the eighth frame the Scene's uploads and the first
/// frames' allocations are over. Baselines record the frames, so
/// moving them refuses every Baseline until it is re-recorded.
const COUNTER_FRAMES: std::ops::Range<u32> = 8..72;

The window is absolute: frames 8 to 71 of the run, which in the reference configuration lie inside the warm-up. The counters-only configurations are exactly that window, with a warm-up of 8 and 64 measured frames, so their measured frames and their counter frames are the same frames.

Settled through the counter frames

Every scene streams its Chunks through the IO pool, whose reads land when the pool finishes them, so a frame that drew whatever had arrived would count a load on one run and not the next. The frames up to the end of the counter frames are therefore Settled frames: on each, render_frames calls update_settled, which waits until the Chunk streamer reports every load it requested Resident, and a load that fails there fails the run.

src/bench/mod.rs
/// The frames a run waits for the Chunk streamer to report Settled: up to
/// the end of the counter frames, so their counters repeat whatever the IO
/// pool's timing, though the world streams in them. Later frames, which the
/// reference and CI configurations time, stream as a game does.
const SETTLED_FRAMES: std::ops::RangeTo<u32> = ..COUNTER_FRAMES.end;

Every scene but the world has loaded what its camera path needs by the counter frames, and the world, whose camera flies on through them, loads each of its Chunks there on the same frame on every run, so the counters of section 2 read the same on every run in the counter frames. The counters-only configurations are Settled throughout. From frame 72 on, a reference or CI run streams as a window does and waits for nothing; a load that lands late in its measured frames counts as a Late Chunk or a Late mip there, as it would in a game, and a Fast travel there holds.

The world's traversal

praxis traverse renders the world along a second path, its traversal: half a Chunk in from the edge, west to east along the south side, then a Fast travel to the north-east corner and the diagonal to the south-west corner, at the reference size and 60 Hz, Settled to the end of the counter frames as a run is, once and with no aliasing. It fails unless every frame read back its GPU stats, and prints one markdown table: the CPU and GPU frames' p99 and maximum over the frames after the reference warm-up, and over every frame the Late Chunks, the late mips, the peak GPU memory and the Fast travel's hold. It writes no result and joins no Bench history.

What is measured

A record holds, for each time, its median, p95, p99 and maximum over the measured frames, and for each counter one value over the measured frames and again over the counter frames: a median for what repeats every frame, the most of any frame for a refusal, the sum for a streaming event. Nothing is a mean, and nothing reads the wall clock.

measure takes the two runs' Frame stats and fails unless every frame of both read back its GPU stats. It drops the warm-up, the first frames drawn, and reduces the rest, held frames included. The lines, as a Measurements holds them and as the Baselines spell them:

Median, p95, p99 and maximum

A Spread is a median, a p95, a p99 and a maximum, each by nearest rank: the values sorted, and the smallest one with at least the fraction at or below it. Over 1,800 measured frames the median is the 900th smallest value, the p95 the 1,710th, the p99 the 1,782nd and the maximum the 1,800th; over the 64 counter frames the median is the 32nd. The rank is a frame that was measured, never a value between two. The p99 and maximum are optional, absent from a record written before results carried them.

src/bench/mod.rs
fn spread(values: Vec<f64>) -> Spread {
    Spread {
        median: percentile(values.clone(), 0.5),
        p95: percentile(values.clone(), 0.95),
        p99: Some(percentile(values.clone(), 0.99)),
        maximum: Some(percentile(values, 1.0)),
    }
}

/// The nearest-rank percentile: the smallest value with at least `fraction`
/// of `values` at or below it.
fn percentile(mut values: Vec<f64>, fraction: f64) -> f64 {
    values.sort_by(f64::total_cmp);
    let rank = (fraction * values.len() as f64).ceil() as usize;
    values[rank.clamp(1, values.len()) - 1]
}

The aliasing counters

Two counters measure how far one frame of the scene lies from the same frame rendered at three times its size and box-filtered down in linear light. The reference is the engine's own render, so they measure aliasing, not bias; they are integers in millionths, so they compare as counters do.

A configuration that measures aliasing captures the scene twice more after its two runs, through capture, which renders 60 Settled frames, waiting for the static TLAS as the Goldens do, and reads the last back: once at the Goldens' size, 640×360, and once at 1920×1080, both with the run's switches. The frame captured, the 60th, lies inside the counter frames. Each pixel of the small image is set against the mean of the 3×3 block of the large one that covers it, every value decoded from sRGB to linear light first, through the EOTF the Khronos data format specification gives.

A pixel's error is the mean of its three channels' squared differences; alpha is no channel of it. aliasing_rmse is the root of the mean of every pixel's error, the whole image's distance; aliasing_p99 is the root of the 99th-percentile pixel's error, the worst hundredth of the image: a few sparkling highlights move it where they barely move the first. Both are rounded to millionths of the unit range. The two counters are written into the record only when measured, so a record from a run that skipped them has no such keys, and a Baseline without them is distinguishable from one that measured zero.

4 · BenchStart · Provenance · src/bench/provenance.rs

Provenance: what was measured, where, and how quietly

A record names the source it measured, as one git tree hash over the paths that can change a measurement, the adapter, backend and driver, the Harness version, the Cooked cache's hash of the Blobs the scene loaded, and whether the machine was quiet. compare reads one field of it, the load average, to print beside a CPU line it fails; the rest is for the Bench history.

BenchStart::read runs once before the first scene: it reads the checkout, and the GPU utilisation, the one-minute load average, the other praxis processes and preflight's power and display warnings while no scene runs, since a reading taken right after a scene would include that scene's load. The source hash

The Checkout holds the commit, the branch, whether a measured path differed from the commit, untracked files included, and the source hash. source_hash asks git for the tree entries of the measured paths at a revision and has git mktree hash them as one tree, so a commit that changes none of those paths has the hash of the commit before it, and a docs-only commit measures as its parent. A dirty checkout hashes its working tree instead: the measured paths are staged as git add --all would stage them, through a temporary index under GIT_INDEX_FILE, so the checkout's own index stays as it is.

    let listing = git(

5 · compare · check_regression · check_budget

Compare: medians against the Baseline, every line against its Budget

A time regresses by its median: past 5% of its Baseline it warns, past 10% it fails, but only when it also grew by more than a noise floor, since a short time moves by more than the ratios between runs. A counter fails on any increase, because it is the same on every run. A Budget fails whatever the Baseline says, and the frame's Budgets bound its tail as well as its median: the p99 answers to the same number, and no frame may outlast one 60 Hz period.

praxis compare reads every result in the directory, finds each one's Baseline by its adapter and backend, and runs compare in one of two modes: Local, every metric, and Ci, with --ci, the deterministic counters only, since times depend on the machine and its load. It prints ok for a clean scene, else one line per Finding, warn or FAIL with the metric and its detail, and exits non-zero when any failed.

What refuses a Baseline

ensure_comparable runs first in both modes and in report: the Baseline must be of the same scene, profile (debug or release, whose allocations differ) and size, and must have counted the same counter frames. A Baseline in another form, from a version of the bench that spelled its records differently, is refused earlier, by Measurements::from_baseline_json, with a message to re-record it. Baselines

A Baseline is a bench result committed under its adapter and backend's directory, and a run compares only with the Baseline of its own adapter and backend, so no machine's times judge another's and no API's judge another's. Re-recording one is running the bench with that directory as its output.

The directory is the adapter's name in lowercase with each run of other characters a hyphen, then the backend's name unless it is Vulkan or Metal: those lead the engine's backend order, so an adapter runs on one of them unless WGPU_BACKEND asks for another, and the suffix marks the ones that were asked for.

10 · ADRs 0013, 0017, 0019, 0020, 0022, 0024

Why this way

Medians over a fixed camera path, not frames per second. ADR 0013 sets the Budgets as per-frame times at 1440p on the reference machine, and ADR 0019 phrases its gate as the median over the scene's camera path. A Reference scene's injected time means frame 700 is the same frame on every run and on every machine, so a median over it is a property of the code, where a frame rate over wall-clock seconds is a property of the run. The p95, p99 and maximum are kept beside the median for the tail, and judged where ADR 0013 bounds it.

The tail answers to the Budget too. A median within its Budget says nothing of the frames a player notices: ADR 0013 holds the CPU frame, the GPU frame and the gate to their Budgets at the p99 as at the median, the CPU frame at its p95 as well, and no CPU or GPU frame to longer than one 60 Hz period, the frame a 60 Hz display would miss. The tail statistics are nearest-rank like the median, so each is a frame that was measured; over 1,800 frames the p99 sets aside 18 of them, and the maximum none, which is why it answers to the period rather than to the per-frame Budget.

Counters exactly, times by ratio and floor. Counters repeat exactly from run to run, so any increase is a change in the code and fails; there is nothing to tolerate. Times move with the machine, so a time is judged by its ratio to the Baseline, with the 5% and 10% set above the noise of GPU medians over repeated runs on the M4 Pro under the owner's usual load. CPU medians of tens of microseconds and short passes move by more than those ratios between runs, so a time fails only when it also grew past a floor in microseconds; within it, it warns.

Settled through the counter frames, then streaming as a game does. Every scene's Chunks come through an IO pool whose reads land when they land, so the frame a load arrives on varies from run to run, and counters that varied would fail on any rise. The frames up to the end of the counter frames wait until every requested load is Resident, so the counter frames repeat on every run, the world's too, though its camera flies on through them and loads Chunks there; the measured frames then stream without waiting, so a reference run's times include what streaming costs a game. Settling only the frames before the counter frames, as the bench once did, held for every scene that has loaded everything by then, and not for the world. The late mips stay reported only, since they still depend on when reads land.

Counter frames wherever the measured frames stream. A measured frame that loads a Chunk loads it when its read lands, so two runs of the world over the same frames count different allocations and uploads there, from the timing alone. Its measured frames' counters would fail on noise, so a run against a Baseline whose measured frames loaded, unloaded or read anything compares the counter frames' instead, which are Settled. The Baseline decides, not the run: a scene whose Baseline streams nothing in its measured frames keeps the stricter comparison over all of them, so a change that starts it streaming there fails rather than buying itself the looser one. And the refusals of the measured frames stay judged either way, since a frame that had no room for something is a defect whenever its loads landed.

A minute of the world, with a Fast travel. The world's segment is 60 s of flight at 50 m/s, into a settlement, over the farmland beyond and, after a jump of about 5 km, through another settlement, so one record times travel, the arrival's hold and the streaming after it. The bench pauses the path through the hold as a game would hold its camera, and times the hold from the jump on the wall clock beside the frames it took, since what a player waits is a time. praxis traverse flies further, a side and the diagonal of the world, and prints its tail and peak figures for the PR or milestone they are pasted on, with no Baseline to judge them.

A refusal counts its worst frame, a streaming event its every frame. The median of a counter that is zero on most frames is zero, so a refusal, a light or Instance something had no room for, is the most of any frame, and a single such frame fails. Streaming counts events, a Chunk loaded or a byte read, so the run's figure is their sum, and over the counter frames, which are Settled, two runs sum to the same.

GPU memory by kind. ADR 0013's 8 GB splits into one Budget per kind of GPU memory (ADR 0017), and the pools are sized from those Budgets at device creation, so a kind that holds more than its Budget is a pool that grew where it should not. The total is still judged, and a test holds the kinds' Budgets to no more than it.

Two runs, so that the gate reads the main pass. ADR 0019's gate is timed with shadow rays off, since every main-pass design it weighs traces the same rays; with them in, the gate would measure the rays as much as the pass. No pass is only the rays, so their cost is read as the difference of the two runs' frames, and the second run is the price of two lines the first cannot give.

A spin wait in the bench alone. The CPU Budgets price the main thread's work when the frame waits on it. Behind a GPU-bound frame on the M4 Pro a blocked main thread runs the whole next frame on a slower clock, so blocking measures the clock. Spinning measures the work, at the cost of a performance core and the thermal headroom Apple asks games not to spend, which is why the window keeps blocking and lavapipe, which rasterises on the CPU the spin would take a core from, blocks too. Reading the CPU lines from a run with one frame in flight was weighed and set aside: the clock's ramp after each wait stays in the lines, and every scene would run a third time.

Aliasing against the engine's own render, in linear light. A reference from another renderer would measure bias, every place the shading differs, as much as aliasing. The same frame at three times the size, box-filtered down, differs from the frame only where one sample per pixel missed what nine resolve: specular sparkle, shimmering edges, terminator steps. The filter runs on decoded values because the average of two sRGB-encoded bytes is not the encoding of their average light; a test holds the block of three white and six black pixels to a third, not to the 0.0908 the encoded mean would decode to. The counters are integers in millionths so that they compare as the other counters do, and the p99 is kept beside the root mean square because a few sparkling pixels move the one and barely the other.

Baselines gate, the history observes. ADR 0024 keeps every run as a Bench record keyed by source hash, adapter, backend and scene, and draws the quiet ones on a Series, but changes nothing about what fails: Baselines stay on main under bench/baselines, chosen by hand, and compare judges against them as ADR 0013 set. The source hash covers only the paths that can change a measurement, so a docs commit inherits its parent's record rather than earning a run; the Harness version rises with any change to what a metric means, so a redefinition never reads as a regression.

The reference machine judges, the floor compares, CI counts. ADR 0013 judges Budgets on the Apple M4 Pro, benches the RTX 2070 SUPER for comparison on Vulkan and on DX12, and gives no ADR to the floor. CI has no GPU: lavapipe's times mean nothing, but its counters repeat exactly, so CI benches the counter frames alone at the Goldens' size and compares counters only, and its records join a Series whether or not the runner was quiet, since the quiet test judges GPU times it never read.

60 Hz. ADR 0022 keeps the Budgets judged at 60 Hz, the display the measurements ran on, so the bench's frame delta is the window's 60 Hz frame whatever the machine would reach.

Limits

A Baseline without the aliasing counters. A run that measured them against a Baseline that lacks them fails both until the Baseline is re-recorded. Only the lavapipe Baselines lack them, and CI's configuration skips them, so the gap fails no run.

No Baseline of the world on the RTX 2070 SUPER. praxis bench with no scene named benches world, and compare fails a scene it finds no Baseline for, naming the command that records one, so a full run there fails until one is recorded. The verify command benches only scenes with a Baseline.

Streaming counters in the older Baselines. The RTX 2070 SUPER's Baselines and lavapipe's but the world's predate the streaming counters, so they read as zero there; the counter frames, where every scene but the world streams nothing, are what the verify command and CI compare.

Held frames count beyond measured_frames. A hold's length follows the IO pool's timing, so a run's measured frames number its measured_frames plus the frames drawn again while it held, and the two runs of one scene can differ by a few. Every time but shadow-ray time reduces them all.

Late mips are not judged. A change that makes more mips arrive late passes compare; only the report shows it.

GPU memory by kind is not in the report. compare judges each kind, but praxis report prints only the total, so a PR quoting the kinds reads them from the result's JSON.

The gate's maximum is not judged. It is measured and reported; only the CPU and GPU frames' maximums answer to the 60 Hz period.

Budgets on every adapter. compare does not know which machine is the reference one, so the RTX 2070 SUPER's town fails the CPU frame's Budget and the dynamic TLAS's CPU ceiling there, lines ADR 0013 judges on the M4 Pro alone.

Harness version unchecked. A Baseline is refused for its form, its scene, profile, size, counter frames, frame count, adapter, backend and passes, not for its Harness version; a Baseline recorded under an earlier version whose records still parse compares as if it were current.

One frame of aliasing, at the Goldens' size. The counters judge the 60th frame at 640×360, not the bench's size or its camera path; aliasing that appears elsewhere along the path, or only at 1440p, is not measured.

Times across backends. report compares times with a Baseline of the same adapter whatever its backend, where compare requires the backend as well.

The dynamic TLAS's CPU line answers to no Baseline. It answers to its Budget alone, so a growth of its cost short of its ceiling is reported, not judged.

The spin wait changes the machine it measures. A busy performance core may take power the GPU would otherwise have; in the town runs behind the choice the GPU frame moved by at most 1.4% across every form of wait, within the ratios but not nothing, so a GPU line compared across the two waits is read with that in mind.

Input latency has no Budget yet. ADR 0013 names one; nothing measures it.

One number per run. The bench records no per-frame series, only the reductions; a frame that was slow once is in the p95 and nowhere else.

History

The bench began beside the Instance buffer's fixed capacity, as a way to prove a change had not moved the counters or the medians: one run per scene, counters held exactly, times by the 5% and 10% ratios with a floor for the short CPU frames, and a scene-order trap found while recording the first Baselines. The dense scene's Baselines brought the second run, with ADR 0019's gate and shadow-ray time read as a difference, the dynamic TLAS build timed from the frame before, and the ceilings on that build. The spin wait came when town's CPU lines on the M4 Pro were found to read a slowed clock behind its GPU-bound frames, told in Waiting on the GPU. Provenance, the Harness version and the publish to the Bench history came with ADR 0024, and the aliasing counters after them, which every hardware Baseline holds. Then the p99 and maximum joined each time with their Budgets, the refusal counters beside the overflow, GPU memory by kind with a Budget per kind, the streaming counters when every scene began to stream its Chunks, then the settled warm-up, once a slow IO pool was found to move a Chunk load into the counter frames, and last the world's minute with its Fast travel, which settled the counter frames themselves, compared on them the counters of a run whose Baseline streams, its measured frames' refusals judged still, brought the world's first Baselines, and the traversal beside the bench. The volumes that narrated the earlier steps are absorbed into this page, which describes the mechanism as it is.
