# HDR, exposure and tonemapping: state of the art

Researched 2026-10-19 for ticket #189; it feeds #200 (HDR, exposure and tonemapping), the units of #191's `DirectionalLight` and of #199's per-scene ambient and background, and M3's criterion 7. Sources: SIGGRAPH Advances and Physically Based Shading course slides and notes, 2014 to 2025 (PDF pages, or PPTX slide numbers); GDC and CEDEC decks; papers (Reinhard et al. 2002, Durand and Dorsey 2002, Chen et al. 2007, Mertens et al. 2007, Spencer et al. 1995, Ritschel et al. 2009, Lipshitz et al. 1992, Ulichney 1993); specifications (glTF 2.0 and KHR extensions, ITU-R BT.2100 and BT.2408, the Khronos Data Format Specification 1.3, Vulkan, D3D11.3, the Metal Shading Language 4.1, WGSL); vendor documentation (Apple, Microsoft, Unreal Engine 5.7 via web.archive.org, as dev.epicgames.com did not resolve, Unity HDRP 17, Godot 4.7, Blender 4.0); source at the commits linked; and a probe on the M4 Pro (Measured on the M4 Pro). Boundaries: the lit pass's BRDF is #199's, GI and probes are ADR 0020's, sky and atmosphere M6's, transparency M6's, anti-aliasing beyond the resolve ADR 0019's.

Temporal class, as in the [GI note](global-illumination-and-lighting.md): (a) complete each frame; (b) converges over frames in a world-space, probe-space or scalar state, so it lags a change but nothing smears on screen; (c) screen-space temporal reuse. Costs carry the GPU and resolution the source states; vendor figures are labelled. "Exposed" values are radiance times the exposure, so 1.0 is about display white. `cap` is the [capability matrix](wgpu-capability-matrix.md). ADR 0013's frame is 2560×1440 = 3,686,400 pixels and 12 ms of GPU time. "Computed" figures were re-derived for this note.

## Techniques

**1. Reinhard.** Reinhard et al. scale luminance so the log-average maps to the key 0.18, after Ansel Adams's Zone System ([p2][rh2]), then apply `L/(1+L)`; `Lwhite`, "the smallest luminance that will be mapped to pure white", lets chosen highlights burn out ([p4][rh4]). The paper maps luminance only. Reapplying colour ratios after compressing luminance gives "strongly over-saturated" colours, compressing R, G and B "under-saturated" ones ([Mantiuk p1][mt1]). Image quality: no clipping, smooth highlights, low mid-tone contrast; per channel saturated light never whitens; on luminance a saturated highlight exceeds the display (64× blue reaches 11.4 before the clamp, computed).

**2. Hable.** The Uncharted 2 curve is a rational per-channel fit with toe, linear section and shoulder, normalised by a white of 11.2 ([slides 142-143][u2]); the slides' constants differ from the blog's (A 0.22 against 0.15, B 0.30 against 0.50, E 0.01 against 0.02, with an exposure bias of 2 in the blog) ([u2][u2], [Hable 2010][h10]). The 2017 curve joins three power segments from five direct parameters, adds overshoot against highlights that look "too cyan-ish", and has a closed-form inverse ([Hable 2017][h17]). Image quality: crisp toe, soft shoulder; pure primaries and secondaries never whiten (computed).

**3. ACES 1.x and its fits.** The RRT applies glow and red-modifier sweeteners, desaturates in AP1 and applies its tonescale "independently in rendering-space RGB" ([RRT][rrt], [RRT_Common][rrtc]); its ODTs cover 48 to 4,000 nits ([Tonescales][tsc]). Per-channel AP1 curves skew hue: the Academy ships an LMT for bright blues ([LMT][lmt]), Unreal a Blue Correction ([UE][ue]). Narkowicz fits `ODT(RRT(x))` per channel in Rec.709, "a very simple luminance only fit, which over saturates brights", pre-exposed so that 1 maps to about 0.8 ([post][nk]); Hill's fit keeps AP1 and both saturation matrices but drops glow, red modifier and surround gamma ([BakingLab][bl]). Image quality: high contrast; saturated highlights whiten through hue skews, blue through magenta (computed).

**4. ACES 2.0.** One parametric algorithm replaces the RRT and ODTs: ACES to JMh in a simplified Hellwig 2022 appearance model, tonescale on J only, chroma compression on M, gamut compression at constant hue, then display encoding ([overview][a2ro], [chroma][a2cc], [gamut][a2gc]). The tonescale is keyed on peak luminance, with less mid-slope contrast than ACES 1's 1.55 ([tone mapping][a2tm]); inversion is near exact "for outputs up to 1000-nit peak luminance and up to P3 gamut" ([ACES][a2fb]). OpenColorIO reports it "somewhat slower on GPU" than ACES 1 ([OCIO 2.4.2][ocio]). Image quality: hue-stable highlights that still whiten, softer contrast; its sky gradation at +2 EV desaturates like ACES 1.3 and AgX ([GT7 p159][gt7]).

**5. AgX.** AgX mixes channels with an inset matrix, encodes log2 from −10 to +6.5 stops around 0.18, applies a per-channel sigmoid and an outset; the minimal form approximates Sobotka's sigmoid with a degree-6 polynomial, mean squared error 3.67×10⁻⁶ ([iolite][iol]). Sobotka puts the mechanism in the matrix, not the curve ([S2O3][s2o3]). Blender 4.0 made it the default, and its LUT adds a hue rotation ([Blender 4.0][bl40], [LUT][agxlut]); Godot clamps before the outset against "a cyan colour that occurs with very bright inputs" ([L217][godcyan]). Image quality: a smooth path to white with little hue skew for primaries (computed), a desaturated look ([Lalish][lal]), SDR only ([Blender 4.0][bl40]).

**6. PBR Neutral.** Built for e-commerce so "the baseColor assigned in the glTF shows through faithfully": the 1:1 range is shifted down 0.04 to cancel dielectric Fresnel, the maximum channel is compressed above 0.8 and highlights blend toward white ([Lalish][lal]). The specification guarantees "no hue shifts", continuous derivatives and an analytic inverse ([spec][pbrs]). Polyphony finds it "preserves saturation but loses luminance gradation" ([GT7 p159][gt7]). Image quality: faithful mid-tones and hues, highlights in the top 12 codes, Rec.709 in and out; the 0.8 knee is "the most natural place to adjust the curve to HDR output" ([Lalish][lal]).

**7. Tony McMapface.** A 48³ LUT sampled after `x/(x+1)` per channel; "There's no tweakables" ([README][tony], [hlsl][tonyhlsl]). It compresses brightness-equivalent luminance, keeps hue except "a deliberate Bezold–Brücke shift" and desaturates selectively "to avoid posterization" ([README][tony]). Its author is credited with Frostbite's 2017 display mapper, row 9 ([Fry slide 91][fry]). Image quality: close to linear in the mid-tones, no clipping (grey reaches code 255 at 126.5, computed), bright blue shifts toward cyan (computed); "tuned to its author's visual system" ([README][tony]). The generator was promised "at a later time" and was not found, so no HDR or wide-gamut variant can be derived.

**8. Gran Turismo.** Uchimura's GT curve (CEDEC 2017, Gran Turismo Sport) has a power toe, a linear mid-section and an exponential shoulder to a peak parameter, so SDR and every HDR display share one curve ([GTS p121][gts121], [Desmos][desmos]); per channel, highlights "converge to cyan, magenta, and yellow" ([GT7 p170][gt7c170]). GT7's operator blends the per-channel result 40/60 with an ICtCp version whose chroma fades to white near the peak, assuming a 250-nit SDR paper white ([code][gt7c], [p153][gt7c153]). Image quality: linear mid-tones, hue held there, "some twist in the highlights" ([p170][gt7c170]). The reference code is MIT and a Filament port exists ([ToneMapper][filgt7]).

**9. Hue-preserving display mapping.** Lottes maps the max channel and keeps the ratio ([p56][l56]); per-channel mapping with the same parameters shows "Desaturation" and "Distorted Color" ([p57][l57]), and the path to white is a choice ([p59][l59]). AMD's LPM applies it to luma as MIT code, with a crosstalk control ([L53-L58][lpmx]). Frostbite works in ICtCp, whose chroma "follows lines of perceptually constant hue" ([Fry slide 34][fry]), and blends 40% per-channel back ([slide 93][fry]); Frostbite and Activision both met VFX built on per-channel hue shifts ([slides 39-41][fry], [Malin slide 99][malin]). Image quality: hue held and a chosen path to white, at the price of re-authored effects.

**10. LUT grading.** A 3D LUT turns per-pixel colour operators on a bounded domain into one trilinear lookup; coordinates are remapped to texel centres, and HDR input passes a 1D shaper first ([Selan §24.2.2, §24.2.4][selan]). Shipped shapers: LogC in Unity ([URP L226][urplut]) and Filament ([colorGrading.fs][filgrade]); PQ in Frostbite ([Fry slide 27][fry]) and Gran Turismo Sport ([GTS p259][gts259]). GT7's tonemap LUT uses `pow(x, 4)` indexing; a linear table of twice the resolution still banded ([GT7 p173][gt7-173], [p174][gt7-174]). Image quality: exact only at lattice points; error set by size, shaper and texel format.

**11. Manual exposure.** Frostbite and Filament define EV100 = log2(N²/t) − log2(S/100) ([Lagarde p84][fb84], [Filament L2758][fil-ev]) and divide luminance by 1.2·2^EV100, the luminance that saturates a sensor of saturation-based sensitivity with q = 0.65 ([Lagarde p85][fb85], [p86][fb86]); the 78 behind it puts an 18% card at 12.7% of saturation, "half a stop of headroom" ([p86][fb86]). Unreal's Manual mode drops the 1.2 ([UE][ue-exp]), 0.26 stops brighter (computed). Image quality: exact and stable; a scene that crosses interiors and sun needs its EV changed by hand or by row 12.

**12. Histogram adaptation.** Reinhard et al. map the log-average luminance to a key of 0.18 ([p4][rh4]). Source kept 5% of pixels in the top bins, used the median as a darkness barrier and made the 95% bin "sticky" against blinking lights ([Valve p29][r2-valve29], [p32][r2-valve32], [p35][r2-valve35]). HDRP's step, 1 − 2^(−Δt·speed), is frame-rate independent ([src][r2-hdrp-adapt]); Bevy's Euler step is not (computed, [wesl][r2-bevy-adapt]). Ghost of Tsushima metered illuminance to take "albedo out of the equation" ([GoT p129][r2-got129]). Class b, so ADR 0008 admits it; Praxis's captures inject the frame delta ([capture][r2-capture]), and HDRP's slower default speed leaves half a step change after 60 frames of 1/60 s (computed).

**13. Local tone mapping.** Durand and Dorsey compress only a bilateral-filtered base layer of log luminance and keep the detail layer ([Durand p6][dd6]); the bilateral grid made it real time ([Chen p1][chen1]). Ghost of Tsushima builds a 64×32×64 grid in about 250 µs on PS4 at 1080p and mixes in a wide Gaussian against ringing ([GoT p135][got135], [p140][got140]). Exposure fusion blends synthetic exposures through Laplacian pyramids ([Mertens p4][mer4]); Wronski's real-time form looks different with image content ([Wronski][wron]). Unreal 5 ships both ([UE][ue-exp]). Image quality: shadow and highlight detail at once, halos and ringing traded per scene.

**14. Photometric units.** KHR_lights_punctual: "`point` and `spot` lights use luminous intensity in candela (lm/sr) while `directional` lights use illuminance in lux", falling off by inverse square within a range window max(min(1 − (d/r)⁴, 1), 0)/d² ([KHR L90][khr90], [L106][khr106]); core glTF puts emissive in cd/m² and calls exposure "an implementation detail" ([glTF L2659][gltf-em]). Frostbite, Filament, Bevy and Godot author point lights in lumens, so Bevy's glTF loader multiplies candela by 4π ([Bevy L1796][bevy-gltf]); Blender exports watts as W/(4π)·683 candela ([lights.py L184][blender]). Relative rigs are "biased by the current scene context" ([Lagarde p23][fb23]). Image quality: plausible ratios; exposure and storage must follow.

**15. fp16 and pre-exposure.** fp16 has a 10-bit mantissa, a maximum of 65504 and a smallest normal of 6.1×10⁻⁵; `Rg11b10Ufloat` has 6-, 6- and 5-bit mantissas and maxima of 65024, 65024 and 64512 ([KDFS 10.1][kdfs16], [10.2, 10.3][kdfs11], computed). On overflow D3D stores "the maximum representable (appropriately signed) value" ([D3D 3.2.2][d3d-conv]) and Vulkan the closest finite value or infinity ([Vulkan L1633][vk-conv]); the MSL specification leaves overflow unstated ([MSL p378][msl378]), and the M4 Pro saturates (Measured). Frostbite pre-exposes with a fixed or the previous frame's exposure and accepts the lag after a teleport ([Lagarde p91][fb91]); Filament pre-exposes the lights so shading can run in half precision ([L1730][fil-pre]).

**16. Formats.** Unreal's scene colour defaults to 64-bit float and rates 128-bit "unreasonable but good for testing" ([UE][r6-ue]); Frostbite's light buffer is Float16 with exposure pre-applied ([p91][fb91]); Bevy, Godot's Forward+ and Filament default to RGBA16F ([camera][r6-bevy], [src][r6-godot-fp], [Options][r6-filament]). R11G11B10F is HDRP's default "for faster rendering" ([src][r6-hdrp-src]) and DOOM 2016's lighting buffer ([slide 46][r6-doom]); Unity warns of banding ([HDRP][r6-hdrp-doc]) and Frostbite of a hue shift from the 10-bit blue ([p91][fb91]). Image quality: 16F holds sign, alpha and the precision row 18's inverse needs.

**17. Custom resolve.** Persson: "Any decent tonemap operator will have a non-linear response, which makes regular resolves that average samples produce low quality antialiasing" ([Persson][humus]). Pettineo preferred B-spline filters 2 to 3 pixels wide and measured every custom resolve slower than the hardware's ([Pettineo][mjp12]). The Order: 1886 resolved in compute with a 2-pixel cubic and 1/(1 + luminance) weights taken after exposure, offset about −2 EV, since unexposed weights made highlights vanish ([slides 21, 24, 27 to 31][rad15]); HDRP's resolve uses the same weights ([ColorResolve L36][unity-cr]). Image quality: correct HDR edges, but the samples must be stored.

**18. Reversible weighting.** Karis's weight 1/(1 + luma) gives "the same as if the samples were tone mapped using T with range of 1, averaged, then inverse tone mapped" ([Karis 2013][karis13]); it is exact for linear luma and darker with max(r, g, b): a red and a green sample of 10 resolve to (0.83, 0.83, 0), where the box gives (5, 5, 0) (computed). Filament compresses in a framebuffer-fetch subpass after its blended passes ([L54][fil-cr], [Renderer L1331][fil-rend]). Without fetch every shader in the render pass writes T(c), and blends run in T: 10 at 50% over 0.1 resolves to 1.0, not 5.05 (computed). Image quality: display-space edges, as the probe's images show (Measured); sub-pixel highlights lose energy and highlights cap.

**19. Dither.** Noise added after quantisation only covers bands; with an HDR buffer the dither goes into the write to 8 bits ([Gjøl p32][g14-32], [INSIDE p53][in53]). Triangular noise of 2 LSB peak-to-peak is the least noise that removes noise modulation ([Lipshitz p18][lip18]); since GPUs round, its range is [−1, 1) LSB ([Gjøl p28][g14-28]), added in the encoded domain: in linear it is 12.92 times too large near black (computed). Peters remaps void-and-cluster masks ([Ulichney p4][uli4]) to that distribution ([Peters][peters]); Bevy and Godot keep the pattern screen-fixed ([Bevy][bevydither], [Godot][godither]). Image quality: a fixed fine grain and no bands.

**20. Mip-chain bloom.** Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare blooms non-thresholded colour; it downsamples with 13 bilinear fetches over five overlapping boxes, upsamples progressively with a 3×3 tent, and weights each 4-sample block of the first downsample by 1/(1 + luma) against fireflies, since a per-pixel weight pulses on small moving objects ([CoD slides 148, 157, 160 to 164][cod]). Bjørge's dual filter matches the Kawase blur at less bandwidth ([ARM p23][arm23]). Bevy's first downsample does not renormalise its weights and keeps 60% of a uniform field at luminance 10 (computed, [bloom.wesl][bevy113]). Image quality: stable without history; a threshold "will break the energy conservation rule" ([HDRP][unity]).

**21. Glare.** Spencer et al. build the eye's point-spread function from scattering terms and a lenticular-halo ring, normalised so it only redistributes energy ([Spencer p5][sp5], [p4][sp4]); added glare raised the apparent brightness of light sources ([p1][sp1]). Ritschel et al. derive the PSF each frame from an animated pupil and convolve by GPU FFT ([Ritschel p6][rit6]). Unreal ships FFT convolution bloom for "cinematics or on high-end hardware" ([UE][uebloom]). Image quality: shaped, energy-conserving glare at a high cost.

**22. HDR display output.** EDR is linear: 1.0 "represents SDR max", brighter values are "subject to clipping" ([WWDC21 11:27][w21b]), and headroom, peak over SDR white, is 1,600/500 = 3.2 on a Pro Display XDR ([12:50][w21f]) and varies at run time ([doc][maxedr]). Windows reads FP16 scRGB 1.0 as 80 nits ([Microsoft L127][ms127]); BT.2100 normalises FP16 1.0 to 203 cd/m² ([p12][b12]). Frostbite maps last, per display ([Fry slide 29][fry]); Activision maps HDR with BT.2390's EETF in ICtCp ([Malin slide 74][malin]); GT7 takes the display peak, 250 to 10,000 nits ([code][gt7c]). Image quality: real highlights where the display has them, one map per peak and paper white.

## What wgpu 30 allows

Links point at tag v30.0.1; every cited file in the cargo registry has the tag's blob hash (checked).

- **HDR target.** `Rgba16Float` renders, blends, takes 4x MSAA and resolves on every adapter, as WebGPU guarantees and wgpu encodes ([format 991](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/texture/format.rs#L991)), so it adds only an entry to the main pass's attachment formats that ADR 0021 checks. `Rg11b10Ufloat` renders, multisamples and resolves only with `RG11B10UFLOAT_RENDERABLE` ([format 938](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/texture/format.rs#L938-L943)), a new floor feature: DX12 always reports it ([dx 479](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/dx12/adapter.rs#L479)), Metal on every Mac family ([mtl 554](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs#L554-L558), [mtl 1294](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs#L1294)), Vulkan when the driver lists colour attachment and blend for `B10G11R11_UFLOAT_PACK32` ([vk 993](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/vulkan/adapter.rs#L993-L1001)); CI's lavapipe, Mesa 25.2.8, accepts that format as a render target ([lp_screen 506](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/blob/mesa-25.2.8/src/gallium/drivers/llvmpipe/lp_screen.c#L506-L517)) and then reports blend ([lvp_formats 161](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/blob/mesa-25.2.8/src/gallium/frontends/lavapipe/lvp_formats.c#L161-L167)). `Rgba32Float` has no MSAA ([format 994](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/texture/format.rs#L994)). The M4 Pro reports 2x and 4x for the 16-bit and 11-bit formats (cap).
- **Memoryless 4x, hardware resolve.** `TRANSIENT_ATTACHMENT` requires `LoadOp::Clear` or `DontCare` and `StoreOp::Discard` ([texture 191](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/texture.rs#L191-L200)); Metal backs it with memoryless storage (cap). The 4x attachment resolves through `resolve_target`, the hardware's box filter. wgpu 30 exposes no custom resolve, programmable blending or tile shading, so a fix runs in the lit pass's fragment output (row 18) or needs the samples stored (row 17).
- **SDR output.** A final pass that dithers in the encoded domain writes a non-sRGB view of the sRGB surface, since `view_formats` may only toggle sRGB ([surface 975](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/surface.rs#L970-L976)), or a `Bgra8Unorm` surface, which Metal lists ([mtl 433](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs#L433-L437)).
- **HDR display output.** `SurfaceConfiguration::color_space` ([surface 914](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/surface.rs#L914)) picks `Srgb`, `DisplayP3`, `ExtendedSrgbLinear` (scRGB, Metal's EDR), `ExtendedSrgb`, `ExtendedDisplayP3`, `Bt2100Pq` or `Bt2100Hlg` ([surface 242](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/surface.rs#L242-L415)); `Auto` keeps the old behaviour, scRGB on `Rgba16Float` surfaces. Metal offers the extended spaces on `Rgba16Float`, PQ and HLG on `Rgba16Float` and `Rgb10a2Unorm`, and sets `wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent` for any HDR space ([mtl 398](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs#L398-L431), [mtl-surface 279](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/surface.rs#L279-L284)); Vulkan maps the `VK_EXT_swapchain_colorspace` spaces a driver lists ([vk-conv 183](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/vulkan/conv.rs#L183-L195)); DX12 offers scRGB on fp16 and PQ on `Rgb10a2Unorm` ([dx 1323](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/dx12/adapter.rs#L1323-L1353)). `Surface::display_hdr_info` reports headroom, an EDR multiplier on macOS and nits on Windows ([wgpu surface 71](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu/src/api/surface.rs#L71), [surface 732](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/surface.rs#L732)). wgpu writes no HDR10 metadata ([surface 564](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/surface.rs#L564-L569)). The M4 Pro's built-in display is a Liquid Retina XDR panel (`system_profiler SPDisplaysDataType`); its headroom was not read.

## Measured on the M4 Pro

One-off measurements with a throwaway probe on the never-merged branch `research/hdr-captures` at [a2f7302](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/tree/a2f73024e831d0558a7bbb3b45502f88d8cf7592/research/hdr-probe): `TONY_LUT=<lut> cargo run --release --offline -- bench` and `-- overflow` in `research/hdr-probe`, whose README pins the LUT's URL and hash. They are evidence for the decision, not Baselines. Setup: wgpu 30.0.1 on Metal, release build, macOS 27.0.1, a 14-inch MacBook Pro (Mac16,8) with a 16-core M4 Pro GPU, 2026-10-19, 1-minute load 4.1 to 4.6 and no other GPU work. Each figure is the median over 40 submissions of 8 back-to-back repetitions, timed from the first repetition's first pass to the last repetition's last pass and divided by 8, after 5 warm-up submissions; three runs agree within 0.006 ms, except the 1x stand-in (0.014 ms) and one bloom run. Timestamps around single render passes spread 3.5 to 4 times between p10 and p90, as consecutive passes overlap on the tile-based GPU, so none is used.

| Pass at 2560×1440 | Median ms, three runs |
|---|---|
| Final pass: read `Rgba16Float`, write 8-bit; clamp only, as today | 0.048 |
| … Reinhard on luminance; Hable; ACES (Narkowicz); ACES (Hill) | 0.055; 0.064; 0.059 to 0.060; 0.071 |
| … AgX (three.js form); PBR Neutral; Tony McMapface (48³ LUT) | 0.118; 0.073 to 0.075; 0.068 |
| … GT7, analytic ICtCp (Polyphony bakes it into a LUT) | 0.289 |
| AgX plus static triangular dither; plus the inverse resolve weighting; plus a bloom composite | 0.142; 0.136; 0.136 |
| Histogram exposure: 256 bins over every pixel, then one workgroup averages and adapts | 0.270 to 0.271 |
| Bloom chain: six 13-tap downsamples, the first with per-block Karis weights, five tent upsamples, `Rgba16Float` | 0.269 |
| Same in `Rg11b10Ufloat` | 0.254 (0.381 in one run) |
| Lit-pass stand-in, 68,666 flat triangles with radiance up to 64: 1x `Rgba16Float` stored | 0.102 to 0.116 |
| Same, 4x memoryless `Rgba16Float` colour and depth, hardware resolve | 0.147 to 0.153 |
| Same, writing c/(1 + luminance) | 0.156 to 0.157 |
| Same, 4x `Rgba8Unorm`; 4x `Rg11b10Ufloat` | 0.137 to 0.138; 0.140 to 0.144 |

Every operator but GT7's analytic form costs under 0.12 ms, 1% of ADR 0013's 12 ms; the weighting adds at most 0.009 ms in the lit pass and 0.018 ms in the final pass. The stand-in's shading is trivial, so it bounds the weighting's per-fragment cost, not its share of a real lit pass. The histogram issues one global atomic per populated bin per 16×16 tile over every pixel; HDRP meters one pixel in four ([src][r2-hdrp-bins]).

**Overflow.** A fragment that writes 65519, 65520, 7×10⁴, 10⁶ or 3×10⁹ to `Rgba16Float` stores 65504, and −10⁶ stores −65504; only an infinite output stays infinite. Under the 4x hardware resolve, one sample of 10⁶ over three of 0.5 resolves to 16376, (65504 + 1.5)/4, and even an infinite sample resolves to 16384, finite. Vulkan allows the infinity (row 15), so lavapipe's Goldens need the shader's own clamp.

**Today's range.** Captured unclamped into `Rgba32Float` by the branch's patched `praxis capture` at 1280×720, today's scenes peak at 499.9 (pbr-sweep), 855.0 (helmet), 266.8 (normal-tangent), 32.0 (minification) and 0.5 (orrery), with 0.10%, 0.09%, 0.03%, 0.56% and 0% of pixels above 1.0.

**Images.** The probe's images are on the never-merged `pr-images` branch at [0c3a105](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/tree/0c3a1054b28e2a7056c7187bf68053e7fd8e4391/219) and in [#219](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/pull/219): every operator on the sweep and three scenes, the resolve comparison and the dither. The probe applies each operator in WGSL. On the sweep's grey and blue columns its output matches the survey's independent Python implementations within 0.5 of a code for the six analytic operators, 0.55 for Tony McMapface's LUT, sampled as a filtered 3D `Rgb9e5Ufloat` texture, and 0.49 for GT7, whose Python port reproduces Polyphony's compiled reference. In the sweep, the per-channel curves (clamp, Reinhard, Hable, Narkowicz) turn saturated hues into yellow, cyan and magenta blocks that never whiten; Hill's fit whitens blue through magenta; AgX, PBR Neutral, Tony McMapface and GT7 whiten with little hue change. In the resolve comparison, the box resolve matches the 64-sample average taken in scene-linear space, so bright sub-pixel wires become full-white stair steps, and the weighted resolve matches the 64-sample average taken after the operator. A dim gradient bands at 8 bits and shows a fixed fine grain with the static dither.

## Options for #200 (no recommendation)

When this was researched, the forward pass wrote linear radiance straight into an 8-bit sRGB target: no exposure and no operator, so values above 1.0 clipped per channel; lights were relative, with a tutorial falloff and a 0.03 ambient that #199 replaces with glTF 2.0 Appendix B, inverse-square falloff and per-scene ambient and background ([shader.wgsl](../../src/shader.wgsl), [capture](../../src/capture/mod.rs)). Today's scenes peak between 0.5 and 855 and exceed 1.0 in at most 0.56% of pixels (Measured). The choices are independent unless a line says otherwise.

- **Light units.** *U1, photometric as glTF defines them:* `DirectionalLight` in lux and `PointLight` in candela (KHR_lights_punctual), emissive and background radiance in cd/m², ambient irradiance in lux, exposure as EV100 (rows 11, 14); the fp16 write needs pre-exposure or a clamp (row 15). Units are hard to change once worlds are authored, so U1 is an ADR. *U2, relative:* a sun near 1 and unitless points, so fewer stops and an exposure near 1; glTF lights need a scale at import, and Frostbite found converting relative rigs later "extremely hard" (row 14).
- **Exposure.** *E1, manual EV100 per Reference scene:* class a, Goldens exact (row 11). *E2, histogram adaptation* over the resolved target, class b, 0.27 ms at 1440p on the M4 Pro (Measured); Goldens start from a fixed or converged exposure (row 12). *E3, local tone mapping:* nothing in M3 needs it; its first need is a scene that joins interior and sun (row 13).
- **Operator** (images: Measured). Each of rows 1 to 9 is a candidate; these are analytic or a shipped LUT, permissively licensed, and take saturated light to white. *O1, PBR Neutral:* base colours kept, no hue shift, SDR only. *O2, AgX* (minimal or three.js form): filmic contrast, little hue skew, SDR only. *O3, Tony McMapface:* a LUT asset, mid-tones closest to linear, no HDR variant. *O4, GT7:* one path for SDR and any HDR peak, per-channel twist kept in highlights, the most ALU (Measured). *O5, an ACES 1 fit:* Hill's or Narkowicz's, hue skews (row 3). ACES 2.0 needs a port of its CTL or a baked LUT (row 4).
- **Target and resolve.** *R1, `Rgba16Float` with the box resolve:* radiance averaged as a sensor would; bright sub-pixel edges thicken to white and step (Measured). *R2, `Rgba16Float` with reversible luminance weighting* written by every shader in the lit render pass and inverted in the final pass: display-space edges for at most 0.009 ms in the lit pass and 0.018 ms in the final pass (Measured); sub-pixel highlights lose energy, and M6's blended passes would blend in the weighted space (row 18). *R3, stored 4x samples and a custom resolve:* 117,964,800 B at 1440p; reopens ADR 0019 (row 17). `Rg11b10Ufloat` halves the target with R1 but needs a floor feature and shifts hue; R2's inverse rules it out (rows 16, 18). Every option clamps to 65504 before the write, so Goldens agree across backends (row 15).
- **8-bit output.** *D1, static triangular dither* of [−1, 1) LSB in the encoded domain, from a hash or a CC0 blue-noise mask, written through a non-sRGB view (row 19). *D2, none:* bands stay in dark gradients (Measured).
- **Bloom.** *B1, none in M3*, a fog line with this note's cost. *B2, the CoD:AW mip chain* without a threshold, 0.27 ms at 1440p (Measured); #200's size ceiling makes it a ticket of its own (row 20). *B3, FFT glare:* a later polish (row 21).
- **Display.** *H1, SDR only in M3,* with HDR output a fog line: wgpu 30.0.1 configures it, and the operator must then take a peak (rows 4, 8, 9, 22). *H2, EDR on Metal now:* a second output path; Goldens stay SDR.

## Not verified

- Operators: the ACES 1.x hue figures come from Hill's fit, not the full transform; ACES 2.0 beyond its tonescale was not transcribed; Godot's AgX parameters are computed on the CPU and were not read; the CEDEC 2017 Gran Turismo slides need a login, so the GT curve comes from its authors' MIT Desmos page; for GT7 on an sRGB display this note converts Rec.2020 to Rec.709 and clips, which the reference code does not do; Tony McMapface's generator and Frostbite's HDR-variant constants are unpublished; Unreal 5.8's move to ACES 2.0 rests on one Epic staff answer on Unreal's forum; whether Unreal animates its dither is unknown.
- Exposure: ISO 12232 and ISO 2720 are paywalled, so the 1.2 factor rests on the engines' own derivations; Unreal's adaptation formula and defaults are not in its documentation, and its source is under the Epic EULA; whether Ghost of Tsushima's and Unreal's local exposure read the current or the previous frame; no published GPU time for a luminance histogram, local exposure, or Unreal's, HDRP's and Bevy's bloom.
- Precision: IEEE 754 was read through D3D11.3 and WGSL; whether D3D12 keeps D3D11.3's conversion rules; what lavapipe stores on fp16 overflow, which Vulkan lets be infinite, was not measured.
- Resolve: Drobot's HRAA was searched in its course notes only; Persson's ShaderX6 article was not read; Unreal's MSAA resolve of HDR is undocumented.
- Bloom: the resolution behind CoD:AW's 0.35 ms and the luma of its Karis weight; the blur settings behind Bjørge's chart.
- wgpu and hardware: lavapipe's `RG11B10UFLOAT_RENDERABLE` is read from Mesa's source, not run on CI; Vulkan drivers' coverage of `B10G11R11_UFLOAT_PACK32` as a colour attachment was not read; the M4 Pro display's EDR headroom was not read; the probe's lit-pass stand-in is flat-shaded, and the weighting's cost in the real lit pass waits for #200.
