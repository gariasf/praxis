# Verification against sources: oracles, reference diffs and tolerances

Researched 2026-11-01 for ticket #477, the input to Lane 1's brief (correctness against intent). Every number below was read at the URL beside it on that date; code lines are quoted from the pinned or `main` revision named. Praxis's own figures are from `main` at 732e58c7.

**Summary.** Each kind of check has its own defensible tolerance, and none of the surveyed projects uses one number for all of them. An analytic oracle is held to the error the shader-accuracy specification allows for the expression's chain plus measured headroom, which is what Praxis's named constants already do (1e-6 to 5e-5, GT7 at 3e-4 where PQ's exponent amplifies rounding); the recommendation is to keep that scheme and write the specification floor beside each measurement. A statistical test is a chi-square at 1% significance with Šidák correction and a pooled minimum of 5 expected per cell (pbrt-v4 and Mitsuba 3 share it), or a per-pixel Z-test at 1% against a converged reference with 97.5% of pixels passing (Mitsuba 3); neither is in Praxis yet and both belong with the first sampled estimator. A reference-image diff is tolerated by what the reference is: Praxis's Goldens at 1 of 255 per channel and no pixel past it sit at the strict end of practice (WebGPU CTS 1 ULP of the format; Blender 0.016 on 1% of pixels; glTF Sample Renderer 0.1 pixelmatch on 0.1%) and should stay, while the Cycles comparison, an independent renderer behind known structural differences, should get a per-scene Baseline on linear-light RMSE and the mean 8-bit difference, judged by its difference map, and no absolute threshold until the ambient term's visibility lands. A cost against a source's published figure is a flag, not a verdict: no primary source states a scaling law, the vendor tables scale sub-linearly with pixels, and the defensible bound is the pass's own hardware floor (roofline), which `docs/performance.md` already applies as "within twice its bandwidth floor".

### What each image metric is for

| Metric | Measures | Good for | Not for | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RMSE in linear light | energy difference per pixel | bias and energy (a furnace, an exposure); comparing candidates against one exact reference | ranking by look: "RMSE favours blur" (`docs/performance.md`, #359) | Praxis's `Aliasing` counters |
| Relative MSE | squared error over (reference + 0.01)² per channel, infinities skipped | noise of an estimator against a converged reference, dark regions weighted up | display-referred judgement | pbrt-v4 `image.cpp` [line 626](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mmp/pbrt-v4/master/src/pbrt/util/image.cpp) |
| Mean absolute difference in 8-bit steps | display-referred error | a single number a human can place against dither (1 step) | linear energy; hides where | Praxis `compare.py` |
| Z-test per pixel | difference in units of the reference's standard error | a noisy reference whose variance is known | a reference without a variance image | Mitsuba 3 `testing.py` |
| FLIP | perceived difference when flipping between two images at 67 pixels per degree (0.7 m from a 0.7 m wide 4K monitor), per pixel in [0, 1] | locating differences a viewer would see; the map first, the mean when a number is needed ("we recommend using the mean FLIP value instead", the authors' hindsight note) | HDR input ("the algorithm is not designed to handle high-dynamic range input"), masking; no pass threshold is stated and the parameters "were chosen based on visual inspection" | [paper](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/node/3260/FLIP_Paper.pdf), [publication page](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2020-07_FLIP), [FLIP.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVlabs/flip/main/src/cpp/FLIP.h) |
| SSIM | luminance, contrast and structure in an 11×11 Gaussian window (σ 1.5), K1 0.01, K2 0.03, L the dynamic range "(255 for 8-bit grayscale images)" | structural loss in 8-bit display-referred luminance (compression) | colour, HDR or linear light: the paper evaluates 8-bit luminance only and the constants "are somewhat arbitrary" | [Wang et al. 2004](http://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/eero/wang03-reprint.pdf) |
| PSNR | peak signal over RMSE in dB | a compressor's error bound | anything perceptual | Praxis `tests/cook.rs`, 44 dB |

## Recommendation per kind for Praxis

| Kind | Recommended tolerance | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Analytic oracle | Keep the present scheme: an f64 or literal reference, a named constant whose doc comment states the worst error measured on the reference GPU, set at 2 to 5 times it. Add to each constant the specification floor for the expression's chain (WGSL: 2.5 ULP a divide, 3 + 2·\|x\| ULP an exponential, 2⁻²¹ absolute a log near 1, `pow` inheriting both), so a reader sees the constant is above what any conformant GPU may return. Re-measure when a second adapter records Goldens. | WGSL and Vulkan bound each operation and let a chain inherit, so the floor is computable and small; measured headroom catches a wrong formula, which lands orders of magnitude off, without failing on a conformant driver. By those bounds one PQ encode may stray about 4e-5 relative (2⁻²¹ × 78.84 in the exponent, plus exp2's own ULPs; this note's arithmetic), the same order as the 7e-5 measured, which is why `GT7_TOLERANCE` at 3e-4 is right and a round 1e-6 would be wrong there. |
| Statistical test | When the first sampled estimator lands (irradiance probes, any importance sampler): a chi-square at 1% significance, Šidák-corrected over the test count, at least 5 expected per cell with pooling, 10⁶ samples; a sampled white furnace within 1% (pbrt's sampled hair bound), an analytic one within the quadrature's own convergence as `QUADRATURE_TOLERANCE` already is. For any noisy render against a noisy reference, Mitsuba's Z-test: 1% significance, Šidák over the pixels, 97.5% of pixels passing, the reference carrying its variance. | The two renderers that test sampling agree on every parameter and state the reasons (normality breaks under 5 per cell; multiple tests need the correction). A Z-test is the only surveyed method that tolerates a noisy reference by its measured noise instead of a guessed band. |
| Reference-image diff, self-regression (Goldens, invariance checks, gallery) | Keep 1 of 255 per channel with no pixel past it on hardware, 1 pixel on lavapipe's culled check; keep the aliasing counters at any increase. When Goldens from a second hardware adapter join, record their noise as the Metal ones were (20 captures each) before loosening anything, and loosen per adapter and per scene, never globally. | Measured noise is 0, which is the only reason a 1-step, 0-pixel rule holds; every suite that loosened did so per platform with a stated cause (EEVEE's per-GPU table), and the WebGPU CTS's 1 ULP of the format is the same rule. |
| Reference-image diff, independent renderer (Cycles) | Record per scene, as a Baseline beside the bench's, the mean absolute difference in 8-bit steps `compare.py` prints and a linear-light RMSE of the two images, and fail on a rise past a noise floor measured from two Cycles renders with different seeds; judge location by the difference image, adding a FLIP map at 67 pixels per degree when a decision needs a viewer's eye. Set no absolute threshold until the ambient term sees visibility (`DIFFERENCES.md`), since that difference is structural and dominates. | No surveyed project thresholds a cross-renderer comparison: Filament explains its differences, glTF declares approximations conformant. The two numeric regimes that exist, ACES's 0.5% per pixel and OCIO's 1e-5, are for a deterministic colour transform, which the display-ramp check already holds to one 8-bit step. A Baseline turns "no threshold" into "no regression", which the aliasing counters show works here. |
| Reference-image diff, exact reference (the 3× box-filtered captures of #359 and #388) | RMSE in linear light, used to rank candidates against one reference, never as a pass mark; pair it with a FLIP map before a technique decision. | The authors of FLIP recommend the map over any pooled number; RMSE alone prefers blur (#359). |
| Cost against a source's published figure | Keep the owner's 2× of the pixel-scaled cost as the trigger for a profile, not as the verdict. The verdict is the pass's ratio to its own floor on the measuring adapter: bytes moved over the adapter's bandwidth, or operations over its rate, whichever binds, as `docs/performance.md` did for the depth pyramid (level 0 at 0.98 of its floor). A pass is an offender when it is past 2× its floor; a pass within 2× of its floor that is still over 2× its source is an architectural finding (different input, different work), recorded and closed. Scale a source by pixels only as an upper bound, and say so. | Roofline is the only published method that defines what a pass should cost on given hardware; the vendors' own tables show pass cost growing sub-linearly with pixels, so a linear scaling over-estimates the source's cost at 1440p and makes 2× lenient, while a floor on the same hardware needs no scaling at all. No source measured on an Apple GPU was found, and Apple's own guidance (no VRAM, tile memory, compression) is why none would transfer. |

## Not verified

1. Vulkan's and WGSL's accuracy tables were read from the specifications' HTML converted to text, not rendered; the ULP figures match between the two documents.
2. pbrt-v4's Hair furnace bounds and Mitsuba's 3% integrator band have no stated reason in their files.
3. Mitsuba's C++ `mitsuba.math.chi2` pooling was read from its Python port in `math_py.py`, which says it is a "Pure-Python/numpy port".
4. piglit's `0.01` is from the Igalia GitHub mirror of 2020-03-20; gitlab.freedesktop.org refused a non-browser client.
5. No primary source states in words that a pass's cost scales with pixel count; the vendor tables are the published practice, and they scale sub-linearly. Real-Time Rendering's chapter on locating the bottleneck is not online.
6. The FLIP paper's pages 13 to 23 (results, user study) were not read; the "no threshold stated" finding rests on its goals, limitations and pooling sections and on the tool's README, whose only numeric tolerance is its self-test's six decimal places on the mean.
7. HDR-FLIP's paper (105 MB) was not opened; its description is from the publication page and `FLIP.h`.
