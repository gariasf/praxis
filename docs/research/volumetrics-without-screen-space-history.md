# Volumetrics without screen-space history: state of the art

Researched 2026-11-14 for ticket #838 (parent #803; queue item 3 of `docs/audit/strategy/2026-11.md`). It surveys the block the pre-mortem left open (`docs/research/world-design-pre-mortem.md`, Volumetric clouds and fog) and feeds two questions the owner answers before M6 is charted: whether a world-space or sky-space cache is enough for clouds, fog and weather's participating media under ADR 0008, and, if not, what admitting screen-space history for this one term would need from the main pass. It ends in options and chooses none.

**Summary.** The sky and clouds have shipped without screen-space history; among the sources read, frustum-volume fog has shipped without it once, in a title presented in 2014, on a blurred shadow map that Praxis does not have.

- **Unshadowed sky and far haze** need no history: Hillaire's look-up tables are rebuilt and the sky drawn every frame for 0.31 ms at 1280×720 on an NVIDIA 1080. Shafts through that atmosphere are marched, and their author recommends reprojection.
- **Clouds** have shipped in all three Temporal classes. Class (c): reprojected ray marches at 0.9 to 2 ms on consoles (Guerrilla, Frostbite, Rockstar). Class (b): Ghost of Tsushima's 768×768 sky-space texture, time-sliced over 60 frames, cost unpublished. Class (a): Guerrilla's fly-through clouds, marched every frame for 2.2 to 4 ms at 960×540 in a PS5-only title (2.1 ms with the near part at 480×270), and Sea of Thieves' meshes. Guerrilla dropped its history for near clouds, and patched it for fast clouds (motion from the cloud shader) and for flashes of 4 to 8 frames (a mask).
- **Fog** shipped in class (a) in Assassin's Creed 4, about 1.1 ms on an Xbox One. Every later frustum-volume fog whose source says how it is stabilised keeps a history of that volume, by default or as a switch, or leans on the frame's TAA, and three titles keep the history off the part of the scattered light that ghosts: all of it in Red Dead Redemption 2, runtime lights in The Last of Us Part II, the view-dependent in-scatter in Enshrouded.
- **World-space caches** (class b) appear in shipped fog only as inputs: probe ambient, cloud and terrain shadow maps, one accumulated shadow volume. No shipped title among the sources accumulates fog's direct light in one, and no source prices a fog shadowed by rays without a temporal filter. That figure has to be measured here.
- **Admitting screen-space history** for this term would ask the main pass to write nothing new, for the forms that keep their own history. A frustum volume's history reads its previous volume and a previous-view matrix; a cloud or far-march reconstruction adds the depth the pre-pass already stores. No source that states its inputs reads a scene motion-vector buffer; Unreal, Unity, Call of Duty and Killzone do not say what theirs read. What changes is what the lit pass reads.
- **A trap**: several systems described as having no history resolve a per-pixel dither through the frame's TAA, which Praxis lacks.

Sources: SIGGRAPH Advances in Real-Time Rendering and Physically Based Shading courses (2014 to 2026), SIGGRAPH Talks, GDC, Digital Dragons and the Graphics Programming Conference; papers from 2009 to 2026 (Eurographics, EGSR, I3D, HPG, SIGGRAPH, arXiv); the public documentation of Unreal Engine 5.9, Unity HDRP 17, Godot 4 and RTX Remix; Godot 4.7.2 and Bevy 0.20.1 source. No engine source under a restrictive licence was read. Each claim relied on is in `docs/references.toml`, cited by key; every quote there was matched against a saved copy of its source on 2026-11-14, and the rows are source-checked adversarially once, when the decision is taken (`docs/agents/workflow.md`, Review). Nothing was measured in this repo.

Boundaries: the medium model (non-exponential transmittance for correlated media) stays with the atmosphere line under *Not yet specified* on the ladder's map (#17); every ray-marched technique below multiplies transmittance step by step. Rain and snow as particles, water, hair and order-independent transparency are not covered.

Terms: a **frustum volume** is the camera-aligned 3D texture the sources call a froxel grid; the word is avoided because a Froxel here holds a light list (`CONTEXT.md`). The classes are `CONTEXT.md`'s Temporal classes. A frustum volume blended with its reprojected previous frame is counted as class (c): it is reprojection, and its sources report trails and smearing on screen. An **input** is a term that does not depend on the view direction (visibility, density, ambient light).

## Sky and far haze

**1. Tables rebuilt each frame.** Hillaire's model keeps small look-up tables, a 32×32×32 aerial-perspective volume over 32 km among them, and aims to evaluate multiple scattering "each and every frame without any delay" [ref:hillaire-2020-atmosphere-luts-every-frame] [ref:hillaire-2020-aerial-perspective-volume]. Updating them and drawing the sky costs 0.31 ms at 1280×720 on an NVIDIA 1080, and about 1 ms on an iPhone 6s in Fortnite [ref:hillaire-2020-atmosphere-cost]. The model it replaces draws in 0.22 ms but takes 250 ms to rebuild its tables, so a changing atmosphere means slicing the rebuild over frames, a class (b) cache with latency [ref:hillaire-2020-vs-bruneton-update-cost]. The reference code is MIT [ref:hillaire-2020-reference-code-licence]; Bruneton's is three-clause BSD [ref:bruneton-2017-reference-code-licence]. Its limit is shadowing inside the atmosphere (a mountain's or a cloud's shaft): epipolar sampling and shadow volumes do not apply to it, so the author marches cascaded shadow maps, which at 32 samples raises the sky's cost to 1.0 ms, and recommends "reprojection to combine samples from previous frames" [ref:hillaire-2020-volumetric-shadows-reprojection]. The cheaper path he names stores the shadows in the sky-view and aerial-perspective tables [ref:hillaire-2020-unreal-cloud-shadow-taa-or-lut].

## Clouds

**2. A sky-space map, time-sliced.** Ghost of Tsushima renders its clouds into a 768×768 paraboloid texture, "timesliced over 60 frames", with two more textures "for scrolling & lerping" between finished renders [ref:patry-2021-tsushima-clouds-paraboloid]. The deck gives no cost, no speaker notes and no limits. By construction the map holds one view point and no depth, so clouds that reach the terrain or that the camera enters are outside it; that is inference, not a quoted limit. Godot documents the same shape for a whole sky, read back from a radiance cube map when it changes slowly [ref:godot-docs-sky-shader-subpasses-and-cubemap]. A sky-space map has a second reader in Praxis: probe and reflection rays that miss need the sky's radiance, clouds included, and Assassin's Creed Shadows uses an 8×8 sky texture where a position falls outside its probe volume [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-cascades], and for specular a 128×128 fallback cube map "containing the terrain, sky, clouds and far entities", one face a frame [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-far-cubemap].

**3. Every frame, at two resolutions.** For clouds the player flies through, Guerrilla dropped its temporal upscale "because the image will not resolve in time" and marches all at once: 480×270 near the camera, 960×540 beyond, blended over depth. They cost "a little over a millisecond" of a 2.5 ms cloud total in the 960×540 mode, the total is 5 ms at 1920×1080, and they did not fit on PlayStation 4 [ref:schneider-2022-nubis-evolved-flythrough-no-temporal]. The voxel clouds of the PS5-only Burning Shores cost 2.2 to 4 ms at 960×540, or 2.1 ms for the 4 ms frame when split at 200 m, with "a bit more pixelation on sharp and dense features"; its march adds "a temporally jittered offset designed to counter under-sampling noise", animated near the camera and static far away, and the deck does not say what resolves it [ref:schneider-2023-nubis-cubed-voxel-cost]. Unreal's reactive cloud modes trace at quarter resolution, reconstruct at half and upsample, by means its documentation does not describe; its full-resolution-looking mode is "less reactive" and cannot intersect opaque meshes [ref:unreal-docs-volumetric-render-target-modes]. Frostbite's 2016 clouds sat behind all geometry, and letting them meet a mountain "would need" a downsampled depth and a bilateral upsample [ref:hillaire-2016-frostbite-low-res-compositing-limit].

**4. Meshes.** Sea of Thieves "chose not to pursue a ray-marched approach": opaque cloud geometry with per-vertex lighting goes to an off-screen buffer, is blurred at quarter resolution and composited with its depth [ref:ang-2018-sea-of-thieves-mesh-clouds]. No cost is published.

**5. A fraction per frame, reprojected.** Horizon Zero Dawn updates 1 pixel in 16 and reprojects the rest, toward a target of about 2 ms on a PlayStation 4 from about 20 ms [ref:schneider-2015-hzd-clouds]; the 2017 deck gives 22 ms "Without any optimizations except temporal reprojection" and 1.2 ms after three more [ref:schneider-2017-nubis-reprojection-cost]; Forbidden West keeps it, 480×270 upscaled to 1920×1080, 16 ms into 2 ms on a PS5 [ref:schneider-2022-nubis-evolved-temporal-upscale]. Frostbite blends a half-resolution march with the previous frame's, reprojected "according to previous and current camera properties", for 0.91 ms at 720p and 1.60 ms at 1080p on an Xbox One in its stated worst case [ref:hillaire-2016-frostbite-clouds-temporal]. Red Dead Redemption 2 casts one ray per 2×2 half-resolution tile, validates history with a 3×3 colour clamp refined by depth [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-raymarch-reconstruction], and upscales by a dither that the frame's TAA resolves [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-upscale-relies-on-taa]. Unity HDRP accumulates in a per-camera history buffer and documents ghosting [ref:unity-hdrp-clouds-temporal-accumulation]. Its authors name the limits. It "worked for distant clouds but not for nearby clouds" [ref:schneider-2023-nubis-cubed-temporal-limit]; a cloud that moves faster than the image builds gives "a sort of pixel soup" until the cloud shader supplies its own motion [ref:schneider-2022-nubis-evolved-motion-vectors]; thin occluders crossing the sky can leave reprojection artefacts unless culling reads a conservative low-LOD depth [ref:schneider-2017-nubis-occluder-artifacts]; and a flash lasting 4 to 8 frames is degraded by a 16-frame build [ref:schneider-2022-nubis-evolved-lightning-16-frames]. Unreal's author sums the trade up as 16 frames of latency that look full resolution, against an instant result that looks blurrier [ref:hillaire-2020-unreal-cloud-amortisation-tradeoff].

**6. Cloud shadow and cloud light in caches.** Praxis's rays would not see clouds. Each source that describes cloud shadows casts them from a map or a grid marched from the light. Red Dead Redemption 2 marches a 768×768 exponential shadow map from the light and updates 1 texel in 16 a frame [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-cloud-shadow-map]; Frostbite projects a 2D transmittance texture that surfaces, particles and its dynamic GI's input sample [ref:hillaire-2016-frostbite-cloud-shadow-texture]. Burning Shores stores density summed toward the sun in a 256×256×32 voxel grid, 0.1 to 0.2 ms amortised over 8 frames, which cut render times by around 40% [ref:schneider-2023-nubis-cubed-light-voxel-grid]. Clouds reach indirect light through 32×32 sky irradiance probes (0.22 ms on a PS4) and a low-resolution reflection cube map (0.15 ms) in Red Dead Redemption 2 [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-sky-probes-and-reflection], and through a time-sliced sky light capture in Unreal [ref:unreal-docs-sky-light-time-slicing]. Where the source says how they update, these are class (b).

## Fog and local media

**7. A frustum volume, no history.** Assassin's Creed 4 fills a 160×90×64 or 160×90×128 volume over 50 to 128 m each frame [ref:wronski-2014-ac4-grid-fixed-cost] for about 1.1 ms on an Xbox One, 0.43 ms of it lighting [ref:wronski-2014-cost-xbox-one]. Shading reads it with "a simple bilinear look-up from a 3D texture", in forward or deferred, for any number of transparent layers, and it is built without scene depth [ref:wronski-2014-forward-and-transparent-apply]. Its stability came from the shadow term: the cascades are downsampled into a 1024×256 exponential shadow map and blurred, whose leaking "wasn’t noticeable" in fog [ref:wronski-2014-shadow-prefilter-esm]. Temporal reprojection is presented as an extension "developed after AC4", which removed "almost all edge artifacts" [ref:wronski-2014-temporal-added-after-ac4]. Frostbite's verdict on one sample per cell without history is "very strong aliasing" in thick media, local lights and shadows [ref:hillaire-2015-one-sample-aliasing]. Ghost of Tsushima removes one such artefact inside the frame: its 128×64×64 volume stores in-scattered radiance divided by opacity and re-applies opacity per pixel, which fixes aliasing at the horizon and in thin layers. Its slide of timings, which does not name the pass, reads 363 µs with trilinear filtering to 667 µs with tricubic on a base PS4 at 1080p, and tricubic filtering "to smooth and denoise" costs "Less than 50 us more than trilinear on PS4 Pro" [ref:patry-2021-tsushima-density-antialiasing].

**8. History on the whole volume.** Frostbite reprojects the previous scattering and extinction and blends in 5% of the current jittered sample [ref:hillaire-2015-froxel-temporal]. On a PS4 at 900p its test scene costs 2.95 ms with 8×8-pixel cells or 0.83 ms with 16×16, the history 0.4 ms of the first [ref:hillaire-2015-cost-ps4]; a 240×135×64 fp16 volume is about 15 MB [ref:hillaire-2015-grid-size]. It samples its shadow cascades without an exponential pre-filter because "our temporal up-sampling is enough to soften the result" [ref:hillaire-2015-sun-shadow-and-ambient]. The price: "Local fog volume and lights will leave trails when moving", with a velocity buffer for media and neighbour clamping named as open research [ref:hillaire-2015-temporal-trails]. Unreal's documentation says the same of its "heavy temporal reprojection filter" (fast-changing lights "leave lighting trails") and gives 1 ms on a PS4 at High and 3 ms on a GTX 970 at Epic, vendor figures without a resolution [ref:unreal-docs-volumetric-fog-temporal-reprojection]. Call of Duty: Infinite Warfare paid "2 x memory & bandwidth" for it [ref:drobot-2017-cod-iw-froxel-volumetrics]. Unity [ref:unity-hdrp-docs-fog-denoising-modes] and Godot [ref:godot-docs-fog-temporal-reprojection] ship it as a switch and document its ghosting; Unity's Gaussian mode and Godot's filter are the per-frame alternatives they offer.

**9. History kept off part of the light.** Red Dead Redemption 2 applies no temporal blend to its scattered light, since history ghosts behind moving lights and trails on static ones in anisotropic fog when the camera moves [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-no-temporal-on-scattered-light]. It filters the shadow term and the scattering and absorption coefficients instead, each in a frustum volume, reprojecting the media by wind velocity, which leaked between interiors and exteriors until a velocity-scale volume was added [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-temporal-on-shadow-and-material]. Its three volumes cost 0.204, 0.33 and 0.34 ms on a PS4, paired with their labels by slide order [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-cost-ps4]. The Last of Us Part II computes runtime lights "in 1 frame" because "they move and ghost", and filters the shadow value [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-runtime-lights-not-temporal], keeping 95% of history per cell [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-temporal-filter]; it usually costs 2 to 3 ms on hardware the slides do not name [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-cost], its fog's probe ambient is filtered over frames too [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-ambient-from-probes], and its history left newly revealed cells stuck on one sample and smeared screen edges, which the deck then fixes ("With these fixes, final result looks good!") [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-history-artefacts]. Enshrouded accumulates its populate pass, point-light, GI and emissive radiance included, and gives the view-dependent in-scatter "No temporal accumulation", as it "leads to smearing" [ref:krause-2025-enshrouded-froxel-split]. Ghost of Tsushima sits between techniques 8 and 9: it filters shadow and ambient fractions and its local lighting too, in a volume spanning 10 cm to 100 km, about 0.5 ms on a base PS4 [ref:patry-2021-tsushima-haze-froxel-temporal]. Red Dead Redemption 2's scattered light is not history-free in effect: it relies on "dithered lookups of the accumulation volume in conjunction with TAA".

**10. Inputs in world-space caches.** Five sources light fog's ambient from the game's probes: Red Dead Redemption 2 [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-visibility-and-ambient], The Last of Us Part II, where probe light leaks into fog [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-ambient-from-probes], Snowdrop, whose DDGI-based probes store an average per probe "mainly used for the fog" [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-average-for-fog], 007 First Light, at one position per volume and two directions per probe [ref:mueller-2026-smolder-ddgi-sampling], and Frostbite [ref:hillaire-2015-sun-shadow-and-ambient]. Enshrouded blends swept transmittance into the voxels of a shadow volume at 1/64 a step, which its march samples beside the cascades (whether that volume is anchored to the world or follows the camera is not in the deck's text) [ref:krause-2025-enshrouded-shadow-volume-accumulation], and keeps fog density for the whole playable area in a sparse world volume of 240 m cells [ref:krause-2025-enshrouded-world-fog-data]. Frostbite voxelizes its media into a three-level clip map "following and containing the camera" for the shadows media cast, 0.04 to 0.14 ms a light on a PS4 [ref:hillaire-2015-extinction-clipmap-volumetric-shadows]. Against a world-space volume for the fog itself stands its first author: a world-aligned cuboid gave "very easy temporal filtering" but was slow to march and aliased, so the shipped layout is frustum-aligned [ref:wronski-2014-world-space-volume-rejected]. No shipped title among the sources accumulates fog's direct light in a world-space cache.

**11. Visibility by rays.** Every shipped title's fog read is shadowed from shadow maps. The nearest source to Praxis is a 2023 paper whose pipeline "does not use shadow mapping but instead uses ray tracing to compute the shadow term" of each cell, where rays are "roughly 80% of the total cost", and which adds a world-space grid of visibility refreshed in alternating halves [ref:stojanovic-2023-shared-shadow-grid]. One 1920×1080 camera costs 1.10 ms in its reference implementation, Frostbite's pipeline modified "to only trace rays within the scene bounds", and 0.91 ms with the grid, on an RTX 3090 [ref:stojanovic-2023-cost]. It still integrates its frustum volume temporally and finds that the likely reason the alternating update shows no inconsistency [ref:stojanovic-2023-temporal-froxels], and its grid spans the whole scene, "impractical" for open worlds, with clouds out of scope [ref:stojanovic-2023-limits]. Unreal's MegaLights traces "a fixed number of shadow rays per voxel" and denoises [ref:unreal-docs-megalights-fog-shadow-rays]; RTX Remix traces a ray per cell per frame and accumulates up to 254 frames [ref:nvidia-rtx-remix-docs-froxel-visibility-rays]. Neither gives a timing. 007 First Light rejects shadow rays for the shadows its volumes cast and renders each into a moment texture instead, 64×64 at the low end and 128×128 where shown [ref:mueller-2026-smolder-volumetric-shadows].

**12. The far field.** Red Dead Redemption 2's 160×88×64 volume reaches 160 m at most, and in such volumes "results with high frequency detail are unreliable and cause instability even with strong temporal integration" [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-froxel-limits]; beyond it a per-pixel march takes over, one ray per 2×2 half-resolution tile, rebuilt from a screen history with some flicker left [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-far-raymarch-history], 1.31 ms plus 0.232 ms on a PS4, the same march that carries technique 5's clouds, its labels paired by slide order [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-cost-ps4]. Enshrouded follows it [ref:krause-2025-enshrouded-raymarch-reconstruction]. The history-free far field in these sources is technique 1's aerial-perspective volume. Ghost of Tsushima's volume spans the whole range and fixes its density aliasing inside the frame, but filters its lighting over frames (technique 9).

**13. Shafts from a shadow map.** Epipolar sampling [ref:engelhardt-2010-epipolar-needs], min-max mipmaps [ref:chen-2011-minmax-needs], prefiltered moment maps (1.49 ms for a full scene at 1920×1080 on a GTX 980) [ref:peters-2016-moment-single-scattering] and NVIDIA's polygonal volumes [ref:hoobler-2016-polygonal-light-volumes] (1.209 ms at 1080p on a GTX 980 Ti in one Fallout 4 scene, a vendor figure [ref:hoobler-2016-cost-1080p]) are complete each frame. All four read a shadow map and the first three state a homogeneous medium; Hillaire rules epipolar sampling and shadow volumes out for an atmosphere [ref:hillaire-2020-volumetric-shadows-reprojection]. A light-propagation lattice rebuilt each frame, "no data is kept between frames", carries multiple scattering at about 30 fps on a GTX 480 with 14 ms of marching [ref:billeter-2012-lpv-media] [ref:billeter-2012-lpv-media-cost].

**14. Local effect volumes.** Smolder, shipped in 007 First Light, set out "to not have any temporal filtering in the system itself", because fast or close effects ghost "due to the lack of motion vectors" [ref:mueller-2026-smolder-no-temporal-filter]. At PS5-equivalent settings it costs 0.63 to 1.82 ms at 1080p on an RTX 5060 Ti and 1.08 to 3.46 ms on an RTX 3060, without its shadow passes [ref:mueller-2026-smolder-cost]. Its per-pixel blue-noise jitter is left to TAA, and it ghosted heavily under FSR 3 until fed to the reactive mask [ref:mueller-2026-smolder-taa-ghosting]. EA SEED's Vaporwave marches each pixel to the depth buffer and does not "want to rely on temporal reprojection"; it has not shipped [ref:lewin-2025-vaporwave-no-reprojection]. Bevy's fog is a per-pixel march of 64 steps against shadow maps that reads depth sample 0 under MSAA [ref:bevy-source-fog-raymarch-per-pixel], with a jitter "intended for use with temporal antialiasing" [ref:bevy-source-fog-jitter-for-taa].

## Filtering without history

**15. A spatial resolve.** Killzone Shadow Fall marches at half resolution with a random step offset and removes the dither with a bilateral blur [ref:valient-2014-killzone-raymarch-dither-blur]; the same deck then reuses the previous buffer at up to 50% [ref:valient-2014-killzone-reuses-previous-buffer]. Tóth and Umenhoffer spread a march's samples over M×M pixel blocks and sum them with a separable filter, taking a 42 fps march back to 100 fps on an 8800 GTX at 800×600 [ref:toth-2009-interleaved-sampling]. FAST shows that noise optimised for one spatial filter tends to have the lowest error after that filter, a four-step march through media included [ref:donnelly-2024-fast-spatial-filter], that a mask built for temporal averaging gives up single-frame quality [ref:donnelly-2024-fast-single-frame], and publishes its masks under what GitHub classifies as BSD-3-Clause [ref:donnelly-2024-fast-code-licence]. NVIDIA's library renders its low-resolution buffer with MSAA and resolves it before compositing; under a TAA-jittered frame that buffer flickered until it got a temporal resolve of its own [ref:hoobler-2016-msaa-and-temporal-resolve]. No source gives the GPU cost of a spatial resolve at 1440p; the nearest figure is Ghost of Tsushima's tricubic lookup (technique 7).

## Research

**16. Path-traced and learned volumes.** Volumetric reservoir resampling costs 55 ms for single scattering and 142 ms for three bounces at 1920×1080 on an RTX 3090 [ref:lin-2021-volumetric-restir-cost]; its authors need a reprojection of their own, "as surface motion vectors fail in volumes", unbiased only for a static volume and camera [ref:lin-2021-volumetric-restir-reprojection]. A 2021 denoising paper describes game volumetrics as ray marching with large steps "and temporal filtering", and found no clear benefit from volume motion vectors [ref:hofmann-2021-realtime-practice]. Learned cloud shading is closer to a frame and needs no history for a directional light: 5.0 to 5.2 ms on an RTX 2080 at 1024×1024 after about four days of training [ref:hu-2023-mrpnn-cost], assuming homogeneous albedo and phase, its ambient term accumulating samples over what the quoted passages do not say are frames [ref:hu-2023-mrpnn-limits]; a preprint of 2026-11-09 reports 10.16 ms on an RTX 5090 "without requiring accumulation over frames", its accuracy tied to its training coverage [ref:leonard-2026-halort-no-accumulation]. Neither has shipped.

## What screen-space history would ask of the main pass

| History of | Read from earlier frames | Read from this frame | The main pass must write | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A frustum volume | The previous volume | A to-previous-view matrix | Nothing | Godot's source; Frostbite |
| The same, occluded cells skipped | Which cells were visible | Depth, reduced per cell column | Nothing new: depth | The Last of Us Part II |
| Moving media in it | The previous media volume | Wind velocity and a velocity-scale volume the volumetric passes fill | Nothing | Red Dead Redemption 2 |
| Clouds, a fraction per frame | The previous cloud buffer, a cloud distance buffer | Camera matrices, the cloud shader's own motion, a low-LOD depth sample | Nothing new: depth | Guerrilla |
| Clouds, moving average | The previous result | Previous and current camera projection and transform | Nothing | Frostbite |
| A far-field march | A half-resolution history | Half-resolution minimum and maximum depth, a 3×3 colour clamp | Nothing new: depth. Its upscale then leaned on the frame's TAA | Red Dead Redemption 2, Enshrouded |

Godot's reprojection transforms a cell by a to-previous-view matrix and samples the previous volume; a cell that falls outside takes no history [ref:godot-source-fog-reprojection-inputs]. Wronski gives the reason little disocclusion handling is needed: the space a moving object left "is still invalid", but "all the data behind it is proper", unless occluded cells are culled [ref:wronski-2014-reprojection-validity], and The Last of Us Part II, which does cull them, checks a 2D texture of which cells were visible [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-temporal-filter]. No source that states its inputs reads a scene motion-vector buffer; Unreal's, Unity's, Call of Duty's and Killzone's sources do not say what their histories read.

So for the forms that keep their own history, the main pass that M6 builds on would write nothing new, and M6 need reserve no attachment for them. The forms that leave a dither to the frame's TAA (Red Dead Redemption 2's scattered light and upscale, Smolder) would need that TAA and whatever it reads, which no entry lists and Praxis does not have. Praxis's depth pre-pass already stores what the screen-space forms read, and its depth pyramid already holds the farthest depth under each texel (ADR 0019). Three things would change instead:

1. The lit pass would read a volume that carries history. `CLAUDE.md`'s checklist line "The main pass reads no screen-space history, and no change rules out its 4x MSAA" (ADRs 0008, 0019, 0020) would need to say whether that read counts.
2. History is memory: a second copy of each volume kept, about 15 MB each by Frostbite's figure for 1080p (16,588,800 bytes, computed), against the 512 MiB render-target Budget.
3. A low-resolution screen-space result (techniques 3, 5, 12, 14) has to be composited against 4x MSAA depth. Frostbite names a downsampled depth and a bilateral upsample [ref:hillaire-2016-frostbite-low-res-compositing-limit]; Bevy reads sample 0, "approximate but good enough" [ref:bevy-source-fog-raymarch-per-pixel]; one of Unity's cloud blending options has an effect only without MSAA [ref:unity-hdrp-clouds-temporal-accumulation]. A frustum volume or a sky-space map is sampled by each fragment at its own depth or direction and has no such step.

## Fit to Praxis

- **ADR 0020, no shadow maps.** Techniques 7, 8, 9, 12 and 13 take the sun's visibility from a shadow map, and technique 7's stability rests on blurring one. Praxis would trace it (technique 11). A 160×90×64 volume has 921,600 cells, a quarter of the 3,686,400 pixels the shadow mask traces at 1440p (computed); what a ray per cell costs through open air, and how many a stable cell needs without a filter over frames, no source says.
- **ADR 0020, probes.** Fog ambient from probes is shipped practice and class (b) already. Two items for M6's charting: Snowdrop's per-probe average, stored for fog, and The Last of Us Part II's probe leaks into fog, which Interior volumes would have to mask for media as for surfaces.
- **ADR 0019, forward 4x MSAA.** A frustum volume is one 3D lookup in the lit and transparent passes, as Assassin's Creed 4 [ref:wronski-2014-forward-and-transparent-apply] and Call of Duty: Infinite Warfare [ref:drobot-2017-cod-iw-froxel-volumetrics] apply it.
- **One queue.** Ghost of Tsushima's haze and Smolder's shadows run on async compute; wgpu exposes one queue [ref:wgpu-29-request-device-one-queue] [ref:wgpu-hal-30-metal-one-queue], so the haze's 0.5 ms, and Smolder's shadow passes, whose cost is not published, would land in the frame.
- **ADR 0013.** The GPU frame's shares that exist sum to 9 of its 12 ms (`docs/audit/strategy/2026-11.md`, S3-04: gate 6, dynamic TLAS 1, probes 2). The published per-frame figures without history are 0.31 ms for the sky (NVIDIA 1080, 720p), about 1.1 ms for fog (Xbox One) and 2.1 to 4 ms for clouds (a PS5-only title, 960×540 or less). None was measured at 1440p or on either of Praxis's adapters, and no conversion is offered.
- **Weather.** A weather change is a change of the tables' parameters and of density: technique 1 follows it the same frame, a sky-space cloud map (2) after its slice count, 60 frames in the one shipped case. A flash shorter than the cache's refresh needs a per-frame term, as Guerrilla's mask is for its 16-frame build.

## Options

Clouds:

- **K1. Sky-space map (class b), technique 2**, with cloud shadow and cloud light in caches (6). Fits the ADRs as written and also feeds ray misses. Open: its cost, never published; clouds that touch terrain or that the camera enters; its lag under fast weather.
- **K2. Per-frame march at low resolution (class a), technique 3.** Fits as written and covers near clouds. Open: 2.1 to 4 ms in a PS5-only title at a quarter of 1080p's pixels or fewer, what resolves its near jitter, and a depth-aware upsample against 4x MSAA with no TAA behind it.
- **K3. Meshes (class a), technique 4.** Fits as written; an authoring choice with no published cost.
- **K4. Reprojected march (class c), technique 5.** About 2 ms on consoles for a full-resolution look. Needs ADR 0008 amended, and its authors' limits are the near, the fast and the briefly lit.
- K1 for the distant sky with K2 for near clouds is the split Guerrilla ships, with K4 where K1 stands here.

Fog:

- **F1. Frustum volume, no history (class a), technique 7**, its dither or aliasing resolved spatially (15). Fits as written. Open: stability without a blurred shadow map, and Frostbite's "very strong aliasing" at one sample per cell.
- **F2. F1 with its inputs in class (b) caches, technique 10**: ambient from M6's probes, visibility in a camera-centred world-space volume refreshed by rays over frames, cloud shadow from a map, scattered light computed each frame. Fits as written. No title ships exactly this. Each part but the camera-centred, ray-refreshed visibility volume has shipped or been published beside a frustum history; the nearest to that part are the 2023 paper's scene-wide grid (technique 11) and Enshrouded's swept shadow volume.
- **F3. History kept off part of the light (class c), technique 9.** The form three recent titles converged on, each drawing the line elsewhere; only Red Dead Redemption 2 keeps it to view-independent inputs, and it then leans on TAA. Needs ADR 0008 amended; reads a previous volume and a matrix; the artefacts its authors report, and fix, are leaks between interiors and exteriors, newly revealed cells and screen edges.
- **F4. History on the whole volume (class c), technique 8.** Unreal's documented form, and a switch in Unity and Godot; trails behind moving lights and fog volumes.

What would settle F1 against F2 against F3 is a measurement no source holds: a frustum volume on a Reference scene with a moving sun, its visibility traced by rays, timed on the M4 Pro and the RTX 2070 SUPER, and judged for flicker with no history, with a world-space visibility cache, and with history on its inputs. That is a prototype ticket's question.
