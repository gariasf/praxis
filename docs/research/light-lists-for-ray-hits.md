# Light lists for secondary hits: state of the art

Researched 2026-11-28 for ticket #1143 (parent #1102, *The Light grid*), which blocks #1122, the ticket that builds the Light grid. It surveys what published and shipped work does to light a ray's hit, a probe ray's in particular, from many local lights: how the lights that reach a hit are found, what a list keeps when it is full, which lights a hit traces a shadow ray toward, and what that costs in rays and memory, sized against the `town` Reference scene on the M4 Pro. It ends in options and chooses none.

**Summary.** On `town` a cell's list stays short and the lamp rays are what cost: a hit is reached by about one lamp and no point tested by more than four, and tracing each one takes 41 to 50% of the 2 ms that ADR 0020's probe rays would otherwise have to themselves, by arithmetic. The grid's fill needs a coarse cull first: tested cell by cell against every lamp, as the Froxels are, 65,536 cells would take about 2 ms more (arithmetic).

- **Shipped practice lists lights in world-space cells and rarely traces a ray to each.** Metro Exodus Enhanced and id Tech 8 list a hit's lights in world-space cells, id Tech 8 in 8 cascades of 16³ cells with at most 64 ids a cell; Snowdrop finds them through a light BVH; Godot keeps a list of at most 128 per cascade. For the shadow at a hit, Metro and DDGI's paper use shadow maps, Lumen's surface cache samples shadow maps and traces only the shadow rays they cannot resolve, and GIBS and GI-1.0 pick one light by resampling and trace one ray. Assassin's Creed Shadows lights its probe hits with all lights and does not say how it shadows local ones. Outside Godot's distance-field cascades, no source read traces a shadow ray to every local light at a hit, as ADR 0020 asks; Lumen does so only where its shadow maps fail.
- **`town` needs a small grid.** From the scene's definition (arithmetic): a point on the ground is reached by 0.94 lamps on average, 1.35 on streets and sidewalks, at most 4; a cell of 1 to 8 m lists at most 4 lamps, a 16 m cell at most 5. A cell capacity of 8 never fills on `town`, and a grid of 65,536 cells of 8 lights takes 2.4 MB, 1.8% of the `probes` Budget. Filling it as the Froxel pass fills its 18,432 Froxels, each cell testing all 1,024 lamps, would take about 2 ms if the cost scaled with the pairs tested, 0.49 ms for today's one Cascade (arithmetic); id Tech 8 culls lights against a coarse level first and fills its 32,768 cells in about 0.2 ms on consoles.
- **The cap decides the rays.** Every lamp that reaches a hit: 0.94 to 1.35 rays a hit, and 2 ms then buys 664,000 to 782,000 probe rays against 1,331,000 with none. The strongest one: 0.63 to 0.80 rays, 836,000 to 908,000 probe rays, keeping 87 to 88% of the lamps' unshadowed light. One light chosen at random with its weight: the same rays, unbiased, with noise wherever lamps reaching a hit differ in visibility. Each figure is arithmetic: the lamp ray is priced at the shadow mask pass's 1.84 ns, which a probe hit's incoherent ray need not cost.
- **Noise against the flicker test.** A deterministic list adds no frame-to-frame variance under a fixed ray set; a stochastic choice does, and DDGI's hysteresis of 0.85 to 0.98 leaves 0.29 to 0.10 of its standard deviation at steady state (arithmetic).
- **Where the list is read matters.** A grid lookup inside the shadow mask pass's ray-query kernel cost 361 µs on `town` (#1108), skipping or not. Lumen reads its lists in one pass and traces the shadow rays it compacted in another.
- **ADR 0013.** Nothing here was measured. No RTX 2070 SUPER figure was taken, and no cost here rejects a technique.

Sources: papers from 2012 to 2023 (HPG, JCGT, Proc. ACM CGIT, arXiv), SIGGRAPH Advances in Real-Time Rendering (2022, 2024, 2025), GDC 2024, 4A Games's technical article, NVIDIA's RTXDI documentation, and the source of Godot 4.7.2 and Bevy 0.20.1 at the commits cited. No engine source under a restrictive licence was read. Each claim relied on is in `docs/references.toml`, cited by key; every quote of an entry this note adds was matched by script against a saved copy of its source on 2026-11-28. The rows a decision rests on are source-checked when it is taken (`docs/agents/workflow.md`, Review).

Boundaries: point lights only; spot cones, area lights, emissive surfaces, the sun and the sky are out, as are lights past the Load radius, walking the light buffer at each hit and a Froxel for a hit, which #1122 rules out. Choosing is the owner's.

Terms: a **hit** is a probe ray's nearest intersection; a light **reaches** a point when the point lies within the light's range, the sphere where KHR_lights_punctual's window falls to zero; a **cell's list** is the lights the Light grid keeps for one cell; the **cap** is the most lamp rays a hit traces. The classes are `CONTEXT.md`'s Temporal classes. **The 2 ms** is ADR 0020's Probe update cost on `town` on the M4 Pro, which buys 1,331,000 probe rays, each with the sun ray from its hit and no light work (`docs/audit/m5.5/scalability.md`, M17).

## Finding the lights

**1. World-space cells.** Metro Exodus Enhanced lets up to 256 lights reach each sample of its ray-traced GI and lists, in each cell of a world-space grid, "all of the light that are in range" [ref:4a-2021-metro-exodus-enhanced-light-clusters]. id Tech 8's Cascaded Light Grid, from DOOM Eternal's ray-tracing update, is clustered binning in world space: 8 cascades of 16³ cells, the first 32 m across, at most 64 ids a cell, filled by an oriented box against each cell's box in one async compute dispatch, about 0.2 ms on consoles, 30 MB with its decals; cache hits read it in "Mostly same code for lighting/shading loop as raster", and coarser cascades list more lights a point does not need [ref:sousa-2025-idtech8-cascaded-light-grid]. Just Cause 2 kept 4 lights per 4 m × 4 m world-space tile [ref:persson-2015-world-space-tiles-and-list-memory]. GI-1.0 keeps a uniform grid over the hits' bounds, tests each light's sphere against a sphere around the cell's box, looser than the box [ref:boisse-2023-gi-1.0-light-grid]. Godot keeps one list per SDFGI cascade, the cascade's box against the light's [ref:godot-4.7.2-sdfgi-cascade-light-list], each voxel tracing its distance field toward each light of it [ref:godot-4.7.2-sdfgi-light-trace]. RTXDI's ReGIR places its cells around the camera and rebuilds them every frame, but fills them with light samples chosen by resampling, not with the lights in range [ref:rtxdi-fdbd9b3-regir-world-space-grid]. Persson's clustered shading stores per cluster an offset and counts into one packed list of light indices [ref:persson-2015-world-space-tiles-and-list-memory]. The fill is what grows with lights and cells: Praxis's Froxel pass tests each of 1,024 point lights against each of 18,432 Froxels in 549 µs on `town` (`bench/baselines/apple-m4-pro/town.json`); the same loop over 65,536 cells would test 3.6 times as many pairs, about 2 ms if it scaled linearly (arithmetic), which is why id Tech 8 culls in two levels, a coarse pass into a bit array in workgroup memory and then the cells.

**2. A light BVH or light tree.** Olsson et al. assign lights to clusters through a BVH built each frame over the lights' Morton codes: 1.5 ms of light assignment for about 2,400 lights on a GTX 480 [ref:olsson-2012-clustered-shading]. Moreau et al. update a two-level light BVH in under 1 ms a frame for thousands of moving lights on an RTX 2080 Ti and sample it [ref:moreau-2019-dynamic-many-light-sampling]; Conty Estevez and Kulla traverse one at each shading point with a single random number, offline [ref:conty-kulla-2018-light-tree]; stochastic lightcuts replace lightcuts' correlation with noise [ref:yuksel-2019-stochastic-lightcuts]. Snowdrop is the shipped case at probe hits: "We are using a BVH to quickly get the relevant lights from anywhere in the world" [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-hit-lights]. A tree has no capacity to fill, so nothing is dropped; it costs a traversal a hit, a loop with a stack in the kernel that traces, and amends the spec's "world-space cells". Falcor, where the light BVH and ReSTIR were built, is BSD-style [ref:falcor-licence].

**3. Hashed sparse cells.** The world-space hashed caches of `docs/research/global-illumination-and-lighting.md`, row 11, hold radiance; GI-1.0 also stores light reservoirs in a hash grid and reuses them over frames [ref:boisse-2023-gi-1.0-light-grid]. A hash keeps memory where hits land and nowhere else; for a list of lights in range, a camera-centred grid over the Cascades bounds the same memory without collisions, and on `town` most cells are reached by a lamp (65 to 87% from 1 to 8 m, arithmetic below), so sparseness buys little there. Kept across frames in world space, a hashed reservoir is class (b) and fits ADR 0008.

## A cell's capacity

Shipped lists cap and drop. Lumen picks "the first 8 lights" of each 8×8 surface-cache tile and wants "a smarter light selection strategy" [ref:wright-2022-lumen-surface-cache-direct-lighting]; Godot stops a cascade's list at 128 dynamic lights, directional lights first and the rest in the order submitted [ref:godot-4.7.2-sdfgi-cascade-light-list], and VoxelGI at 32 [ref:godot-4.7.2-gi-light-limits]; id Tech 8 at 64 ids [ref:sousa-2025-idtech8-cascaded-light-grid]; Praxis's Froxels at 127 in light-buffer order, counting the rest (`froxel.wgsl`, `FroxelLightsOverflowed`). GI-1.0 alone orders: each cell holds "a fixed-size list of the most important lights", by each light's radiance at the cell's eight corners interpolated over its volume [ref:boisse-2023-gi-1.0-light-grid]. Persson sizes the packed list from a worst case doubled and asserts at runtime [ref:persson-2015-world-space-tiles-and-list-memory]. What a dropped light costs: in light order, a lamp vanishes or appears when another's index changes, which on `town` follows only the light buffer's cut, farthest from the camera first (`prepare.rs`); by contribution at the cell, the dropped lights are the weakest there, and the set changes only when a light moves or toggles, but neighbouring cells can keep different sets, a seam the Probes' interpolation blurs. No source measures either. On `town` the question does not arise: no cell of 1 to 8 m is reached by more than 4 lamps.

## A hit's cap

**4. Every listed light, up to a cap.** ADR 0020's clause: a shadow ray to each light of the hit's cell that reaches the hit, up to a cap that Frame stats count. It is the exact unshadowed sum with a hard shadow per lamp, deterministic, so with a fixed ray set the lamps add nothing to frame-to-frame variance. Its rays are the lamps that reach a hit: 0.94 to 1.35 on `town`, 1.04 by B21's uniform density. Lumen traces this form, but only for the shadows its shadow maps cannot resolve [ref:wright-2022-lumen-surface-cache-direct-lighting].

**5. The strongest K.** Rank the lamps reaching the hit by unshadowed contribution and trace the first K. On `town`'s ground the strongest lamp carries 87 to 88% of the lamps' unshadowed illuminance and the strongest two 98% (arithmetic), so K = 1 drops 12 to 13% of lamp light, as a bias concentrated where two lamps overlap, and K = 2 under 2%. It stays class (a) and deterministic; the ranking costs a sort of at most the cell's list at the hit. It fits ADR 0020's "up to a cap", if the cap counter counts what the order drops.

**6. One light by resampled importance sampling.** Draw candidates from the list, keep one in proportion to its unshadowed contribution, and trace one ray, weighting the result by the candidates' sum over the kept one's target. GIBS does it at its surfels' secondary hits and taps a shadow map when the light has one [ref:apers-2024-gibs-one-shadow-ray]; GI-1.0 traces "a single shadow ray after the resampling", accepting darkening bias [ref:boisse-2023-gi-1.0-light-grid]; Bevy Solari's world cache resamples candidates from one of 128 tiles of 1,024 presampled lights and traces one visibility ray [ref:bevy-0.20.1-solari-world-cache-light-sampling], the tiles of Wyman and Panteleev, which cut candidate generation from 18.3 to 0.8 ms on an RTX 3090 in their Amusement Park scene [ref:wyman-panteleev-2021-presampled-light-tiles]; its world cache updates 40,000 cells in 0.47 ms on a GPU it does not name [ref:jms55-2026-solari-0.19-world-cache]. Stochastic light culling restricts shadow rays to one by the same resampling [ref:tokuyoshi-harada-2016-stochastic-light-culling]. ReSTIR [ref:bitterli-2020-restir] and MegaLights [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-visible-light-list] reuse samples across screen pixels and frames, class (c). In a Probe, with no reuse beyond its hysteresis, the estimator is class (b). Its rays are the share of hits any lamp reaches, 0.63 to 0.80 on `town`. Its noise, by arithmetic: with the target equal to each lamp's unshadowed contribution, a hit whose reaching lamps are all visible, or all hidden, gets the exact answer; where a share v of the unshadowed light is visible, one sample's relative standard deviation is √((1 − v)/v), 1 at v = ½. A Probe blends each update into its value with hysteresis α, DDGI's α between 0.85 and 0.98 [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-hysteresis], and an update with variance σ² leaves σ²(1 − α)/(1 + α) at steady state: a standard deviation 0.29σ at α = 0.85 and 0.10σ at α = 0.98. Whether that stays within one 8-bit step of the median over 64 frames, #1126's test, depends on the lamps' share of a pixel's indirect light, which no figure here gives; DDGI's thresholds lower α after a change [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-hysteresis-thresholds], which raises the noise during it. It amends ADR 0020's "through a shadow ray of its own" and the spec's "each through a shadow ray up to a cap".

**7. Shadow maps at the hit.** Metro gives each of its lights a shadow map "to provide accurate secondary occlusion" [ref:4a-2021-metro-exodus-enhanced-light-clusters]; DDGI's paper shades hits with the same direct pass as pixels, a deferred renderer with variance shadow maps [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-hit-shading]. No lamp rays, but ADR 0020 drops shadow maps, and `docs/research/shadow-ray-cost.md` (technique 13) prices maps for `town`'s 1,024 lamps at 805 MB unpaged.

**8. Lights left out.** On Xbox Series S, Snowdrop's ray-traced GI lighting "doesn’t use any point lights" [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-hit-lights]. The spec already gives a light a flag that keeps it out; leaving every lamp out narrows ADR 0020's "every light bounces", which #1122 brings to the owner if the lamps cannot hold the 2 ms.

**9. The list read outside the ray-query kernel.** Lumen's direct-lighting pass samples shadow maps and builds "a compacted list of shadow rays, which couldn’t be resolved by shadow maps", traced afterwards [ref:wright-2022-lumen-surface-cache-direct-lighting]. In Praxis the lookup and the walk over a cell's list would move out of the kernel that traces probe rays: one pass traces the probe rays and stores the hits, another reads each hit's cell and writes the lamp rays, a third traces them. #1108 measured the hazard this avoids: a grid read added to `build_shadow_mask` cost 361 µs of `town`'s pass whether or not it skipped a ray, and #1107 378 µs for code never taken, the occupancy cost of any code in a ray-query kernel. Unreal's source is under Epic's EULA [ref:ue-eula-2026-11-engine-code] and was not read. The cost is a pass, about 22 heap allocations a frame each (`docs/audit/m5.5/scalability.md`, M18), and a buffer of hits.

## Shipped practice at probe hits

- **DDGI, RTXGI.** The paper's hits are shaded by the routine that shades pixels, direct light from shadow maps [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-hit-shading]; how RTXGI's integrations find local lights is not in the sources read.
- **Assassin's Creed Shadows.** A probe's hits are relit from a stored G-buffer: the sun's shadow from a shadow map around the camera, else a ray, and "the direct lighting from all lights" in a lighting pass [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-hit-lighting], 0.49 ms of its 1.24 ms on a PS5 [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-timings]. How local lights are found and shadowed at a hit the deck does not say.
- **Snowdrop.** The sun from the cascade shadow map, else a ray; point lights through the light BVH; their shadows not stated [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-hit-lights].
- **id Tech 8.** Cache hits read the Cascaded Light Grid and run the raster light loop [ref:sousa-2025-idtech8-cascaded-light-grid]; whether that loop shadows local lights at hits the deck does not say.
- **GIBS.** One light by reservoir sampling, a shadow-map tap where the light has one, else one ray [ref:apers-2024-gibs-one-shadow-ray]; its probes' hit lighting is not described apart.
- **Lumen's surface cache.** First 8 lights a tile, shadow maps first, compacted rays for the rest [ref:wright-2022-lumen-surface-cache-direct-lighting]; Unreal 5.9's world cache, filled by paths from the screen, says nothing of lights [ref:ue-5.9-preview-lumen-world-cache].
- **Metro Exodus Enhanced.** World-space clusters of lights in range, a shadow map each [ref:4a-2021-metro-exodus-enhanced-light-clusters].
- **Godot.** SDFGI: a list per cascade, 128 dynamic lights, each traced through the distance field; VoxelGI: 32 lights [ref:godot-4.7.2-gi-light-limits]. Godot is MIT [ref:godot-4.7.2-licence].
- **Bevy Solari.** One resampled light from presampled tiles, one visibility ray [ref:bevy-0.20.1-solari-world-cache-light-sampling]; MIT or Apache-2.0 [ref:bevy-0.20.1-licence].
- **Cyberpunk 2077 and RTXDI.** RTXDI's ReGIR fills world-space cells by resampling and leaves shadowing to the caller [ref:rtxdi-fdbd9b3-regir-world-space-grid]; RTXDI is under NVIDIA's RTX SDK licence [ref:nvidia-rtx-sdks-licence]. What Cyberpunk does at secondary hits is not in the sources read.

## What it costs on `town`

**Lamps reaching a point and a cell.** Arithmetic from `src/scenes/town.rs`: 1,024 lamps at the lowest-ranked of 1,600 block corners, each light 4.85 m up and 1.2 m over the street, range 18 m, replicated by a script kept outside the tree with the kit of #1143. Points on a 1 m lattice at the height given; cells from the ground to 16 m, the Cascade's height, a lamp listed where its sphere meets the cell's box.

**Rays and the 2 ms.** Arithmetic, from a probe ray at 1.50 ns with its sun ray (2 ms over 1,331,000), 60.9% of probe rays hitting (`docs/audit/m5.5/probe-rays/prototype-resolution.md`), and a lamp ray at the 1.84 ns that `docs/research/shadow-ray-cost.md` measured in the shadow mask pass, coherent pixel rays; a probe hit's lamp ray is incoherent and unmeasured.

**Memory.** A cell of capacity K is (K + 1) × 4 bytes as the Froxels lay it out. Today's one Cascade, 32 × 16 × 32 cells of 1 m: 589,824 bytes at K = 8 (0.4% of the `probes` kind's 134,217,728), 8,388,608 at K = 127. Four such Cascades of 1, 2, 4 and 8 m cells: 2,359,296 bytes at K = 8 (1.8%), 17,039,360 at id Tech 8's 64 (12.7%), 33,554,432 at 127 (25%). Arithmetic; the Cascades beyond the first are not decided.

## Fit to Praxis

- **ADR 0013.** Nothing in this note was measured. No RTX 2070 SUPER figure was taken; its `town` Baselines come from another engine source, so no ratio is quoted, and no cost here rejects a technique.
- **ADR 0008.** Every option keeps its history in the Probes, in world space; MegaLights's and ReSTIR's screen-space reuse stays excluded.
- **ADR 0020.** Techniques 4, 5 and 9 fit as written; 6 amends its ray per light; 7 its direct-light clause and its Consequence on shadow maps; 8 its "every light bounces"; 2 the spec's world-space cells.
- **`town`'s Load radius is 1,024 m** (`src/scenes/mod.rs`), not the 256 m of `world`, so on `town` the grid's bound is the Cascades, not the Load radius.

## Options

None is chosen. Each gives the rays a hit traces on `town` and the share of the 2 ms the lamps take, the rest left to probe rays, both by arithmetic, the lamp ray at the shadow mask pass's 1.84 ns. Each grid option also pays its fill, which the spec does not place inside or outside the 2 ms: about 2 ms over 65,536 cells tested against every lamp, 0.49 ms over today's one Cascade, less after a coarse cull (arithmetic, technique 1).

- **L1. Cells over every Cascade, every reaching lamp up to a cap (1 and 4).** The spec as written. 0.94 to 1.35 rays a hit, 41 to 50% of the 2 ms; at 1,331,000 probe rays the update takes 3.4 to 4.0 ms. A capacity and a cap of 8 never fill on `town`, and 65,536 cells take 2.4 MB. Amends nothing; it is the case #1122 brings to the owner if the lamps break the 2 ms.
- **L2. As L1, the strongest K (5).** K = 1: 0.63 to 0.80 rays, 32 to 37%, 12 to 13% of lamp light dropped; K = 2: 0.86 to 1.20 rays, 39 to 47%, under 2% dropped. Within ADR 0020's "up to a cap" if the counter counts what the order drops; the spec's *A hit's shading* would name the order.
- **L3. As L1, one lamp by resampled importance sampling (6).** 0.63 to 0.80 rays, 32 to 37%, unbiased, adds noise #1126 must absorb. Amends ADR 0020's "through a shadow ray of its own" and the spec's "each through a shadow ray up to a cap".
- **L4. Any of L1 to L3, with the list read in a pass of its own (9).** The same rays; removes the lookup from the ray-query kernel, at a pass and a hit buffer; the 361 µs it would avoid is the shadow mask pass's, unmeasured in the Probe update. No clause amended.
- **L5. A light BVH in place of cells (2).** Rays as L1 to L3 by the cap chosen, 0.63 to 1.35 a hit, 32 to 50%; no capacity to overflow and no grid to fill; a traversal with a stack in the kernel. Amends the spec's *The Light grid*, "World-space cells over the Cascades".
- **L6. No lamp rays at hits (7 or 8).** 0 rays, 0%. Shadow maps amend ADR 0020's direct-light clause and its Consequence; leaving lamps out narrows "every light bounces".

## Not verified

1. Talbot et al.'s resampled importance sampling (2005) was not opened; it is cited through GI-1.0 and stochastic light culling.
2. Lin and Yuksel's real-time stochastic lightcuts (2020), Boissé's world-space ReSTIR (2021) and path-space filtering (Binder et al. 2019) were not opened.
3. Unreal's ray-tracing light grid and its count a cell: no Epic documentation stating them was found; a third-party page quotes engine source, which was not read.
4. ReGIR's own chapter (Ray Tracing Gems II) and its cell layout were not read; it is cited through RTXDI's documentation.
5. How Assassin's Creed Shadows, id Tech 8 and Snowdrop shadow local lights at probe hits, and how RTXGI's integrations and Cyberpunk 2077 find them: not in the sources read.
