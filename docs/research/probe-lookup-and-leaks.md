# Probe lookup and leak control: state of the art

Researched 2026-11-28 for ticket #1142 (parent #1102, map #1101), before #1112 builds the lookup. It surveys what published and shipped work does when a shaded point reads a field of Probes: how it weights the eight around it, which it ignores, how it keeps a wall's light on its own side without a line at a doorway, and how it blends Cascades that nest and move, under ADR 0020 as amended on 2026-11-27 and the spec (#1102). The GI survey (`docs/research/global-illumination-and-lighting.md`) chose the family; this note compares the choices left inside it, for #1112, #1116, #1117, #1118, #1120 and #1121, and ends in options without choosing one.

**Summary.** Every shipped lookup read here that holds depth moments follows DDGI: a trilinear weight, a weight on the normal, a Chebyshev test on the depth moments, a sample point moved off the surface, and small weights crushed, multiplied together. They differ in constants and in what replaces the plain normal weight. None stops light through a wall thinner than the Probe spacing; what shipped against that is a classification of Probes and shaded points, and the only blend at an opening found is a value authored per surface.

- **The weights.** The normal weight removes Probes behind the surface and, as a plain dot product, can remove all eight, which Assassin's Creed Shadows and Snowdrop answer with a wrap weight. The Chebyshev test removes Probes that cannot see the point and leaks at the surface, where the depth estimate's variance is highest, so the point is moved along the normal and toward the camera: since DDGI 2021 by one bias, 0.2 of the normal plus 0.8 of the view direction, times 0.75 of the spacing, times 0.3. Too large a bias leaks, and a view bias moves with the camera. Crushing weights below 0.2 removes the faint light the test lets through and darkens what a weak but correct Probe would light.
- **Probes that should not count.** A Probe whose own rays meet mostly back faces is inside geometry. DDGI 2021, NVIDIA's SDK and Assassin's Creed Shadows turn it off, and also move Probes off surfaces (the first two by at most half a cell and 45% of one), which Snowdrop tried and dropped; Unity fills a culled Probe from its neighbours in a bake, Ghost of Yōtei at runtime from a list baked beside it. An off Probe's weight goes to the rest of the cage: beside a wall, the open side's Probes, or, past a wall thinner than the spacing, the far side's.
- **Leaks the test lets through.** NVIDIA's SDK asks for walls as thick as the Probe density; Assassin's Creed Shadows says "Probe interpolation always leaks"; Snowdrop, Overwatch 2 and Unity say the same. The shipped answers classify Probes and points: Assassin's Creed Shadows and Ghost of Yōtei in two states, The Division and Overwatch 2 by a stencil per volume, Unity by up to four masks. Ghost of Tsushima and Ghost of Yōtei blend at transitions (Ghost of Yōtei names doors, windows and cave mouths) by a fraction w authored on each surface there, weighting interior Probes by w and exterior ones by 1 − w; Ghost of Yōtei's interior volume geometry, which needs no surface authoring, is "pixel perfect, with no blending". No system read gives each interior an identity and blends at openings from the volumes alone, which is what ADR 0020 decided.
- **Between Cascades, and while they move.** DDGI 2021 fades a finer grid over its last cell, reads the finest first and stops when the weights reach 1; Godot fades over two cells and fades its last cascade to the ambient. A camera-centred grid that steps can pop, which DDGI 2021 answers by tightening the fade region by a cell and centring it on the camera. Scrolling Probes keep their place in the world in NVIDIA's SDK, Assassin's Creed Shadows and DDGI 2021, as #1117 already decides.
- **Cost.** The published lookups are DDGI 2019's 0.5 ms on an RTX 2080 Ti and Overwatch 2's 330 µs on a GTX 1080, neither at a stated resolution; Overwatch 2 counts 16 texture samples. No lookup exists in Praxis (#1112 builds it), so nothing here was measured and no cost in this note rejects a technique: by ADR 0013 a rejection needs the M4 Pro's and the RTX 2070 SUPER's figures from one engine source, and this note has neither. What each option adds to the lit pass is given as counts, marked as arithmetic.

Sources: JCGT (2019, 2021), I3D 2017, GPU Gems 3 (2007), SIGGRAPH Advances in Real-Time Rendering (2021, 2025), GDC (2016, 2024, 2026) and REAC 2025 decks with their speaker notes, the documentation of NVIDIA's RTXGI-DDGI SDK at commit f33e496 and of Unity's HDRP 17.0.4, and Godot 4.7.2's `gi.glsl` at commit ed1daf0 (MIT). No source under a restrictive licence was read for code: of NVIDIA's SDK only its licence and `docs/` were opened, of Unity only its manual. Each claim relied on is in `docs/references.toml`, cited by key; every quote of an entry this note adds was matched by script against a saved copy of its source on 2026-11-28.

Boundaries, ruled out by the ticket: what a Probe holds (ADR 0020 and the spec); a per-pixel gather or anything that keeps screen-space history (ADR 0008), which also rules out Assassin's Creed Shadows' inverse-ray offset as it ships, at the hits of a per-pixel gather; two-state Interior volumes and a rule on a wall's thickness (map #1101, *Decisions so far* item 3); glossy reflections; choosing. Placement other than camera-centred grids (octrees, tetrahedral meshes) is outside ADR 0020's form and appears only where its blending carries over.

Terms: the **cage** is the eight Probes of a Cascade around a shaded point; the **sample point** is the shaded point moved by any bias, where the cage is read; a point's **belonging** is ADR 0020's, one Interior volume or none. Each weight is computed every frame (class a) from Probes that converge over frames (class b), in `CONTEXT.md`'s Temporal classes.

## The weights

**W1, trilinear.** Each Probe of the cage is weighted by its distance to the sample point along each axis: DDGI 2019 folds its other weights into "a standard trilinear interpolation" [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-trilinear-blend], DDGI 2021 lists it first of three weights [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-probe-query], and Snowdrop and Godot write it as the product of one minus the offset along each axis [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-sampling] [ref:godot-4.7.2-sdfgi-probe-weights]. Alone it is the traditional irradiance grid, whose leaks McGuire et al. split in two: a Probe inside an object "leaks darkness around itself", and one in free space where the light changes quickly leaks light "through the wall" [ref:mcguire-2017-light-field-probes-visibility]. It is continuous only between cells of one size; past a face into a larger or smaller cell, "we'll be sampling different probes" (Ghost of Yōtei) [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-octree-blend], which is why nested Cascades need C1. Cost: a few dozen ALU operations (arithmetic).

**W2, the normal weight.** McGuire et al. weight each Probe by "a clamped dot product" of the normal and the direction to it [ref:mcguire-2017-light-field-probes-visibility]; DDGI 2019 uses "a soft threshold that falls off smoothly" toward a zero dot product [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-lookup-weights]. It removes light from Probes behind the surface; Ghost of Yōtei shows a wall brightening once the dark Probes behind it stop counting [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-octree-blend]. What it brings: "a pure dot product might rule out all the probes", so Assassin's Creed Shadows uses wrap shading [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-weights]; Snowdrop, for opaque surfaces, wraps the Probe's signed distance from the tangent plane in cell units as `saturate((d + 1.f) / 2.f)` and squares it [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-sampling]; Godot floors the dot product at 0.005 [ref:godot-4.7.2-sdfgi-probe-weights]. A wrap lets Probes behind the surface back in at a reduced weight, the leak the weight exists to stop, in exchange for never zeroing the cage. Cost: a normalisation and a dot product per Probe (arithmetic).

**W3, the Chebyshev test.** McGuire et al. store the mean and mean square of the distance to the nearest surface per direction and weight a Probe by a Chebyshev test on them, "spherical analogues of a variance shadow map test" [ref:mcguire-2017-light-field-probes-visibility]; DDGI 2019 keeps "mean- and variance-biased Chebyshev interpolants" [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-lookup-weights], and ADR 0020 adopts the form [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-probe-encoding]. It answers both kinds of leak. It brings its own: the variance of the estimate is highest "at the surface" (DDGI 2021) [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-self-shadow-bias]; Snowdrop needs walls thick enough "to not cause any leaks due to low precision in the Chebyshev inequality" [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-indoor-filtering]; Assassin's Creed Shadows finds it "not precise enough for thin wall, angled wall or corners" [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-interior-classification], and clamps each distance to three spacings before averaging the moments, which "help reduce leaks in some cases (interior corner)" (W3', an update-side choice for #1110) [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-weights]. Cost: a bilinear fetch per Probe from the depth moments in the direction from that Probe to the point, so an octahedral encoding per Probe (arithmetic); Overwatch 2 counts the whole lookup as "16 texture samples" [ref:pearce-2025-overwatch-ddgi-volumes].

**W4, moving the sample point.** DDGI 2019 offsets the point "based on the surface normal and the camera-view vector" with scene-tuned biases [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-lookup-weights]. DDGI 2021 replaces them with one self-shadow bias, `(n · 0.2 + ωo · 0.8) · 0.75 · minDistanceBetweenProbes · TunableShadowBias`, ωo toward the camera, the tunable 0.3 by default, and shows the price of overdoing it, "light leaking due to overly high self-shadow bias" [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-self-shadow-bias]. NVIDIA's SDK takes the normal and the camera direction (`DDGIGetSurfaceBias`) and warns that its view bias "is a world-space value, scene scale matters!" [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-shader-api] [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-walls-and-view-bias]. Unity offers a normal and a view bias in metres, and "the results of View Bias vary depending on the camera position" [ref:unity-hdrp-17-apv-sampling-options]; Godot uses the normal only [ref:godot-4.7.2-sdfgi-probe-weights]. A view term makes indirect light shift as the camera moves through a still scene: not history, so inside ADR 0008, but a change a still-camera flicker test cannot see. At a 1 m finest spacing DDGI 2021's default moves the point at most 0.225 m (arithmetic: 0.75 × 1 m × 0.3), to be set against the thickness of `house`'s thin walls. Cost: a few ALU operations and the camera position (arithmetic).

**W5, crushing small weights.** DDGI 2019 reduces "very low-irradiance values (i.e., less than 5% of the representable intensity range)" along a decreasing curve [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-lookup-weights]; Snowdrop and Assassin's Creed Shadows crush weights below 0.2 [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-weights], Snowdrop by multiplying such a weight by its square over the threshold's square [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-sampling]. The idea is Lauritzen's light-bleeding reduction for variance shadow maps: on a fully occluded surface a Chebyshev bound never reaches full intensity, so values under a threshold map to 0 and the rest are rescaled, which "can greatly reduce the light bleeding, at the cost of somewhat darkening the penumbra regions", and set too high "will decrease shadow detail" [ref:lauritzen-2007-vsm-light-bleeding]. In a lookup the darkened penumbra is a dim but correct Probe losing its share. DDGI 2019 also bounds each weight by "conservative epsilon tests" before normalising [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-lookup-weights]; with the crush, that decides what a cage of small weights returns. Cost: a compare and two multiplies per Probe, written as a select: on the M4 Pro a uniform `if` around the lit pass's first light cost the main pass 10 to 12% (`docs/performance.md`, #222).

**W6, a perceptual blend space.** DDGI 2021 stores irradiance under an exponent of 5.0, because light-to-dark changes lag most, and decodes each Probe read to a gamma of 2 before the trilinear sum [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-perceptual-encoding]. It buys faster-looking change and fewer fireflies and costs a `pow` per Probe read, 8 a Cascade (arithmetic); it changes what a Probe stores, which the spec fixes as light "stored at a fixed scale that fp16 holds" (*What a Probe holds*).

## Probes that should not count

**P0, back faces near and dark.** In DDGI 2021 a probe ray that hits a back face records zero irradiance and a depth shortened by 80%, so "the probe will see backface surfaces as shadowed and not light them"; a depth of zero would drive the Chebyshev weight to zero, to be renormalised upward, and skew Probes that see a few back faces through modelling faults [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-self-shadow-bias]. NVIDIA's SDK does the same and marks the hit for classification [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-shader-api]. It is the update's choice (#1110) but decides what a lookup beside a wall reads. Praxis's probe rays see every triangle opaque (ADR 0020); a hit's back face can come from the triangle it already fetches for its normal (spec, *A hit's shading*).

**P1, a Probe turned off by its own rays.** DDGI 2019 left the visibility test to ignore "probes that fall inside walls or other geometry" [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-probes-in-walls]. DDGI 2021 turns Probes left inside static geometry "off", never traced or updated, while those inside moving geometry "correctly turn on when appropriate" [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-probe-off-state], beside sleeping states that save rays [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-rays-against-hysteresis]. NVIDIA's SDK marks a Probe inactive when its share of back-face hits passes a threshold, judged from 32 fixed rays whose directions do not rotate and which stay out of the blend [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-relocation-classification]. Assassin's Creed Shadows disables a Probe it cannot move out of geometry or that lies deep under terrain [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-relocation]; Snowdrop marks such Probes invalid and divides its average volume by a validity volume, since black invalid Probes "might lead to leaks of dark lighting" [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-sampling]. Beside a wall, an off Probe's weight goes to the rest of the cage: right where they are on the open side, a leak where they include Probes past a thin wall that the test passes, and a fallback (L3) where all eight are off. #1116 builds it on #1109's state word; fixed directions, as NVIDIA's SDK uses, would sit within ADR 0020's fixed count of rays. Cost: 8 state loads, or none if the state rides in a channel a fetch already returns (arithmetic).

**P2, relocation.** DDGI 2021 moves each Probe "through the closest backface it can see" and off close front faces, five iterations, never more than half the minimum spacing, and not around moving geometry, as "a stable result is preferable to an unstable result with lower average error"; it removes a shadow leak in an acute corner and can make a cage cover a surface twice [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-probe-relocation]. NVIDIA's SDK caps the move at 45% of a cell [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-relocation-classification]. Assassin's Creed Shadows moves a Probe whose nearest depth is too small toward its largest depth, disabling it where that fails [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-relocation], in 0.11 ms for 1,024 Probes a frame on PlayStation 5 [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-timings]. Snowdrop's "didn't gave any significant benefit in Avatar so it was not used" [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-cascade-placement]; Unity's Virtual Offset does it in a bake and warns of "probe capture points pushing through neighboring geometry" [ref:unity-hdrp-17-apv-dilation-settings]. A moved Probe adds an offset per Probe to the lookup, off the grid #1117 places by Anchor and offset. #1116 rules it out unless disabling fails.

**P3, filling an off Probe.** Unity's Dilation "gives them data from valid Light Probes nearby", and a wider search reaches Probes "in different lighting conditions", "resulting in unwanted behaviors such as light leaks" [ref:unity-hdrp-17-apv-leak-fixes] [ref:unity-hdrp-17-apv-dilation-settings]. Ghost of Yōtei culls embedded Probes at bake time, records their neighbours, and at runtime blends the neighbours' light into the culled Probe, since empty positions cause "interpolation issues" [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-embedded-probes]. A full cage moves no weight across a wall by renormalisation, but a neighbour past the wall may stand in. Neither builds the list at runtime, as a scrolling Cascade would need.

## Leaks the test lets through

**Thin walls.** Every source that discusses it puts the limit at the spacing. NVIDIA's SDK wants walls with a thickness "proportional to the probe density" and a Probe every 2 to 3 m [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-walls-and-view-bias]; Unity's first fix is to "adjust walls so their width is closer to the distance between probes" [ref:unity-hdrp-17-apv-leak-fixes]; Overwatch 2 leaks "mainly due to probe spacing and thin geometry" [ref:pearce-2025-overwatch-ddgi-volumes]; and outdoor light is "many factors stronger than indoor", so lowering outdoor Probes' weights "does not compensate the difference" (Assassin's Creed Shadows) [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-weights]. M6's charting declined the rule on content these imply; the classifications below shipped beside it.

**L1, hard belonging.** Assassin's Creed Shadows classes each Probe, and each hit of its per-pixel rays at which it reads them, as interior or exterior against planes from its baked GI, "and then only use probes having the same classification"; its deck describes no blend at an opening [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-interior-classification]. Ghost of Yōtei marks each Probe by the surfaces it sees, "no blending", and a pixel by drawing watertight interior meshes into a stencil like shadow volumes [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-interior-blend], and finds "no good way to prevent light leaking between adjacent interior areas" [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-probes-and-interiors]. The Division chose interior or outdoor volume by stencil, with a "sharp transition between indoors and outdoors" [ref:stefanov-2016-division-probes]; Overwatch 2 draws nested volumes through a stencil so "each pixel will only receive full GI evaluation once" [ref:pearce-2025-overwatch-ddgi-volumes]. Unity gives each Probe one of at most four Rendering Layer Masks, has renderers read only matching Probes, suggests an Interior and an Exterior mask, and lets an object carry several to read both [ref:unity-hdrp-17-apv-leak-fixes]. ADR 0020's identity per volume is the many-state form, which none ships; Unity's four masks are nearest. It brings the edge at each opening (L2, #1121) and cages with no Probe of the point's belonging (L3). Cost: a compare per Probe against an identity read with its state (arithmetic).

**L2, a fraction authored per surface.** Ghost of Tsushima gives every surface an interior mask w from 0 to 1, default 0.5, by geometry property, shader parameter, vertex colour or deferred decal; the lookup multiplies interior Probes' weights by w and exterior ones' by 1 − w, renormalises, and falls back to the unmasked weights when all are zero [ref:patry-2021-got-interior-mask-blend] [ref:patry-2021-got-interior-mask]. Ghost of Yōtei keeps it: doors, windows and cave mouths "are marked up as a blend", and its volume geometry is "pixel perfect, with no blending", "so we still had to vertex paint windows and doors" [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-interior-blend]. It is the only blend at an opening found in shipped work. With identities it becomes a weight toward one volume and the belonging beyond the opening; carried per vertex or per Material, it amends ADR 0020's belonging from "oriented boxes under one identity" and the spec's "Chunk Blobs do not change" (*Interior volumes*). Cost: an interpolant or a Material read, and a multiply per Probe (arithmetic).

**L3, a cage with no Probe of the belonging.** Ghost of Yōtei found surfaces "falling back to sampling exterior probes, causing light leaking" where an octree node held no interior Probe, and moves the sample point into a neighbouring node that has some [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-octree-blend]; Ghost of Tsushima's fallback to unmasked weights reads the other side [ref:patry-2021-got-interior-mask-blend]. DDGI 2019 asks for "at least one full cage of vertices in each room-like space" [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-probes-in-walls], which a room under two cells wide breaks. In `house` it is a fragment of room B whose cage lies in the shared wall or in room A; #1120 fixes that room A's Probes give it nothing, which leaves a coarser Cascade's Probes of the same belonging, a moved sample point, or sky light, #1120's choice.

**L4, a moving object's belonging.** Ghost of Yōtei blends the masks of the Probes a character can see into one value for the object, and blends that with last frame's "to help reduce popping as probes are occluded or disoccluded" [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-interior-blend]. Its history is per object, not per pixel, so not class (c). A Praxis fragment takes its belonging from the volumes whatever its Instance, so this matters only for an object straddling an opening, and it needs visibility per object, which a lit pass without ray queries lacks.

**Ray-side filters.** Snowdrop lists, as next steps, filtering Probes "between outdoor and indoor" and a portal BVH that rays starting indoors trace to learn whether they left the room [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-indoor-filtering]. A ray in the lit pass is out: ray-query code left in it cost 1,362 to 4,611 µs against 429 to 754 µs, even unexecuted (`docs/performance.md`; ADR 0019). ADR 0020 already brings light through an open door by the Probe update's rays.

**X1, finding a point's volume.** DDGI 2021 tests each pixel against every volume, a loop it calls "fast to evaluate" under 10 volumes, and lists rasterised bounds, a tree or tiles for more [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-volume-blending]. Overwatch 2, The Division and Ghost of Yōtei rasterise volumes into a stencil [ref:pearce-2025-overwatch-ddgi-volumes] [ref:stefanov-2016-division-probes] [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-interior-blend]; Praxis's depth target is `Depth32Float` (ADR 0019), with no stencil aspect. A point-in-box test is about 12 ALU operations a box (arithmetic); past a few volumes a list per cell, as Froxels list lights, whose buffers count against the Capability floor's storage buffers a stage, nine when this note was written and ten since 2026-11-28, which the shadow mask pass all binds.

## Between Cascades, and while they move

**C1, fading between nested grids.** DDGI 2021 fades a grid from 1 to 0 over its last cell, "starting at the second-to-last plane of probes", reads grids "in density order" and skips the rest once the weights reach 1; nested axis-aligned grids need no search [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-volume-blending]. NVIDIA's SDK's blend weight is 1 inside and falls toward 0 outside [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-shader-api]. Godot's SDFGI takes the first cascade holding the point, runs a smoothstep from 2.5 cells inside its edge to half a cell inside, measured from the camera along the longest axis, does a second full lookup in the next cascade there and mixes them, and at the last cascade fades to the ambient instead [ref:godot-4.7.2-sdfgi-cascade-blend], the form of ADR 0020's sky light past the last Cascade. Baked systems blend so at authored edges: Ghost of Tsushima's tetrahedral meshes "blend at the boundary" [ref:patry-2021-got-interior-mask-blend], Ghost of Yōtei fades its octrees to the grid [ref:wohllaib-2026-yotei-octree-blend], Overwatch 2 blends a child volume's last ring toward its parent at capture [ref:pearce-2025-overwatch-ddgi-volumes]. A fade brings a band of two lookups, 32 fetches instead of 16; a band a cell deep on every face of today's 32 × 16 × 32 Cascade holds 23% of its cells (arithmetic: 1 − (30 × 14 × 30)/(32 × 16 × 32)). Branching on the band diverges at its edge; doing both lookups everywhere doubles every fragment's fetches.

**C2, a fade region centred on the camera.** A camera-locked grid that steps a plane at a time "can cause popping in the GI": a point goes from the coarse grid to "heavily shaded by the camera cascade" in a frame. DDGI 2021 tightens the fade region by a cell on each axis and centres it on the camera, so "points that are newly within that volume will not immediately be shaded by it" [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-tracking-window]. The fade then moves smoothly with the camera while the Probes step, and the entering plane lies outside it. It costs a finest region a cell thinner on each side, and C1's fetches (arithmetic).

**C3, scrolling.** NVIDIA's SDK invalidates only "the planes of probes on the edges of the volume" as it scrolls, while "all interior probes remain stationary" [ref:nvidia-rtxgi-f33e496-scrolling]; DDGI 2021 uses a circular buffer whose planes "leapfrog" the camera [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-tracking-window]; Assassin's Creed Shadows follows the camera with a forward offset and toroidal addressing [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-relocation]; Snowdrop pulls each cascade forward along the view by a factor of its own and gives newly entered Probes the first share of its 1,024 a frame [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-cascade-placement] [ref:kuenlin-2024-snowdrop-probe-cascades]. DDGI 2021 can converge new Probes in a frame with many rays at hysteresis 0 [ref:majercik-2021-ddgi-rays-against-hysteresis], which ADR 0020's fixed count of rays a frame allows only as a priority within the count. #1117 fixes the scrolling; C2 keeps the entering plane out of the lookup until it has had updates.

## What it costs the lit pass

Published: DDGI 2019's "Sample irradiance probes for primary ray shading" took 0.5 ms of 2.6 ms, 32 × 8 × 32 Probes, on an RTX 2080 Ti, into a screen-resolution texture of unstated size [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-timings]. Overwatch 2's apply pass over baked Probes took about 330 µs at full resolution and 115 µs at half on a GTX 1080, pixel count unstated [ref:pearce-2025-overwatch-ddgi-apply]. Unity's "Quality" leak reduction samples "between 1 and 3 times" [ref:unity-hdrp-17-apv-sampling-options], untimed. None ran on the M4 Pro or the RTX 2070 SUPER, and none is scaled here.

Praxis has no lookup to measure; ADR 0020 gives its figure a line of its own beside ADR 0019's gate, from #1112's first measurement. A cost that rejects a technique needs the M4 Pro's and the RTX 2070 SUPER's figures from one engine source (ADR 0013), and this note has neither. The rest is arithmetic.

- **Per pixel, not per sample.** `fs_main` reads `sample_mask` and no `sample_index` (`src/render/shaders/main.wgsl:303`), so under 4x MSAA it runs once per pixel each triangle covers: at least 3,686,400 lookups a frame at 2560 × 1440.
- **One Cascade.** 8 bilinear irradiance fetches in the normal's direction and 8 depth-moment fetches each in its own Probe's direction, with nine octahedral encodings: 16 fetches, at least 59.0 million a frame. W2, W4 and W5 add ALU only.
- **State and belonging** (P1, L1): 8 loads, or none in a channel a fetch already returns. **A Cascade band** (C1, C2): 32 fetches and 16 loads in the band, or everywhere without a branch. **Finding the volume** (X1): about 12 ALU operations a box. **Opening blend**: an interpolant or Material read (L2), or a distance to a face (a band from the volumes).
- **What the M4 Pro is known to pay for.** Metal does not unroll a loop over a pixel's samples: a depth reduction took 656 µs looping over pixels and samples, 175 µs over samples and 150 µs with explicit loads (`docs/research/source-audit.md`, #240), so the eight Probes are best written out. A uniform `if` cost the main pass 10 to 12% (#222), so a Cascade or band chosen by `if` is to be measured against a select, the form #1112 already names.
- **Non-uniform control flow.** WGSL forbids `textureSample` there; `textureSampleLevel`, which a Probe fetch uses anyway, is allowed.

## Options (no recommendation)

- **A. DDGI 2021 as published.** W1, DDGI 2019's soft normal weight, W3, DDGI 2021's one bias (0.2, 0.8, 0.75 and 0.3 as starting values) and the 0.2 crush; P0 in the update (#1110) and P1 as #1116 states it; L1 as #1120 states it, L3's fallback chosen there; each opening blended over a band computed from the volumes' boxes (#1121); C1 with C2 on the camera-locked Cascades (#1117, #1118). Lookup: 16 fetches, 32 in a band, plus state. Amends nothing in ADR 0020 or the spec. The band at an opening has no shipped precedent: it carries C1's fade over to belongings.
- **B. The shipped cascade form.** As A, with Snowdrop's squared wrap weight, the depth clamped to three spacings before the moments (W3', #1110), and relocation within 45% of a cell where disabling fails (P2, #1116 as written). Lookup: A's, plus an offset per Probe where P2 runs, carried in #1117's placement. Adds a value per Probe beside the spec's "Interior volume's identity, or none, and a flag" (*What a Probe holds*).
- **C. Surface-masked openings.** A's weights and Cascades, with L2 at openings: a value on the surfaces there weighting the volume's Probes against those of the belonging beyond, renormalised, falling back as L3 decides (#1120, #1121). The only shipped form of the opening blend. Lookup: A's, plus an interpolant or Material read and a multiply per Probe. Amends ADR 0020's "each a set of oriented boxes under one identity, give every Probe and every shaded point a belonging" and the spec's "oriented boxes under one identity" and "Chunk Blobs do not change" (*Interior volumes*), unless the value stays in scene code at M6.
- **D. A full cage.** A, with P3: each off Probe filled from neighbours of its own belonging, built in the update as the Cascade scrolls, which no source read does at runtime; no weight then moves across a wall by renormalisation. Lookup: A's. Adds update work within the fixed count of rays, and amends #1116's "the lookup gives it no weight" (a ticket's text, not an ADR or spec clause).
- **E. Faster-looking change.** Any of A to D with W6 (#1110, #1112): 8 `pow` per Cascade read. Amends the spec's "Its light is stored at a fixed scale that fp16 holds" (*What a Probe holds*), whose scale ADR 0023 takes.

In each, the constants (bias, crush threshold, band width) are the sources' starting values, to be tuned on `house`.
