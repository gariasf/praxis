# Vegetation and grass: state of the art

Researched 2026-11-21 for ticket #949 (parent #944). It surveys grass, undergrowth and trees from the camera to the View distance for an engine that draws them at 4x MSAA with no screen-space history (ADR 0008), writes partial coverage as a `sample_mask` (ADR 0027) and traces every shadow and probe ray against a TLAS (ADR 0020). It ends in options and chooses none.

**Summary.** Shipped vegetation is shown under TAA or a temporal upscaler almost everywhere, and the swaying trees in shipped ray tracing are still in the acceleration structure. Neither is settled for Praxis by any source read.

- **Stability without history is unshown at 4x MSAA in motion.** The one source that shows foliage at 4x MSAA, TAA not mentioned, and calls it clean, Golus's, is still images: alpha sharpened to a pixel-wide edge and written as coverage. Raw coverage from alpha looks worse there than alpha test: it bands, and overlapping cards with equal alpha keep the same samples, which is what ADR 0027's fixed sample order does. The evidence in motion is old or second-hand: coverage from alpha flickers less than supersampled alpha test (Persson, 2005, a claim, sample count unstated); and Forza Horizon 5, Forward+ with MSAA, alpha-to-coverage on its vegetation and no TAA, was judged by two press tests to flicker, one naming its vegetation, the other comparing its 4x MSAA with TAA over the whole image (grade 2). A still figure at 8x MSAA (Wyman and McGuire, 2019) shows hashed coverage about like hashed alpha test under TAA, on distant hair. Guerrilla calls its grass with no anti-aliasing "noisy and flickery" and never tried MSAA. Stability in motion is unknown: a prototype's question.
- **Opaque geometry is the shipped way out of masks.** Ghost of Tsushima's grass is opaque Bézier blades made on the GPU each frame, about 83,000 drawn of a million candidates in about 2.5 ms (hardware not stated), thickened when seen edge-on; Fortnite's Nanite trees and grass dropped masked materials for opaque triangles, with the area of removed leaves grown back into those kept. Neither says how its images are anti-aliased.
- **Rays mostly see still vegetation, or none.** Assassin's Creed Shadows, Indiana Jones's ray-traced GI, Unreal by default and Unity's HDRP keep wind out of the acceleration structure, and cut small vegetation or grass from it. Their cheap leaf stand-ins (triangles shrunk by average opacity and forced opaque, triangles dropped, a dithered reject) are shipped for GI rays; Epic states that shrinking or culling triangles "don't work well for direct shadows", re-traces alpha-masked hits with any-hit evaluation in MegaLights, and offers virtual shadow maps for the sun on "animated / alpha masked foliage". The one shipped shadow ray stated to cross swaying vegetation is Indiana Jones's path-traced mode, its vegetation BLASes updated and never rebuilt, behind DLSS Ray Reconstruction; Alan Wake 2 skins its vegetation for ray tracing, which rays unstated.
- **Swaying shadows in rays are 2026 research.** A plant as rigid parts moved as TLAS instances (NVIDIA, HPG 2026) and a plant as a cage of tetrahedra each its own instance (AMD, HPG 2026) avoid any BLAS refit; both pay in TLAS size and build, 9.66 of 12.43 ms at 1080p on an RX 9070 XT for the second. Per-plant refit is priced by AMD's blog at over 300 ms and up to 80 GB for 25,000 plants. Praxis's dynamic TLAS is capped at 10,000 Moving Instances, which `town`'s pigeons already fill.
- **Scale and placement shipped on the GPU at runtime**, from painted and baked maps: Horizon Zero Dawn places all its nature that way, over 100,000 objects for about 250 µs of GPU (hardware not named on the slide); Unreal 5.8 claims its runtime scatter level with its GPU grass. Whether grass made on the GPU each frame from a cooked map sits inside ADR 0018 is the owner's reading. Cook-time rules (Far Cry 5's Houdini biome recipes, Snowdrop's parent-child scatter) feed the proposed *Placement rules* line.
- **Distance.** SpeedTree shrinks a share of leaf cards away while the rest grow, and hands a tree to its billboard through alpha-to-coverage; octahedral impostors (Fortnite) are the next form. ADR 0026's Far proxy carries no alpha and no Thin Primitive, so a forest does not pass through its cook as it stands.
- **Open engines.** Wicked Engine (MIT), the nearest to ADRs 0019, 0020 and 0027, writes coverage from alpha as an undithered sample mask under MSAA, refits its grass BLAS every frame and forces probe rays opaque. Only inkwell-webgpu-grass publishes a cost, about 9.1 ms of GPU for 234,000 blades on an unnamed Apple GPU.

Sources: GDC (2017 to 2026), SIGGRAPH Advances in Real-Time Rendering (2017 to 2025), the Rendering Engine Architecture Conference (2024, 2025), HPG 2016 to 2026, I3D 2010 to 2018, JCGT, GPU Gems 3; studio and vendor posts; the documentation of Unreal Engine 5.9, Unity 6 and HDRP 17, Godot 4, the SpeedTree SDK, Filament, Metal, Vulkan and Direct3D 12; two German press tests (grade 2). Engine source read where `docs/research/open-engines-register.md` permits, at its commits; no Unreal, Unity or Flax source, and nothing of Spartan Engine past `cb258a1`. Each claim relied on is in `docs/references.toml`; every quote there was matched against a saved copy of its source on 2026-11-21. Nothing was measured in this repo.

Boundaries: terrain itself is #948's and tree and plant sources are `docs/research/world-content-sources.md`'s; strand hair is cited only where it is thin geometry. Terms: the classes are `CONTEXT.md`'s Temporal classes. *Coverage from alpha* is a fragment's alpha turned into the samples it keeps, by alpha-to-coverage or a written mask; *sharpened* coverage rescales alpha by its screen-space derivative first, so the partial band is a pixel wide.

## Coverage of thin geometry without history

**1. Alpha test with cooked coverage.** Averaged alpha mips pass a fixed threshold less often, so leaves thin with distance; Castaño rescales each mip so the share passing matches mip 0, at cook time, and notes it holds for alpha-to-coverage as well [ref:castano-2010-alpha-coverage-mips]. Horizon Zero Dawn ships the same rescale and tests alpha once, in a depth-only pass, shading at depth equal with "Zero percent Overdraw" [ref:sanders-2018-hzd-depth-prepass-alpha], the order ADR 0027's twins already have; Unreal documents the same mask-only early pass [ref:unreal-mask-only-in-early-z]. The criticism: coverage-preserving mips "dilate" thin leaves [ref:wyman-2019-coverage-preserving-mips-dilate], and a 2025 comparison made without TAA finds they still lose thin stems [ref:lisyarus-2025-alpha-mips-without-taa]. Horizon's 256×128 grass textures "Need really good Anti-Aliasing", a two-frame TAA under FXAA [ref:sanders-2018-hzd-needs-good-aa]; Guerrilla shows that grass with no anti-aliasing as "noisy and flickery" and with SMAA as "still quite noisy", and says multisampling was not tried for cost on PS4, not judged [ref:decarpentier-2017-decima-grass-flicker-without-taa] [ref:decarpentier-2017-decima-taa-for-thin-alpha-tested]. At 4x MSAA a plain threshold is "shader aliasing which generally MSAA can't help with" [ref:golus-2017-msaa-alone-does-not-fix-alpha-test], as Epic, Godot and Unity also document [ref:unreal-msaa-does-not-touch-materials] [ref:godot-msaa-alpha-scissor] [ref:unity-alpha-to-mask].

**2. Raw coverage from alpha.** Alpha straight to coverage is Godot's, Unity's and Filament's answer under MSAA [ref:godot-msaa-alpha-scissor] [ref:unity-alpha-to-mask] [ref:filament-masked-enables-a2c], and Papavasiliou's 8x MSAA grass uses it [ref:papavasiliou-2015-msaa-alpha-to-coverage]; Meta, recommending 4x MSAA, tells developers to prefer it over cutout [ref:meta-a2c-over-cutout]. Golus judges it at 4x MSAA worse than alpha test, banded and "simultaneously fuzzy and aliased", and "Two or more overlapping triangles with 50% Alpha to Coverage results in no change in opacity" [ref:golus-2017-raw-a2c-fails]. Wyman and McGuire name the same layer correlation: leaves "appear as a single layer and overly transparent" [ref:wyman-2017-a2c-layer-correlation]; Yuksel: "two fragments on a pixel with the same coverage pattern perfectly occlude each other" [ref:yuksel-2018-sample-mask-texture]. ADR 0027's mask is this form when cut from alpha: the samples a fragment keeps follow from its count alone, the same in every fragment of a pixel. That serves a Thin Primitive, whose faces must share samples; a canopy of crossing cards it thins. In motion, Persson's 2005 demo judges coverage from alpha to flicker less than supersampled alpha test under minification [ref:persson-2005-alpha-to-coverage-demo]. The nearest shipped case to Praxis is Forza Horizon 5, Forward+ with MSAA and an alpha-to-coverage filter for fine vegetation and no TAA until 2022: one press test found residual flicker of vegetation that FXAA only dampens and for which TAA "would be really effective" (our translation), another found the whole image under its TAA at 1080p calmer than under its 4x MSAA at 2160p (grade 2) [ref:pcgh-2021-forza-horizon-5-vegetation-flicker] [ref:computerbase-2022-forza-horizon-5-msaa-flicker].

**3. Sharpened coverage.** Golus rescales alpha by its derivative so the partial band is "the width of a single pixel", and scales alpha up with mip level to keep density, calling the result anti-aliased at 4x MSAA with no TAA [ref:golus-2017-sharpened-a2c] [ref:golus-2017-mip-alpha-scale]; Persson's demo already boosts contrast under magnification [ref:persson-2005-alpha-to-coverage-demo]. Coverage then anti-aliases the cut edge only, so the interior overlap of row 2 no longer arises. Godot's Alpha Edge modes give coverage a sharp edge with a tunable threshold, and warn the look can change as the camera nears [ref:godot-alpha-edge-modes]; whether they rescale by the derivative was not read. Golus does not say TAA is off; his images are stills. Whether a forest of it holds steady in a moving camera at five levels a pixel, no source shows.

**4. Hashed and distributed coverage.** Hashed alpha replaces the threshold with a hash anchored to the surface; its claim is "without introducing more temporal flicker than traditional alpha testing", measured at one sample under sub-pixel motion, with noisy edges near the camera, for 0.1 to 0.3 ms at 1920×1080 on a GTX 1080 [ref:wyman-2017-hashed-alpha-stability-claim] [ref:wyman-2017-hashed-alpha-subpixel-motion] [ref:wyman-2017-hashed-alpha-cost]. The 2019 paper's still figure of distant hair shows hashed coverage at 8x MSAA alone "largely similar" to hashed alpha with TAA [ref:wyman-2019-msaa-a2c-matches-taa], and that eight TAA samples do not converge it [ref:wyman-2019-hashed-alpha-taa-insufficient]. Yuksel distributes alpha offline so a plain test keeps the right coverage, and for MSAA cooks a per-texel sample mask the shader writes [ref:yuksel-2018-alpha-distribution-no-shader-change] [ref:yuksel-2018-sample-mask-texture], with a pattern that repeats when tiled and fails in close-up [ref:yuksel-2018-alpha-distribution-limits]. Stochastic transparency found 8 samples too few without alpha correction and a seed by depth that "causes the noise pattern of moving objects to change completely each frame" [ref:enderton-2010-stochastic-transparency-noise]. A texel-anchored mask needs no state across frames, but it chooses different samples for fragments of one pixel, which ADR 0027 rules out today.

**5. Opaque geometry blades.** Sucker Punch dropped cards because wind moved whole cards and overdraw limited density [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-cards-rejected]. Per render tile a compute pass places blades from a jittered grid and maps of one texel per ~39 cm, culls them, and draws cubic Bézier blades of 15 or 7 vertices; the far tile is twice the size with three blades in four dropped [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-compute-per-tile] [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-lod-by-tile], a coupling its author names as a limit [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-lod-limit]. About 83,000 of a million candidates are drawn in about 2.5 ms, hardware not stated [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-scale-and-cost]. Against aliasing it widens edge-on blades in view space and pulls far normals toward a clump normal [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-thin-blades-and-specular-aliasing]; the grass writes no velocity, being "a poor target for temporally accumulated effects" [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-no-velocity]. How the frame is resolved, the talk does not say; on PS4 Pro the game renders half its pixels a frame into a checkerboard [ref:patry-2021-got-checkerboard-resolution]. Jahrmann's blades, at 8x MSAA with no temporal filter, draw 43,128 of 397,881 in an 8.13 ms frame at 1024×768 on a GTX 780M, reshape a blade toward a quad as it narrows below a pixel, and report popping from their distance and orientation culls [ref:jahrmann-2017-msaa-8x-cost] [ref:jahrmann-2017-width-correction] [ref:jahrmann-2017-culling-as-anti-aliasing]. Thinning without a pop is Cook's rule: keep a fraction, scale survivors' area by its inverse, pull their colour toward the mean, and let a leaving element shrink rather than vanish [ref:cook-2007-area-preservation] [ref:cook-2007-contrast-and-smooth-animation]; AMD's mesh-shader grass widens survivors and scales the last blade's width by the fractional count [ref:faber-2024-gpuopen-mesh-shader-grass-lod].

**6. Opaque leaves and Nanite Foliage.** Fortnite found it "usually faster to avoid masked materials" for trees and grass under Nanite, and grows the area of removed triangles into those kept [ref:wihlidal-2023-nanite-foliage-opaque-geometry]. Nanite Foliage generalises it: no alpha cards, Assemblies that encode a tree's parts once and instance them inside one mesh, the parts merged into one mesh on platforms without Nanite, and voxels past a distance [ref:epic-2026-nanite-foliage-no-alpha-cards] [ref:epic-2026-nanite-assemblies-tree-as-instanced-parts] [ref:unreal-5.9-nanite-foliage-voxels]. Its anti-aliasing: Nanite supports neither forward rendering nor MSAA [ref:unreal-5.9-nanite-unsupported], the feature ships with "TSR Thin Geometry detection" [ref:unreal-5.7-nanite-foliage], and a voxel's normal is chosen per pixel at random, noise whose remover is not stated [ref:unreal-5.9-nanite-foliage-voxels]. `docs/research/unity-and-unreal-horizon.md` (C1) judges ADR 0019's trigger unmet; the content idea that survives is a tree as instanced parts, which discrete LOD can cook. Unreal 5.8's Procedural Vegetation Editor, Experimental, builds such meshes in the editor [ref:unreal-5.8-procedural-vegetation-editor]: an authoring tool, for the sources note's tool list.

**7. Thin parts at a pixel.** Persson clamps a wire to a pixel and fades by the width lost, and suggests the same for other thin geometry [ref:persson-2012-phone-wire-aa]; Frostbite widens strands to the pixel, finds them too thick, and finds that MSAA alone does not solve it [ref:frostbite-2019-strand-pixel-width]; MachineGames sums analytic coverage of sub-pixel hair segments in compute [ref:kulikov-2025-analytic-line-coverage]. Wyman and McGuire state that alpha methods do nothing for a card narrower than a sample [ref:wyman-2017-subpixel-billboards-not-solved]. The Thin Primitive is Persson's rule with the fade as a mask; a conifer needle or a stem is a tube; a blade is a ribbon whose drawn width also turns with its angle to the view, which Ghost of Tsushima answers by widening and the rule does not model.

**8. Continuous LOD and billboards.** SpeedTree morphs small branches to their centre line and shrinks a share of leaf cards away while the rest grow, from LOD data cooked per vertex, with no dither and no history, while its branch meshes still pop between LODs [ref:speedtree-2023-sdk-continuous-leaf-and-branch-lod], then fades the tree into its billboard through alpha-to-coverage [ref:speedtree-runtime-billboard-a2c]. Its forest advice: three to five models a species, interior leaves trimmed against overdraw, a short billboard distance [ref:speedtree-2023-sdk-forest-budget-advice]. Cook's paper is the origin of pruning with area kept [ref:cook-2007-area-preservation].

## Rays through vegetation

**9. Still and opaque.** Assassin's Creed Shadows' ray tracing scene has "no vertex animation support, so no skinning (no character) or moving vegetation", culls small vegetation and clutter, and rebuilds its TLAS each frame [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acshadows-bvh-no-vertex-animation], while every plant on screen moves with a simulated wind [ref:ubisoft-2025-acshadows-vegetation-animated-on-screen]. For diffuse GI its leaf cards are shrunk by average opacity and forced opaque, at 60% of the alpha-tested reference's cost on an unnamed NVIDIA GPU [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acshadows-alpha-test-opaque-approximation], after capping any-hit calls cost 85 to 90% [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acshadows-alpha-test-cost-ladder]; for sharp reflections the stand-in was "very visible" and real tests capped at 4 returned [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acshadows-specular-needs-alpha-test]. Indiana Jones's GI keeps vegetation static and drops whole triangles in proportion to transparent texels [ref:devahl-2025-indiana-gi-vegetation-static-no-alpha]; Snowdrop rejects hits by blue noise against a constant alpha per instance, "great for diffuse GI", and adds only static meshes by default [ref:koshlo-2024-snowdrop-rt-alpha-dither] [ref:koshlo-2024-snowdrop-rt-scene]. Unreal leaves vertex wind out of rays unless a component opts in, naming the artefact, self-intersection [ref:ue-docs-rt-wpo-ignored-by-default] [ref:epic-2026-hardware-ray-tracing-wpo-opt-in], and Unity's HDRP leaves terrain trees, detail meshes and wind out entirely [ref:unity-hdrp17-rt-no-vertex-animation-no-terrain-trees]. For direct shadows Epic says shrinking or culling triangles "don't work well for direct shadows" and re-traces hits that need the mask with any-hit evaluation [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-alpha-tricks-fail-for-direct-shadows], calls ray tracing "not practical" for some animated, alpha-masked foliage, and offers a fall-back to virtual shadow maps, in practice for the directional light [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-foliage-falls-back-to-shadow-maps], and the mismatch between drawn and traced geometry self-shadows, which a 20 cm screen trace from the origin hides [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-proxy-mismatch-screen-traces]. Every image in this row is denoised over frames.

**10. Alpha-tested canopy in rays.** With inline ray queries an alpha-tested triangle is a candidate the shader confirms after a fetch; Indiana Jones's path-traced mode does that in fragment shaders, because the engine is Forward+, and kept it off outside path tracing "for performance reasons" [ref:bavoil-2025-indiana-alpha-test-off-outside-path-tracing]. NVIDIA's account of why it is slow is divergence between rays of a group [ref:nvidia-2022-ada-whitepaper-omm]. Opacity micromaps resolve most texels in traversal; in the jungle scene they cut the inline sun-shadow pass 14%, 0.83 to 0.71 ms, rays at 1080p on an RTX 5080, against 55% for the any-hit pass (vendor) [ref:bavoil-2025-indiana-omm-cost], and an independent test at 1920×1080 found frame time between +2% and −29% on RTX 3080 and 4080, emulation sometimes slower [ref:waldemarson-doggett-2024-omm-independent-frame-times]. Vulkan frames the choice as tessellate, test mid-traversal, or micromap [ref:khronos-vk-opacity-micromap-ext-and-khr]. wgpu 30 exposes no micromap (`docs/research/wgpu-capability-matrix.md`) and Metal resolves cut-outs with an intersection function per candidate [ref:apple-wwdc22-metal-alpha-test-intersection-function]. A hard shadow through an alpha-tested canopy at 4x MSAA with no denoiser is shown by none.

**11. Wind by refit.** Indiana Jones's path-traced mode gives each dynamic vegetation model its own BLAS, updated and never rebuilt since wind returns to rest, about 1 GB of BLASes in the jungle, 606 MB after compaction, on an RTX 5080 (vendor) [ref:bavoil-2025-indiana-vegetation-blas-refit]. Remedy deforms vegetation once in compute for both raster and BVH, having dropped vertex-shader wobble that rays could not see [ref:remedy-2024-reac-deformed-geometry-shared-with-bvh] [ref:kandar-2024-alan-wake-2-gpu-skinned-vegetation], with a few skinned variants a plant model, instanced (grade 2) [ref:vanantwerpen-2026-nanite-voxels-and-alan-wake-in-rays]. Unreal's opt-in turns every instance into its own BLAS, off by default "because of its extreme cost", works within 120 m, and is budgeted at 10,000 to 30,000 updated triangles a frame, round-robin [ref:ue-docs-rt-instanced-wpo-extreme-cost] [ref:ue-docs-rt-wpo-ignored-by-default] [ref:ue-docs-rt-dynamic-blas-budget]. AMD's blog prices refits for 25,000 independent plants at "up to 80GBs" and "more than 300ms" on an RX 9070 XT [ref:gruen-2026-tetrahedral-cages-classic-blas-cost]; Epic calls many animated instances "currently impossible" to represent accurately [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-dynamic-geometry-unsolved].

**12. Wind as instances.** NVIDIA's HPG 2026 forest cuts each plant into rigid parts along its skeleton, moves them as TLAS instances by a stateless function of the wind, refits nothing, and stops animation past a cutoff [ref:vanantwerpen-2026-rigid-bones-no-blas-update] [ref:vanantwerpen-2026-animation-cutoff-and-far-field]; it holds 60 M plants in 3 M instances at 60 fps, 1440p upscaled to 2160p on an RTX 4090, path-traced at one sample and denoised [ref:vanantwerpen-2026-forest-path-tracing-cost], and relies on that denoiser to hide LOD pops [ref:vanantwerpen-2026-lod-pops-hidden-by-temporal-denoiser]. AMD's cages give each tetrahedron its own instance over static geometry and bend the ray into rest pose [ref:gruen-2026-tetrahedral-cages-method]: 584 M triangles as 2.8 M instances, 0.35 ms animation, 9.66 ms TLAS rebuild, 2.42 ms for a primary and a shadow ray at 1080p on an RX 9070 XT [ref:gruen-2026-tetrahedral-cages-cost]. Both pay where Praxis is tight: Moving Instances, capped at 10,000.

**13. Grass out, a contact term.** Epic advises skipping grass in rays for its traversal cost [ref:ue-docs-rt-grass-skip] and drops instanced meshes, its foliage path, from its 60 fps console scene [ref:ue-docs-rt-60fps-drops-instanced-meshes]. Ghost of Tsushima casts grass's shadow from the terrain raised to grass height with dithered depth, and blade detail from screen-space shadows [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-shadows]. Bend's screen-space sun shadow, 0.19 ms at 1440p on PS5 for 60 samples, is "temporally stable" with no dither or random sampling, though its disocclusion artefacts "can be quite significant", gives most distant tree shadows in its example, and was first used for the sun in Days Gone [ref:aldridge-2023-bend-screen-space-shadows-distant-trees]. id Tech 8 puts back what its BLASes miss with a screen-space term, denoised over frames [ref:sousa-2025-idtech8-blas-missing-detail-screen-space-occlusion]. Cook's area-preserving thinning also keeps the probability of shadow [ref:cook-2007-shadow-probability], an argument for a coarse caster. Horizon's casters are separate meshes: an animated alpha-tested low LOD to 8 m, an opaque still one to 80 m [ref:sanders-2018-hzd-shadow-casters].

## Wind and response

Every shipped tree wind read but Alan Wake 2's, whose rigs are animated in compute and skinned (11), is stateless vertex work on data cooked per vertex: Crysis's main and detail bending from painted channels [ref:sousa-2007-crysis-main-and-detail-bending], a shallow branch hierarchy from a wind field [ref:zioma-2007-stateless-branch-hierarchy-wind], SpeedTree's tiers, dropped to global or none "as soon as possible" with distance [ref:speedtree-2021-wind-cost-and-lod], and Ghost of Tsushima's sway on "pretty much every vert" including distant impostors [ref:rockenbeck-2021-got-tree-wind-in-vertex-shader], with grass pushed by scrolled 2D Perlin noise [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-wind]. Horizon simulates a wind field around the camera in about 150 µs of compute, hardware not stated [ref:sanders-2018-hzd-grass-lods-and-wind]. Unreal's Nanite Foliage moves bones instead, 100,000 in about 0.1 ms (vendor, hardware not stated), and stops animating in the distance for its shadow maps' sake [ref:epic-2026-nanite-skinning-wind-cost]; 5.9 adds a spring per bone with mass and area cooked at import [ref:epic-2026-dynamic-wind-per-bone-data]. A bone-driven tree is what row 12 needs: parts that move rigidly. Bend is the closest thing here, a rest Mesh curved by a curvature the fixed schedule sets, into a bent Mesh whose BLAS must be refitted.

Response to a character is a displacement buffer around the camera written by particles and read by grass and branch skeletons (Ghost of Tsushima) [ref:rockenbeck-2021-got-displacement-buffer]; Jahrmann's blades keep state across frames so trampled grass recovers [ref:jahrmann-2017-blade-model-and-collision]; Spartan presses tyre tracks into a field scrolled by whole texels [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-grass-tracks]. A buffer written by the fixed schedule and read by the GPU fits ADR 0022; state per blade does not fit a blade made anew each frame.

## Scale, placement and distance

**14. Placement on the GPU at runtime.** Horizon Zero Dawn places "all nature" at runtime from streamed 2D maps of about 4 MB a km², a density per layer discretised against a dither scaled to the asset's footprint, deterministic and locally stable, over 100,000 objects for about 250 µs of average GPU, hardware not named on the slide [ref:vanmuijden-2017-hzd-placement-method] [ref:vanmuijden-2017-hzd-placement-cost]. AMD's work graphs place 79,674 grass patches with flowers and insects in 0.39 ms and draw the teaser's 79,710 instances in 3.74 ms on an RX 7900 XTX [ref:kuth-2024-work-graphs-grass-cost]; trees generated each frame cost 3.13 ms at 1920×1080 on the same card, in a frame that includes TAA [ref:kuth-2025-gpu-tree-generation]. Unreal 5.8 claims runtime scatter "within the same performance range as the landscape GPU grass system" [ref:unreal-5.8-pcg-runtime-scatter]. Ghost of Yōtei culls over a million far trees, rocks and bushes to about sixty thousand items and credits its temporal upsampler for foliage stability [ref:suckerpunch-2025-yotei-far-trees-and-pssr].

**15. Placement by cook-time rules.** Far Cry 5 built biome recipes in Houdini that read terrain features [ref:carrier-2018-far-cry-5-biome-recipes]; Snowdrop scatters plants in a radius around a parent object, a first pass artists correct [ref:massive-2023-snowdrop-scatter-parent-child]; Unreal's procedural foliage simulates seeds competing by collision and shade over years, in the editor [ref:epic-2026-procedural-foliage-ecosystem-rules]. Kopf's recursive Wang tiles give blue noise that follows any density map, deterministic per region [ref:kopf-2006-recursive-wang-tiles-density]; `docs/research/stealthy-blue-noise.md` holds per-Chunk sets with ghost points across seams for this, at cook time, and it is not redone here.

**16. Impostors and far fields.** Octahedral impostors blend the three nearest of a grid of captured views; Fortnite's pines use 12×12 [ref:brucks-2018-octahedral-impostors], and its HLODs moved to impostors at 6.8 MB of textures a tree type [ref:brucks-2018-impostor-hlod-cost]. A Godot implementation under MIT recommends a 16×16 hemisphere for foliage, with depth and an alpha pre-pass [ref:godot-2020-octahedral-impostors-mit]; Torque3D captures views at load [ref:torque3d-9521d43-imposters]. Ghost of Tsushima's grass past blade range is the terrain wearing a texture per grass type, a view-dependent model having proved too expensive [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-far-field-texture]; NVIDIA's forest stamps small plants into the terrain and ends each plant in three orthogonal micromapped triangles [ref:vanantwerpen-2026-animation-cutoff-and-far-field], partial opacity baked as a dither [ref:vanantwerpen-2026-opacity-micromaps-instead-of-alpha-test]. Snowdrop draws trees far off as impostors with baked depth and traces that depth for sun shadows past its cascades [ref:koshlo-2024-snowdrop-rt-scene]; Unreal's rays do not support dithered LOD transitions [ref:epic-2026-hardware-ray-tracing-wpo-opt-in]. `docs/research/lod-hlod-and-far-field.md` (technique 9) holds the impostor survey; octahedral impostors left M5 for this milestone.

## Shading

Leaves need light through them: Unreal's Two Sided Foliage transmits by a subsurface colour, the default lit model leaving undersides "almost black" [ref:unreal-two-sided-foliage]; HDRP's Translucent type uses a thickness map with no screen-space blur [ref:unity-hdrp-translucent-leaf]; Godot moves a share of light to the far face [ref:godot-back-lighting]; Crysis approximated it per vertex [ref:sousa-2007-crysis-leaf-shading]. None of these needs history. Specular aliasing on blades is answered in geometry and material each frame: Ghost of Tsushima's clump normal [ref:wohllaib-2021-got-grass-thin-blades-and-specular-aliasing], Valve's geometric roughness from normal derivatives under MSAA [ref:vlachos-2015-geometric-specular-aliasing], projected-space NDF filtering, about 0.5 ms at 1920×1080 on Intel Iris Plus graphics, which "cannot" handle geometric discontinuities [ref:tokuyoshi-2021-projected-space-ndf-filtering] [ref:kaplanyan-2016-ndf-filtering-limits]. Epic and Godot both recommend TAA over MSAA for photoreal specular [ref:unreal-forward-msaa-versus-taa] [ref:godot-which-antialiasing], against Valve's 4x MSAA floor [ref:vlachos-2016-forward-because-msaa]. Under a canopy, Ghost of Tsushima's 12.5 m probe grid carries the colour of the leaves around a temple [ref:patry-2021-tsushima-probe-grid-sky-visibility]; Assassin's Creed Shadows' probes at 2 to 32 m spacing "always" leak, and its moving grass needed its own denoiser rules [ref:leblanc-2025-acshadows-probes-leak-and-vegetation-denoise] [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acshadows-grass-denoiser]. What a probe ray sees of a canopy is row 9's question for M6.

## What the open engines hold

The register's reading list, each at its pinned commit; class A is *adaptable with citation*, class E *evidence only* (#944, Notes). Nothing was built, run or copied.

- **Wicked Engine** (MIT, class A, source read). Grass is strands drawn as cards and simulated in compute [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-grass]. Under MSAA its depth pre-pass writes coverage from alpha as a sample mask, no alpha test and no dither; only with TAA does it dither alpha with animated blue noise [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-coverage-mask]. The mask keeps the first samples by count, ADR 0027's order. The grass BLAS is refitted each frame with a periodic rebuild and traced by GI rays, "excluded" if too costly [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-grass-blas-refit]; strands past the view distance collapse to zero area for rays, and drawn ones dither out over its last fifth [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-grass-far-collapsed]. Probe rays force every triangle opaque [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ddgi-force-opaque]. Its shadow-ray shader was not read. Its per-frame refit is what wgpu-core lacks [ref:wgpu-30-tlas-rebuild-only].
- **Spartan Engine** at `cb258a1` (MIT there, class A, read there only): blades made and culled in compute each frame in a ring of cells, hashed on world-space cells so a blade stays put whatever the camera's path, thinned by a keep probability, slotted by an atomic counter [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-grass] [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-grass-ring]; one wind texture for grass, flowers and trees [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-wind-field]. Deferred with TAA and no MSAA [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-rendering]; atomic slot order is what `CLAUDE.md`'s checklist rules out of a draw.
- **bevy_feronia** (MIT OR Apache-2.0, class A), WGSL on wgpu: wind after Ghost of Tsushima [ref:bevy-feronia-0.8.4-wind], LOD ranges faded by Bevy's per-pixel dither [ref:bevy-feronia-0.8.4-lod-dither], the fade M5 ruled out (ADR 0027), examples with TAA on [ref:bevy-feronia-0.8.4-taa] and wind noise kept from breaking motion vectors [ref:bevy-feronia-0.8.4-wind-noise].
- **inkwell-webgpu-grass** (MIT, class A): one opaque triangle a blade, tile culling and compaction in WGSL, no alpha test, no multisampling found [ref:webgpu-grass-9a14b51-readme]; about 234,000 blades in 9.11 to 9.16 ms of GPU at 1440×900 on an unnamed Apple GPU [ref:webgpu-grass-9a14b51-cost].
- **Torque3D** (MIT, class A): trees bent by vertex colour [ref:torque3d-9521d43-forest], views captured at load as the last detail level, faded by alpha [ref:torque3d-9521d43-imposters]. **Terrain3D** (MIT, class A): 32 m cells switching LOD a cell at a time, and a shadow impostor, the role of `CONTEXT.md`'s Shadow proxy [ref:terrain3d-1.1.0-instancer-lod-shadow]; no wind shader [ref:terrain3d-1.1.0-instancer]. **godot_voxel** (MIT, documentation read): instances per terrain LOD block, faded by distance in a shader [ref:godot-voxel-1.7-instancing]. **O3DE** (Apache-2.0 OR MIT): placement only, sectors filled around the camera [ref:o3de-2610.0-vegetation].
- **GarrettGunnell/Grass** (MIT): culled by vote, prefix sum and compaction, which keeps order [ref:acerola-grass-6de5844-cull], its readme asking not to be used as is [ref:acerola-grass-6de5844-readme]. **GodotGrass** (MIT): blades widened edge-on [ref:godot-grass-3785bec-readme]. **warbler_grass**, **bevy_procedural_grass** (MIT OR Apache-2.0): blades from a density map, Bézier blades with chunk culling [ref:warbler-grass-bdb4228-shader] [ref:bevy-procedural-grass-f5b5081-readme]. **HTerrain** (MIT): alpha-scissored quads fading with distance [ref:hterrain-49887ff-detail-shader]. ## Fit to Praxis

Read at `main` on 2026-11-21; nothing here was measured for this note.

- **Coverage (ADR 0027).** A masked leaf is a `sample_mask` cut from alpha in the depth passes through the twins: five levels, samples chosen by count alone, no dither. For crossing cards that order is technique 2's layer correlation; sharpened coverage (3) confines it to the edge pixel, varied samples (4) amend the ADR. In every pipeline the mask cost `town`'s GPU frame 0.73 ms with shadow rays on at 2560×1440 on the M4 Pro, following the share of indices drawn through the twins; a forest's indices are mostly masked.
- **Thin Primitive.** A needle or a stem is a tube, drawn a pixel wide with its true width as coverage; a blade is a ribbon whose drawn width turns with its angle, which the rule does not model. A Far proxy holds none.
- **Capacities** (`docs/audit/m5/scalability.md`): 517,376 Instance slots, 264,720 static TLAS entries, 10,000 Moving Instances. A blade cannot be an Instance; grass is an Instance per patch, or a path outside the Instance buffer. `town`'s 10,000 pigeons already fill the Moving Instances (`src/scenes/town.rs`).
- **Rays (ADR 0020).** Shadow rays set `RAY_FLAG_FORCE_OPAQUE` (`src/render/shaders/shadow_mask.wgsl`); an alpha-tested BLAS means a candidate loop with a fetch per candidate. A TLAS entry's eight mask bits are spent, one ordinary and seven Thin Primitive shadow width classes (`instance_mask`, `src/render/acceleration_structures.rs`), so a ray that should skip vegetation has no bit to test.
- **Deforming BLASes.** wgpu-core rebuilds and does not refit [ref:wgpu-30-tlas-rebuild-only]. `crowd` refits 100 BLASes of 5,120 triangles through wgpu-hal on Metal in 1.10 to 1.12 ms of GPU on the M4 Pro at 2560×1440, over its 1.0 ms Budget; the same figures' 1,600 rigid Shadow proxies built in about 0.60 ms when first measured, and now beside the previous frame (`docs/performance.md`, the crowd's two forms). The refit has no RTX 2070 SUPER figure: it exists on Metal alone.
- **ADR 0018, 0026, 0022.** Trees and bushes as Placements per Chunk fit as written; blades made each frame from a cooked map are derived at runtime from a Blob, the owner's reading (G2). The Far proxy merges static Placements and bakes planar charts, with no alpha and no Thin Primitive, and a canopy of cards has no planar faces. The draw is built in slot order and wind moves by the fixed schedule's injected time, so Goldens hold.
- **Capability floor (ADR 0021).** Mesh shaders and work graphs are outside it (`docs/research/wgpu-capability-matrix.md`); wgpu 30 exposes no opacity micromap.

## Options

Each coverage, generation and distance option is drawn with one of the rays options, which decides what the TLAS holds; each line says what it puts there.

Coverage:

- **C1. Cards, sharpened coverage through ADR 0027's twins, coverage-preserving mips cooked** (techniques 1, 3). Fits as written. In the TLAS: the cards at rest, still while the drawn cards sway; a shadow ray sees them forced opaque (whole cards) unless R1 shrinks them or R2 tests their alpha. Open: stability in motion at five levels, unshown; crossing cards still share samples in the edge pixel; the twins' cost at a forest's share of indices.
- **C2. Geometry first: opaque blades and leaves, thinned with area kept, thin parts widened** (5, 6, 7, 8). Fits as written; extends the Thin Primitive rule to a ribbon. In the TLAS: opaque leaves at rest, which a ray sees as drawn but still, with no candidate loop; blades are left out (13) or, as Wicked does, refitted. Open: triangle counts against the culling pass, and a blade's look edge-on.
- **C3. Varied samples per fragment** (4), a cooked sample-mask texture or a surface hash. Fixes crossing layers; amends ADR 0027's sample order. Changes nothing in the TLAS: rays see what C1 puts there. Open: noise at four samples in motion, which no source shows.

Rays:

- **R1. Still and opaque stand-ins; grass out with the contact term** (9, 13). Fits ADR 0020 as written. A shadow or probe ray sees each tree at rest, its leaves as shrunk opaque cards or dropped triangles; grass casts nothing. Open: self-shadowing where swaying leaves meet still ones, and whether a hard shadow of a stand-in reads right, which Epic says shrinking or culling does not do well.
- **R2. Still, alpha-tested canopy** (10). The shadow ray sees exact leaf silhouettes at rest; the probe ray may keep R1's stand-in. Fits wgpu 30 as a candidate loop; its cost is unpublished on either adapter.
- **R3. Near trees sway as rigid parts in the dynamic TLAS** (12), still past a cutoff. The ray sees swaying parts near the camera and R1 or R2 beyond. Needs the 10,000 cap shared or raised; unpriced.
- **R4. Near trees sway by Bend and refit** (11). Metal only through wgpu-hal; `crowd`'s refits already exceed their Budget.

Generation and placement:

- **G1. All vegetation as cooked Placements**, grass as patches. Fits ADR 0018 literally; patches spend Instance slots and, if not masked out of rays, static TLAS entries, which a ray sees still.
- **G2. Grass made on the GPU each frame from cooked maps** (5, 14), hashed in world space, compacted in slot order. Needs the owner's reading of ADR 0018. Nothing of it enters the TLAS: rays see no grass, and blades receive the contact term (13).
- **G3. Placement rules at cook time** (15), feeding G1's Placements and G2's maps: the line `docs/research/unity-and-unreal-horizon.md` proposes. Its Placements enter the static TLAS as cooked, still.

Distance:

- **D1. Discrete LOD with area-preserving pruning, ending in an octahedral impostor** handed over by mask (8, 16). Adds beside ADR 0026. In the TLAS: every BLAS is LOD 0 (M5's decision), still; an impostor has no BLAS, so a shadow ray past its distance sees the LOD 0 tree or, as Snowdrop does, a traced impostor depth.
- **D2. Forests in the Far proxy**, as an opaque canopy shell or a baked dithered card set (16). Changes ADR 0026's cook. In the TLAS: the shell as part of the Chunk's merged proxy, still; a shadow or probe ray sees an opaque canopy, or the baked dither NVIDIA uses for its last LOD.

## Cost of delay

**Would rebuild a decision M5 took.** *C3* reopens ADR 0027's sample order, cheapest before masked content exists; the twins' fragment stages change and Thin Primitives keep their rule. *D2* changes ADR 0026's cook (alpha or a canopy shell in the Far proxy) and re-cooks every proxy; before forests are cooked it costs only the cook. *R1 with a class of ray that skips grass or a canopy* needs a TLAS mask bit, and all eight are spent, one on the ordinary class and seven on the Thin Primitives' shadow width classes, part of M5's thin features (#883); freeing one rebuilds `instance_mask` and the shadow rays' masks. *G2* makes a path beside the culling pass that M5 built in slot order; it rebuilds nothing if it keeps that order.

**Would rebuild a decision M6 is about to take.** M6 implements probe GI. What a probe ray sees of a canopy (R1's stand-in, R2's loop, Wicked's forced opaque) and whether probe rays skip grass are flags and masks M6 writes. M6's ray costs will be measured with the dynamic TLAS near empty, while *R3* and the living-world note's crowds both fill it; the living-world note already asks M6 to measure rays with the cap full, and R3 adds trees to that check.

**Only adds beside.** C1 and C2 (the twins exist), R2 (a loop inside the shadow mask pass, its cost aside), R4 (a refit path that exists on Metal), G1 and G3 (cook steps), D1 (impostors deferred from M5 for this milestone), wind as stateless vertex work, the displacement buffer, two-sided leaf shading.

## Costs no source publishes: prototype questions
