# State of the art: asset compression and cooked data

Researched 2026-10-12 for ticket #48; it feeds #23 (cooked format v1: what a cooked Mesh, texture and Material contain, identity, versioning and chunking, where the cooker lives). Sources: papers (NVIDIA's NTC, arXiv 2305.17105v1, 2023, and AMD's NTBC, arXiv 2407.09543v2, 2024, both read as arXiv PDFs; AMD's DGF, Proc. ACM CGIT 2024, through the [GPU memory note](gpu-memory-residency-streaming.md)); author and vendor publications (Aras Pranckevičius's 2020 encoder survey, Rich Geldreich's blog, RAD Game Tools' Oodle pages, NVIDIA's RTXNTC SDK documentation, the Bazel and Unity manuals); specifications (KTX 2.0 at `ac39917`); source and READMEs at the tags or commits linked (bc7enc_rdo, ISPC Texture Compressor, Basis Universal v2_50, astc-encoder 5.7.0, betsy, meshoptimizer v1.3, Draco 1.5.7, zstd v1.5.7, LZ4 v1.10.0, lzbench v2.4, xxHash v0.8.4, BLAKE3 1.8.7, GDeflate, nvCOMP, Bevy v0.20.0, wgpu v30.0.1); Google Patents and, for two grants Google had not indexed, the USPTO front pages (searched 2026-10-12; status as Google lists it, not a legal conclusion); and Rust probes in a scratch directory, not in the repo, on the M4 Pro under macOS 27.0.1. Measured figures come from one run each while other processes loaded the machine (load average 11 to 17); a second run of the first probe, under heavier load, was slower on most timed rows, by up to 9x on one. Boundaries: memory, residency, page and tile sizes and where decompression runs are in the [GPU memory note](gpu-memory-residency-streaming.md) (rows 6 and 11 there; cited as `mem`); Nanite's disk format and AMD DGF are `mem` row 6 and are not repeated. Unreal's IoStore, Zen and derived-data cache could not be read (Not verified).

## Techniques

**1. BC encoders.** Every BC format is fixed-rate, so encoders differ only in cook time and error: Aras found "very little quality variance in DXTC compressors", bc7e "a clear winner" over ISPC for BC7, and BC7 at "very similar quality to ASTC 4x4, while being faster to compress" ([Aras 2020](https://aras-p.info/blog/2020/12/08/Texture-Compression-in-2020/)). The NTC authors put the median BC7 encoder at "a few seconds" per 4096² texture and BC1 at a fraction of a second ([NTC p10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17105v1#page=10)). Measured here, ISPC's basic BC7 profile takes 0.43 to 0.49 s per 2048² texture on 12 cores and its slow profile 1.6 s for at most 0.7 dB more, while ultra-fast loses 2.9 to 5.5 dB against basic on four of the five textures (computed from the measured rows). Intel discontinued the ISPC compressor ([ISPC L1](https://github.com/GameTechDev/ISPCTextureCompressor/blob/79ddbc90334fc31edd438e68ccb0fe99b4e15aab/readme.md#L1-L16)); bc7e has a plain C++ port and a newer analytical encoder, bc7f ([bc7enc 3](https://github.com/richgel999/bc7enc_rdo/blob/b9438627eef73a1157e84201b6fa6eb2ffd6d9f0/README.md#L3)); betsy's GPU encoders cover BC1, BC3 to BC6H and ETC but not BC7 ([betsy L66](https://github.com/darksylinc/betsy/blob/v1.1-beta/README.md#L66-L77)). No peer-reviewed BC encoder work since 2019 was found. For Praxis the choice is cook time against PSNR, with no runtime cost and no ADR 0008 issue; normal maps need BC5 and HDR needs BC6H, which ISPC also encodes.

**2. RDO for BC.** RDO picks, per block, an encoding that is slightly worse but makes the next lossless stage find longer matches; bc7enc_rdo uses a Lagrangian multiplier with rate "approximated using a fixed Deflate model", tuned for Deflate, LZHAM or LZMA ([bc7enc 7](https://github.com/richgel999/bc7enc_rdo/blob/b9438627eef73a1157e84201b6fa6eb2ffd6d9f0/README.md#L7)), and a "reduced entropy" mode gives 5 to 10 % with no slowdown ([bc7enc 5](https://github.com/richgel999/bc7enc_rdo/blob/b9438627eef73a1157e84201b6fa6eb2ffd6d9f0/README.md#L5)). Oodle Texture adds BC7Prep, a lossless bit rearrangement worth 5 to 15 % that must be reversed at load, on the CPU or GPU ([Oodle](https://www.radgametools.com/oodletexture.htm)); these percentages are vendor claims without a published corpus. The published RDO points are single images, and the 32 dB at lambda 3 is far below the 42 dB high-quality class, so under the owner's quality ranking RDO is a per-texture lambda to cap, not a default. The 2023 Sony and Nintendo filings (Comparison) cover the same space; their claims were not read.

**3. Basis Universal and KTX2.** Supercompression (the GST paper linked at [basisu 16](https://github.com/BinomialLLC/basis_universal/blob/v2_50/README.md#L16)) stores one intermediate and transcodes it at load: UASTC is a subset of both BC7 and ASTC, so its BC7 transcode "is particularly fast and simple" ([basisu 95](https://github.com/BinomialLLC/basis_universal/blob/v2_50/README.md#L95)), and v2.5 adds XUASTC (DCT-coded ASTC weight grids, with arithmetic or zstd profiles, the arithmetic one "roughly 5-18% better" at lower transcode throughput, [basisu 279](https://github.com/BinomialLLC/basis_universal/blob/v2_50/README.md#L279)) and XUBC7. The README publishes no transcode speed; measured with the older basisu 1.16, UASTC to BC7 runs at 160 to 173 Mpix/s on one core and lands 2.1 to 2.5 dB under a direct ISPC BC7 encode, and ETC1S lands near 31 dB. The format buys smaller downloads and one file for BC and ASTC targets; Praxis is desktop-only (ADR 0010), so the portability is worth only the ASTC path on Apple silicon, and the quality cost is the transcode error on top of the encode error.

**4. ASTC against BC on the M4 Pro.** Both are sampled and filterable on the M4 Pro (cap, Measured). At 8 bits per texel ASTC 4x4 and BC7 reach similar PSNR, ASTC encodes several times slower, and ASTC's larger blocks reach lower rates (6x6 at 3.6 bits) that BC cannot ([Aras 2020](https://aras-p.info/blog/2020/12/08/Texture-Compression-in-2020/)); the NTC paper's corpus puts ASTC 12x12 at 28.90 dB and 10x10 at 30.08 dB ([NTC p9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17105v1#page=9)). DX12 never exposes ASTC (cap), so ASTC would be a second texture set for Apple builds, cooked at its own cost, or a Basis transcode target.

**5. Neural texture compression.** NTC (Vaidyanathan et al. 2023) trains one small MLP per material over multi-resolution latent grids covering all channels and mips; its Table 3 puts NTC at 39.92 dB for 1.13 bits per pixel per channel against BC high at 40.23 dB for 3.94 ([NTC p9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17105v1#page=9)), and trilinear filtering inside NTC cost "an 8× slowdown", so the paper filters stochastically and resolves temporally ([p10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17105v1#page=10), Fig. 8 on [p7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17105v1#page=7)). The SDK says on-sample decoding "should be used in combination with Stochastic Texture Filtering (STF) instead, and filtered by a denoiser or DLSS after shading", that it needs cooperative-vector hardware to be fast, and that NTC adds noise that can show as "a static boiling pattern" under alpha cutoffs ([InferenceOnSample 5](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC/blob/v0.10.0-beta/docs/integration/InferenceOnSample.md#L5-L8), [SettingsAndQuality 43](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC/blob/v0.10.0-beta/docs/SettingsAndQuality.md#L43)); that mode conflicts with ADR 0008 and ranks lowest. The on-load mode decodes at load and transcodes to BCn, so disk shrinks (2.5 MB against 12 MB for a 2k bundle, [ntc 36](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC/blob/v0.10.0-beta/README.md#L36-L42)) while memory and sampling stay BC; the DX12 cooperative-vector path is "for testing purposes only" ([ntc 54](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC/blob/v0.10.0-beta/README.md#L54-L60)). AMD's NTBC predicts BC1 and BC4 blocks directly, so its output is ordinary BC at load, at a PSNR cost: 30.98 dB against 37.44 dB for Compressonator on one diffuse texture ([NTBC p8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09543v2#page=8)). wgpu 30 has `EXPERIMENTAL_COOPERATIVE_MATRIX` (8x8 tiles, Metal Apple7+ and Vulkan, [wgt-feat 1407](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-types/src/features.rs#L1407-L1425), [mtl 1153](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs#L1153-L1155)) but no cooperative vectors, and the SDK has no WGSL or Metal decoder, so any use would be a port.

**6. Mesh and vertex codecs.** meshoptimizer's codecs are lossless over already quantised, cache-ordered data and keep the GPU layout, which is why their ratio sits between quantisation alone and Draco ([mo 391](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/v1.3/README.md#L391-L399)); Draco quantises positions to 11 bits by default and trades decode speed for ratio through levels 0 to 10 without publishing either figure ([draco 283](https://github.com/google/draco/blob/1.5.7/README.md#L283-L308)). On DamagedHelmet the meshopt-plus-zstd path was 9.08 B/triangle against 10.19 for zstd on the quantised buffers alone, an 11 % difference, at multi-GB/s decode (measured); the cited README is v1.3 while the probe ran 0.22, before the v1 vertex codec. Quantisation is the lossy step and the one with image-quality consequences (normals at 8 bits per component in the probe; the README suggests octahedral 10 to 12 bits, [mo 427](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/v1.3/README.md#L427)). For clustered geometry the meshlet codec and its GPU decoder sample apply, and the in-memory cluster formats (Nanite, DGF at 2.9 to 3.73 B/triangle, NVIDIA's compressed clusters) are `mem` row 6.

**7. General-purpose codecs.** On Silesia, zstd spans ratio 2.89 to 4.05 at a decode speed that barely moves (1.07 to 1.37 GB/s across the listed levels), LZ4 decodes about 2.8 times faster than zstd -1 at ratio 2.10, and Brotli -11 and xz -6 buy 4 to 6 % over zstd -22 at about 36 % or less of its decode speed (computed from lzbench rows; zstd's and LZ4's own READMEs give similar ratios on an i7-9700K, [zstd 31](https://github.com/facebook/zstd/blob/v1.5.7/README.md#L31-L53), [lz4 40](https://github.com/lz4/lz4/blob/v1.10.0/README.md#L40-L58)). On BC7 data the same order held (measured). Oodle's figures ("3-5× faster than zlib", Leviathan "10× faster" than LZMA) are vendor claims, and Oodle is licensed per game and platform ([Oodle compressors](https://www.radgametools.com/oodlecompressors.htm), [RAD](https://www.radgametools.com/sales.htm)). zstd also trains dictionaries for small blobs ([zstd 82](https://github.com/facebook/zstd/blob/v1.5.7/README.md#L82-L90)), and KTX2 names it as supercompression scheme 2.

**8. GPU decompression codecs.** Only GDeflate is an open codec with shipped GPU decoders; it costs DEFLATE's ratio (zlib -6: 3.11 on Silesia, against zstd -22's 4.05, computed) in exchange for 32-way parallel decode. nvCOMP needs CUDA and ships binaries only. Where decompression sits, and its throughput, are `mem` row 11.

**9. Content hashing.** SHA-256 and BLAKE3 are cryptographic hashes; with ARMv8 SHA instructions through `sha2` 0.11 (already a Praxis dev-dependency), SHA-256 hashed 2.89 GB/s on one M4 Pro core, faster than single-threaded BLAKE3 at 2.39 GB/s, so the README's "Much faster than ... SHA-2" did not hold on one core here; BLAKE3 is "Highly parallelizable" ([BLAKE3 L3](https://github.com/BLAKE3-team/BLAKE3/blob/1.8.7/README.md#L3-L8)) and reached 22.3 GB/s on 12 threads; XXH3 is about 15 times faster than SHA-256 but is "not a cryptographic hash" (xxh 80) (all measured). Bevy keys processed assets on BLAKE3 over the asset bytes and its `.meta`, plus each dependency's hash ([meta.rs L94](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_asset/src/meta.rs#L94-L98), [L281](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_asset/src/meta.rs#L281-L300)); Bazel uses SHA-256.

**10. Identity and incremental cooking.** Three identities appear: the source path, a GUID stored beside the source (Unity: an ID per asset so it "can move or rename the asset without breaking anything", lost if the `.meta` file is lost, [Unity metadata](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/AssetMetadata.html)), and the content hash of the cooked output (Bazel's content-addressable store). Incremental cooking keys a cached result on everything that determines it: Unity re-imports when the asset name, importer ID, importer version or target platform changes, and records dynamic dependencies found during import ([Unity refresh](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/AssetDatabaseRefreshing.html)); Bazel maps an action hash to results and warns that tools outside the workspace are not tracked, so "two users with different compilers installed will wrongly share cache hits" ([Bazel](https://bazel.build/remote/caching)). For Praxis the lesson is that the cook key must include the cooker's own version and every setting, and that a GUID and a content hash answer different questions (what an asset is, and whether its bytes changed).

**11. Containers and chunking.** KTX2 already carries BC, ASTC and Basis payloads, mip levels supercompressed independently "to permit streaming and random access to the levels" and stored smallest first, so a reader can take the resident tail first; with a supercompression scheme its alignment requirement drops to 1 ([ktx 644](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/KTX-Specification/blob/ac39917e8240f51db97715a2453134a42ff959fd/ktxspec.adoc#L644-L652), [884](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/KTX-Specification/blob/ac39917e8240f51db97715a2453134a42ff959fd/ktxspec.adoc#L884-L893)). Custom archives, such as Unreal's .utoc and .ucas (not read), are the alternative for many assets per file. For wgpu the upload path decides alignment: `write_texture` accepts any row pitch, `copy_buffer_to_texture` needs 256-byte rows, so a cooked layout for direct staging-buffer copies pads mips under 64 texels wide (BC7) or keeps them in a resident tail (`mem` row 1).

## Options for #23 (no recommendation)

Each option fixes what a cooked texture, Mesh and Material contain, how they are named, and how they are chunked; all assume offline cooking (ADR 0011) and CPU decompression on worker threads (#20), and all end with the image-quality consequence the owner ranks first.

- **A. Standard per-asset files.** Textures as KTX2 with BC7 (colour), BC5 (normals), BC4 (single channels) and BC6H (HDR), each mip zstd-supercompressed (scheme 2), non-RDO; Meshes as quantised, meshopt-encoded vertex and index streams in a small engine file, zstd over them; Materials as a small engine file naming textures by ID. Identity: a GUID per source asset in a sidecar file, plus a cook key hashing source bytes, settings and cooker version; outputs stored under their content hash. Chunking: one file per asset, mips addressable through KTX2's level index. Cost: cook seconds per texture (row 1), load at zstd speed. Consequences: reuses a reader crate (`ktx2`, Apache-2.0) and external viewers; many small files on disk; streaming granularity is a mip or a whole Mesh. Image quality: the encoder's error only (row 1: 44.7 to 57.2 dB measured at the basic profile).
- **B. Engine archive with a table of contents.** Same payloads as A, packed into large archives of fixed-size chunks (64 KiB is the recurring size, `mem`), each chunk compressed (zstd or LZ4 chosen per chunk class) and addressed by content hash through a table of contents; assets keep GUIDs, chunks are deduplicated by hash. Cost: an archive writer and reader to own, and a rebuild step after cooking; cook cache as in A. Consequences: few files, aligned reads, chunk boundaries that can match Chunks of the Game world and future direct I/O (`mem` row 11); RDO (row 2) becomes worthwhile here because the archive codec sees long runs, but only where its lambda keeps quality. Image quality: as A, minus whatever RDO lambda is allowed.
- **C. Universal intermediate, transcoded at load.** Textures as KTX2 with UASTC (or XUASTC) payloads, transcoded to BC7 on Windows and Linux and BC7 or ASTC 4x4 on Apple silicon; Meshes, Materials and identity as A or B. Cost: transcode on load (UASTC to BC7 about 25 ms per 2048² texture on one core, measured), one cooked set for every target. Consequences: smaller downloads with ETC1S or XUASTC, a single texture pipeline. Image quality: the intermediate's error plus the transcode's, 2.1 to 2.5 dB under a direct BC7 encode for UASTC and about 31 dB for ETC1S (measured).
- **D. Neural texture sets decoded at load, with A or B elsewhere.** Materials cooked as NTC bundles (about 5 bits per texel for the whole bundle, NTC) or NTBC weights, decoded and transcoded to BC on the GPU at load; on-sample decoding excluded by ADR 0008. Cost: compression of minutes per material on an NVIDIA GPU, a port of the decoder to WGSL with or without cooperative matrix, licence terms of the NVIDIA SDK and the pending NVIDIA application above. Consequences: disk and download near a fifth of BC (NTC README) while GPU memory stays BC; no shipped title in the sources read. Image quality: NTC's error plus the BC encoder's; the SDK warns of noise under alpha cutoffs; NTBC measured about 15 % lower PSNR than reference BC.
