# How engine and renderer references explain how things work

Researched 2026-11-01 for ticket #476, for the guides decision of the M3.5 map (#475, decision 9): the guides are rebuilt as a reference organised by how the engine works, and the prototype that fixes their shape needs the field's practice first.

**Summary.** The references that explain a system best keep two things apart that the praxis volumes fuse: a reference page per mechanism, divided the way the code is divided and opened with what the mechanism does and the data it reads and writes, and a bounded explanation of why it is that way, with the history of how it got there kept out of both, in a changelog or an archive. Every one of them names what it describes (an engine version in the page title, a branch per build, a book frozen to its code), and none appends a running log of changes to the page itself: a change rewrites the description. Costs, where they appear at all, come as one table with the hardware, resolution, scene and settings stated first, or as ratios; prose carries only "cheaper" and "doubles". The recommended shape is the one decision 9 already names, made concrete below: a frame overview in data-flow order, a page per pass or subsystem that opens with a definition and its interface and states one commit, a short "Why this way" section at its end for alternatives, no ticket or PR in its prose, and costs as a table pasted from `praxis report` or linked to the Bench history.

Sources: pbrt 4th edition (pbr-book.org), Filament's two documents, Godot's manual and architecture pages, Bevy's book, migration guides and crate docs, Unreal Engine's 5.8 documentation, the Nanite (SIGGRAPH 2021) and Lumen (SIGGRAPH 2022) course slides, Mitsuba 3's documentation and its documentation build, and Diátaxis; every claim links the page it was read on. The praxis facts are measured on `main` at 439ccc22 (2026-11-01) by a script over `docs/guides/*.html` with markup, scripts and styles stripped.

## Recommendations

**Page division.** Every source that explains a running system divides it the way the system runs or the way the code is laid out, and puts the "why" and the "how it came to be" somewhere else: pbrt's system overview walks the data flow between its base types, Unreal's programming overview gives the "High level Rendering order", Nanite's slides follow a triangle through the pipeline, Godot's and Bevy's architecture pages mirror source directories and frame stages, Mitsuba makes a page per plugin, and Diátaxis states the rule outright: reference mirrors the machinery. Recommendation: one frame-overview page in data-flow order (the hub's "The frame on main" list already is this, with a guide beside each step), then one page per pass or subsystem, named as `src/render/` names them (culling pass, depth pyramid pass, shadow mask pass, Froxel pass, prepare and upload, acceleration structures), each a reference page with a bounded explanation section rather than a top-level split into four kinds, as Diátaxis permits for a system with many subsystems. The 27 volumes become source material for those pages, and techniques that span passes (MSAA, camera-relative placement, light units) get a concept page the pass pages link, as pbrt's radiometry chapter serves its later chapters.

**Opening.** The field opens with what the thing is and does, in a sentence, then its boundary: Unreal's definition line, Godot's scope sentence followed by the version, audience and prerequisite, Filament's equation followed by the chosen approximation and the shader code, Mitsuba's parameter table before any prose. None opens with learning objectives or questions to check afterwards; those are tutorial devices, and Diátaxis says reference is consulted while working, not read. Recommendation: a page opens with one sentence of definition, the inputs it reads and the outputs it writes (buffers, targets, bind groups, Render switches), the commit it describes, and the pages to read first; the body follows the data through the mechanism with the actual WGSL and Rust quoted as Filament quotes GLSL; "You'll learn" and "Check yourself" are dropped from reference pages, and live demos stay where a demo shows the mechanism rather than the lesson.

**History and alternatives.** Three patterns recur: alternatives considered sit inline at the decision in one or two sentences with the reason (Filament, Godot's "Why not deferred rendering?", Lumen's "we tried X, so Y"), or in a dedicated section (Nanite's "Options?", pbrt's Further Reading and chapter 16); the record of what changed when sits outside the page, in release notes, migration guides or a revisions list (Mitsuba, Bevy, Filament); and no source narrates its own tickets. Recommendation: each page ends with a short "Why this way" section, a paragraph per alternative with the reason and the ADR it rests on, which is where decision 9's "history cut to a note" lands; what changed and when goes to one changelog page in the guides (the page's "Since then" lines moved out and dated), and prose names no ticket or PR, which the map's bar already makes a test. The volumes' development narrative is archived, not deleted, as pbrt keeps its third edition at its own path.

**Truth maintenance.** Each source states what it describes in one place a reader cannot miss: the engine version in Unreal's page title and URL, Godot's per-branch build with its banners, pbrt's code frozen to the book, Mitsuba's and Godot's reference generated from source, Bevy's docs.rs per release. What none of them does is append a running log of later changes to the page: a change rewrites the description, and the log lives in the changelog. Recommendation: one line per page, "Describes `main` at `<commit>`", and the rule decision 9 names, a session that changes a mechanism rewrites that mechanism's page and moves the commit, replacing the appended Status paragraphs; generate what can be generated and let `cargo test` check the rest, in the spirit of Mitsuba's plugin pages and Godot's class reference: the pass order, each pass's bind groups and Render switches, buffer capacities and Budgets can be emitted from the code or checked against it, and a test that every identifier a page names in code font exists on `main` (the map's decision 11 candidate for comments, applied to guides) catches the drift the Status lines now record by hand.

**Numbers.** Two defensible treatments appear: Lumen's, a Context slide that fixes resolution, settings and frame-rate target, then tables naming scene, hardware and mode per row; and pbrt's and Filament's, ratios and operation counts in prose with absolute times only when hardware is named. Unreal's feature pages, Godot's architecture pages and Bevy's book carry no measurements at all, and Mitsuba's "1.5-2X" without hardware is the weakest form. Recommendation: each page carries one "What it costs" table pasted from `praxis report`, as the workflow already requires of PRs, with the scene, adapter, resolution and commit in its caption, and links the Series on the Bench history page for the live figure; prose keeps only ratios and relative words ("the mask pass costs a third of the lit pass's rays"), never a retyped millisecond, so that a page's numbers go stale in one table rather than across its text.

## What praxis's guides do differently today

At `main` 439ccc22 the hub lists 27 volumes beside itself, each tied to a ticket and its PRs ("M3 · ticket #190 · PR #220") and ordered by a suggested reading path that follows the order the work happened, not the frame; the hub's "The frame on main" section, dated at commit 270c5e4 (2026-10-26), is the one page that follows the data flow, and it points at a guide per step. A volume opens with In brief, You'll learn, Read first and Status, then chapters, a reading list and a glossary, and ends with Check yourself questions; its chapters open with history ("Until #194, the Renderer drew each Primitive of each Instance with a `draw_indexed` of its own", `instance-culling.html`), which is the development narrative decision 9 names. Measured over the 27 volumes with markup stripped, the prose runs 3,434 to 13,580 words (mean 6,962; `shadow-rays.html` the longest) and names tickets or PRs by `#` 10 to 159 times (mean 63; 17 to 26 of them as links in the two volumes the ticket names); the HTML source averages about 14,500 words, which is where the ticket's "about 15,000 words" comes from, and no volume reaches 190 references by either count. Status is an appended log: 492 words before chapter 1 in `instance-culling.html`, 898 in `shadow-rays.html`, each later PR adding a sentence ("PR #290 re-recorded the M4 Pro Baselines, in which the town scene's median GPU frame on the shadow mask reads 18.1 ms, not 17.6"), so a reader reconstructs the current mechanism by applying the log to the body, the opposite of every source above. The workflow rule (`docs/agents/workflow.md`, "A session that introduces important new concepts") requires this shape and this appending, so the rule changes with the pages. `tests/field_guides.rs` checks that every link and image lands, that the hub links every guide and that each guide's series links reach its neighbours in the hub's order; nothing checks a page against the code. Costs appear in "What it costs" chapters as prose figures with their PR and commit, and in Status corrections, not as one table. The volumes do carry what the field's best pages carry and the prototype should keep: the real WGSL and Rust quoted at a named commit, the oracle tests named beside the mechanism, concept tooltips defined as `CONTEXT.md` defines them, and a reading list of primary sources.
