# Visibility and the GPU-driven main pass: state of the art

Researched 2026-10-12 for ticket #45; it blocks #22 (visibility strategy for the main pass), whose options already assume instanced or GPU-driven draws because one CPU draw per Instance costs 73.5 to 73.8 ns on one thread on wgpu 30.0.1 over Metal ([threading note](job-systems-and-frame-threading.md#after-2901)). Sources: SIGGRAPH Advances slides (2015 to 2025); GDC, Digital Dragons and REAC decks; papers from SIGGRAPH, Eurographics, HPG, I3D, JCGT, TOG and arXiv (1988 to 2023); Apple documentation, WWDC20 and WWDC22 transcripts and the Metal Feature Set Tables dated 2026-05-21 (MFST pN); DirectX-Specs `a610933`, Vulkan-Docs `193ebe1`; Unreal documentation through web.archive.org, Unity 6 and Godot 4.6 documentation; source at pinned tags or commits; two scratch probes on the M4 Pro, not in the repo, one for capabilities and one for GPU timings. Boundaries: shadowing and GI are #44's, so this note covers only what each main-pass shape gives ray-traced shadows; virtual-geometry memory is [row 6 of the memory note](gpu-memory-residency-streaming.md#comparison); command recording is #47's; cluster encodings are #48's ([compression note](asset-compression-and-cooked-data.md)).

Praxis moved to wgpu 30.0.1 on 2026-10-12 ([#55](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/55)), so wgpu links point at tag v30.0.1 and reuse the [capability matrix](wgpu-capability-matrix.md) labels (`mtl`, `vk`, `dx`, `-cmd`, `-dev`, `core-*`; `cap` is the matrix, researched on 29.0.1, its Capability floor rows re-checked on 30.0.1 in [#95](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/95)), plus `wgt-*` for wgpu-types files (the matrix's `feat`, `lim`, `inst`), `naga-*` and `cl30`, the [CHANGELOG at v30.0.1](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/CHANGELOG.md). PDF links open a page (`#page=N`); `.pptx` and `.ppsx` decks are cited by slide in presentation order (`sN`). "Vendor figure" marks a vendor measuring its own product.

## Techniques

**1. Forward with a depth pre-pass.** A depth-equal main pass shades only visible fragments, and later passes reuse the depth. Apple GPUs remove hidden surfaces per pixel, independent of submission order; discard or a fragment depth write sends fragments back to that stage and translucent draws flush it, so Apple orders opaque, then alpha-tested or depth-writing, then translucent meshes, and a tile's transformed geometry fills a tiled vertex buffer that, when full, splits the pass into partial renders ([WWDC20 a](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10602/)). A pre-pass done only for performance is redundant there: HSR "can reject hidden fragments as well as depth pre-passes can, but without any additional costs" ([WWDC20 b](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10632/)). On the M4 Pro that held at 0.36M triangles but not at ADR 0013's scale: at 4.6M, drawing back to front cost 2.7 times front to back, and a pre-pass in the same render pass brought it to 1.09 times (Measured on the M4 Pro). Forward shading pays quad overdraw: pixel shaders run in 2×2 quads, so a one-pixel triangle costs 4x and a ten-pixel one about 2x ([Hable s27](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Hable/Advances_SIGGRAPH_2024_VisibilityVRS-SIGGRAPH_Advances_2024.pptx)); Activision saw forward foliage at quad occupancy as low as 20% ([ATVI s6](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/reac2021_geometry_pipeline_rendering_architecture_final.pptx)), and ADR 0013's 5M visible triangles at 2560×1440 average 1.36 per pixel (computed).

**2. Tiled light culling.** Forward+ inserts a compute pass between a depth pre-pass and shading that lists, per tile, the lights inside the tile's frustum between its depth bounds ([Harada p2](https://takahiroharada.files.wordpress.com/2015/04/forward_plus.pdf#page=2)); tiled deferred culls lights per tile and evaluates them all with one read of the G-buffer ([Lauritzen n13](https://web.archive.org/web/20121116175851/http://bps10.idav.ucdavis.edu/talks/12-lauritzen_DeferredShading_BPS_SIGGRAPH2010_Notes.pdf#page=13)). Where a tile spans a depth discontinuity its range includes the gap and its list grows ([Lauritzen n15](https://web.archive.org/web/20121116175851/http://bps10.idav.ucdavis.edu/talks/12-lauritzen_DeferredShading_BPS_SIGGRAPH2010_Notes.pdf#page=15)). Persson: Forward+ forces a pre-Z pass, MSAA works, transparency needs another pass ([Persson p5](https://www.humus.name/Articles/PracticalClusteredShading.pdf#page=5)).

**3. Clustered shading.** Clusters subdivide tiles in depth, and optionally by normal, so a sample is over-represented by at most a fixed volume; Olsson et al.'s forward variant used a pre-Z pass and "inherently support[s] MSAA" ([Olsson p2](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~uffe/clustered_shading_preprint.pdf#page=2), [p6](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~uffe/clustered_shading_preprint.pdf#page=6), [p8](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~uffe/clustered_shading_preprint.pdf#page=8)). Avalanche assigns lights on the CPU into 64×64-pixel tiles with 16 exponential depth slices and reuses the lists for forward passes in a deferred engine ([Persson p12](https://www.humus.name/Articles/PracticalClusteredShading.pdf#page=12), [p13](https://www.humus.name/Articles/PracticalClusteredShading.pdf#page=13)); Activision's Z-binning keeps per-tile light bit masks plus a 1D table of the lowest and highest light index per depth bin ([Drobot p30](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2017/2017_Sig_Improved_Culling_final.pdf#page=30)); id moved from Doom's clusters to compute rasterization of light and decal volumes into 256×256 and 32×32 tiles plus clusters, each fragment reading the shorter list ([DE p9](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=9), [p10](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=10), [p13](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=13), [p14](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=14), [p16](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=16)). Stochastic many-light sampling is #44's.

**4. Deferred shading.** Foundations, as Olsson et al. trace them ([Olsson p2](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~uffe/clustered_shading_preprint.pdf#page=2)): Deering et al.'s deferred shading hardware ([1988](https://doi.org/10.1145/54852.378468)) and Saito and Takahashi's G-buffer ([1990](https://doi.org/10.1145/97879.97901)). Under MSAA the G-buffer holds every sample and loses MSAA's decoupling of shading from coverage unless lighting finds edges, comparing sample depths with depth derivatives and normals, and shades only those per sample ([Lauritzen n19](https://web.archive.org/web/20121116175851/http://bps10.idav.ucdavis.edu/talks/12-lauritzen_DeferredShading_BPS_SIGGRAPH2010_Notes.pdf#page=19), [n20](https://web.archive.org/web/20121116175851/http://bps10.idav.ucdavis.edu/talks/12-lauritzen_DeferredShading_BPS_SIGGRAPH2010_Notes.pdf#page=20)). Kerzner and Salvi halve 8x MSAA memory and cut 30% of run time on an Intel Iris Pro, ordering writes with Intel's PixelSync ([Kerzner p1](https://diglib.eg.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/6e9a1dd4-8a28-4322-baa6-38c6a52d2a3f/content#page=1), [p5](https://diglib.eg.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/6e9a1dd4-8a28-4322-baa6-38c6a52d2a3f/content#page=5)); Crassin et al.'s two shaded aggregates per pixel approach 8x MSAA with 30% less memory, up to 1.3x faster, GTX 980 at 1920×736 ([AGAA p1](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2015-02_Aggregate-G-Buffer-Anti-Aliasing/AGAA_I3D2015_authors.pdf#page=1), [p6](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2015-02_Aggregate-G-Buffer-Anti-Aliasing/AGAA_I3D2015_authors.pdf#page=6)). HDRP allows MSAA only in forward and not with ray tracing ([HDRP AA](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Anti-Aliasing.html)); Godot rejected deferred as it "requires using hacks to be able to use MSAA" ([Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html)). On Apple GPUs programmable blending and memoryless attachments keep a G-buffer on chip; stored and read back, it consumes "a significant amount of memory bandwidth" ([WWDC20 b](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10632/)).

**5. Visibility buffer.** Burns and Hunt shade in one compute pass per material over the tiles it covers and leave transparency to a forward pass ([Burns p3](https://jcgt.org/published/0002/02/04/paper.pdf#page=3), [p5](https://jcgt.org/published/0002/02/04/paper.pdf#page=5)); Schied and Dachsbacher store visible triangles with screen-space derivatives ([Schied p1](https://cg.ivd.kit.edu/publications/2015/dais/DAIS.pdf#page=1)). Nanite's material passes began as a full-screen draw per material with depth-equal tests and became compute "shade binning", a counting sort of pixels per material ([Wihlidal24 p13](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Nanite+GPU+Driven+Materials.pdf#page=13), [p63](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Nanite+GPU+Driven+Materials.pdf#page=63)); analytic UV derivatives through the material graph cost it under 2% ([Karis p107](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=107)). Horizon Forbidden West encodes primitive IDs by XOR over three indices, since base PS4 has no primitive ID without a geometry shader and provoking-vertex IDs had "issues with HW rotating vertices", and cut pixel-shading waves to 40% and 25% in two PS5 4K scenes ([HFW s48, s49, s101](https://d3d3g8mu99pzk9.cloudfront.net/JamesMcLaren/AdventuresWithDeferred_McLaren_James.pptx)). Activision's triangle-ID pre-pass marks the triangles owning a pixel in 0.21 ms where a Hi-Z test took over 1 ms, making its forward pass 10 to 20% faster; its test framework gave V-buffer quad occupancy of 87 to 89% against 18 to 78% forward ([ATVI s37, s39, s43](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/reac2021_geometry_pipeline_rendering_architecture_final.pptx)). In measurements by Aaltonen that Hable relays, `SV_PrimitiveID` costs 1.5x (NVIDIA) and 1.8x (AMD RDNA1) a flat-interpolated "leading vertex" ID, and non-indexed drawing 2.0x and 2.6x; on the M4 Pro `@builtin(primitive_index)` made an ID pass 19 to 24% slower than writing the instance alone (Measured on the M4 Pro); the material pass computes its own derivatives, fetches three vertices per pixel and needs animated vertices transformed beforehand ([Hable s70, s72, s101, s102, s105](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Hable/Advances_SIGGRAPH_2024_VisibilityVRS-SIGGRAPH_Advances_2024.pptx)). Under ADR 0001 every Material is data for one shader, the case The Forge shades in "one regular draw call" ([README L338](https://github.com/ConfettiFX/The-Forge/blob/cd5046893faba2dc7869243873bf01f02a6f0df9/README.md#L338)); bins are needed per shader permutation. On mobile GPUs Aaltonen counts over 20 non-uniform loads per pixel and `SampleGrad` at an eighth rate or slower ([HypeHype p7](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/AaltonenHypeHypeAdvances2023.pdf#page=7), [p8](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/AaltonenHypeHypeAdvances2023.pdf#page=8)); Apple's rates are unpublished.

**6. GPU-driven instance culling and indirect draws.** A compute pass culls Instances and writes indirect arguments or a compacted index buffer. Multi-draw issues one indirect draw per mesh section and must compact empty draws ([Wihlidal16 p22](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Wihlidal_Graham_OptimizingTheGraphics.pdf#page=22)). Single draws merge the surviving triangles of many meshes into one index buffer, packing vertex and instance IDs into each 32-bit index: Doom Eternal's merged draws share a pipeline, fetch vertices from buffers and need a completely bindless pipeline ([DE p46](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=46), [p47](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=47), [p48](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf#page=48)). Metal's own route to GPU-written draws, indirect command buffers run over an indirect range ([Apple ICB](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/encoding-indirect-command-buffers-on-the-gpu), [executeCommandsInBuffer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlrendercommandencoder/executecommandsinbuffer%28_:indirectbuffer:offset:%29)), is absent from wgpu. Bevy on wgpu culls on the GPU only with `INDIRECT_FIRST_INSTANCE` and `IMMEDIATES` and uses count draws except on DX12 ([gpu_preprocessing L1563](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_render/src/batching/gpu_preprocessing.rs#L1563-L1565), [render_phase L1202](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_render/src/render_phase/mod.rs#L1202-L1206)), so on Metal its multi-draws stay a CPU loop, 55.2 to 55.4 ns per draw measured on 30.0.1 ([threading note](job-systems-and-frame-threading.md#after-2901)).

**7. Cluster and triangle culling.** Clusters of 64 (Assassin's Creed Unity, Activision) to 256 triangles (Frostbite) carry a bounding volume and a cone of normals; surviving triangles are tested for back-facing, zero area, missing every sample and depth, then compacted ([Wihlidal16 p28](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Wihlidal_Graham_OptimizingTheGraphics.pdf#page=28), [p29](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Wihlidal_Graham_OptimizingTheGraphics.pdf#page=29), [p43](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Wihlidal_Graham_OptimizingTheGraphics.pdf#page=43); [HA15 p12](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/aaltonenhaar_siggraph2015_combined_final_footer_220dpi.pdf#page=12); [ATVI s9, s26](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/reac2021_geometry_pipeline_rendering_architecture_final.pptx)). Cone culling was "not typically worthwhile" for Nanite, since back-facing clusters are almost all occlusion culled ([Karis p17](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=17)), and "wasn't worth it" for Alan Wake 2, which builds 64-triangle meshlets with meshoptimizer ([AW2 s19, s34](https://presentations.remedy.fi/gpu-driven-rendering-with-mesh-shaders-in-alan-wake-2-dd2024.ppsx)). Jensen, Frisvad and Bærentzen timed meshlet generators on an RTX 2080 Ti (Vulkan, 4x MSAA, 1280×720) and recommend vertex-complete meshlets with maximal primitive fill, then cullability ([Jensen p14](https://jcgt.org/published/0012/02/01/paper.pdf#page=14), [p24](https://jcgt.org/published/0012/02/01/paper.pdf#page=24)).

**8. Mesh shaders.** Task (amplification) shaders cull and launch mesh shader workgroups that emit meshlets to the rasterizer; NVIDIA introduced them with Turing in 2018, recommending 64 vertices and 126 primitives ([NVIDIA 2018](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/introduction-turing-mesh-shaders/)); Metal 3's object and mesh stages run from Apple7 and Mac2 with a 16 KB payload and up to 256 vertices and 512 primitives per mesh ([WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10162/)). AMD recommends 128 vertices and 256 triangles, or 64 and 126 ([AMD24 p64](https://gpuopen.com/gdc-presentations/2024/GDC2024_Mesh_Shaders_in_AMD_RDNA_3_Architecture.pdf#page=64)). Alan Wake 2's per-primitive culling in the mesh shader was slower on RTX 3090 and 4090 and 1.8 ms faster on an RX 6700 XT ([AW2 s51](https://presentations.remedy.fi/gpu-driven-rendering-with-mesh-shaders-in-alan-wake-2-dd2024.ppsx)). As Apple excludes ray tracing from render pipelines that use mesh shading, a mesh-shaded forward pass on Metal would take its shadows from a separate compute pass.

**9. Occlusion culling.** A Hi-Z pyramid tests a bound's screen rectangle at one coarse level ([Greene 1993](https://doi.org/10.1145/166117.166147); [Karis p17](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=17)). Two-phase culling draws last frame's visible set, builds the pyramid, retests the rest and draws what is new ([HA15 p52](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/aaltonenhaar_siggraph2015_combined_final_footer_220dpi.pdf#page=52)); Nanite tests current clusters against last frame's pyramid with last frame's transforms, the second pass being "usually a tiny fraction" ([Karis p75](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=75), [p76](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=76)). This is temporal coherence, not accumulation: what is visible this frame is drawn in one of the passes, so nothing pops in, but it "only falls apart under extreme visibility changes" ([Karis p19](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=19)), such as a camera cut, where last frame's set stops predicting this one; a single test against last frame's pyramid instead leaves newly visible geometry "missing for a frame" ([vk_lod_clusters](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/20352f04a5bce46938ca63871c1389b0fac921fa/docs/raster_twopass_culling.md#L11-L13)). The pyramid must be conservative "to avoid flickering" ([AW2 s37](https://presentations.remedy.fi/gpu-driven-rendering-with-mesh-shaders-in-alan-wake-2-dd2024.ppsx)), and Bevy's keeps the minimum depth, as ADR 0015 requires ([depth.rs L54](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_core_pipeline/src/mip_generation/experimental/depth.rs#L54-L57)). Alternatives: CPU occluder rasterization, which Godot chose for having no frame delay at camera cuts ([Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html)), Intel's masked library being AVX and SSE only while the M4 Pro is ARM64 ([README L16](https://github.com/GameTechDev/MaskedOcclusionCulling/blob/1fd7974456cffa481a1a534328a1d02523d19ce8/README.md#L16-L19)); baked cell visibility after Teller and Séquin ([1991](https://doi.org/10.1145/122718.122725)); hardware occlusion queries, CHC++ being up to an order of magnitude faster than earlier query schemes ([CHC++ p1](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2008/mattausch-2008-CHC/mattausch-2008-CHC-draft.pdf#page=1)); Lee et al. test coarse BVH groups against occluders found by temporal coherence with constant draw calls, then ray cast per pixel (abstract only, [Lee 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.142649)).

**10. Virtual geometry.** Nanite simplifies groups of clusters with locked boundaries, alternating groups between levels so no cracks form, and picks at runtime the cut whose error is under a pixel ([Karis p33](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=33), [p35](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=35), [p46](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=46), [p67](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=67)), building on Quick-VDR and batched multi-triangulation ([p44](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=44)); it does not cross-fade, because below a pixel "temporal antialiasing smoothes out any change" ([p67](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=67)). Unreal lists MSAA, forward rendering and ray tracing against Nanite meshes as unsupported (ray tracing uses a fallback mesh) and supports opaque and masked materials ([UE Nanite](https://web.archive.org/web/20251221042229/https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)). Bevy's meshlets select clusters under one pixel of error and cull in two passes against the previous view, are opaque only and incompatible with MSAA ([cull_shared L13](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/cull_shared.wesl#L13-L34), [L170](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/cull_shared.wesl#L170-L181), [mod.rs L94](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/mod.rs#L94-L99), [asset.rs L30](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/asset.rs#L30-L33)). vk_lod_clusters traverses one LOD hierarchy for raster and ray tracing and builds cluster acceleration structures for the selected clusters ([README L28](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/20352f04a5bce46938ca63871c1389b0fac921fa/README.md#L28-L33)); wgpu has no such structure (memory note). With proxy acceleration structures, rays leave the rasterized surface and hit the proxy: Epic reports self-shadowing near the ray origin, hidden with screen-space traces ([MegaLights p43](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/MegaLights_Stochastic_Direct_Lighting_2025.pdf#page=43), [p46](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/MegaLights_Stochastic_Direct_Lighting_2025.pdf#page=46)), and Ubisoft found raster and BVH geometry up to 50 cm apart ([ACS p133](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin's%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=133)).

**11. Software rasterization.** Nanite rasterizes clusters whose edges are under 32 pixels in compute and larger ones in hardware, both through a 64-bit atomic max of depth (30 bits), cluster (27) and triangle (7) ([Karis p84](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=84), [p87](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=87), [p88](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=88)). Laine and Karras's CUDA rasterizer supports MSAA ([Laine p1](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2011-08_High-Performance-Software-Rasterization/laine2011hpg_paper.pdf#page=1)); Kenzel et al.'s software pipeline stayed within an order of magnitude of hardware on over 100 game scenes ([Kenzel p1](https://www.tugraz.at/fileadmin/user_upload/Institute/ICG/Images/team_steinberger/Pipelines/SIGGRAPH-2018_cuRE-authorversion.opt.pdf#page=1)); the 64-bit atomic path that Schütz et al. extend reached 50 billion points per second at 85% of memory bandwidth ([Schütz p4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.01287#page=4)). A compute rasterizer has no hardware MSAA and no quad derivatives; Nanite derives barycentrics from its edge equations ([Wihlidal24 p48](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Nanite+GPU+Driven+Materials.pdf#page=48)).

**12. Work graphs.** D3D12 work graphs let nodes launch nodes, avoiding `ExecuteIndirect`'s "serial processing through worst case sized buffers" ([WorkGraphs L340](https://github.com/microsoft/DirectX-Specs/blob/a610933eac2103cd502bacfb5193ae7f02c6fa54/d3d/WorkGraphs.md#L340)); mesh nodes are "proposed" ([L587](https://github.com/microsoft/DirectX-Specs/blob/a610933eac2103cd502bacfb5193ae7f02c6fa54/d3d/WorkGraphs.md#L587)), and `VK_AMDX_shader_enqueue` is provisional, adding mesh nodes in revision 2 ([appendix L12](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/Vulkan-Docs/blob/193ebe195f47ffc5004683d1659e71c725658c4d/appendices/VK_AMDX_shader_enqueue.adoc#L12-L17), [L40](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/Vulkan-Docs/blob/193ebe195f47ffc5004683d1659e71c725658c4d/appendices/VK_AMDX_shader_enqueue.adoc#L40-L41)). Epic prototyped Nanite's compute materials on work graphs on an RX 7900 XT ([Wihlidal24 p131](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/Slides/GDC+slide+presentations/Nanite+GPU+Driven+Materials.pdf#page=131)); Remedy names them as its next step ([AW2 s56](https://presentations.remedy.fi/gpu-driven-rendering-with-mesh-shaders-in-alan-wake-2-dd2024.ppsx)).

**Anti-aliasing and stability without TAA.** SMAA 1x and S2x are spatial, S2x adding 2x MSAA samples; T2x and 4x add temporal supersampling with reprojection and so collide with ADR 0008 ([SMAA p7](https://www.iryoku.com/smaa/downloads/SMAA-Enhanced-Subpixel-Morphological-Antialiasing.pdf#page=7)); on a GTX 470 at 1080p, 1x costs 1.02 ms and S2x 2.04 ms ([p8](https://www.iryoku.com/smaa/downloads/SMAA-Enhanced-Subpixel-Morphological-Antialiasing.pdf#page=8)). MSAA anti-aliases coverage, not shading, so specular stability needs geometric specular anti-aliasing, which widens roughness from screen-space derivatives: temporally stable and compatible with deferred shading ([Kaplanyan p1](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2016-06_Filtering-Distributions-of/NDFFiltering.pdf#page=1)), later corrected at grazing angles, with a deferred variant taking `ddx` and `ddy` of the normal in the G-buffer pass because cheap derivatives exist only in pixel shaders ([Tokuyoshi19 p4](https://www.jp.square-enix.com/tech/library/pdf/ImprovedGeometricSpecularAA.pdf#page=4), [p5](https://www.jp.square-enix.com/tech/library/pdf/ImprovedGeometricSpecularAA.pdf#page=5); [Tokuyoshi21 p1](https://jcgt.org/published/0010/02/02/paper.pdf#page=1)). Forward and G-buffer passes have those derivatives; a compute material pass must derive them analytically. Discrete LODs pop: Alan Wake 2 reports "cases when they pop" ([AW2 s56](https://presentations.remedy.fi/gpu-driven-rendering-with-mesh-shaders-in-alan-wake-2-dd2024.ppsx)), and Bevy's author finds transitions "noticeable, even with a crossfade effect" ([JMS55 0.14](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/)).

**Ray-traced shadows and transparency.** Forward traces in the fragment shader, as wgpu's `ray_shadows` example does ([shader.wgsl L45](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/examples/features/src/ray_shadows/shader.wgsl#L45-L75)); on Metal `EXPERIMENTAL_RAY_QUERY` already requires `supportsRaytracingFromRender` ([mtl 1157](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs#L1157-L1166)). MSAA shades per pixel, not per sample ([Lauritzen n19](https://web.archive.org/web/20121116175851/http://bps10.idav.ucdavis.edu/talks/12-lauritzen_DeferredShading_BPS_SIGGRAPH2010_Notes.pdf#page=19)), so an edge pixel's samples share one shadow ray unless the shader runs per sample. Deferred and visibility-buffer shapes trace in compute from reconstructed positions and normals (ray tracing in compute pipelines from Apple6, MFST p5) or write a shadow mask; HDRP disables screen-space shadows under MSAA ([HDRP AA](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Anti-Aliasing.html)). Transparency is usually a forward pass: Burns and Hunt leave it out ([Burns p3](https://jcgt.org/published/0002/02/04/paper.pdf#page=3)), HDRP forces forward for transparent surfaces ([HDRP](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Forward-And-Deferred-Rendering.html)), and clustered lists serve it ([Persson p5](https://www.humus.name/Articles/PracticalClusteredShading.pdf#page=5)); The Forge instead sorts transparent triangle IDs in per-pixel linked lists ([README L342](https://github.com/ConfettiFX/The-Forge/blob/cd5046893faba2dc7869243873bf01f02a6f0df9/README.md#L342)).

## What wgpu 30 allows

New facts only, read at v30.0.1; [cap](wgpu-capability-matrix.md) covers indirect draws, mesh shaders, ray queries, subgroups, 64-bit atomics, `PRIMITIVE_INDEX` and MSAA counts. "Measured" here is the capability probe: wgpu 30.0.1 on Metal, macOS 27.0.1, 2026-10-12, three runs with identical output.

| Question | wgpu 30.0.1 |
|---|---|
| Tile memory | No programmable blending, framebuffer fetch, input attachments, tile shaders, imageblocks or raster order groups in `wgpu-types/src/features.rs` (searched "framebuffer", "subpass", "input attachment", "tile", "imageblock", "programmable", "raster order", "interlock"), though Metal has them from Apple2 and Apple4 (MFST p3, p4). A `TRANSIENT_ATTACHMENT` texture (`TRANSIENT` before 30.0.0) loads only with `Clear` or `DontCare` and stores only with `Discard` ([cl30 266](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/CHANGELOG.md#L266)), so a memoryless G-buffer cannot feed a later pass. |

Metal compiles with `preserveInvariance` from macOS 11 ([mtl-dev 275](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/device.rs#L275-L278)), so `@invariant` positions hold for a depth-equal second pass. |

## Measured on the M4 Pro

GPU time of the main-pass shapes, from the timing probe, a one-off measurement: the probe is not in the repo, so these figures cannot be re-run from committed code, and ADR 0019 cites them as evidence for its choice, not as Baselines. Setup: wgpu 30.0.1 on Metal, release build, macOS 27.0.1, 2026-10-12. 2560×1440, `Rgba16Float` color, `Depth32Float` reverse-Z, back-face culling and no other culling; one instanced `draw_indexed` of 3,033 unit UV spheres in six layers 12 to 42 m from the camera on a 2.2 m grid (expected depth complexity about 3.9), tessellated to 0.36M, 4.6M or 27.4M triangles; one material of three RGBA8 1024² layers with mips, trilinear with 8x anisotropy, and GGX shading of a sun and 16 point lights for every shaded fragment. Deferred writes RGBA8 albedo and metallic plus RGBA16F normal and roughness, then lights each pixel, or each sample at 4x without edge detection. The visibility buffer writes `instance << 20 | primitive_index` to `R32Uint`; a full-screen pass fetches the triangle, recomputes perspective-correct barycentrics and their screen derivatives, and samples with `textureSampleGrad`. Memoryless is `TRANSIENT_ATTACHMENT`. Frames run back to back, so one pass's vertex work overlaps the previous pass's fragment work; a cell is the spread of three process runs, each the median of five runs of 40 frames. Deferred and visibility-buffer frames differ from forward by a mean of 0.0002 and 0.0014 to 0.0015 per channel, tone-mapped to 0 to 1, so the shapes draw the same image. Runs on 29.0.1 earlier the same day showed the same pattern.

Hidden surface removal made submission order irrelevant at 0.36M triangles, as Apple states ([WWDC20 a](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10602/)), but back to front cost 2.7 times front to back at 4.6M and 3.2 times at 27.4M; that partial renders, which split a pass when the tiled vertex buffer fills, cause this was not verified. ## Options for #22 (no recommendation)

When this was researched (2026-10-12), Praxis issued one `draw_indexed` per glTF primitive per Instance in one forward pass, `Depth32Float` cleared to 1 and compared with `Less` (reverse-Z since [#114](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/114)), no MSAA, Instance data by `instance_index`, Materials as IDs into one buffer plus five texture arrays, vertex and index mega-buffers, and one PBR shader with four fixed point lights ([state.rs](../../src/state.rs), [shader.wgsl](../../src/shader.wgsl), [material.rs](../../src/assets/material.rs), [pools.rs](../../src/assets/pools.rs)). Shared by every option: a compute culling pass composes each Instance's camera-relative transform once from its anchor and offset (ADR 0014), tests the frustum and a two-phase Hi-Z pyramid of minimum reverse-Z depth (ADR 0015), and writes a compacted index buffer drawn with one `draw_indexed_indirect` per pipeline (one native call on every backend) or per-mesh indirect arguments (count draws on Vulkan and DX12 only), or hands culled clusters to a mesh-shader pipeline (row 8), which on Metal cannot also trace rays; the four point lights become a light buffer with clustered or Z-binned lists. Indirect draws run in the order the culling pass wrote them; on the M4 Pro, back to front cost 2.7 times front to back at 4.6M triangles without a pre-pass (Measured on the M4 Pro). ADR 0008 takes Forward+ as the working assumption for the main pass, to be revisited with real scene data. Each option names its pre-pass output and ends with image quality, which the owner ranks first ([map decision](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/17#issuecomment-6128603845)).

- **A. Clustered forward with a depth pre-pass.** Pre-pass: 4x MSAA reverse-Z depth, which seeds the Hi-Z pyramid and the depth-equal lit pass. Main pass: the PBR shader loops over its cluster's lights and traces shadow rays inline. Changes: light loop, ray query, MSAA targets, one pipeline per material permutation. Needs: Tier 1, ray queries (Tier 2, cap). Measured at 1x: a same-pass pre-pass cost 3 to 70% over front-to-back forward, and 4x MSAA added 7 to 26%, both highest at 27.4M triangles. Image quality: 4x MSAA on geometric edges (the Apple9 maximum) and alpha-to-coverage for masked materials; shading once per pixel, so specular stability rests on geometric specular anti-aliasing from quad derivatives, which forward has; one shadow ray per pixel; discrete LOD switches can pop; transparency shares the light lists; nothing accumulates over frames.
- **B. Forward with a visibility pre-pass.** Pre-pass: depth plus a 32-bit Instance-and-triangle ID per sample (R32Uint; 14,745,600 bytes at 1x and 58,982,400 at 4x at 2560×1440, computed) from `PRIMITIVE_INDEX` or provoking-vertex IDs. A compute scan marks triangles that own a sample and compacts the lit pass's indices, as Activision does; the IDs also give compute passes positions and normals. Needs: A's, plus `TEXTURE_ADAPTER_SPECIFIC_FORMAT_FEATURES` for MSAA IDs. Measured: `primitive_index` made the ID pass 19 to 24% slower than an instance-only one. Image quality: as A, since the lit pass rasterizes the same surviving triangles at depth equal; per-sample IDs also mark edge samples for any later per-sample pass.
- **C. Deferred G-buffer with clustered or tiled lighting.** Pre-pass: optional; the G-buffer pass writes depth, normals and material properties, which compute lighting reads with the light lists and traces from. Changes: a G-buffer layout, a lighting compute pass, a separate forward path for transparency. Needs: Tier 1, ray queries in compute. Measured, lighting in a full-screen fragment pass: from 10% slower to 33% faster than forward at 1x, and 1.2 to 3.7 times forward 4x when every sample is lit at 4x. Image quality: without MSAA, edges rely on SMAA 1x, which cannot reconstruct sub-pixel geometry such as a distant grid ([SMAA p7](https://www.iryoku.com/smaa/downloads/SMAA-Enhanced-Subpixel-Morphological-Antialiasing.pdf#page=7)); a per-sample G-buffer with edge detection restores MSAA at four times the G-buffer memory; G-buffer encoding quantizes normals (HDRP notes compression artifacts, [HDRP](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Forward-And-Deferred-Rendering.html)); specular anti-aliasing uses the isotropic deferred kernel; transparency is lit by a second path.
- **D. Visibility buffer with compute material and lighting.** Pre-pass: as B. A compute pass rebuilds each pixel's triangle from the IDs and the mega-buffers, interpolates attributes with analytic derivatives, samples with `textureSampleGrad`, shades every Material in one shader or one bin per shader permutation (ADR 0001), and traces shadow rays inline. Needs: B's; animated vertices transformed before the pre-pass. Measured, resolving in a full-screen fragment pass: from 16% slower to 4% faster than forward at 1x; 4x IDs added 4 to 12% before any per-sample shading. Image quality: no quad overdraw or helper lanes; texture filtering, anisotropic included, is right only where derivatives are analytic, as finite differences across discontinuities give "nonsense" mip levels in Nanite ([Karis p106](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=106)); MSAA needs per-sample IDs and per-sample shading at edges, otherwise SMAA 1x; specular anti-aliasing needs analytic normal derivatives; transparency forward or in per-pixel triangle-ID lists.
- **E. Virtual geometry for dense static meshes, beside A to D.** Pre-pass: a 64-bit depth-and-ID visibility buffer written by atomics from hardware and software rasterization over a cluster DAG culled in two passes; other meshes use A to D. Needs: `TEXTURE_INT64_ATOMIC` (Apple9; wgpu excludes M1 and M2, cap), a cooked cluster format (#23, #48). Image quality: no MSAA in Nanite or Bevy, leaving SMAA 1x; LOD switches stay under a pixel, but Nanite relies on TAA to hide them, so without TAA nothing smooths the change; shadow rays against proxy geometry self-shadow unless the acceleration structure follows the cut, which wgpu 30 cannot build; materials limited to opaque (Bevy) or opaque and masked (Nanite).
