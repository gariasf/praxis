# Global illumination and lighting: state of the art

Researched 2026-10-12 for ticket #44; it feeds #22 (visibility strategy for the main pass), the map's fog lines "RT shadows, reflections, dynamic GI" and "Baked lighting pipeline" (#17), and any revision of ADR 0008's lighting clause. Sources: SIGGRAPH Advances slides, 2010 to 2025 (PDF pages, or PPTX slide numbers); papers and memos (TOG, CGF, JCGT, I3D, HPG, Proc. ACM CGIT, The Visual Computer, Activision, NVIDIA, arXiv; 2002 to 2026) and the unpublished radiance-cascades preprint; GDC Vault abstracts; vendor documentation (Unreal Engine 5.8 via web.archive.org, as dev.epicgames.com did not resolve; CRYENGINE V, Godot 4.7, AMD, NVIDIA); source code at the commits linked; foundational papers by DOI (metadata only); and a scratch program on the M4 Pro (see Measured on the M4 Pro). Boundaries: main-pass shape, clustered light lists, culling and virtual geometry are #45's; streaming and residency of baked data are #46's, so only sizes appear here; lightmap and probe codecs are #48's; atmosphere, sky and volumetrics are out of scope except as the light that changes with time of day.

Temporal class, per the owner's [image-quality decision](https://github.com/gariasf/praxis/issues/17#issuecomment-6128603845): (a) complete each frame; (b) converges over frames in a world-space or probe-space cache, so lighting lags a change but nothing smears on screen; (c) screen-space temporal reuse or denoising (reprojection, history buffers, temporal reservoirs), with ghosting and disocclusion risks, shipped with TAA, TSR or DLSS. Costs carry the GPU, resolution and upscaler the source states; vendor figures are labelled. `cap` is the [capability matrix](wgpu-capability-matrix.md) and its tiers; wgpu links point at tag v30.0.1, whose registry sources match the tag (checked). ADR 0013's frame is 2560×1440 = 3,686,400 pixels, 12 ms of GPU time, 8 GB.

## Techniques

**1. Baked lightmaps.** Lightmaps store irradiance per texel and have been "used for almost thirty years" ([CoD p15](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Roughton/SIGGRAPH%20Advances%202024%20-%20Hemispheres%20Presentation%20Notes.pdf#page=15)); radiosity is the foundation ([Goral et al. 1984](https://doi.org/10.1145/964965.808601)). Activision lists directional models: SH ([Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1145/383259.383317)), H-basis ([Habel and Wimmer 2010](https://doi.org/10.1145/1730804.1730835)), AHD and the spherical Gaussians of The Order: 1886, which also reconstruct specular ([p17](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Roughton/SIGGRAPH%20Advances%202024%20-%20Hemispheres%20Presentation%20Notes.pdf#page=17)). Class a: the light is a texture. id Tech 7 lists "Leaks/seams/banding/resolution" ([id p7][id7]); its pipeline applied to DOOM: The Dark Ages was estimated at 44 to 110 GB on disk and 27 to 68 days of bake turnaround, so id Tech 8 replaced it ([id p10](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf#page=10)). Image quality: stable and sharp where texel density allows; no noise or lag; static only; seams and leaks need authoring.

**2. Baked probe volumes.** Ghost of Tsushima covers its island with quadratic-SH probes, 16×16×3 per 200 m tile on 20×80 tiles ([GoT p16][got16]), 19,200 probes per km² (computed); for time of day and weather it bakes sky visibility and "bounced sky visibility", relit from the current sky ([p24](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021.pdf#page=24), [p25][got25]). Class b there: 8×8 tiles relit per frame plus the nearest 3×3, 123 µs on base PS4 ([p50](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021.pdf#page=50)). Thin walls leaked until an authored interior mask weighted the probes ([p52](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021.pdf#page=52)). RDR2 keeps 32×32 sky probes 256 m apart, position and occlusion prebaked, re-traced through clouds in 0.22 ms on PS4 ([slides 66, 68][rdr2]). Assassin's Creed Shadows bakes 11 time-of-day keyframes with "Dynamic transitions" per season set, 33 in all ([AC p4](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin%27s%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=4), [p5][ac5]), class a. Image quality: smooth, stable, low-frequency indirect; leaks at thin walls; no bounce from local lights.

**3. Precomputed relightable GI.** PRT precomputes how each point transfers low-frequency incident light, so relighting is a dot product ([Sloan et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1145/566654.566612)). Enlighten in Frostbite solves radiosity into small indirect-only lightmaps and converges "in 4 frames or less", "a lag of 4*1000/60 = 66 ms" at 60 fps, hence class b ([EN p15](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Martin-Einarsson-RadiosityArchitecture%28SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course%29.pdf#page=15)); the sun "moves slowly so we can stagger updates across frames" ([p16](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Martin-Einarsson-RadiosityArchitecture%28SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course%29.pdf#page=16)). Far Cry 3 relit SH transfer-matrix probes for time of day, muzzle flashes and explosions ([abstract][fc3]); The Division's transfer probes gave "real-time bounce lighting from completely dynamic light sources" ([abstract][div]). Ghost of Tsushima rejected transfer matrices at "at least nine times the storage" ([GoT p25][got25]); Activision tested moving basis decomposition on light-transport volumes ([CoD slide 72][mbd]). Image quality: stable multi-bounce indirect that follows sun and lights within frames; low-frequency; static geometry only.

**4. Screen-space GI and AO.** GTAO uses "temporal reprojection and spatial filtering", class c ([p2](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/Practical_Real_Time_Strategies_for_Accurate_Indirect_Occlusion_NEW%20VERSION_COLOR.pdf#page=2)). Horizon methods "cannot properly simulate light passing behind thin surfaces"; the visibility bitmask replaces the two horizon angles with sector bits under a constant thickness ([p1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11376#page=1)) and also integrates "spatially and temporally" ([p4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11376#page=4)). Bevy "strongly recommended" TAA with its SSAO ([ssao 106](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/ssao/mod.rs#L106-L108)); Godot's SSIL reads last frame's colour and shows artefacts "when objects enter and leave the screen" ([Godot][godot]). Image quality: fine contact detail; screen-edge and disocclusion artefacts; noisy without accumulation.

**5. Voxel GI.** Voxel cone tracing voxelises the scene on the fly and gathers two bounces at 25 to 70 fps on a GTX 480 ([Crassin et al. 2011 p1](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/publications/GIVoxels-pg2011-authors.pdf#page=1)). SVOGI voxelises "on CPU, asynchronously and incrementally", needs light probes for specular, and lists "ghosting, aliasing, light leaking and noise", a "delay of 1-2 frames" and "a few seconds" after a teleport; class b, though the source does not say what causes the ghosting ([SVOGI][svogi]); Warhorse credits it with making "the days and nights look much more real" in Kingdom Come: Deliverance ([Crytek][kcd]). Lumen dropped cone tracing: "Leaking or over-occlusion unavoidable" ([Lumen p141](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=141)). Image quality: soft indirect; leaks where walls are thinner than a voxel ([Godot][godot]).

**6. SDF and software-traced GI.** Lumen's software path sphere-traces mesh distance fields ([p6](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=6)) merged into four 256³ clipmaps ([p40](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=40)) beside a 320 MB mesh-SDF pool ([p35](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=35)); Epic limits it to 200 m (800 m at most) and walls of at least 10 cm ([UE][uelumen]). Epic's 2015 mesh SDFs were computed offline, so "it only supports rigid meshes" ([p19](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/DynamicOcclusionWithSignedDistanceFields.pdf#page=19)). Godot's SDFGI supports dynamic lights but not dynamic occluders and takes frames to "fully converge", class b ([SDFGI][sdfgi]). Brixelizer builds 64³-voxel cascades of distance bricks ([AMD p4](https://gpuopen.com/download/GDC2024_GI_with_AMD_FidelityFX_Brixelizer.pdf#page=4)); its GI, based on GI-1.0, reprojects, accumulates and denoises screen probes, class c ([p16](https://gpuopen.com/download/GDC2024_GI_with_AMD_FidelityFX_Brixelizer.pdf#page=16)). Image quality: smooth, blurry indirect; leaks; cascade shifts; screen history in Brixelizer and Lumen.

**7. DDGI.** Light-field probes ([McGuire et al. 2017](https://casual-effects.com/research/McGuire2017LightField/McGuire2017LightField.pdf#page=1)) gave static probes per-texel visibility; DDGI updates them by ray tracing and blends with hysteresis 0.85 to 0.98, class b ([p10](https://jcgt.org/published/0008/02/01/paper-lowres.pdf#page=10)); slow probes give "noticeable lag", "most salient in light-to-dark transitions" ([p9](https://jcgt.org/published/0010/02/01/paper.pdf#page=9)). Assassin's Creed Shadows uses five camera-following cascades of 16×16×8 probes, 2 to 32 m apart and 512 m at the widest ([AC p36][ac36]), 73 MB ([p127](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin%27s%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=127)), as the cache for a per-pixel pass with a temporal denoiser ([p59](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin%27s%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=59)); each probe is relit about 3 times per second ([p40](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin%27s%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=40)), and "Probe interpolation always leaks" ([p133](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin%27s%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=133)). Image quality: smooth, stable, low-frequency indirect bounded by probe spacing; lag after changes; fewer leaks, not none.

**8. Surfel GI.** GIBS spawns surfels from the G-buffer ([p14][gibs14]), keeps them in world space, and accumulates lighting "over many frames", class b ([p74][gibs74]), adding rays when a moving sun raises variance ([p77](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/SIGGRAPH%20Advances%202021%20-%20Surfel%20GI.pdf#page=77)). Its clipmap probes amortise updates, which "introduces some update latency" ([p19](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/EA-GIBS2/Apers_Advances-s2024_Shipping-Dynamic-GI.pdf#page=19)); College Football 25 freezes GIBS in 60 Hz gameplay to stay under 2 ms ([p58](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/EA-GIBS2/Apers_Advances-s2024_Shipping-Dynamic-GI.pdf#page=58)); a ray offset that "behaved too conservatively" leaked light ([p22](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/EA-GIBS2/Apers_Advances-s2024_Shipping-Dynamic-GI.pdf#page=22)). kajiya, listed here by the ticket, uses a clip-mapped irradiance cache with ReSTIR-style diffuse, not surfels ([gi-overview](https://github.com/EmbarkStudios/kajiya/blob/9fdec0f074f9f822d395f9cfd041b58749e32303/docs/gi-overview.md#L220)). Image quality: stable world-space indirect; slow on newly seen surfaces; leaks at ray offsets.

**9. Lumen.** Lumen traces screen space, then software or hardware rays, then the sky ([p9](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=9)); its surface cache relights 1024² direct and 512² indirect texels per frame ([p74](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=74)). Class c: the temporal filter shows "slow reaction to lighting changes - ghosting" ([p166](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=166)), and its interpolation jitter is stabilised by "expanding the Temporal Anti Aliasing's neighborhood clamp" ([2021 slide 39](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Radiance%20Caching%20for%20real-time%20Global%20Illumination%20%28SIGGRAPH%202021%29.pptx)). In The Matrix Awakens' 16 km² city ([RTOW slide 10][rtow]) the TLAS was rebuilt every frame and early tests suggested a 100k-instance limit, so traces stop at 200 m ([p118](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Lumen-Wright%20et%20al.pdf#page=118)); RTOW gives a 150 m near field and a 1 km HLOD far field ([slide 15][rtow]). Epic quality costs ~8 ms on consoles at 1080p "relying on Temporal Super Resolution" ([UE][uelumen]). Image quality: detailed near-field indirect; ghosting and lag on lighting changes; depends on TSR.

**10. Radiance cascades.** Sannikov's preprint, never submitted to a journal ([README](https://github.com/Raikiri/RadianceCascadesPaper/blob/9d1fd290bb08f2589024ce83b75a7cc3323c6a4d/README.md#L4)), stores radiance with spatial resolution near and angular resolution far, keeps memory near twice cascade 0 ([p16](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Raikiri/RadianceCascadesPaper/9d1fd290bb08f2589024ce83b75a7cc3323c6a4d/out_latexmk2/RadianceCascades.pdf#page=16)), and "explicitly does not rely on temporal reuse", class a ([p6](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Raikiri/RadianceCascadesPaper/9d1fd290bb08f2589024ce83b75a7cc3323c6a4d/out_latexmk2/RadianceCascades.pdf#page=6)). In 3D "storing M0 for full 3d is usually the limiting factor" ([p17](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Raikiri/RadianceCascadesPaper/9d1fd290bb08f2589024ce83b75a7cc3323c6a4d/out_latexmk2/RadianceCascades.pdf#page=17)); Split Radiance Cascades (arXiv, July 2026) keeps world-space probes in a sparse hash map fed by rays from visible surfaces and computes one bounce in a single frame ([p1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20384v1#page=1)); more bounces come from a secondary cache that samples itself through temporal accumulation, class b ([p9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20384v1#page=9)). Image quality: one bounce in a single frame, without history or lag (Split RC also shows a 10-frame accumulation, [p1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20384v1#page=1)); 3D results exist only for research scenes.

**11. World-space hashed caches.** Path-space filtering keys path vertices by jittered, quantised positions in a hash table ([Binder et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05942)); SHaRC traces about 4% of paths per frame into such a cache ([L98](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/SHARC/blob/4e21b585c33c83d723ca9a1e11bbb1090d145793/docs/Integration.md#L98)); NRC trains a network online, settling in "as few as 8 frames (∼ 70 ms)" ([p5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.12372#page=5)). id Tech 8 is the shipped form: probe rays store hit records with a shader binding table index ([id p17](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf#page=17)) into a 14 MB hash of 25 cm cells ([p18](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf#page=18)), shaded in compute and reused for N frames ([p20](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf#page=20)); a ½ or ¼ resolution one-ray gather reads screen, cache and irradiance volumes ([p22](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf#page=22)), then a temporal filter, with TAA as "another temporal filter" ([p23](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf#page=23)): cache b, gather c. It needs "0 disk space" ([p33][id33]). Image quality: indirect detail bounded by cell size; lag set by cache history; screen history in the gather.

**12. ReSTIR DI and many lights.** ReSTIR resamples light candidates per pixel and reuses reservoirs from neighbours and, through motion vectors, the previous frame, class c ([p9](https://benedikt-bitterli.me/restir/bitterli20restir.pdf#page=9)). MegaLights picks a stochastic light set, traces screen then hardware rays and denoises once for all lights ([UE][ueml]); it avoids ReSTIR's reuse because "Denoisers don't like correlation" ([ML p11](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/MegaLights_Stochastic_Direct_Lighting_2025.pdf#page=11)); its output "ends up being further processed by TSR" ([p77](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/MegaLights_Stochastic_Direct_Lighting_2025.pdf#page=77)), and it falls back to virtual shadow maps where hardware RT is impractical ([p49](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/MegaLights_Stochastic_Direct_Lighting_2025.pdf#page=49)). Epic warns of "blurry lighting and eventually noise or ghosting" when many important lights reach a pixel ([UE][ueml]). Bevy Solari's shadows lagged moving objects until 0.20 tested reuse against the previous frame's TLAS ([jms55](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2026-09-18-solari-bevy-0-20/)). Image quality: correct soft shadows from many area lights; denoiser blur, noise and ghosting grow with light density.

**13. ReSTIR GI and ReSTIR PT.** ReSTIR GI improves MSE "from 9.3× to 166×" at one sample per pixel and works "In conjunction with a denoiser" ([p1][rgi1]); GRIS extends the resampling to whole paths as ReSTIR PT ([GRIS p1][gris1]); ReSTIR PT Enhanced is 2 to 3× faster and adds duplication maps because correlation is "detected as signal" by denoisers ([p9](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/lin2026restirptenhanced/lin2026restirptenhanced.pdf#page=9)); its reservoirs take 2×64 bytes per pixel ([p13](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/lin2026restirptenhanced/lin2026restirptenhanced.pdf#page=13)), 471,859,200 bytes at 1440p (computed). Cyberpunk 2077's Overdrive mode path-traces GI for its day-night cycle with RTXDI and NRD ([NVIDIA][cp77]). Bevy Solari runs ReSTIR DI and GI with a world cache on wgpu ray queries: 8.22 to 14.55 ms on an RTX 3080 at 1600×900, including 5.75 to 6.29 ms of DLSS Ray Reconstruction ([0.17](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2025-09-20-solari-bevy-0-17/)), "NVIDIA only in practice due to relying on DLSS-RR" ([0.18](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2025-12-27-solari-bevy-0-18/)); v0.20.0 requires `Msaa::Off` and deferred, depth and motion-vector prepasses ([mod.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_solari/src/realtime/mod.rs#L107-L111)). Image quality: every light path is dynamic; noise, correlation, ghosting and disocclusion fall to the denoiser.

**14. Specular indirect.** Ghost of Tsushima relights 235 reflection probes from captured G-buffer cubemaps, one per frame in async compute, at 1.5× the lit probes' storage, class b ([GoT p61](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021.pdf#page=61), [p63][got63]); id Tech 8 rescales probes by the frame's irradiance ([p27](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf#page=27)). Stochastic SSR relies on a temporal filter, class c, and "Can't kill all the lag anyway" ([SSSR slide 90][sssr]). Battlefield V's RT reflections cost 6.29 ms including a temporal filter, GPU not stated ([BFV p52](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/video/gputechconf/gtc/2019/presentation/s91023-it-just-works-ray-traced-reflections-in-battlefield-v.pdf#page=52)). Epic: hardware RT "is the only way to achieve high quality mirror reflections" ([UE][uelumen]). Image quality: probes stable but low-frequency with parallax error; SSR loses off-screen content and lags; RT is correct but denoised.

**15. Shadow maps.** Z-partitioning, "also called cascaded shadow maps", is the common form; SDSM fits partitions to a depth min/max reduction and aims for sub-pixel resolution so that partition shifts are invisible, class a ([SDSM p4][sdsm4], [p22](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Lauritzen-SDSM%28SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course%29.pdf#page=22), [p29](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Lauritzen-SDSM%28SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course%29.pdf#page=29)). PCSS varies the PCF kernel for contact hardening ([Fernando 2005](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/shaderlibrary/docs/shadow_PCSS.pdf#page=1)). Unreal's virtual shadow maps are 16k² virtual maps in 128² pages, rendered for visible pixels and cached, with directional clipmaps to about 40 km; "Any light movement or rotation will invalidate all cached pages for that light", they are "designed to be used with Nanite", and page-pool overflow corrupts shadows ([VSM][vsm]); the MegaLights demo filled the 4 GB page cache ([ML p4](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/MegaLights_Stochastic_Direct_Lighting_2025.pdf#page=4)). For distance, Ghost of Tsushima keeps a 128² shadow map per 200 m tile ([GoT p63][got63]) and Epic's Kite demo ray-marched SDFs ([Wright 2015][w15]). Image quality: stable when snapped or sub-pixel; aliasing at grazing angles; a moving sun keeps invalidating cached pages.

**16. Ray-traced shadows and complements.** One ray per light gives hard shadows each frame; area lights need more rays or a spatio-temporal denoiser such as NRD's SIGMA, which uses normals, depth and motion vectors ([NRD L9](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/NRD/blob/e06852bd545973cb224eb28820b891205cfc1624/README.md#L9-L16)). Battlefield V's acceleration-structure builds fell from 64 ms to 14.5 ms by culling, with "Occasional popping", and to 1.15 ms with staggered refits and fast-build flags ([BFV p63](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/video/gputechconf/gtc/2019/presentation/s91023-it-just-works-ray-traced-reflections-in-battlefield-v.pdf#page=63), [p65](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/video/gputechconf/gtc/2019/presentation/s91023-it-just-works-ray-traced-reflections-in-battlefield-v.pdf#page=65)). Screen-space contact shadows "complement shadow maps", and Days Gone was the first title to use them for the sun ([Bend](https://www.bendstudio.com/blog/inside-bend-screen-space-shadows/)). Image quality: correct penumbrae, no shadow-map aliasing; denoised soft shadows lag; contact shadows miss off-screen and thin occluders.

## Options (no recommendation)

ADR 0008 as first accepted paired baked GI for static environments with ray-traced shadows and GI for dynamic objects and cascaded shadow maps; ADR 0020 has since replaced that clause. Rows 14 to 16 combine with every option; memory is judged against the 8 GB ledger.

- **A. ADR 0008 as written.** Rows 1 and 2 baked, row 15 as baseline, row 16 and ray-traced GI for dynamic objects on the high preset. Time of day needs keyframe bakes ([AC p5][ac5]) or row 2's relit sky visibility; moving lights do not bounce on static geometry. Class a for baked light, c if the dynamic-object GI is denoised. Needs Tier 1, Tier 2 for the high preset, which rebuilds the TLAS whenever anything moves and the BLAS of every deforming object each frame (costs measured below); Forward+ with lightmap UVs per Instance; MSAA unaffected. Bake pipeline: a path-traced baker and cooked lightmaps and probes (ADR 0011), at DOOM Eternal's 4 to 10 h per level ([id p7][id7]). Image quality: static indirect at texel detail, no noise or lag; seams and leaks need authoring; static and dynamic objects come from two systems; Ubisoft, which shipped baked GI beside ray-traced GI as alternative platform tiers, found two GI systems "time-consuming" to ship ([AC p132](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin%27s%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=132)).
- **B. Relightable precomputed GI for time of day.** Rows 2 and 3 (sky-visibility and transfer probes), relit probes from row 14, row 15. Sun and sky change every frame or in amortised tiles; local lights bounce only with transfer data. Class a or b. Needs Tier 1 only; any main pass, Forward+ and MSAA included. Memory: probe grids (19,200 per km² at GoT density) plus transfer data at least 9× irradiance ([GoT p25][got25]) unless compressed (44:1, [CoD slide 76][mbd]). Bake: per-Chunk visibility and transfer precompute, cooked. Image quality: stable, smooth, low-frequency indirect; leaks need authored masks; no bounce from moving or destroyed geometry.
- **C. Ray-traced probe GI with world-space accumulation only.** Row 7, 8 or 11's cache, shaded straight from probes or cache by position and normal, with no per-pixel gather or screen denoiser; hard RT shadows. Lighting converges over frames. Class b only. Needs Tier 2 and a TLAS rebuilt whenever an Instance moves (no refit in wgpu-core; on the M4 Pro about 6 to 8 ms of CPU for 100k Instances, under 1 ms for 10k, measured); any main pass. A 2.1M-ray probe update costs 2.7 to 3.5 ms on the M4 Pro in the open scene and 5.5 to 6.5 ms in the dense one (measured). Memory: GIBS fits up to 100k probes in 100 MB ([p16][gibs16]); AC's urban BVH data was 320 MB ([p126](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Advances%202025%20-%20Raytracing%20the%20world%20of%20Assassin%27s%20Creed%20Shadows.pdf#page=126)); on the M4 Pro a BLAS takes 94 bytes per triangle (45.5 compacted), outside the ledger as then worded (measured). No bake ([id Tech 8][id33]); the 2 km view needs a far fallback such as RDR2's sky probes ([RDR2][rdr2]). Image quality: smooth, stable indirect without contact detail, which AC and id Tech 8 add with class c passes; lag after changes; leaks.
- **D. Per-pixel ReSTIR path tracing.** Rows 11 to 13 with a denoiser; shadows and reflections from the same paths. Per-frame response through temporal reservoirs. Class c. Needs Tier 2; hit shading can read ADR 0002's shared vertex and index buffers, as the probe below does, where Bevy Solari needs storage-buffer binding arrays (not on DX12); a G-buffer or visibility buffer, motion vectors and the previous frame's G-buffer, so not Forward+ as assumed. Memory: reservoirs ~472 MB at 1440p plus BVH and denoiser history. No bake. Image quality: every light path dynamic, but noise, ghosting, disocclusion and correlation depend on a temporal denoiser; NRD targets D3D12, Vulkan and D3D11, and on Metal only MetalFX's denoising upscaler was found. Published costs are 8.9 to 80 ms at 1080p on desktop GPUs; on the M4 Pro one bounce per pixel costs 4.0 to 4.6 ms before shading, resampling or denoising (measured).
- **E. Radiance cascades.** Row 10 in 3D, with a probe or baked fallback for distance. Class a for one bounce, b for more. Needs Tier 2 and a hashed probe store; memory unpublished. Image quality: one bounce without history or lag; research scenes only, 8.6 to 11.5 ms on a laptop RTX 3080.

## Measured on the M4 Pro

A scratch program on wgpu 30.0.1 and Metal, a one-off measurement: the program is not in the repo, so committed code cannot re-run it. The scene: a 2 by 2 km heightfield in 64 BLAS (2,097,152 triangles) and 100,000 instances of four 5,120-triangle rocks, spread over the whole square (open) or packed into a 500 m square with heavy overlap (dense, a worst case); the camera 1.8 m above ground facing the horizon at 2560×1440; opaque geometry built with `PREFER_FAST_TRACE`. Rays are ray queries in compute shaders, and every hit fetches its triangle for a normal, with no material work. GPU times are pass timestamps resolved after their submission completed (cap), medians of 10 passes, as the range over 4 open and 3 dense runs, with rays capped at 2 km or 100 m (the cap saved at most 17 %).

| Work | Open | Dense |
|---|---|---|
| A sun shadow ray from every visible pixel, ended by the first hit (2.0 M and 2.3 M rays) | 1.8 to 2.0 ms | 3.7 to 3.9 ms |
| One cosine bounce per visible pixel plus a sun shadow ray at its hit (2.4 M and 2.7 M rays) | 4.0 to 4.6 ms | 8.2 to 8.4 ms |
| 8,192 probes (32×8×32, 4 m apart) × 256 rays plus a sun shadow ray at each hit (3.0 to 3.2 M rays) | 2.7 to 3.5 ms | 5.5 to 6.5 ms |
| For scale: one primary ray per pixel to 2 km (3,686,400 rays) | 4.1 to 5.7 ms | 8.4 to 9.4 ms |

- **Memory** (Metal `currentAllocatedSize` before and after creation, through `as_hal`): BLAS 94.0 bytes per triangle (199,098,368 bytes for 2,117,632 triangles), 45.5 after `compact_blas`; TLAS 288.5 bytes per instance (28,868,608 bytes for 100,064). ADR 0013's ledger then counted buffers and textures only (ADR 0017 has since added acceleration structures), and wgpu's internal counters track acceleration-structure bytes only on DX12 ([dx-sub 245](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/dx12/suballocation.rs#L245)).
- **BLAS builds**, always full: 2,097,152 terrain triangles take 29 to 36 ms of GPU; one 5,120-triangle BLAS 0.6 to 0.75 ms, ten 1.2 to 1.55 ms, a hundred 7.8 to 9.3 ms, which is the per-frame price of deforming meshes.
- **TLAS rebuild**: 100,064 instances take 4.8 to 6.4 ms of CPU to record and finish (one run 15.3 ms) and 1.1 to 1.4 ms in `submit`, then 3.7 to 4.8 ms of GPU; 10,064 instances take 0.5 to 0.75 ms, 0.14 to 0.17 ms and 0.8 to 1.1 ms. A `sample` profile puts the CPU time in wgpu-core converting and copying every instance when the encoder finishes and when the build is recorded. Epic measured 2 ms to build 100K instances and 13 ms for 1.5M, platform not stated ([RTOW slide 34][rtow]).
- Against ADR 0013's 12 ms of GPU and 6 ms of CPU: one bounce per pixel in the open scene takes a third of the GPU budget before shading or denoising, a 2.1M-ray probe update a quarter, and a 100k-instance TLAS rebuild about the whole CPU budget.

## Not verified

- Published costs were not re-measured, and no scaling to the M4 Pro is implied. The measured scene is procedural and has no materials, so its figures are lower bounds for shading; other sessions ran on the machine during the runs.
- Slides not read (members-only or unpublished): Far Cry 3 (GDC 2012), The Division (GDC 2016), Snowdrop ray tracing for Avatar and Star Wars Outlaws (GDC 2024, [abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034763/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Raytracing-in)), Cyberpunk 2077 Overdrive (GDC 2023, 2024; NVIDIA's page shows a truncated abstract), Alan Wake 2 path tracing (GDC 2024; absent from Remedy's slide page).
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance II's use of SVOGI: press reports only; the Crytek quote concerns the first game. Decima and Horizon: no primary source on their GI found. The Witcher 4 tech demo ran on PS5 at 60 fps "with raytracing" ([Epic](https://web.archive.org/web/20260606012601/https://www.unrealengine.com/news/all-the-big-news-and-announcements-from-the-state-of-unreal-2025)); no primary source names its GI technique.
- The GTAO PDF at Activision's URL is technical memo ATVI-TR-19-01, a revision of the 2016 work.
- Contexts the sources leave open: the GPU behind Battlefield V's 6.29 ms; the platform of The Matrix Awakens' build timings; the resolution of Split Radiance Cascades' Table 2; the methods behind SVOGI's and NRD's figures (vendor).
- Bevy Solari timings are from 0.17; none for 0.20 were found.
- RTXGI's repository now holds NRC and SHaRC; DDGI is in RTXGI-DDGI (last pushed 2024-02-05). Licences were read for grant and distribution terms only.
