# Living open worlds: what Rockstar's titles show, and what it asks of an engine

Researched 2026-11-21 for ticket #954 (parent #944, the world-systems track). It reads Grand Theft Auto V (2013; PlayStation 4, Xbox One and PC versions 2014 and 2015), Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018) and Grand Theft Auto VI system by system: what the player sees, how it is made where a source says, and what it would ask of Praxis. It surveys no technique and chooses none. It ends in lines proposed for the ladder's map (#17) and in the costs no source publishes, each named as a prototype's question.

**Summary.** Rockstar says what its worlds do and almost never how. Its own people describe a mechanism for a minority of the 23 systems below, most of them in a sentence, and give numbers for two: locomotion in Red Dead Redemption 2, and traffic in the titles before Grand Theft Auto IV. Every count of people simulated, every tier radius and every CPU cost in this note comes from another studio, named as that studio's.

- **Grand Theft Auto VI is out.** Rockstar's site, read on 2026-11-21, dates its release 19 November 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Its makers have published five videos, among them a 26-minute *Extended Look* (27 August 2026), and have given interviews (Rob Nelson, Aaron Garbut, Phil Hooker, David Hynd). No talk, paper or technical post by the studio was found. Fifteen rows rest in part on VI; none of them says how VI is made beyond a reporter's account of traits assigned to pedestrians.
- **What reads as alive is authored content at a very large count, laid over a few engine capabilities.** The counts are Rockstar's: about 55,000 animations in V, 300,000 in Red Dead Redemption 2, over 600,000 in VI; 500,000 lines of dialogue in Red Dead Redemption 2. The capabilities are other studios': a small, capped set of fully simulated individuals near the player (40 to 70) with cheaper tiers behind (hundreds, then thousands at tens of kilobytes each); behaviour chosen from data when something happens, not run every frame; an identity that outlives the entity; a handful of scalars per character for wetness and dirt.
- **Four things bind a decision Gameplay core has not made**: the simulation tiers and their caps, an identity for placed and spawned objects, the per-Tick Budget, and the form a crowd takes in the dynamic TLAS. At 16 Shadow proxies a figure, ADR 0020's 10,000 Moving Instances hold 625 figures and nothing else. Each published cost of one subsystem (physics 2.1 ms, AI 5 ms, a crowd 5 ms) is as large as the 1.84 to 2.26 ms a Tick leaves for all of them.
- **Nothing here rebuilds what M5 took or M6 is about to take.** One cook filter touches M5 (a Placement that can move must stay out of its Chunk's Far proxy), and M6 gains two checks (ray cost with the dynamic TLAS full, and Interior volumes at a city's count). Neither asks for work ahead of M6.
- **One tension to settle**: the sources assign simulation detail by a time budget. ADR 0022's fixed schedule reads no wall clock, so in Praxis every such budget has to be a count, and `ROADMAP.md`'s "frame-budget aware" coroutine scheduler needs that reading too.
- **Observation only, for want of a source**: sweat as its own input, blood and wetness on Rockstar's characters, body change over time, faces, NPC schedules in all three titles, spawn radii and simulated counts in V, Red Dead Redemption 2 and VI, how witnesses' knowledge is stored and spreads, what persists and for how long, animal populations, prop and debris budgets.

Sources: Rockstar's site and Newswire; interviews with named Rockstar developers (2013 to 2026); one GDC talk and one SIGGRAPH course talk by Rockstar staff; posts and interviews by a former Rockstar North technical director, on earlier titles; named frame studies and technical reviews (Courrèges, NVIDIA's guide, Digital Foundry); and, where Rockstar is silent, talks, papers, documentation and developers' own posts from Ubisoft, Warhorse, IO Interactive, Guerrilla, Naughty Dog, CD Projekt RED, Epic, Valve, GSC, 2K Czech, Insomniac, Bethesda's editor wiki and others, with the free chapters of *Game AI Pro*. Each claim relied on is in `docs/references.toml`, cited by key; every quote there was matched by script against a saved copy of its page, slide deck or paper on 2026-11-21. Excluded: coverage whose content is a report of a patent filing (much public talk about unreleased Rockstar technology is of that kind; none was read and none is named), anything from leaked code or footage, and press speculation. No engine source under a restrictive licence was read; Unreal and Unity appear through their documentation only, and Jolt (MIT) through its documentation and its author's talk, not its source. Nothing was measured in this repo.

Grades, per row: **1** primary (the studio's own talk, paper, post or page, or a named developer's quoted words); **2** analysis (a frame study or technical breakdown by a named author, or a named reporter's account of what a studio showed); **3** observation (what the shipped game or an official video shows). A claim about how something is made rests on grade 1 or 2 and names the studio it is true of. "Stand-in" marks another studio's work brought in where Rockstar is silent: it is evidence of how such a system has been built, not of how Rockstar built it.

Boundaries: audio keeps its line on #17 and is not surveyed, though talk (row 15) says what it would ask. How skin or hair is shaded gets a row and a pointer. Techniques for skeletal animation, AI and navigation are not compared; the note says what a living world asks of them.

## Grand Theft Auto VI on the day of writing

Read on 2026-11-21, Rockstar's page for the game lists an "Out Now" video dated November 19, 2026, and its Newswire post announcing that the game "is now available worldwide for PlayStation 5 and XBOX Series X|S" carries the same date there [ref:rockstar-2026-gta6-now-available]. The site lists what the studio has shown: Trailer 1 (4 December 2023), Trailer 2 (6 May 2025), *An Extended Look* (27 August 2026, "Captured entirely from in-game footage on the PlayStation 5"), a launch trailer (15 October 2026) and the "Out Now" spot [ref:rockstar-2026-gta6-videos]. The post names no other platform.

Beyond the videos, what its makers have published is interviews. Rob Nelson, co-studio head of Rockstar North, played the game for IGN and for TROISCOULEURS, in previews published in late August 2026 [ref:weber-2026-ign-gta6-preview] [ref:weber-2026-ign-nelson-interview] [ref:rouillon-2026-troiscouleurs-gta6-preview]; Aaron Garbut spoke to The New York Times, whose page refused the fetch and is read here through a relay [ref:techradar-2026-garbut-animation-counts]; Nelson, Garbut and Phil Hooker spoke to Edge for its November cover story [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-pedestrians]; David Hynd, vice president of technology, answered written questions [ref:techradar-2026-hynd-wanted-system]. Digital Foundry published a review and two analyses between 18 and 20 November [ref:linneman-2026-df-gta6-review] [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-cpu] [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-police]. No talk, paper or technical post by Rockstar on VI was found, and the *Extended Look*'s narration could not be opened as text.

Rows 1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 21 and 23 rest in part on VI. What those sources establish is behaviour, three counts (over 600,000 animations, against about 55,000 and 300,000; a team in Los Angeles for pedestrians; "an order of magnitude more interiors" [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-interiors]), and one design history: Nelson says V's simulation could not be reworked ("You couldn't tear it apart and get in there and rework the traffic system or the law system"), that Red Dead Redemption 2 rebuilt the open world around "crime, witnesses, NPC behaviour and player interaction", and that VI carries that to a city's density [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-simulation-rebuilt]. He gives one sentence on hardware: the game takes "advantage of all the memory that we have on the latest generation of consoles" [ref:weber-2026-ign-gta6-preview]. Digital Foundry's reading of the shipped game is that its crowds and its streaming are bound by the CPU: when a crowd panics, "these CPU-based calculations quickly overwhelm the frame-time budget" [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-cpu]. That is an analyst's inference from frame rates, and the only published statement found on what VI's living world costs.

## On a character

**1. Sweat, dirt, mud, wetness and blood.** Asked whether the grime, sweat and mud on Arthur Morgan's clothes change in real time, Nelson answered that there is a system, that it was "essentially" done by hand and built in "according to the situations", that the game counts how long clothes have been worn, and that rolling in mud dirties them [ref:jeuxactu-2018-nelson-dirt-authored-hair-by-subtraction]. That sentence, read in a French outlet's rendering, is the whole of what Rockstar has said on the subject; for VI there is a reporter's "the sweat on your skin after a sprint" [ref:rouillon-2026-troiscouleurs-gta6-preview], which is observation. Naughty Dog publishes the inputs. Uncharted 4's wetness is one function of two values, a wetness and an optional porosity for which roughness stands in, and darkens by a blend toward the squared base colour [ref:brinck-2016-u4-wetness-and-porosity]; in The Last of Us Part II it is one parameter applied to the final BRDF values, from damp to standing water [ref:brinck-2020-tlou2-wetness-one-parameter], and a wet character adds one authored mask from which the shader derives a second normal used for specular only [ref:brinck-2020-tlou2-wet-skin-mask-and-specular-normal]. Its character dirt changes colour, normal and roughness [ref:brinck-2020-tlou2-character-dirt-package]. Blood that must outlast its particles is written into a render target each object owns [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-blood-render-target-persistence], and up to 16,000 drip particles on a character update in about 0.04 ms because they live in screen space [ref:kovalovs-2020-tlou2-drip-cost]. Sucker Punch stores wet, bloody, muddy and snowy state in a grid deformed around the character instead of in its textures [ref:bentley-2025-yotei-directional-grid], and Infinity Ward dries each object at a rate set by its material type [ref:activision-2022-mw2-wetness-dries-by-material]. No source gives the size of such a texture or grid, says how dirt or blood washes off, or treats sweat as an input of its own.

**2. Wear and growth.** Hair and beards grow in Red Dead Redemption 2 and can only be cut shorter, "by subtraction", unlike V's barber [ref:jeuxactu-2018-nelson-dirt-authored-hair-by-subtraction]; Digital Foundry observes the same [ref:df-2018-rdr2-cloth-muscle-hair-on-characters]. The one account of a mechanism is CD Projekt's for The Witcher 3, relayed twice: a few discrete states that time and travel advance [ref:platkow-gilewski-2015-witcher3-beard-states]. Body weight and muscle changing with play have no developer source in any title read.

**3. Hair, cloth and muscle.** Digital Foundry reads VI as running "a cutting-edge, physics-driven strand hair system across all characters", every bystander included [ref:linneman-2026-df-gta6-review], and reads Red Dead Redemption 2's folding cloth and horse muscle as animated normal maps [ref:df-2018-rdr2-cloth-muscle-hair-on-characters]. Both are an analyst's reading of the image; Rockstar confirms neither. Where studios publish, strand hair is priced by the head: MachineGames budgeted 2 ms of GPU on its slowest console for about a dozen characters [ref:kulikov-2025-indiana-jones-strand-hair-budget] and holds a frame to 2 million segments, without which seven hair models fit [ref:kulikov-2025-indiana-jones-strand-hair-lod]; Capcom spends 3.6 to 4.0 ms on a PlayStation 5 in a cutscene and removes 70% of strands in play [ref:capcom-2023-re4-strand-hair-cost]. Frostbite's character cloth costs 0.4 ms per 30 Hz step on one thread [ref:lewin-2018-frostbite-cloth-cost]. How skin and hair are shaded is outside this note; the pre-mortem's Blocks section holds what strands would cost ADR 0008.

**4. Faces.** Rockstar names "facial and body animation" in a list of what Red Dead Redemption 2 upgraded and says no more [ref:thr-2018-bass-gtav-ps3-concepts-rdr2-persistence]. Crytek's hero face in Ryse had 260 joints and 230 corrective shapes, the shapes gone past two metres, then fewer joints and fewer influences with distance [ref:evans-2014-ryse-face-rig-and-distance-lod]. Naughty Dog's gameplay faces are picked by a system from about 20 emotional states for 25 key characters [ref:paciello-2020-tlou2-systemic-facial-counts]. No studio publishes its crowd's face rig.

**5. Bodies that react.** Hooker places Euphoria: it handles the physics-based reactions of people and animals (a rider falling from a shot horse, dragged or trapped) and is one part of character simulation, the animation system around it having been rebuilt [ref:vg247-2018-hooker-euphoria-is-one-part]. Its vendor described it, for Grand Theft Auto IV, as animation synthesised on the CPU at run time from a simulation of the body [ref:naturalmotion-2007-euphoria-in-gta4]. Respawn drives ragdoll bodies toward animation through joint motors and falls back to a free ragdoll when the target cannot be reached [ref:waszak-2020-jedi-fallen-order-physical-animation]. The only cost per character is from research: 340 µs for a fully simulated humanoid of 23 bodies on one thread of an Intel Xeon E5-1650 V3, 200 µs of it physics [ref:bergamin-2019-drecon-cost-per-character]. Six such characters would fill a Tick's whole allowance (computed).

**6. Movement that reads the ground.** This is the one system Rockstar has presented. Red Dead Redemption 2 began by unifying V's separate movement systems for people, animals and horses [ref:kleanthous-2021-movement-systems-unified]. Locomotion is chosen by data: about 210 archetypes, 2,500 motion types, 6,300 motions and 1,800 conditions [ref:kleanthous-2021-locomotion-data-counts]. The controller is integrated forward to predict a path, checked with the navigation mesh and physics casts, and what it finds (the surface's name, the depth of snow, water or mud, the wind) is written to a blackboard that picks animation [ref:kleanthous-2021-terrain-analysis-blackboard]. Horses alone take about 3,000 animations across 23 styles, "Already a big runtime budget" [ref:kleanthous-2021-horse-animation-budget]. The counts have grown with each title, to 300,000 animations in Red Dead Redemption 2 [ref:vulture-2018-animation-and-dialogue-counts] and over 600,000 in VI against V's 55,000 [ref:techradar-2026-garbut-animation-counts], and Garbut said of V that its variety depended on streaming animations in and out [ref:techradar-2013-garbut-animation-variety-through-streaming]. CD Projekt RED streams 3,000 to 4,000 animations inside 40 MB [ref:tremblay-2024-cp2077-crowd-animation].

## Ambient population

**7. Pedestrians.** Rockstar calls it the ambient population system, and says V's pedestrians had few reactions that worked together [ref:jeuxactu-2018-nelson-ambient-population-system]. What V holds at run time is "the immediate area around the player, plus mission objects and so on, and the nav mesh for AI" [ref:mcv-2013-hooker-everything-streamed-around-player]; NVIDIA's guide finds its pedestrians "almost always constrained to short and medium range" [ref:nvidia-2015-gtav-distant-vehicles-as-blobs]. Red Dead Redemption 2 changed the requirement from a crowd to a town of individuals each doing something [ref:vg247-2018-schilstra-crowd-versus-individuals]. In VI Nelson speaks of "the density of population" [ref:weber-2026-ign-nelson-interview] and Digital Foundry sees pedestrian crowds kept "right up to the horizon" [ref:linneman-2026-df-gta6-review], dozens of individuals in a dense place [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-cpu]. None of this gives a count or a radius. The stand-ins do. Assassin's Creed Unity put 10,000 crowd members on screen behind a limit of 40 real AIs and 120 high-resolution models [ref:cournoyer-2015-acu-crowd-limits]. Hitman: Absolution simulated 1,200 agents with 500 on screen for 5 ms of a PlayStation 3's main processor, about 20 ms across its co-processors and 8 ms of GPU [ref:fauerby-2012-hitman-crowd-cost], at about 256 bytes an agent [ref:fauerby-2012-hitman-agent-memory], and promoted an agent to a full NPC by lending it one of a small pool [ref:fauerby-2012-hitman-possession]. Kingdom Come: Deliverance II keeps 2,400 named NPCs simulated at all times on two 4 km maps and unloads none [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-scope]; its city holds 1,400 at 60 frames a second under a cap of 70 visible, with the streets shaped to hide the cap [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-city-density]. For removal, Mafia II takes a pedestrian out when it is too far, unseen for too long and not in an action [ref:kratochvil-2010-mafia2-pedestrian-spawning]; Sunshine-Hill's rule is distance or time unseen, longer for a character the player has noticed [ref:sunshine-hill-2013-deleting-characters]; Watch Dogs: Legion stores a persistent character's data on despawn and attaches it to an entity with the same ID on respawn [ref:dragert-2021-wdl-loading-bubble]. Cyberpunk 2077 at launch shows what the player sees when the rule is only "out of view" [ref:ehrhardt-2020-cyberpunk-deletes-out-of-view].

**8. Traffic.** The only distances Rockstar's line has ever published are for Grand Theft Auto III, Vice City and San Andreas: cars created about 70 m from the player where they would be in view, removed at about 90 m, and from San Andreas only after two seconds off screen [ref:vermeij-2024-traffic-created-70m-removed-90m]; past 20 m a car ran reduced physics with no collision, switching to full physics on approach and never back [ref:vermeij-2024-reduced-physics-beyond-20m]. For V there are two analyses: distant headlights are 32×32 quads that move along the roads with no car drawn [ref:courreges-2015-gtav-distant-headlights-are-quads], and vehicles can spawn as "blobs" out to the view distance, to be removed or made full models on approach [ref:nvidia-2015-gtav-distant-vehicles-as-blobs]. The newer versions of V added traffic and wildlife without touching behaviour [ref:rockstar-2014-gtav-newgen-wildlife-and-traffic] [ref:vg247-2018-hooker-gtav-newgen-ai-left-alone]. VI has buses and trains that Hooker rides to check "they always turn up" [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-transit-and-studios]. How a traffic system is built comes from elsewhere. Mafia II's cars spawn moving, parked or reused from across the city, and are never removed in view [ref:kratochvil-2010-mafia2-car-spawning]; its whole road map is splines in about 400 KB that never stream, and its drivers know that map and nearby objects but not the physics scene [ref:kratochvil-2010-mafia2-roadmap-and-driver]. Angel Studios ran ambient cars on lane splines and gave one full physics only when it hit something, until it came to rest [ref:adzima-2001-midtown-rails-and-physics]. Epic's city demo holds 45,073 parked cars, 38,146 of them drivable, and 1,248 simulated intersections [ref:epic-2021-matrix-awakens-city-counts]. No source gives the number of vehicles simulated at once, the share on full physics or its CPU time.

**9. Animals.** Rockstar's page for Red Dead Redemption 2 counts around 200 species with herds, pack hunters and scavengers that find carrion [ref:rockstar-2018-wildlife-200-species-ecosystem], and Hooker describes prey fleeing predators and predators fighting passing riders with no player involved [ref:vg247-2018-hooker-predators-prey-and-ambient-riders]. The one mechanism published is a horse's agitation: a small utility system of motivations raised by stimuli, with a baseline per breed [ref:kleanthous-2021-agitation-utility-system]. Whether animal populations are simulated or spawned, Rockstar does not say. Warhorse spawns animals with no identity and nothing referring to them, which halves its count of simulated NPCs [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-animals-and-battles]; Avalanche paints a habitat per species and moves herds between need zones on a schedule, a leader deciding [ref:cederskoog-2017-thehunter-herds]; Guerrilla, by an analyst's account, makes the herd itself an agent and has one manager spawn every machine [ref:thompson-2019-hzd-collective].

**10. Variety.** NVIDIA's guide reads V's "Population Variety" setting as the number of distinct pedestrian and vehicle models held in video memory: it costs memory, not frame time [ref:nvidia-2015-gtav-population-variety-is-memory]. The earlier titles kept a small set of vehicle models in memory and phased one out for another by area [ref:vermeij-2024-vehicle-models-phased-by-area]. For VI the studio's account is the opposite of assembly from parts: "We art-directed each individual one of them" [ref:techradar-2026-garbut-animation-counts], by a team in Los Angeles set up for it, with districts given their own "demographic and professional textures" [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-pedestrians]. Hitman: Absolution varied a crowd with a 5% scale and swapped diffuse textures, and gave each visible agent its own blended pose once shared clips looked robotic [ref:fauerby-2012-hitman-variety-and-animation]. Watch Dogs: Legion generates a profile of 19 fields from 919 values under 18,825 relations in 0.6 ms at worst [ref:dragert-2021-wdl-census-cost].

**11. Simulation by distance and by sight.** This is where Rockstar is most silent and other studios most exact. Assassin's Creed Origins tracks every person and animal in one of three states: *real* within 80 m and updated every frame; *bulk*, updated every other frame in sight and once a second out of it; *virtual*, out of sight and updated every 5 to 15 seconds [ref:ubisoft-2018-aco-three-states]. S.T.A.L.K.E.R. kept every character alive in one of two modes, full detail within about 150 m [ref:iassenev-2008-stalker-online-offline]. Kingdom Come: Deliverance II has three: *Close*, 70 visible NPCs to 150 m; *Middle*, up to 400 NPCs or 600 m, with full AI and exact position but no visible animation; *Far*, the rest [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-three-lods]. A Far NPC keeps a position good to about 10 m, has its behaviour selected from time of day and its own properties but not executed, moves along paths only and perceives nothing [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-far-lod]; it gives back 150 kB and keeps under 50 kB, about a quarter [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-far-lod-memory]. The tier is assigned by "Budget, not pure distance", a switch runs in stages and waits on resource streaming [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-lod-selection]. Warhorse's earlier paper gives the catch-up: an unseen NPC is updated rarely and each update carries the time elapsed, so actions that fit inside it take effect at once [ref:plch-2014-lod-delta-catch-up]. Hitman's 300-odd behaviour-tree NPCs update less often with distance [ref:thompson-2019-hitman-ai-lod-and-crowd]; Unreal's Mass gives each of four levels a distance and a maximum entity count [ref:epic-mass-lod-max-counts]; Sunshine-Hill's allocator picks a level per feature per character against a CPU budget for 57 µs a frame and 48 bytes an entity [ref:sunshinehill-2013-lod-trader-cost]. For VI there is only the analyst's note that a panicking crowd raises the AI load until frames drop [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-cpu].

## Behaviour

**12. Routines and schedules.** Garbut describes Red Dead Redemption 2's townspeople "going about their business", building houses and selling papers, as systemic parts of the world joined to scripted ones [ref:thr-2018-garbut-townspeople-and-systemic-plus-scripted], and Rockstar's own video shows the camp eating, sleeping and working [ref:rockstar-2018-gameplay-video-camp-and-witness]. No source says how either is scheduled. The one Rockstar statement on schedules is that Grand Theft Auto IV tried a home, a schedule and a commute for every NPC and dropped it: "There were so many bugs" [ref:vermeij-2026-gta4-npc-schedules-abandoned]. Others shipped them. Oblivion's producer said all of its 1,000-plus NPCs had 24-hour schedules [ref:carter-2005-oblivion-schedules]. Warhorse budgeted its whole AI at 5 ms a frame on one core and measured 30 NPCs on production day-cycle trees at 0.96 ms mean with every NPC updated every frame [ref:plch-2014-ai-budget-5ms]; the first game shipped about 600 NPCs, drawn to 60 to 90 m [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd1-naive-lod], and its AI update passed 10 ms and moved to its own thread; in the second, with 2,400, the update's parts sum to about 5.85 ms [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd-ai-update-cost]. Watch Dogs: Legion fills a day of one-hour slots from 4,143 tagged locations and 96,100 activities, with 38 MB of cached queries [ref:dragert-2021-wdl-schedules]. Project Highrise runs up to 1,000 NPCs at 60 frames a second on authored daily scripts in place of a planner [ref:zubek-2017-highrise-daily-scripts]. Two papers say how to run them: things that mostly wait belong in a queue keyed by due time, so a frame looks only at its front [ref:graham-2021-event-based-updates]; and Warhorse told its scripters to use Lua "as little as possible", since entering it was expensive [ref:plch-2014-lua-as-little-as-possible].

**13. Reactions.** In V, Nelson says, a pedestrian did not react to a shotgun produced in front of them until it was aimed, and "That barely held" [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-simulation-rebuilt]; Hooker's reason it was not fixed for the newer consoles is that V's behaviour was "such a complex mixture of animation, AI, and dialogue that we couldn't unravel it" [ref:vg247-2018-hooker-gtav-newgen-ai-left-alone]. Red Dead Redemption 2 has a saloon stop, look and appraise the player [ref:vg247-2018-hynd-witnesses-and-saloon-appraisal]. In VI pedestrians notice how a weapon is carried [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-witnesses] and notice an eavesdropper; the interaction system "is returning and evolved from Red Dead Redemption 2" and took work "in design, in code behaviors, in animation, in writing" [ref:weber-2026-ign-nelson-interview]; they may cower or escalate [ref:weber-2026-ign-gta6-preview], more often armed outside the city by an analyst's observation [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-police]. The nearest thing to a mechanism is Edge's account that pedestrians "are assigned personality traits that help determine how they respond: temperament, aggression, where they are, what they have seen, and how the player has behaved towards them" [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-pedestrians], which is a reporter's sentence, not a developer's. Stand-ins: Mafia III's perception is typed sight and sound events that a detector on each NPC picks up in range [ref:holba-2021-mafia3-perception-events]; Final Fantasy XV's invisible "smart locations" query for nearby NPCs and cast them into the roles of a small script [ref:skubch-2017-ffxv-smart-locations]; Warhorse's ambient "situations" choose 2 to 5 NPCs by constraint solving under 100 µs on average and never over 1 ms [ref:cerny-2014-situations-budget]; Watch Dogs 2, by an analyst's account, runs on over 700 hand-written reaction rules weighted by five traits [ref:thompson-2020-wd2-reaction-rules].

**14. Witnesses, memory and consequences.** In Red Dead Redemption 2 a witness runs and can be chased [ref:vg247-2018-hynd-witnesses-and-saloon-appraisal] or intimidated into silence [ref:rockstar-2018-gameplay-video-camp-and-witness]. In VI "The simulation is attentive, too, to whether anyone sees what you are doing", and a "heat" record holds what the police have gathered: description, partner, clothes, cameras, weapon [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-witnesses]. Hynd says the law system saw "lots and lots of iteration" [ref:techradar-2026-hynd-wanted-system]. None of it says where a witness's knowledge is kept, how it spreads or how long it lasts. Valve keeps memory as a table of key and value facts per character and one for the world, a fact carrying an optional expiry [ref:ruskin-2012-dialog-memory]. Warhorse gives a crime its own representation, which pulls the far NPCs it concerns up a tier [ref:smrcek-2026-kcd2-far-lod]. Bethesda's editor documentation warns what remembering costs: a reference kept persistent is not unloaded with its area and goes on costing processor time and memory [ref:ck-papyrus-persistence-cost].

**15. Talk.** Red Dead Redemption 2 has 500,000 lines of dialogue and 1,200 actors, 700 of them speaking, in a feature writer's count [ref:vulture-2018-animation-and-dialogue-counts], and a Rockstar writer says each pedestrian has an 80-page script [ref:vulture-2018-jones-peds-80-page-scripts]. VI had a team in Los Angeles for it [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-pedestrians]. How a line is picked, Rockstar does not say. Valve does: the speaker's situation is a set of facts, each rule a list of criteria, and the rule matching the most criteria wins, so specific lines override general ones [ref:ruskin-2012-dialog-rule-matching]. The Left 4 Dead series had over ten thousand lines; a character polls for idle talk every few seconds; with rules bucketed to about fifty a lookup takes under a microsecond, and a region's rules can stream with its level [ref:ruskin-2012-dialog-volume-and-cost].

**16. Groups.** The gang camp is Rockstar's showpiece of group life [ref:rockstar-2018-gameplay-video-camp-and-witness] and has no technical source. A herd in Horizon Zero Dawn is an agent of its own that shares facts with its members and assigns them roles, by an analyst's account of Guerrilla's talk [ref:thompson-2019-hzd-collective]; Final Fantasy XV casts whoever is near into a script's roles, families together [ref:skubch-2017-ffxv-smart-locations].

## World dynamics

**17. Time and weather.** Rockstar's SIGGRAPH talk covers Red Dead Redemption 2's weather as rendering (time of day, weather cycles and climate regions inside a 30 frames a second budget) and lists "Localized weather" under future work [ref:bauer-2019-time-of-day-weather-cycles-climate-regions]. On the behaviour side, the depth of snow or mud and the wind reach a horse's animation through its blackboard [ref:kleanthous-2021-terrain-analysis-blackboard]. VI is described as "much more granular": clouds shaped by wind, humidity and season; a storm visible from sunshine and driven into; roads that wet, buildings that drip and "surfaces dry at different rates"; pedestrians "seeking shelter as weather deteriorates" [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-weather]. No source in any studio's hand says how weather reaches behaviour; Assassin's Creed Origins' needs follow the day and night cycle only [ref:ubisoft-2018-aco-three-states].

**18. Events without the player.** Rockstar's developers describe the effect (predators attacking riders unprompted [ref:vg247-2018-hooker-predators-prey-and-ambient-riders], a world where the player cannot tell mission from system [ref:thr-2018-garbut-townspeople-and-systemic-plus-scripted]) and one fact of organisation: in VI the San Diego studio "helped drive the development of ambient and interactive events" [ref:parkin-2026-edge-gta6-transit-and-studios]. Digital Foundry sees pedestrians "spontaneously starting altercations after traffic accidents" [ref:linneman-2026-df-gta6-review]. GSC published how its world went on off screen: characters beyond the radius travel a coarse graph that links the levels, fight by a formula and trade, and the player hears of it as news [ref:iassenev-2008-stalker-offline-events]. It also replaced each character's needs with "smart terrains", places that hand out a task and hold the character for a while [ref:iassenev-2008-stalker-smart-terrains]. Epic says its city demo's characters and traffic are not placed by where the player looks but belong to a global simulation [ref:epic-2021-matrix-awakens-global-simulation]. Guerrilla confines anything a script spawns to an encounter circle of at most 125 m, activated within 200 m of it, with collision guaranteed under it [ref:rouwe-2022-hfw-scene-radii].

**19. Persistence.** Rockstar names "persistence" among Red Dead Redemption 2's aims [ref:thr-2018-bass-gtav-ps3-concepts-rdr2-persistence] and draws its limit: structures burn, but a forest fire was ruled out because a lasting change would alter a hand-made map [ref:ign-2018-nelson-no-forest-fires-handcrafted-world]. Digital Foundry observes trails in snow and mud that persist "a long way behind the player" [ref:df-2018-rdr2-snow-and-mud-trails-persist]. What is kept, for how long and under what key is unpublished for all three titles. The stand-ins come from one engine family and from individual developers. Bethesda's editor wiki documents resets: a location restores itself 30 game days after the player left it cleared, or 10 otherwise, and only on re-entry [ref:ck-cell-reset-timers]; creatures, items, doors and containers come back, while destructibles, script-made references and dropped items do not [ref:ck-cell-reset-what-resets]; and a value already in a save wins over a patched master file [ref:ck-save-versus-patched-masterfile]. Obsidian's Josh Sawyer describes that engine's save as every changed placed instance stored as a difference, all of it resident, with saves near 19 MB hurting the game [ref:sawyer-2011-save-as-differences]. A developer of Watch Dogs: Legion's save data separates spatial persistence (leave and return) from session persistence (quit and reload) [ref:horneman-2024-spatial-and-session-persistence]. On keys: Unity's stable object identifier exists in the editor only [ref:unity-globalobjectid-editor-only], and Jolt's saved physics state is valid only while bodies keep the same IDs [ref:jolt-docs-state-snapshot-and-body-ids]. No source gives bytes per saved object.

**20. Economies and ecologies.** Red Dead Redemption 2's camp morale follows its supplies [ref:rockstar-2018-gameplay-video-camp-and-witness]; nothing says whether its animal numbers respond to hunting. Every source that simulated needs in full reports cutting it back. Ultima Online gave each creature three rules over tagged resources; the searches and pathfinding cost too much, creatures were put to sleep away from players, and the ecology went with them [ref:koster-2006-uo-ecology-ai-and-cost]; its designer's remedy is level of detail by area, storing individuals and computing the population forward on return [ref:koster-2006-uo-lessons-lod]. Project Highrise built hunger and tiredness per NPC and removed them [ref:zubek-2017-highrise-needs-removed]. S.T.A.L.K.E.R. replaced needs with places (row 18).

## Things

**21. Props.** In Red Dead Redemption 2, Rockstar's lead physics programmer says, animation and physics are joined closely enough that a wagon body "can break into hundreds of separate pieces" and brush moves aside [ref:vg247-2018-lyons-animation-physics-coupling]; in VI an analyst sees bottles drain, glass cases shatter and shelves collapse [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-police]. Neither gives a count of bodies. Guerrilla does: Horizon Forbidden West holds about 20,000 physics bodies loaded across 28 tiles and tens of Scenes [ref:rouwe-2022-hfw-20k-bodies]; adding a streamed section's bodies stalled 5 to 10 ms in its previous engine and is now a batch prepared off the main thread [ref:rouwe-2022-hfw-add-remove-cost]; its worst-case physics update went from 3.3 ms on average to 2.1 ms while stepping twice as often [ref:rouwe-2022-hfw-physics-time]. Jolt, which is MIT-licensed, sleeps whole islands of resting bodies at no simulation cost [ref:jolt-docs-sleeping-islands] and adds a section's bodies in one prepared batch that can be abandoned [ref:jolt-streaming-batches-and-licence]. Remedy held Control to 200 active rigid bodies or fewer, by a Japanese report of its talk [ref:richter-2020-control-200-active-bodies].

**22. What is left behind.** The cheapest liveliness on record is Vermeij's: litter drawn along in the slipstream of cars in Grand Theft Auto III, a large effect for little cost, set against his ranking that a pedestrian walking into a wall breaks the illusion more than a poor model does [ref:vermeij-2026-litter-and-what-breaks-the-illusion]. Tracks in Red Dead Redemption 2 are observed, not explained [ref:df-2018-rdr2-snow-and-mud-trails-persist]. Santa Monica carves snow displacement on the GPU every frame from the characters' capsules, so anything with a body leaves a track [ref:surricchio-2023-gowr-snow-carving]. The only stated rule for retiring marks is Source's: one variable caps the number of decals [ref:valve-source-decal-cap]. No open-world title publishes a budget for decals, debris or bodies left lying.

**23. Streaming under a moving population.** Courrèges reads V's driving and flying speeds as set by what streaming can serve [ref:courreges-2015-gtav-streaming-bounds-player-speed], which is his inference. In VI, Digital Foundry finds frame drops in fast travel and puts them on the CPU "organising these incoming assets", and on "Coordinating bounding volume hierarchies (BVHs) and ray-traced acceleration structures for dense city blocks, moving traffic and pedestrians" [ref:judd-2026-df-gta6-cpu]; again an analyst's reading. Guerrilla states its own layout: 256 m tiles, a 3×3 block at full detail, a ring of medium tiles around it that carries full-detail collision, one more row loaded ahead of the player, and no collision beyond [ref:rouwe-2022-hfw-tiles-and-collision]. Insomniac's city is about 800 tiles of 128 m, one new tile a second at top speed on a PlayStation 4 [ref:fitzgerald-2018-spiderman-tile-per-second]. CD Projekt RED lets no job chain in a frame wait on resource streaming, and measures its whole CPU frame, vehicles and NPCs included, at 10.2 to 16.1 ms on a laptop Core i9 depending on worker count [ref:tremblay-2024-cp2077-frame-and-streaming]. No source says what becomes of a moving NPC or vehicle when the ground under it unloads.

## What it asks of Praxis

**Entities, components and Lua (ADRs 0006, 0009, 0012).** The counts are modest for an ECS: 2,400 persistent people, 20,000 bodies, 45,000 parked cars. The asks are about shape. A far-tier NPC is pure data already (a position, a schedule entry, a few facts, under 50 kB in Warhorse's engine and 256 bytes for a crowd agent in IO's), which is ADR 0006's form and saves as it stands. Behaviour at this count is tables read by systems: schedules, reaction rules and dialogue rules are data in every source that publishes them, and the one studio that measured Lua told its scripters to enter it as little as possible. That fits ADR 0012: Lua handles what a schedule or a stimulus fires, and authors neither. `ROADMAP.md` lists "NPC behaviors" among what lives in Lua; at a population's count that can mean the content of a behaviour, not its selection per NPC per Tick.

**Budgets must be counts.** Warhorse assigns tiers by budget, Sunshine-Hill's allocator trades detail against CPU time each frame, and `ROADMAP.md` plans a coroutine scheduler that is "Frame-budget aware so a Lua loop can't starve the renderer". Under ADR 0022 the fixed schedule reads no wall clock and the same inputs must give the same state. A tier assigned, or a coroutine deferred, because a frame ran long would break that. In Praxis each such budget has to be a count: entities per tier, updates per Tick in an order fixed by identity, Lua instructions per Tick.

**Moving Instances and the crowd's form (ADR 0020, #17's line).** The owner picked rigid per-bone Shadow proxies for skinned figures: `crowd`'s 100 figures of 16 bones are 1,600 Moving Instances and update in about 0.30 ms of CPU (`docs/performance.md`, The crowd's two forms). At 16 proxies a figure the cap of 10,000 holds 625 figures with no vehicle or prop beside them (computed). Warhorse's 70 visible NPCs would take 1,120, Ubisoft's 120 high-resolution models 1,920, IO's 500 on screen 8,000 (computed). So the published near tiers fit and a crowd does not, unless proxies per figure fall with distance or far figures cast no traced shadow. No source prices that, since no source traces shadows for a crowd this way. Vehicles and awake props draw on the same 10,000.

**The per-Tick Budget (#17's line, ADR 0022).** A Tick has 1.84 to 2.26 ms on the M4 Pro for physics, animation, AI and Lua together. Against that: Guerrilla's physics update averages 2.1 ms in its worst case on hardware its talk does not name; Warhorse's AI was budgeted at 5 ms on one core and runs at about 5.85 ms on a thread of its own; IO's crowd took 5 ms of a PlayStation 3's main processor and four times that on its co-processors; one fully physical character costs 340 µs in research; cloth costs 0.4 ms a character. Each is the size of the whole allowance or larger, on other hardware. The shipped titles reach their counts with tiers and with threads: Warhorse's AI on a thread of its own, Guerrilla's game on 13 worker threads on a PlayStation 5. The Budget line therefore has two things to say that it does not yet: whether the figure is main-thread time or the fixed schedule's wall time across the compute pool, and how it splits. The figures here cannot set the split; only a bench of the fixed schedule on the M4 Pro can.

**Identity (#17, Capabilities the game needs).** A cooked Placement holds a Mesh, a Material, a translation, a rotation and a scale (`CookedPlacement`, `src/assets/cooked.rs`), and no name. The sources ask for three identities. A placed object's (a door, a container, a parked car) must survive a re-cook, or a patched game orphans every save, which is the hazard ADR 0018 already notes for Asset IDs that hash a glTF name; Bethesda's rule that the save's value wins over the patched file shows the same coupling from the other side. A spawned object's (a dropped item, a wreck) is minted at run time and saved with its kind. A person's outlives its entity: Watch Dogs: Legion restores a character under the same ID, and a witness's facts or a group's members can only name someone that way, since an `Entity` does not survive a despawn. A save then holds differences keyed on these, as Sawyer describes, and its size is a Budget to state before it is discovered.

**Chunks under a moving population (ADRs 0017, 0018).** The published full-simulation radii (80 m, 150 m, 70 to 90 m for traffic) sit inside Praxis's 256 m Load radius, so the near tier always stands on drawn Chunks. The middle tiers do not: Warhorse's reaches 600 m with full AI and exact positions, past the 512 m hold radius (`docs/audit/m5/scalability.md`, S19), and Guerrilla keeps full collision a ring wider than full graphics for exactly this. So collision and navigation need their own residency, wider than Meshes, and the far tier needs a graph (roads, paths) that is Resident for the whole Game world, as Mafia II's 400 KB road map was. A fixed-schedule system cannot wait on a load without tying the simulation's state to IO timing, so a Late Chunk has to be a case the tier rules name: an entity over ground that is not loaded stays in a tier that needs none.

**A Placement that moves (ADRs 0020, 0026).** A prop knocked over, a door, a parked car driven away: each starts as a Placement, drawn from its Chunk's slots and traced in the static TLAS, and becomes a Moving Instance. That is an entry leaving the static TLAS, whose rebuild is off the frame and lands a frame or more later, and one joining the dynamic TLAS at once. It also sits in its Chunk's Far proxy, which merges "the Chunk's static Placements": from past the Load radius the car would still be parked. Nothing marks a Placement as able to move today.

**Material inputs (#951, #17's line on Materials past glTF's core).** A wet, dirty or sweating character in the sources reads a wetness scalar, an optional porosity for which roughness stands in, a dirt amount, and one authored mask; wetness is applied to the final BRDF inputs, not as a lobe. In Praxis's terms that is two or three scalars in what an Instance holds, which prepare writes to its slot (ADR 0003), and a mask that is either a sixth Channel or a free component of one of the five. A wet road wants the same scalars per Chunk or from the weather state, which is why this belongs with #951 and the materials survey. The form matters: a uniform `if` around one shading term cost the M4 Pro's main pass 10 to 12% (#17, Materials past glTF's core), so wetness as an always-evaluated blend of inputs is the cheap form and a per-character texture for blood is a different, costlier thing: a texture each character owns, outside the Material table.

## Cost of delay

**Would rebuild a decision M5 took: nothing; one cook filter.** M5 decided the Far field: one Far proxy per Chunk merged from its static Placements, a hard swap at the Load radius, every BLAS at LOD 0, no lights past the Load radius. A living world adds beside all of it but one clause. The merge has no way to leave a movable Placement out, because a Placement carries no such mark; adding one changes the Chunk Blob and re-cooks proxies, and it is cheapest before world content is authored. It rebuilds no code M5 wrote. Distant life (headlights on far roads, lit windows) is drawn in the sources as lights with no model behind them, which is a pass beside the Far proxies and belongs with #17's line on lights past the Load radius.

**Would rebuild a decision M6 is about to take: nothing; two checks.** M6 implements probe GI and Interior volumes on Reference scenes. First, probe rays and shadow rays trace the dynamic TLAS, and M6's ray costs will be measured on scenes whose dynamic TLAS is nearly empty; a population fills it to its cap. Measuring M6's rays with the cap full costs a scene variant now and saves re-taking the Budget at M8. Second, ADR 0020 authors an Interior volume per enterable space, and VI's makers describe "an order of magnitude more interiors" than its predecessors; M6 should say how many Interior volumes a Chunk can hold before the form is fixed on a scene with a handful. Night traffic as shadowed lights is already rated in the pre-mortem (a cast-shadow flag per light) and adds beside.

**Binds a decision Gameplay core (M8) has not made.** These are the expensive ones to get wrong, and all four are cheap to decide early because they are data shapes, not code:

1. *Tiers.* Whether an NPC far away is an entity with fewer components, a row in a table, or nothing, decides what the fixed schedule iterates, what a save holds and what the per-Tick bench measures. Every source with a large population has three tiers and caps them by count.
2. *Identity.* It reaches the Chunk Blob (ADR 0018), the save and every reference between NPCs. #17 holds it until "before the editor writes Placements" (M10). If the key comes from the source file, as an Asset ID does, it binds earlier: at M7, when world content is first authored at scale.
3. *The per-Tick Budget.* Unstated, it will be spent by whichever system lands first. Physics is M8's first tenant and, by Guerrilla's figure, can take all of it.
4. *The crowd's form past the near tier.* Proxies were measured at 100 figures. The cap, not the cost, binds at 625.

The physics engine's choice (M8) is also touched: batch insertion off the main thread, sleeping islands and a state snapshot keyed on stable body IDs are what the streaming and save rows ask of it, and Jolt documents all three under an MIT licence. The vehicles survey (#952) owns that question.

**Only adds beside.** Character surface state (rows 1 and 2), hair and cloth, faces, physical reactions, locomotion data, dialogue selection, groups, weather-driven behaviour, decals and tracks, economies. ## Costs no source publishes: prototype questions

Each would be measured on the M4 Pro and the RTX 2070 SUPER from one engine source (ADR 0013).

2. **A Tick with a population.** The fixed schedule's time for 70, 400 and 2,400 people in three tiers (animation sampled and proxies written for the near tier, a schedule lookup for the far one), with no AI beyond that. 3. **Lua per event.** The cost of one handler dispatch through mlua with LuaJIT, and so how many events a Tick can deliver to Lua inside a stated share of it.
4. **A Placement that moves.** Frames until shadows are right when an Instance leaves the static TLAS for the dynamic one, and the static rebuild's cost when 1, 10 and 100 do so in a frame. On `town`.
5. **Surface state on a character.** Scalars and a mask against a texture each character owns: bytes per character and lit-pass time at 4x MSAA. On `crowd`, with #951's wet-surface prototype if one is charted.
6. **A save of differences.** Bytes and load time when 1% and 10% of `world`'s Placements carry changed state, keyed on a Placement identity.
7. **Rays through a full dynamic TLAS.** Shadow-ray and probe-ray time with 10,000 Moving Instances against the same scene with none. At M6, on `town` with `crowd`'s figures added.
