# Antialiased edges of hard ray-traced shadows: state of the art

Researched 2026-10-30 for ticket #364; it blocks #358 (shadow edges alias at one sample per pixel beside 4x MSAA geometry), whose decision may change ADR 0020's "one hard shadow ray per pixel per light".

**Summary.** Only more visibility samples per pixel recover a shadow edge inside one surface; filtering a one-sample mask blurs or straightens the staircase but cannot restore what no sample saw, and coverage storage alone (what #357 added) antialiases only geometry edges. The published way to pay for more samples only where they matter is edge-adaptive sampling (Whitted 1980), shipped for ray-traced shadows in Shadow of the Tomb Raider. A prototype of it on `town` adds 6.1 ms of GPU frame for the sun's edges and 11.2 ms for every light's, against 26.3 ms for a ray per sample at every pixel; it smooths every edge some pixel's ray saw, and misses shadows narrower than a pixel that no pixel's ray hit. Options close the note; the recommendation is on its PR.

Sources: papers (CACM, SIGGRAPH, EGSR, TOG, HPG, I3D, Eurographics, TVCG, Graphics Interface, Ray Tracing Gems; 1980 to 2019) by author or publisher copy; GDC 2015 and 2016, GTC 2019 and SIGGRAPH 2016 talks; vendor documentation (Unreal Engine 5.8, Unity HDRP 17.0, NVIDIA, AMD); source at the commits linked; a prototype on the M4 Pro (Measured on the M4 Pro). Each claim the note relies on is in `docs/references.toml`, cited by key. No source in this survey names a renderer that ships MSAA beside ray-traced shadows, so shipped practice is the nearest: engines that trace one shadow ray per pixel and antialias it some other way. "Vendor figure" marks a vendor measuring its own product. ADR 0013's frame is 2560×1440 = 3,686,400 pixels.

## Techniques

**1. Point sampling.** A shadow ray per pixel is a point sample of visibility, as SotTR's authors put it ([GTC 2019 s60][sottr] [ref:gruen-2019-sottr-point-sampling]): an edge on one surface steps by whole pixels, and a shadow narrower than the pixel spacing appears where it crosses a pixel's ray and vanishes between, which is #358's curbs, wires and roof props.

**2. Supersampling every pixel.** Tracing from each of the depth target's four samples is what alias-free shadow maps do with MSAA, each sample tested on its own ([Aila & Laine §3][afsm] [ref:aila-2004-alias-free-shadow-maps-multisample]), and its cost is linear in samples ([Wyman 2015 §1][ftizb] [ref:wyman-2015-frustum-traced-shadows]). It is the reference the other options approximate: four levels along every edge, and a thin shadow seen wherever one of 36 samples per 3x3 neighbourhood meets it rather than one of 9. #358 ruled it out unmeasured at about 4x; the prototype measures it.

**3. Edge-adaptive extra rays.** Whitted traced rays at pixel corners and subdivided only where their results differ ([Whitted p. 346][whitted] [ref:whitted-1980-adaptive-supersampling]), and named the miss himself: small objects fall between samples, with a bounding-radius fix that he says will not always work for secondary rays ([Whitted pp. 346, 349][whitted] [ref:whitted-1980-small-object-radius]). Shadow of the Tomb Raider's ultra DXR mode casts one ray per pixel, then up to two more where its 3x3 neighbourhood of verdicts needs them ([GTC 2019 s40-41][sottr] [ref:gruen-2019-sottr-adaptive-rays]); the slides show only the current frame as input but give no cost. Boksansky et al. adapt soft-shadow ray counts from a 4-frame variation measure (temporal, ruled out) and find a ray for one pixel of four costs about as much as rays for all four, from SIMD divergence ([RTG ch. 13 p. 168][rtg13] [ref:boksansky-2019-sparse-ray-divergence]); compacting the chosen pixels into a list before tracing saved 13 to 29% in ATAA ([RTG ch. 22 pp. 364-365][ataa] [ref:marrs-2019-ataa-compaction]), whose authors also say sub-pixel geometry may be missed ([RTG ch. 22][ataa] [ref:marrs-2019-ataa-subpixel-miss]). Sub-pixel shadow mapping applies the same split to shadow maps: multisampled evaluation only at uncertain pixels, +3% of a frame in one view ([Lecocq §5][spsm] [ref:lecocq-2014-sub-pixel-shadow-mapping]). In Praxis the detection fits the mask as it stands: a pixel's bits against its 4-neighbours' for the lights both traced, within one Froxel (bits follow a Froxel's list), and extra rays from the pixel's other samples that show the same surface, listed and dispatched indirectly as #357's second surfaces are [ref:lauritzen-2010-per-sample-scheduling]. Both Ray Tracing Gems chapters are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0: citable, not adaptable.

**4. Footprint coverage tests.** Frustum-traced irregular z-buffers get 32 samples per pixel by testing each occluder triangle's shadow planes against the pixel's footprint on the receiver's tangent plane, one lookup per edge giving a 32-bit mask ([TVCG 2016 §4.1][ftizb16] [ref:wyman-2016-frustum-traced-izb]), at 1.5 to 2.3x the cost of one sample on a Titan X at 1080p ([TVCG 2016 Table 1][ftizb16] [ref:wyman-2016-frustum-traced-izb-timings]). It is cheap because the light-space raster hands each pixel every nearby occluder; a ray query returns only the triangle its ray hit, so the test does not carry over, and a light-space raster per point light does not scale to `town`'s 1,024. The shipped product on this work, HFTS in The Division ([HFTS talk §2][hfts] [ref:story-2016-hfts-division]), kept a binary frustum-traced term, "(0 or 1)" ([GDC 2016 s37][hftsgdc] [ref:story-2016-hfts-gdc]), and its edges took the engine's SMAA, extra rays being "rather expensive" ([blog][hftsblog] [ref:story-2016-hfts-blog]).

**5. Coverage-aware storage and resolve.** Streaming G-buffer compression, DCAA and SBAA keep a coverage mask per surface and weight each surface's single shaded colour by its sample count ([Kerzner & Salvi p. 4][kerzner] [ref:kerzner-2014-streaming-gbuffer-resolve], [DCAA §3][dcaa] [ref:wang-2015-dcaa-resolve-and-cost], [ref:salvi-2012-sbaa-surfaces]); the shadow term inside a surface stays one sample, so this fixes #357's silhouettes, already merged, and not #358. AGAA is the one paper that filters shadow inside a pixel, with 3-4 shadow-map taps over each aggregate's footprint, and rejects per-sample shadowing because its cost scales with samples, "especially when many lights" ([AGAA §3.5.2][agaa] [ref:crassin-2015-agaa-shadowing]); with rays that footprint estimate is technique 2 or 3 again. Storage, computed for Praxis at 2560×1440: the mask is 8 B per pixel, 29.5 MB; a bit per sample is 4x that, 118 MB; a 2-bit count of the three other samples per light is 2x, 59 MB; a sparse list of edge pixels holds only those. Shipped engines pair neither: Unreal Engine 5.8 supports hardware ray tracing only on its deferred path, and its MSAA path is forward ([UE 5.8][uert] [ref:epic-ue58-hwrt-forward-unsupported]); HDRP 17 lists MSAA as incompatible with ray tracing ([HDRP 17][hdrp] [ref:unity-hdrp17-ray-tracing-msaa-incompatible]); URP's screen-space shadow texture is single-sample ([URP L172-181][urp] [ref:unity-urp-screen-space-shadows-texture]), #357's defect shipped.

**6. Morphological filtering.** MLAA's black-and-white mode was proposed for hard shadows: it finds the lines between lit and shadowed pixels and blends by the area under a piecewise-linear silhouette ([MLAA §2.1][mlaa] [ref:reshetov-2009-mlaa-hard-shadows]). Long, near-straight edges are its best case; features under the Nyquist limit are its stated weakness ([MLAA §1][mlaa] [ref:reshetov-2009-mlaa-nyquist]), and SMAA 1x lists spurious pixels and gaps from any one-sample input ([SMAA §5][smaa] [ref:jimenez-2012-smaa-1x-subpixel]). SMAA 1x costs 1.02 ms on a GTX 470 at 1080p on colour ([SMAA p. 8][smaa] [ref:jimenez-2012-smaa-cost]). Shipped ray-traced shadows used it this way: NVIDIA's hybrid shadows ran FXAA on the shadow buffer rather than extra rays ([GDC 2015 s26][hybrid] [ref:story-2015-hybrid-shadows-aa]), and The Division leaned on its SMAA. On Praxis's mask it runs per light bit, an edge search per light with edges nearby, or on final colour, where it filters every edge MSAA already resolved. Shadow-map revectorization is the same idea on a light-space grid a ray-traced mask does not have.

**7. Edge-stopping spatial filters.** À-Trous blurs over log₂ passes with weights that stop at colour, normal and position edges; with the colour weight on, a hard shadow keeps its staircase, and without it the shadow blurs ([À-Trous §3.1][atrous] [ref:dammertz-2010-atrous-sharp-shadow]). AMD's shadow denoiser, MIT-licensed, reads exactly a one-ray-per-pixel bitmask and runs three such passes ([AMD p. 4-6][amddn] [ref:amd-2021-shadow-denoiser-spatial]); without history it stays, as we read its code, on its widest blur. NRD SIGMA scales its blur by the penumbra, which is zero for a hard shadow ([NRD.hlsli L985-991][sigma] [ref:nvidia-nrd-sigma-penumbra]). Neither restores a thin shadow, and at a coplanar contact only the normal weight keeps the occluder from bleeding into the receiver. Percentage-closer filtering, the foundation of filtered visibility, filters many visibility tests of one receiver point, not neighbouring pixels' ([Reeves §2][pcf] [ref:reeves-1987-percentage-closer-filtering]): with rays, that is technique 2 or 3.

## Measured on the M4 Pro

A prototype on branch `prototype/364-shadow-edge-aa` (`eec1e33d`, never merged) adds two dispatches to the shadow mask pass. The first lists each pixel whose nearest surface's bits differ from a 4-neighbour's, comparing only the lights both pixels traced a ray toward (a light past its range or behind the surface flips a bit with no visible change) and only within one Froxel (its bits follow that Froxel's list). The second traces, at each listed pixel, the lights again from its other samples that show the same surface and keeps a 2-bit count of hidden samples per light; the lit pass shades each light by the share of samples that see it. `PRAXIS_SHADOW_EDGES` picks the mode: `off` compiles it out; `detect` lists and traces nothing; `sun-edges` traces again only the directional lights that differ; `edge-lights` every light that differs; `edges` every light at a listed pixel; `all` every light at every pixel (technique 2). Each mode ran `PRAXIS_SHADOW_EDGES=<mode> target/release/praxis bench <dir> --skip-aliasing town` twice, interleaved with `off`, on 2026-10-30: `town`'s 1,800-frame path at 2560×1440, medians in ms.

| Mode | GPU frame | Δ against the `off` runs beside it | Shadow-ray time (rays on minus off) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `off` (5 runs) | 17.63 to 18.14 | — | 8.49 to 8.83 |
| `detect` | 20.71, 21.16 | +2.6, +3.0 | 11.30, 11.33 |
| `sun-edges` | 23.74, 23.74 | +6.1 | 14.48, 14.49 |
| `edge-lights` | 28.91, 28.91 | +11.2 | 19.62, 19.70 |
| `edges` | 30.94, 31.53 | +12.9, +13.4 | 21.50, 22.06 |
| `all` | 44.22, 44.62 | +26.1, +26.5 | 34.86, 34.98 |

`town`'s Baseline GPU frame is 17.63 ms against ADR 0013's 12 ms Budget, which M3's exit re-decides (criterion 10); the gate, rays off, read 8.67 to 8.97 ms in every mode. Detection alone costs 2.6 to 3.0 ms, most likely (not profiled) because the prototype writes 32 B per pixel and reads each of five pixels' 24 B from storage buffers; a pass that loads the mask's 8 B and a traced-lights word once per pixel into a shared-memory tile would move about a fifth of those bytes (computed, not measured). The prototype's buffers add 162 MB of GPU memory; a sparse edge list whose index rides in the samples target's spare bits needs about 29.5 MB of traced-lights bits plus 20 B per listed pixel (computed). Listed pixels, counted from `PRAXIS_SHADOW_EDGE_SHOW=1` captures: frame 300 (street level) 65,613 (1.8%), 33,818 for the sun alone; frame 700 (overhead, roof props under 1,024 point lights) 510,269 (13.8%), 359,015 for the sun. Tracing again where frames list 2 to 14% of the pixels costs a third to two fifths of tracing every pixel (8.4 to 10.1 ms of about 25 ms, net of detection), after compaction: rays from scattered pixels diverge, as Boksansky et al. found for sparse rays [ref:boksansky-2019-sparse-ray-divergence].

What the list misses, against `all` (pixels that differ by more than one 8-bit step from `edges`): 106,242 (2.9%) on frame 700, the thin shadows of roof props and railings no pixel's nearest sample hit; 6,771 on frame 300; 399 on `crowd` and 18 on `shadows`, whose edges are long. `edge-lights` matches `edges` but at 230 pixels on frame 300 and 8,205 on frame 700. `sun-edges` matches `edges` on `crowd` and differs on `shadows` only at its point light's edge (1,383 pixels). Crops, each before, `edge-lights` and `all`: [`crowd` 6x](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gariasf/praxis/9a8dd39c01c981ed29c45826c608095f11aa5440/364/before-after-reference-crowd.png), [`shadows` 8x](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gariasf/praxis/9a8dd39c01c981ed29c45826c608095f11aa5440/364/before-after-reference-shadows.png), [`town` frame 700 4x](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gariasf/praxis/9a8dd39c01c981ed29c45826c608095f11aa5440/364/before-after-reference-town-700.png), [`town` frame 300 4x](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gariasf/praxis/9a8dd39c01c981ed29c45826c608095f11aa5440/364/before-after-reference-town-300.png); frame 700's listed pixels [in magenta](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gariasf/praxis/9a8dd39c01c981ed29c45826c608095f11aa5440/364/town-700-edge-pixels.png).

## Options (no recommendation)

Each option keeps one ray per pixel per light as its base and reads no history. Costs are the prototype's GPU frame medians on `town`.

- **A. One ray per pixel, as now.** No cost. Shadow edges step by whole pixels beside 4x MSAA geometry, and thin shadows break into dashes and speckle (#358). ADR 0020 unchanged.
- **B. Edge-adaptive rays for directional lights.** +6.1 ms, about 2.6 to 3.0 ms of it the prototype's detection, which a leaner layout should cut. Sun shadow edges (the figures on `crowd`, trees, the street) get four levels; point-light edges keep their steps; a thin sun shadow is refined only where some pixel's ray saw it. Shipped precedent: SotTR's adaptive phase. ADR 0020's "one hard shadow ray per pixel per light" gains: and, where a directional light's verdict differs from a neighbour's, one from each other sample that shows the same surface.
- **C. Edge-adaptive rays for every light.** +11.2 ms. Every shadow edge some pixel's ray saw gets four levels; thin shadows as B. ADR 0020 changes as B, for every light.
- **D. A ray per sample at every pixel.** +26.3 ms. Four levels everywhere, and a thin shadow is seen wherever any of a pixel's four samples meets it. ADR 0020 becomes a ray per depth sample.
- **E. Morphological filtering of the mask, per light bit or on final colour.** No rays; not prototyped, so its M4 Pro cost is unmeasured (SMAA 1x: 1.02 ms on a GTX 470 at 1080p, on colour). Long straight edges get a reconstructed silhouette; thin shadows and speckle stay. ADR 0020 unchanged.
