# Unity and Unreal on the horizon: what the ladder holds and what it does not

Researched 2026-11-21 for ticket #955 (parent #944, the world-systems track). It holds what Unity and Unreal Engine have shipped since late 2025, or announced, against the lines of the ladder's map (#17), the ADRs and the notes in this folder. It decides nothing: it ends in lines proposed for #17, challenges stated for the owner, and pointers for the track's other tickets.

**Summary.** Nothing either engine has shipped or announced meets an ADR's reopen trigger, and most of what is new is already held. Four things have no line and Praxis's target would want them; two features bear on a choice an ADR made that no note has weighed, and neither meets its trigger.

- **Versions, as the publishers state them on 2026-11-21.** Unreal Engine 5.8 is the current release (2026-06-17); 5.9 is in Preview 2, a release Epic had not planned, built in about a week for a game jam, with the full release "next year"; Unreal Engine 6 is announced, Early Access "at the end of 2027". Unity 6.6 is the current release (6000.6.10f1 of 2026-11-19, an Update release; 6.3 is the current LTS); Unity 6.7 LTS is in beta (6000.7.0b10); Unity 7 is in alpha (7000.0.0a20), announced for the first quarter of 2027. The owner's names match the publishers'.
- **Missed, four lines proposed:** a first-person view, which ray-traced shadows make an architectural question; the rules that decide what a cook-time scatter places where; scripted input for a running game, so agents can play-test it; subtitles and accessibility.
- **Challenges, two, no trigger met:** Nanite Foliage against ADR 0019's rejection of virtual geometry, and Unity's Surface Cache GI against ADR 0020's probes. Nanite still supports neither forward rendering nor MSAA and picks a voxel's normal at random per pixel; Unity's cache accumulates per patch over frames and does not say how its resolve to the screen is filtered.
- **Already weighed:** MegaLights, Lumen Lite, the 5.9 Preview's Lumen World Cache and Virtual Shadow Maps' soft shadows are in the GI survey and its refresh of 2026-11-14, which found nothing that reopens ADR 0020. They are *Held* here, not challenges.
- **ADR 0007** (wgpu): no feature read here bears on its three reopen conditions, which are about wgpu's releases.
- **The headline of both engines this year is not rendering.** Unreal 5.9 Preview and Unity 7 are led by agent tooling (MCP servers, command lines, assistants), which Praxis has by construction; their web and WebGPU targets are out of scope (ADR 0010).

Sources: the publishers' release notes (Unreal Engine 5.7, 5.8 and 5.9 Preview; Unity 6.3 to 6.6), Epic's public roadmap, the Unreal Engine 5.9 and Unity 6.7 documentation, Epic's announcements forum, Epic's posts *The road to Unreal Engine 6* and *State of Unreal 2026*, Unity's Unite Seoul 2026 keynote recap and press release, its Unity 7 page and release pages, and its Unite Tokyo 2026 roadmap deck. Both engines are under restrictive source-available terms, the track's *evidence only* class: their documentation was read, no source code was opened, and nothing is copied or adapted. Each claim a *Missed* or *Challenges* row or a pointer relies on is in `docs/references.toml`, cited by key, and each quote there was matched by script against a saved copy of its page on 2026-11-21. Being a survey, its rows are source-checked adversarially only if a decision is taken on them (`docs/agents/workflow.md`, Review). A figure a publisher gives is written as its claim. Nothing was measured in this repo.

Boundaries: terrain, vegetation, water, weather, vehicles and living-world systems belong to #948 to #952 and #954 and are listed as pointers, not surveyed. Unreal Engine 5.6 and earlier and Unity 6.2 and earlier were read only where a newer page builds on them. No patent is named and none was searched.

## Versions

Read on 2026-11-21 from the publishers' own pages.

- **Unreal Engine 5.8**: released 2026-06-17. "Unreal Engine 5.8 is now available" [ref:unreal-5.8-released].
- **Unreal Engine 5.9**: Preview 2, its notes last updated 2026-11-19. "an eventual 5.9 release next year"; "about a week of development and a shorter QA pass than usual" [ref:unreal-5.9-preview-announcement] [ref:unreal-5.9-preview-release-notes].
- **Unreal Engine 6**: announced. "Early Access release at the end of 2027, with the full release of UE6 coming 12-18 months later" [ref:unreal-road-to-ue6-timeline].
- **Unity 6.6** (6000.6.10f1, a patch dated 2026-11-19): released, an Update release. "Update releases are production-ready"; 6.3 is the LTS, "supported until December 2027" [ref:unity-6000.6.10f1-released] [ref:unity-6-release-support].
- **Unity 6.7 LTS** (6000.7.0b10): beta. "The Beta phase starts after all major features are added" [ref:unity-beta-program-6000.7.0b10].
- **Unity 7** (7000.0.0a20): alpha; announced 2026-07-21. "early Beta testing in December, with the Full Release scheduled for Q1 2027" [ref:unity-7-press-release-timing] [ref:unity-alpha-program-7000.0.0a20].

Three things a reader should know about these names. Epic said in June that it was "reserving the option to release a 5.9, if needed" and planned none [ref:unreal-road-to-ue6-timeline]; the Preview exists to carry agent tooling ported from the UE6 branch and a web target rebuilt from UE4's retired HTML5 platform, and its rendering content is what had accumulated on UE5's main branch. Epic's public roadmap had no 5.9 tab on the day read; its newest was 5.8. Unity calls Unity 7 "the next major version of the Unity Editor and runtime", built on what the 6.x releases have delivered [ref:unity-7-faq], so most of what Unity 7 brings to rendering arrives first in 6.7.

## Missed

**M1. A first-person view.** Unreal's roadmap calls First Person Rendering production ready in 5.7 [ref:unreal-roadmap-5.7-first-person-rendering]. It draws the player's hands and held items at a field of view of their own, pulled toward the camera so they avoid "most cases" of entering a wall, in the same pass as the world, by warping their vertices before projection. The warped geometry is "unsuitable for other perspectives", so it is kept out of scene shadows and out of the ray-traced scene; a second, world-space mesh casts the body's shadow and appears in reflections; and the hands shadow themselves by a screen-space trace [ref:unreal-5.9-first-person-rendering]. No line of #17, no ADR and no note says whether the game is seen in first person, third person or both. It is a line of its own because it binds decisions already taken. ADR 0020 shadows every surface by rays against a TLAS of world-space geometry, and ADR 0019's shadow mask pass rebuilds each ray's origin from depth, so a ray from a warped hand would start from a point that is not where the hand is in the world; that consequence is this note's reading of the two ADRs, not a measured defect. Unreal's two answers are a screen-space term, which Praxis does not have, and a stand-in mesh the camera does not draw. Fit: a vertex-stage warp and a per-Instance flag; no wgpu feature. Unity's notes and roadmap hold no counterpart.

**M2. Placement rules for a cook-time scatter.** Unreal's PCG framework is Production Ready since 5.7 [ref:unreal-5.7-pcg-production-ready]; Epic describes it as tools "ranging from asset utilities, such as buildings or biome generation, up to entire worlds" [ref:unreal-5.9-pcg-framework-scope]. 5.8 adds GPU scatter at runtime, by Epic's claim "within the same performance range as the landscape GPU grass system", and generation with no actor or component [ref:unreal-5.8-pcg-runtime-scatter]. The notes and roadmap read from Unity hold nothing comparable. Part of this is held. #17's *Blue-noise sampling patterns* line names "terrain scatter's per-Chunk sets", which `stealthy-blue-noise.md` places at cook time, per Chunk, and `world-content-sources.md` offers content generated in the repo as an option for a Reference scene. Those hold where a scatter's points fall and where a test scene's content comes from. Nothing holds what decides, from terrain and biome maps, which Mesh stands where and how densely: M4's Chunks "come from the generator", which is Rust code per Reference scene; the authoring path named for M6 is a Blender scene through glTF nodes; the World editor is M10. For a world of `world`'s size, 16.2 million Placements, that rule layer is how one owner gets content that looks authored. For Praxis it is a cook step, deterministic and content-hashed under ADR 0018, with no runtime code and no wgpu feature. Scatter at runtime, as for grass, is #949's question.

**M3. Scripted input for a running game.** Unreal 5.9's MCP server "isn't tied to the editor": a cooked build can host it, and the engine toolsets callable at runtime include Scene Capture, Perf and Input [ref:unreal-5.9-mcp-runtime-toolsets], so an agent can drive, capture and profile a running game. Unity's Tokyo roadmap lists "End-to-end device validation" as what comes next for its command line [ref:unity-tokyo-2026-cli-agents]. Praxis's agents already have that reach over rendering, on scripted camera paths. Once M8 adds input and game state, no line gives the equivalent: a recorded or scripted stream of per-Tick input fed to the fixed schedule, and World state read back. ADR 0022 already guarantees the property that makes it exact on one machine and one build ("fed the same per-Tick inputs, reaches a bit-identical simulation state", with nothing "guaranteed across platforms or builds"), which neither page read claims for its engine; what is missing is the recorder and the test seam built on it, so that an agent can play a build with no window, and a bug can be kept as an input file that replays on the build that showed it. #17's *Input latency measurement* line measures something else.

**M4. Subtitles and accessibility.** Unreal 5.8 moves its subtitle plugin to Beta and calls subtitles "required for accessibility – dialogue systems, closed captioning, and voice chat" [ref:unreal-5.8-audio-subtitles]; Unity 6.3 extends its screen reader support to Windows and macOS [ref:unity-6.3-screen-reader]. #17's line of capabilities the game needs (strategy review, S1-08) names game UI and text, localisation and dialogue systems, and neither subtitles nor accessibility. For a lore-heavy RPG subtitles are not optional, and their needs (timed text per spoken line, durations per language, a text path a screen reader can follow) fall on the same choice of text and UI dependency that line already asks to weigh. It is the smallest of the four: an addition to that line, not a system.

## Challenges

Each quotes the clause it bears on. None is decided here. The ADRs were accepted on 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-13, after Unreal 5.8 shipped, so what has changed is mostly what their sources did not read.

**C1. Nanite Foliage, against ADR 0019's rejection of virtual geometry.** The clause: "Virtual geometry beside A (option E): no MSAA in Nanite or Bevy [...], Nanite hides its LOD switches with TAA [...]. Reopens only when wgpu can build or refit one that follows the cut and a non-temporal method keeps sub-pixel switches stable." Since the ADR, only the 5.9 Preview is new, with culling improvements. What neither the ADR nor the visibility survey read is Nanite Foliage, Experimental since 5.7: Epic now draws whole trees as geometry. Assemblies instance a tree's parts inside one Nanite mesh; skinning moves it by bones; and past a distance triangles become "near pixel-sized aggregate voxels", which is Epic's answer to the sub-pixel aggregate geometry the clause worries about. Its claims are a tree's file falling from 3.5 GB to 29 MB and foliage "within a 60fps budget on current generation hardware" [ref:unreal-5.9-nanite-foliage-voxels] [ref:unreal-5.7-nanite-foliage]. It is still Experimental in the 5.9 documentation. The trigger is not met, and that rests on what Epic states: Nanite's 5.9 documentation still lists Forward Rendering and MSAA as unsupported, with ray tracing of Nanite meshes as an Experimental mode [ref:unreal-5.9-nanite-unsupported], and a voxel's normal "is chosen stochastically per pixel", which Epic says a future release will replace to "reduce noise". Epic does not say what resolves that noise today; that it is the temporal upscaler, whose "Thin Geometry detection to better handle foliage" arrived with the feature, is inference. The clause's first half is about wgpu, and no wgpu release was read here. What the owner may want from it anyway is the content idea, not the renderer: a tree as instanced parts is a saving at cook time that discrete LOD can use, and it goes to #949.

**C2. Unity's Surface Cache GI, against ADR 0020's probes.** The clause, among ADR 0020's considered options: "Within C, surfels (GIBS) spawn from a G-buffer that ADR 0019 does not write; a world-space hash cache (id Tech 8) is sparser and finer but its shipped form adds class (c) passes for detail." Since the ADR, the feature has reached beta in 6.7 and gained manual pages; the GI survey and its refresh did not read it. Unity announced it as real-time GI for URP, scaling "from high-end PCs with hardware ray tracing down to mobile" [ref:unity-seoul-2026-surface-cache-gi]. It "stores indirect lighting on the surfaces of your scene rather than in lightmaps or probes": camera-visible surfaces are cut into patches, each patch traces rays and accumulates over frames, and the result is read into a full-screen buffer the lit shaders sample [ref:unity-6.7-surface-cache-gi]. The accumulation is per patch, in world space, which is Temporal class (b) as `CONTEXT.md` defines it; the page does not say whether the resolve to the screen keeps a history. Its stated limits are the ones Praxis's content will hit: no specular, no light on transparent materials, and "Unity treats every triangle as opaque", so foliage blocks and bounces light as if solid. Unity publishes no cost. ADR 0020's trigger (the probe update missing 90% within 0.5 s or 2 ms of GPU, on both adapters) cannot be judged before M6 builds the probes: not met. It is a second engine's evidence, in beta, after Lumen's, for a cache on surfaces in place of probes. It is one of the two forms the clause names within C; the page does not say which, and by cutting patches from camera-visible surfaces it starts from what the camera sees, as the surfel form does.

**ADR 0007, wgpu.** Nothing read bears on its reopen conditions (the newest wgpu failing the Capability floor, more than five vendored commits or a fix upstream has declined, or a pin more than two major releases behind). Of the features above, those that need what wgpu 30 lacks sit behind other decisions already: ray tracing of Nanite meshes behind ADR 0019, and Unity's neural rendering, which its roadmap ties to DX12, behind the asset-compression note.

**ADRs 0008 and 0020, per-pixel and shadow-map lighting.** No new challenge. MegaLights, Lumen and Virtual Shadow Maps, the features that would be one, were weighed by the GI survey and its refresh, and Epic's pages still describe a denoiser behind MegaLights, with "blurring of the lighting" or "ghosting" as lights multiply [ref:unreal-5.9-megalights-denoiser]. The 5.9 Preview adds a second soft-shadow method to shadow maps that already had one; it does not change what ADR 0020 weighed.

## Pointers for the track's other tickets

Passed on, not surveyed here.

- **#948, terrain.** Mesh Terrain, Experimental in Unreal 5.8: terrain as meshes, "not based on classic height field workflows", with overhangs and tunnels and a resolution that need not be uniform [ref:unreal-5.9-mesh-terrain]. 5.9 Preview gives its Mesh Partition "mesh simplification settings, a ray-tracing proxy option" [ref:unreal-5.9-preview-release-notes]. Unity's notes add terrain shading nodes only.
- **#949, vegetation and grass.** Nanite Foliage's Assemblies, Voxels and Skinning (C1) [ref:unreal-5.9-nanite-foliage-voxels] [ref:unreal-5.7-nanite-foliage]; the Procedural Vegetation Editor, Experimental, which grows vegetation meshes in the editor [ref:unreal-5.8-procedural-vegetation-editor]; PCG's GPU scatter at runtime, claimed level with the landscape grass system [ref:unreal-5.8-pcg-runtime-scatter].
- **#950, water and oceans.** Unreal's baked river simulation and buoyancy work in 5.8 (its claim: "90% CPU memory reduction" from sparse grids) [ref:unreal-roadmap-5.8-chaos-fluids]; shallow-water rivers gain capture exclusion and a paint tool in 5.9 Preview [ref:unreal-5.9-preview-release-notes]. Nothing new on oceans in either engine's notes read here.
- **#951, rain, snow, wetness and wind.** Dynamic Wind, Experimental: wind as a GPU simulation of a plant's bones, "instead of offsetting vertices in the material", with local forces [ref:unreal-5.9-dynamic-wind]. Neither engine's notes read here name rain, snow or wetness.
- **#952, simulated vehicles.** Chaos Modular Vehicles: vehicles built from components, with "two-way interaction between the vehicle and the ground" added in 5.8 [ref:unreal-5.8-chaos-modular-vehicles].
- **#954, the living-world note.** Unreal 5.8's Mass overhaul (entities "created off the game thread") and MetaHuman Crowd, "tens to thousands of characters" between full actors and instanced skinned meshes [ref:unreal-5.8-mass-and-crowds]; 5.9 Preview's Mass Characters plugin and a navmesh that builds progressively and keeps tiles as levels stream [ref:unreal-5.9-preview-release-notes]; on Unity's roadmap, entities "for every GameObject" and navmesh queries from jobs to "Run many NPCs in parallel" [ref:unity-tokyo-2026-ecs-navigation].
