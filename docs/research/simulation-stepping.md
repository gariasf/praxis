# State of the art: simulation stepping

Researched 2026-10-13 for ticket #79; it blocks #78 (Simulation step model). Pinned crates were read in the cargo registry, and every cited registry file is byte-identical to its tag: bevy_ecs 0.18.1 at [`v0.18.1`](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/tree/v0.18.1), wgpu and wgpu-hal 30.0.1 at [`v30.0.1`](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/tree/v30.0.1), glam 0.32.1 at [`0.32.1`](https://github.com/bitshifter/glam-rs/tree/0.32.1). bevy_time and bevy_app are cited as shipped practice only (Praxis uses bevy_ecs alone, ADR 0009) and were read at the same Bevy tag. Physics engines were read at their latest tags as evidence of stepping practice, not as candidates (the engine choice waits for M7): Jolt [`v5.6.0`](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/tree/v5.6.0), Rapier [`v0.36.1`](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/tree/v0.36.1), Box2D [`v3.1.1`](https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/tree/v3.1.1), PhysX 5.6.1 documentation; Godot at [`4.7.2-stable`](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/tree/4.7.2-stable) with its manual at godot-docs [`9adca4c`](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/tree/9adca4c1c72917bfe1b7be3108abed5ce26696a6); Unity 6.6 and Unreal Engine 5.8 documentation.

Link labels: `time-` and `app-` name a bevy_time or bevy_app file, `ecs-` a bevy_ecs file, `hal-mtl` wgpu-hal's Metal backend; the number is the first cited line, and `pN` is a PDF page. Symbols: h is the timestep, P the display's refresh period, α the interpolation fraction (accumulated time over h). Reference machine (ADR 0013): Apple M4 Pro in a MacBook Pro (14-inch, 2024), model `Mac16,8` ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108052)), macOS 27.0.1; its built-in display has ProMotion up to 120 Hz ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121553)), but the measurements ran on the attached external display, a BenQ PD2700U at 60.00 Hz (`system_profiler SPDisplaysDataType`). ADR 0013 names no reference display. Input latency is as defined in `CONTEXT.md`: receipt of the event to the present of the first frame whose simulation consumed it. No technique here accumulates screen-space history; ADR 0008 is engaged through motion stability (judder) only.

## Techniques

**Variable step.** The loop measures the previous frame and advances the simulation by it. Nystrom's objection is that it makes a game "non-deterministic and unstable": the same play on two frame rates sums floats differently ([Nystrom](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/game-loop.html#one-small-step,-one-giant-step)); PhysX warns that its solver is sensitive to very small or large steps and to "too much variation between time steps" ([PhysX](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.6.1/docs/Simulation.html#the-simulation-loop)). Ladavac traced the "heartbeat" stutter of Croteam's games to the measured delta itself: the CPU's frame-start timestamps jitter while the display shows frames at a fixed rate, so motion animated by measured time is uneven on screen ([Ladavac](https://medium.com/@alen.ladavac/the-elusive-frame-timing-168f899aec92); the GDC 2018 talk, [abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025031/)). Caveat: Praxis already runs this model, with an f32 delta sampled before the drawable is acquired ([src/lib.rs](../../src/lib.rs)).

**Semi-fixed step.** Fiedler's second loop splits each frame's time into steps no longer than h, which bounds the step but adds "one additional step to consume any the remainder", and opens the spiral of death when simulation dominates the frame ([Fiedler](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)). Unreal's physics substepping works this way, "takes the total frame time and divides it into sub-steps", runs on a physics thread and delays collision callbacks until the last substep ([UE](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/physics-sub-stepping-in-unreal-engine)). Caveat: the remainder step makes results depend on frame timing; the measured toy diverged by 9.7 mm between two runs.

**Fixed step without smoothing.** Doom runs its game at 35 tics per second and records demos as per-tic input commands that replay exactly ([doomdef.h 122](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM/blob/a77dfb96cb91780ca334d0d4cfd86957558007e0/linuxdoom-1.10/doomdef.h#L121-L122), [g_game.c 1491](https://github.com/id-Software/DOOM/blob/a77dfb96cb91780ca334d0d4cfd86957558007e0/linuxdoom-1.10/g_game.c#L1491-L1527)); Witters' loop updates at a fixed rate and renders as fast as possible, with a cap on catch-up updates ([Witters](https://dewitters.com/dewitters-gameloop/)). Bevy defaults to 64 Hz rather than 60 because 60 Hz on a 60 Hz display can alternate between two steps and none, and because 1/64 s is exact in f32 and f64 ([time-fixed 24](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_time/src/fixed.rs#L24-L30)). Caveat: the image changes only on ticks, so it judders without interpolation, even with the tick rate equal to the display rate (measured below).

**Fixed step with interpolation.** Fiedler keeps the previous and current states and renders `current * alpha + previous * (1 - alpha)`, `alpha = accumulator / dt`, slerping orientations ([Fiedler](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/), "The final touch"). After the loop, the simulation stands at real time minus the accumulator, so the blend shows the state one step behind real time. Godot calls it "fixed timestep interpolation", off by default, and states the delay ([Godot 180](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/9adca4c1c72917bfe1b7be3108abed5ce26696a6/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.rst#L180-L197)); Unreal's async physics block mode 1 keeps "a future output for interpolation from 2 frames in the past" ([UE API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Chaos/FPhysicsSolverBase/SetAsyncPhysicsBlockMode)). Bevy runs `FixedMain` while `Time<Fixed>` can expend a step from its `Duration` overstep, and exposes `overstep_fraction` for the blend ([time-fixed 205](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_time/src/fixed.rs#L205-L227), [time-fixed 243](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_time/src/fixed.rs#L243-L258)); its example accumulates input before the loop, steps physics in `FixedUpdate` and interpolates after it, all in `RunFixedMainLoop` ([example 64](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/examples/movement/physics_in_fixed_timestep.rs#L64-L82), [app-main_schedule 309](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_app/src/main_schedule.rs#L309-L327)). Caveat: bevy_time depends on bevy_app ([time Cargo.toml 51](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_time/Cargo.toml#L51-L52)), so under ADR 0009 Praxis rebuilds the accumulator, the fixed-time resource, the loop and the blend itself; the loop in Bevy is 16 lines.

**Fixed step with extrapolation.** Witters and Nystrom draw objects ahead of the last tick by velocity times the fraction; Witters accepts that an object may be "rendered into another object right before a collision gets detected" ([Witters](https://dewitters.com/dewitters-gameloop/), [Nystrom](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/game-loop.html#stuck-in-the-middle)). Godot declines it for now because a wrong prediction may place an object inside a wall and must "jump or snap back" ([Godot 200](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/9adca4c1c72917bfe1b7be3108abed5ce26696a6/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.rst#L200-L215)). Caveat: corrections are exactly the frame-to-frame motion errors ADR 0008's stability target is about, and they cluster at contacts, where players look.

**Substepping inside a fixed step.** Macklin et al. show that n substeps with one constraint iteration each beat one step with n iterations, at similar cost per iteration ([p1](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf#page=1), [p6](https://mmacklin.com/smallsteps.pdf#page=6)). Box2D v3 adopts substepping with soft constraints ("Soft Step"), updating contact separation per substep without recomputing contacts ([Catto](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/)), and asks for a fixed step, "Usually 1/60", with 4 substeps ([box2d.h 35](https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/v3.1.1/include/box2d/box2d.h#L35-L39), [simulation.md 147](https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/v3.1.1/docs/simulation.md#L147-L160)); Jolt splits a step into collision steps, stable "at 60 Hz with 1 collision step" ([Architecture 733](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/v5.6.0/Docs/Architecture.md#L733-L740)); Rapier defaults to `dt` 1/60 with 4 solver iterations ([integration_parameters.rs 174](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/v0.36.1/src/dynamics/integration_parameters.rs#L174-L208)). Caveat: every surveyed engine expects a constant outer step and substeps inside it; none publishes a frame-loop scheduler.

**Spiral-of-death guards.** Fiedler clamps the frame time to 0.25 s and suggests capping steps per frame ([Fiedler](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)); Bevy clamps virtual time to 250 ms, about a lagged minimum frame rate ([time-virt 41](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_time/src/virt.rs#L41-L71), [time-virt 85](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_time/src/virt.rs#L85)), which allows up to 16 steps at 64 Hz; Unity's `maximumDeltaTime` defaults to one third of a second and stops physics from simulating the excess ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/time-handling-variations.html)); Godot caps 8 steps per frame, so the game slows below 7.5 fps at 60 Hz ([ProjectSettings 2684](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml#L2684-L2688)). PhysX's "well of despair": a 9 ms physics step at 60 fps, after one 50 ms spike, never returns to 60 fps; remedies are a decoupled physics rate, dropping time, or varying the step at the cost of determinism ([PhysX](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.6.1/docs/BestPractices.html#the-well-of-despair)). Caveat: in PhysX's example a step costing over half the frame never recovers from one spike under V-Sync; a guard only chooses how the game slows.

**Frame pacing.** Ladavac's fix is to animate by the time a frame is displayed, which no API offered in 2018 except `VK_GOOGLE_display_timing` ([Ladavac](https://medium.com/@alen.ladavac/the-elusive-frame-timing-168f899aec92)). Since then `VK_EXT_present_timing` (last modified 2024) reports each present and schedules presents ([Khronos](https://docs.vulkan.org/refpages/latest/refpages/source/VK_EXT_present_timing.html)); Metal has `presentedTime`, presented handlers, `present(afterMinimumDuration:)` and `CAMetalDisplayLink` for variable-rate displays (macOS 14) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtldrawable/presentedtime), [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametaldisplaylink)). wgpu 30 has none of these: its Metal backend returns only a presentation clock ([hal-mtl 490](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/v30.0.1/wgpu-hal/src/metal/adapter.rs#L490-L494)), and the API investigation is open ([#2869](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/issues/2869)). Godot instead estimates the refresh rate and passes whole refresh periods as the delta, carrying the remainder ([main_timer_sync.cpp 252](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/main/main_timer_sync.cpp#L252-L312)). Caveat: snapping assumes a fixed refresh; on ProMotion or other variable-refresh displays the period itself varies, and only present feedback gives it.

**Per-frame camera and late input.** Bevy's example rotates the camera every frame before the fixed loop and places it at the interpolated player position after it, because a camera moved only on ticks "will very quickly" look choppy ([example 54](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/examples/movement/physics_in_fixed_timestep.rs#L54-L62)). Spjut et al. found that 30 ms of latency significantly slowed skilled players' targeting, while refresh rates above 60 Hz, with latency equalized, had minor effects ([p1](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2019-11_Latency-of-30/FPS_Tasks_sa2019_AuthorVersion.pdf#page=1), [p2](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2019-11_Latency-of-30/FPS_Tasks_sa2019_AuthorVersion.pdf#page=2)); Kim et al. recovered up to 80% of an 80 ms penalty by warping the finished image with late-sampled input ([p1](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2020-07_Post-Render-Warp-with/HPG_2020_Latewarp_AuthorVersion_0.pdf#page=1)). Caveat: both studies measure aiming, Spjut et al. with eight players; parallax and disocclusion leave holes in the warped image that must be filled ([Kim p5](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2020-07_Post-Render-Warp-with/HPG_2020_Latewarp_AuthorVersion_0.pdf#page=5)).

**Determinism on one machine.** Every surveyed physics engine is deterministic for the same binary and the same order of API calls: Jolt, Rapier ("locally deterministic"), PhysX regardless of worker count; Box2D v3 also across platforms by disabling contraction and shipping its own `atan2`, sine and cosine ([Architecture 787](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/v5.6.0/Docs/Architecture.md#L787-L800), [Rapier](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/determinism), [PhysX](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.6.1/docs/RigidBodyDynamics.html#enhanced-determinism), [simulation.md 1929](https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/v3.1.1/docs/simulation.md#L1929-L1945)). Rust matches IEEE 754 exactly for arithmetic and `sqrt` and never fuses a multiply-add on its own ([RFC 3514 50](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/f17e8623ee2e2854570dcdb936a9f4ab08c0fcd4/text/3514-float-semantics.md#L50-L54), [228](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/f17e8623ee2e2854570dcdb936a9f4ab08c0fcd4/text/3514-float-semantics.md#L228-L236)), but `sin`, `powi` and the like have precision that "can even differ within the same execution" ([std](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/primitive.f64.html#method.powi)); glam keeps platform-specific `fast-math` opt-in ([glam Cargo.toml 33](https://github.com/bitshifter/glam-rs/blob/0.32.1/Cargo.toml#L33-L39)). bevy_ecs builds schedules from `IndexMap`s with a fixed hasher and the single-threaded executor runs systems in that sorted order ([ecs-graph_map 52](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_ecs/src/schedule/graph/graph_map.rs#L52-L76), [ecs-single_threaded 77](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_ecs/src/schedule/executor/single_threaded.rs#L77)); systems with conflicting access and no order are "ambiguities", ignored unless `ambiguity_detection` is raised to `Error` ([ecs-schedule 1536](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_ecs/src/schedule/schedule.rs#L1536-L1541), [1579](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.1/crates/bevy_ecs/src/schedule/schedule.rs#L1579)). Caveat: the guarantee covers the simulation only if inputs are recorded per step, not per frame.

## Measured on the M4 Pro

Scratch probes, not in the repo: release builds against wgpu 30.0.1, winit 0.30.13, bevy_ecs 0.18.1 (with `multi_threaded`) and glam 0.32.1, 2026-10-13, macOS 27.0.1, with other sessions running on the machine.

**Frame timing.** A 640×360 window shaped like Praxis's shell (sample time, optional busy work, acquire, clear pass, `Queue::present`; FIFO, `desired_maximum_frame_latency` 2) recorded 1,800 frames per run on the 60 Hz display, idle and with 8 ms of busy work, two runs each; the first 60 frames are dropped. An accumulator replayed each run's frame-start deltas at several tick rates. "Step error" is how far the simulated time shown advances from the frame's display interval, taken as the measured delta rounded to whole refresh periods.

| Probe | Result |
|---|---|
| Frame-start deltas | Median 16.65 to 16.67 ms; standard deviation 1.0 to 2.2 ms; 2 to 14 deltas over 25 ms per 1,739 |
| Steps per frame at 60 Hz | 0 steps on 4.0 to 4.4% of frames, 2 or more on 4.2 to 4.7%; 8.2 to 9.0% in all |
| At 64 Hz | 0 steps on 0 to 0.4%, 2 or more on 6.7 to 7.4% |
| At 120 Hz | 2 steps on about 91%; 1 or 3 on 4 to 5% each; one run had a frame of 10 steps after an 85 ms stall |
| Step error without interpolation | 60 Hz: p50 0.01 ms, p99 16.7 ms; 64 Hz: p50 1.0 ms, p99 14.6 ms; 120 Hz: p99 8.3 ms; 30 Hz: every other frame shows no motion |
| Step error with interpolation, extrapolation or variable step (measured delta) | p50 0.14 to 0.20 ms, p99 1.7 to 4.5 ms, max 5.5 to 16.4 ms |
| Same, delta sampled after acquire | p50 0.13 to 0.16 ms, p99 0.9 to 16.3 ms, max 32.9 to 68.5 ms |

**Interpolation and Frame data.** `Transform` as ADR 0014 lays it out (`DVec3`, `Quat`, `Vec3`; 64 bytes), median of 300 runs on one thread:

| Work for N Instances | N = 10k | N = 100k |
|---|---|---|
| Snapshot current into previous (slice copy; bevy_ecs query) | 9 µs; 25 µs | 76 µs; 234 µs |
| Build Frame data by copying current (slice; query) | 16 µs; 21 µs | 129 µs; 206 µs |
| Build Frame data by interpolating, `lerp` + `slerp` (slice; query) | 28 µs; 49 µs | 249 µs; 499 µs |
| Same with normalized `lerp` for rotation (slice) | 20 µs | 195 µs |

The second state costs 6.4 MB at 100k Instances (64 bytes each, computed).

**Fixed schedule overhead.** `World::run_schedule` on a single-threaded schedule of 8, 32 and 128 systems reading one resource: 0.12 to 0.15, 0.29 to 0.31 and 0.96 to 0.98 µs per run, the same at 1 and 4 runs per frame; the job-systems note measured the multi-threaded executor at 0.4 to 1.2 µs per system per run ([job-systems-and-frame-threading.md](job-systems-and-frame-threading.md#measured-on-the-m4-pro)).

**Determinism.** 2,048 particles with gravity, drag and bouncing walls, f64, simulated for 20 s of game time against the frame deltas of the idle and busy runs. Fixed 64 Hz: the same bits from both traces and on a rerun. Variable step: a rerun of one trace repeats its bits; the two traces end up to 15.8 mm apart, and 15.0 mm from the fixed run. Semi-fixed with steps ≤ 1/64 s: up to 9.7 mm apart. Three unordered systems writing one resource, rebuilt 2,000 times under each executor, ran in insertion order every time; bevy_ecs documents that order as indeterminate, so this is behaviour, not a guarantee.

What these mean for #78: 60 Hz without interpolation on a 60 Hz display judders on about one frame in eleven, and an input waits one more frame on the frames with 0 steps; 64 Hz trades most 0-step frames for 2-step frames (a second simulation step on 7% of frames); the CPU's frame-start timestamps carry 1.7 to 4.5 ms of p99 noise into every model that advances displayed time by measured time; interpolating 100k Instances costs 0.12 to 0.29 ms more than copying them.

## Options for #78 (no recommendation)

Common to every option: the camera's rotation reads the newest input every frame, before any fixed loop, as Bevy's example does, so view latency is that of the variable frame; a delta clamp of 250 ms or less bounds catch-up; the frame delta is either the measured delta or one snapped to refresh periods (Godot's smoothing), an independent choice that decides how much of the measured 1.7 to 4.5 ms p99 jitter reaches the image; and simulated time is kept as integer ticks or `Duration`, not summed f32 seconds.

### A. Variable step (today)

- Step model, rate, cap: one schedule run per frame with the measured or snapped delta; no cap beyond the clamp. A physics engine would receive variable steps, which Box2D and PhysX advise against.
- Input latency: adds nothing to ADR 0016's frame.
- Frame data: one owned state, as today.
- bevy_ecs scheduling: the current single `Schedule`; `Time` stays a per-frame resource.
- Image stability: frame-time noise reaches motion directly (measured p99 1.7 to 4.5 ms of simulated time per frame) unless deltas are snapped.
- ADR 0014 bar: positions stay f64; results are not reproducible across frame timings (15.8 mm apart in the toy), so no simulation Golden can assert to 0.1 mm across runs with different timing.
- Determinism guarantee: none across runs.

### B. Semi-fixed step

- Step model, rate, cap: the frame delta split into steps of at most h (for example 1/60 or 1/64 s), plus a remainder step; cap n steps.
- Input latency: adds nothing.
- Frame data: one state.
- bevy_ecs scheduling: a simulation schedule run k times per frame with a per-run delta resource (0.3 to 1 µs per run for 32 to 128 trivial systems).
- Image stability: as A.
- ADR 0014 bar: as A (9.7 mm apart in the toy).
- Determinism guarantee: none across runs.

### C. Fixed step with interpolation

- Step model, rate, cap: fixed h at 60, 64 or 120 Hz; the cap (Godot uses 8) or the clamp decides when the game slows; display at α between the two newest states.
- Input latency: the frame that consumes an event shows its effect at weight α, full weight up to h later: the image trails real time by one step, 16.7 ms at 60 Hz, 15.6 ms at 64 Hz, 8.3 ms at 120 Hz (Godot says 1 to 2 ticks). Whether that trail counts against ADR 0013's 33.3 ms depends on the Input latency definition, which counts the frame that consumed the event, not the frame showing it at full weight. A frame with 0 steps also delays consumption by one frame: 4.0 to 4.4% of frames at 60 Hz, at most 0.4% at 64 Hz.
- Frame data: two states per moving Instance (previous and current Transform, 128 bytes) or the renderer's interpolated copy built in prepare; 0.12 to 0.29 ms more than a plain copy at 100k Instances on one thread, and 6.4 MB more memory. Teleports and spawns need a reset flag so they do not interpolate (Godot's `reset_physics_interpolation`, [ProjectSettings 2689](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7.2-stable/doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml#L2689-L2694)).
- bevy_ecs scheduling: rebuild Bevy's split in bevy_ecs: a fixed schedule run while an accumulator resource holds a step, a fixed-time resource the systems read, a before-loop set for input and camera, an after-loop set for the blend.
- Image stability: tick and refresh alignment no longer shows; the residual is the delta jitter shared with A unless deltas are snapped.
- ADR 0014 bar: f64 `lerp` of World positions keeps the bar (glam's `DVec3::lerp`, [dvec3.rs 945](https://github.com/bitshifter/glam-rs/blob/0.32.1/src/f64/dvec3.rs#L945-L947)); the simulation is bit-reproducible across frame timings (measured).
- Determinism guarantee: same binary, same machine, same per-step inputs, provided the fixed schedule has no ambiguities (`ambiguity_detection` at `Error`) and the physics engine keeps its own same-binary guarantee.

### D. Fixed step with extrapolation

- Step model, rate, cap: as C; display the newest state advanced by velocity times α·h.
- Input latency: no trail for motion that keeps its velocity; consumption as C.
- Frame data: one state plus linear and angular velocity per moving Instance.
- bevy_ecs scheduling: as C.
- Image stability: snaps where predictions fail, at contacts and direction changes ([Godot 200](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/9adca4c1c72917bfe1b7be3108abed5ce26696a6/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.rst#L200-L215)).
- ADR 0014 bar: the simulation as C; drawn positions may cross geometry by up to one step's motion.
- Determinism guarantee: as C.

### E. Fixed step at a multiple of the display rate, no smoothing

- Step model, rate, cap: h = P / k (120 Hz for a 60 Hz display, k = 2), cap n steps; draws the newest state.
- Input latency: the image trails real time by 0 to h (8.3 ms at 120 Hz); consumption delays are rare (0 steps on 0 to 4 of 1,739 frames at 120 Hz, measured).
- Frame data: one state.
- bevy_ecs scheduling: as C without the blend, and k schedule runs on most frames (2 on about 91% at 120 Hz).
- Image stability: smooth only while the refresh divides the tick rate; measured p99 step error 8.3 ms at 120 Hz on the 60 Hz display; a variable-rate ProMotion panel or a 144 Hz display breaks the divisor (not measured).
- ADR 0014 bar and determinism: as C.

## Not verified

- Ladavac's slides (the ticket names GDC 2019; GDC Vault lists "The Elusive Frame Timing" under GDC 2018) and Ford's Overwatch slides: GDC Vault pages show abstracts only; the blog post was read from a web.archive.org snapshot because medium.com refused the request.
- Unity's default `fixedDeltaTime` and Unreal's substepping and async-physics defaults: the pages read state no values. Unreal's engine source needs an Epic account and was not read; Havok's documentation is not public.
- Source's tick interval is the SDK's default and is mod-specific; Valve's networking wiki returned a bot check.
- Overwatch's tick model: only the abstract's statement that the simulation "leverages determinism" was read.
- Present times: the probe records CPU timestamps only; actual display times (`presentedTime`) were not read, so "step error" assumes every frame showed for whole refresh periods. ProMotion, 120 Hz and variable-refresh displays were not measured, nor Vulkan or DX12 frame timing.
- Input latency was not measured end to end; the per-option figures are added delays derived from the step model and the measured step counts.
- The determinism probe is a toy integrator, not a physics engine; the multi-threaded executor's order held in 2,000 runs, which does not show it always will.
