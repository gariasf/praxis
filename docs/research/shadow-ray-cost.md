# Fewer and cheaper shadow rays: state of the art

Researched 2026-11-27 for ticket #1098 (parent #602, `town`'s GPU frame offender on the M4 Pro), placed at M6's charting so that M6's probe-ray decisions see the shadow mask's cost. It surveys what published and shipped work offers to cut the cost of hard ray-traced shadows from many point lights and a sun under ADRs 0008, 0019 and 0020, sizes each technique against `town`'s `shadow_mask_pass` on the M4 Pro, re-measured for this note, and proposes how shadow and probe rays share a frame. It ends in options and chooses none.

**Summary.** On `town`, rays are 83% of the shadow mask pass. Each published technique that keeps Praxis's hard, history-free shadows and its rays reaches a fifth to a third of it at most, measured or by arithmetic; the larger ceilings are in what each ray asks of the TLAS.

- **Where the time goes.** Re-measured on the M4 Pro: a frame traces 5.76 million shadow rays at 1.93 ns each on average. The sun takes 54% of the pass (its 2.87 million rays from each pixel's surfaces 31%, its 0.83 million edge rays 23%), the 1,024 lamps 28% (2.07 million rays), the second surfaces 14% of both, and the dynamic TLAS's queries 17%; holding ray-query code costs 4% with no ray traced. The shares are #404's of a month ago within four points, but for the lamps, down from 37%.
- **Inside the ADRs and wgpu 30, the ceilings are in the query.** Each ray asks first for the nearest hit, so that it can pass a back face near its origin: asking every query for any hit instead saves 27.5%, though it changes the image, so the saving available is what a query order that asks for the nearest hit only where a back face can be passed keeps of it. The dynamic TLAS's queries bound 17%: one TLAS root needs a wgpu-hal change, and tighter boxes than #412's one box would chase the same 17% inside wgpu 30. wgpu's runtime checks (1.30 ms, #873) are counted as already available.
- **Fewer rays amends ADR 0020's one ray per light, and buys little on `town`.** A bound of 1/64 of the light already reaching a surface skips 22% of the lamps' rays but saves 3% of the pass: the bound's own code takes back half of what the skipped rays cost, the occupancy #404 found in this kernel. A shadow range half the light's would skip 78% of lamp rays (22% of the pass, arithmetic) where a lamp still gives a quarter of the sun's illuminance. Tracing the sun from a quarter of the pixels saves 21% before any fill (measured), and every published fill reads history; a history-free one (Whitted's subdivision) is unpriced and misses thin occluders.
- **Reuse and hybrids change what decides a shadow.** Light-space caches and precomputed voxel shadows answer with a map or a voxel instead of a ray, and at `town`'s 1,024 lamps pass the 512 MiB render-target Budget; an occlusion grid in front of the rays keeps every verdict a ray's or a certain cell's but settles only deep umbra; a sun depth map used only to classify the sun's rays amends ADR 0020's Consequence that shadow maps are dropped, not its direct-light clause.
- **No precedent.** Among the sources read, the titles with ray-traced probes shadow their lights with shadow maps, and those that trace direct shadows denoise them. Class (a) hard shadows from about a thousand lights beside probe GI are Praxis's alone.
- **One ray budget.** A shadow ray costs 1.93 ns on `town`, a probe ray 1.50 ns timed alone with the sun ray from its hit. Fixed shares (B1) leave the shadow mask pass 3.27 ms at today's gate, 27% of its Baseline; B2 gives the frame to the shadows and the rest to the probes, decided on the GPU in the same frame; B3 applies the shadow culls at probe hits.
- **ADR 0013.** Every figure measured here is the M4 Pro's. No RTX 2070 SUPER figure was taken, so no cost here rejects a technique.

Sources: papers from 1980 to 2026 (CACM, Graphics Interface, IEEE CG&A and TVCG, SIGGRAPH and ACM TOG, I3D, HPG, JCGT, Ray Tracing Gems), SIGGRAPH Advances in Real-Time Rendering (2022 to 2026) and REAC, Apple's WWDC sessions and Tech Talks, NVIDIA's and AMD's developer documentation, the DXR specification, the Unreal Engine 5.9 documentation, and the source of naga, wgpu-hal and wgpu-types 30.0.2, RTXDI, the FidelityFX SDK and Bend's screen-space shadows at the commits cited. No engine source under a restrictive licence was read. Each claim relied on is in `docs/references.toml`, cited by key; every quote of an entry this note adds was matched by script against a saved copy of its source on 2026-11-27. The rows a decision rests on are source-checked when it is taken (`docs/agents/workflow.md`, Review).

Boundaries: soft shadows and area lights are out, as are the runtime checks (#873), which a build ticket takes. Choosing a technique is the owner's.

Terms: a **base ray** is one of the rays a pixel's nearest or second surface traces toward each light that reaches it; an **edge ray** is one of those ADR 0020 adds from a pixel's other samples where a directional light's verdict differs from a neighbour's or between the pixel's samples. The classes are `CONTEXT.md`'s Temporal classes. A **share** is a fraction of `town`'s `shadow_mask_pass` median on the M4 Pro.

- **Counts.** Each frame of the path has 2,850,303 lit pixels on average, 390,950 of them with a second surface and 356,310 listed for edge rays. Of 5,842,403 queries, 77,822 continue a ray past a back face and 2,711,801 (46%) reach the dynamic TLAS, by #412's geometry nearly all of them the sun's (not counted apart): its one box spans the town at the flocks' height, so every ray toward the sun from below crosses it, while the lamps hang 4.7 m up. The second surfaces' rays are 358,253 toward the sun and 184,143 toward lamps.
- **What the parts mean.** The parts overlap: the second surfaces trace both kinds of ray, and the dynamic queries are both kinds' too. A part turned off is a ceiling for any option that removes those rays, not a saving any option is measured to keep. The prices per ray are the part's saving over its rays: an edge ray's includes the listing dispatch, which reads every pixel, and its surface rebuilt per sample, and the dynamic query's is a traversal that mostly finds nothing.
- **Against #404.** #404's shares (2026-10-30) were of shadow-ray time (the frame with rays less without), 11.0 ms, rather than of the pass. Since then each surface's rays go through one ray-query call, rays skip the dynamic TLAS outside the box of its triangles (#412), and the edge rays also trace at silhouettes and terminators (#407). Every share moved by four points or less but the lamps', 37% then and 28.5% now, and the cap at one lamp a surface, 11% then (1.2 ms) and 6.4% now.

Method: a probe build of `main` at `aa0f0655` (wgpu 30.0.2; #1097, merged since, changes no WGSL) replaced pieces of `shadow_mask.wgsl` at module assembly from an environment variable, one variant per file, as #404's probe did with pipeline overrides. Each variant ran `praxis bench <dir> --no-publish --skip-aliasing town` on 2026-11-27 between 14:12 and 14:43, interleaved with the others over three rounds in alternating order, on mains power, no other praxis session running; Bitdefender's scanner held a core through most of it. The first five runs read 5 to 10% faster than every later run, the gate's line (rays off, untouched by every variant) included: 6,849 to 7,004 µs against 7,086 to 7,356. So the table uses rounds 2 and 3, each variant's two rounds within 2% of each other, against the shipped pass's five later runs (13,224 to 13,953 µs, median 13,412). Round 1's first five runs give the same shares within 2.1 points (sun 53.4%, its edge rays 21.3%, lamps 26.8%, second surfaces 15.0%). The pass's Baseline, recorded quiet at `78f2291e`, is 12,292.2 µs; the shares carry over to it, the microseconds do not. Ray counts come from a second set of variants that add one to the shadow mask pass's overflow counter at each ray of a kind and write the counter of every frame; they are means over the bench's 1,800 measured frames. Medians come from the bench JSON by a script kept with the probe, outside the tree: `praxis report` reads one run against the Baselines, not a variant's rounds. Every variant is a timing probe and not every one draws the same image.

## Fewer rays

**1. A bound on the unshadowed contribution.** Ward traces a surface's lights in order of their potential contribution and stops once the untested remainder is under a threshold, which "can guarantee an absolute error below a given threshold" [ref:ward-1991-adaptive-shadow-testing]; lightcuts bounds a cluster's error by a fixed share of the total, after Weber's law [ref:walter-2005-lightcuts-error-bound]. Both are offline. The deterministic form is class (a) and keeps every traced shadow hard; it changes ADR 0020's one hard shadow ray per pixel per light into one per light above the bound, and a lamp whose contribution crosses the bound as the camera moves gains or loses its shadow at once. Measured on `town`, a lamp is skipped where its unshadowed illuminance at the surface, by the lit pass's falloff and the interpolated normal's cosine, is under 1/64 of the ambient plus the sun where the sun's ray cleared: 454,080 lamp rays a frame, 21.9%, saving 388 µs (2.9%, rounds 2 and 3) with the sun's reach read back from its call, or 435 µs (3.3%, three more rounds against three of the shipped pass) with it read from the sun's bit, which skips 446,594. At 1.84 ns a lamp ray the skipped rays cost about 835 µs (computed): the bound's own code in the ray-query kernel takes back about half, the occupancy cost #404 and #417 found of any code there. At 1/256 it skips 169,140 rays (8.2%), counted and not timed. `town`'s lamps are bright by design, the sun's illuminance 4.5 m away, so the bound skips less there than it would under dimmer lamps.

**2. Stochastic light selection.** Stochastic light culling draws each light's range at random against an error bound, so shading cost grows slower than the light count; its 65,536 virtual lights take about 5 ms on a Radeon R9 290X at 1920×1152 with an interleaved pattern and a 17×17 filter [ref:tokuyoshi-harada-2016-stochastic-light-culling]. A light BVH renders thousands of moving lights with ray-traced shadows in 16.1 ms of frame with SVGF on an RTX 2080 Ti at 1080p [ref:moreau-2019-dynamic-many-light-sampling]; ReSTIR resamples candidates across pixels and frames, 15 to 50 ms at 1080p on an RTX 2080 Ti and a Titan RTX [ref:bitterli-2020-restir]; MegaLights, in Unreal Engine 5.9, traces a fixed number of rays a pixel toward the important lights and denoises [ref:unreal-5.9-megalights-denoiser]: 5.51 ms for all direct light of 941 area lights at 1080p on a PS5, 1.35 ms of it ray tracing [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-cost]. Each decouples rays from lights and each filters over frames: class (c), which ADR 0008 excludes and ADR 0019 already lists. MegaLights's price for 20 to 80 lights a pixel is the scale the deterministic techniques here are held against. RTXDI is under NVIDIA's RTX SDK licence [ref:nvidia-rtx-sdks-licence]; Falcor, where the light BVH and ReSTIR were built, is BSD-style [ref:falcor-licence].

**3. A shadow range shorter than the light's.** Boksansky et al. trace no ray toward a light past the range where its attenuation is negligible [ref:boksansky-2019-distance-light-culling], which Praxis's Froxel lists already do at the glTF range. Olsson et al. give each cluster of visible samples a near-minimal light list, cull each light's shadow casters to the clusters it reaches, and trace the lights whose shadow maps would be under 96 texels [ref:olsson-2015-clustered-shadows-hybrid], 16 to 34 ms of frame on a GeForce Titan at 1080p. The lever left to Praxis is a shadow range shorter than the light's: on `town` 1,618,933 lamp rays a frame, 78.1%, start more than half a range (9 m) from their lamp, which at 1.84 ns is 2,979 µs, 22.2% (arithmetic). Unlike technique 1 the cut ignores what the pixel shows: at 9 m a `town` lamp still gives 0.23 of the sun's illuminance (computed from its intensity, range and window). Capping each surface at its first lamp, which #404 measured, saves 858 µs (6.4%), measured. Class (a); amends ADR 0020 below the light's range.

**4. Lights grouped for visibility.** Row-column sampling treats pixels by lights as a low-rank matrix and finds, from a few hundred sampled rows and columns, which clusters of 100,000 lights matter where, in seconds per image [ref:hasan-2007-row-column-sampling]. Its real-time descendant is MegaLights's list of the lights visible in each 8×8 tile, built from the previous frame's samples [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-visible-light-list]: class (c). AMD's neural visibility cache learns light-to-point visibility in a world-space hash grid and feeds light selection, 3.95 to 4.41 ms for 32 lights on a Radeon RX 7900 XT; trained from scratch it halves its error in about 16 frames, and as a sampler it leaves noise for a filter [ref:boksansky-meister-2025-neural-visibility-cache]. The class (a) transfer, a same-frame coarse pass that marks a lamp hidden from a whole tile and skips its rays there, is unpublished and not exact: a lamp hidden at the tile's samples can light a pixel between them.

**5. A lower ray rate with an edge-aware fill.** Boksansky et al. adapt one to eight rays a pixel from a visibility variation measured over four reprojected frames, 2.94 against 4.25 ms on an RTX 2080 Ti at 1080p [ref:boksansky-2019-adaptive-shadows-temporal], and found that "shooting a single ray for one pixel out of four in a close neighborhood yields similar performance as shooting rays for each of these pixels", so they skip whole blocks [ref:boksansky-2019-sparse-ray-divergence]. Shadow of the Tomb Raider adds rays only where a neighbourhood needs them [ref:gruen-2019-sottr-adaptive-rays], as Praxis's edge rays do. Call of Duty traced local lights' shadows at a quarter of 1440p, then one ray per 2×2 quad under a budget of rays a pixel [ref:olejnik-2026-cod-light-load-balancer]; MegaLights samples at a quarter rate with a stochastic upsample [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-visible-light-list]; AMD's classifier lists the 8×4 tiles holding any pixel that needs a ray [ref:amd-fidelityfx-shadow-classifier]. Every published fill of the skipped pixels reads history or a temporal denoiser. The class (a) form is Whitted's adaptive subdivision [ref:whitted-1980-adaptive-supersampling]: trace a sparse lattice, fill a block whose rays agree, trace densely where they differ; it misses an occluder thinner than the lattice, and no source prices it. On `town`, the sun's base rays traced from one SIMD group in four (8×4 pixels each, whole groups idle) save 2,821 µs against the pass with edge rays off, 21.0% of the pass and 68% of those rays' 4,158 µs, against the 75% their count gives: measured, before any fill. If the fill traces the three other pixels of every 2×2 block within a pixel of an edge pixel, assumed twice the edge pixels' 12.5% of lit pixels, it adds 537,386 rays, 779 µs at a base ray's price or 2,037 µs at an edge ray's, leaving 5.8 to 15.2% (arithmetic). Under 4x MSAA the edge rays stay as they are. FidelityFX is MIT [ref:amd-fidelityfx-sdk-licence].

## Cheaper rays

**6. Coarser geometry for shadow rays.** NVIDIA advises a level of detail per Instance and size-based culling in the TLAS [ref:nvidia-2022-rtx-best-practices-lod-culling]; stochastic LOD in DXR gained up to 2.2 times on an RTX 2080 Ti, and its authors state the condition: a secondary ray "must 'see' the same LODs for objects as the primary ray (or rasterizer)" or it leaks and self-shadows [ref:lloyd-2020-stochastic-lod-dxr]. Lumen traces its far field against merged proxies [ref:wright-2022-lumen-far-field-proxies-in-one-tlas], and Epic finds that "Shadows for direct lighting can easily expose the underlying ray tracing representation" [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-dynamic-geometry-unsolved]. Praxis builds every BLAS from LOD 0 (ADR 0020), and ADR 0019 records that shadow rays against proxies self-shadow, so a coarser shadow BLAS needs a per-receiver rule, as `shadowed`'s LOD-error test already is for back faces. `town` draws 32 triangles an Instance on average at its LODs (2,713,433 over 85,321, Baseline), so what share of its rays' time is triangle work rather than instance traversal no measurement here splits. Class (a); amends ADR 0020's "Every BLAS is built from its Mesh's LOD 0".

**7. Fewer, non-overlapping Instances.** Apple names "an overhead for each instance that your rays hit", more often with overlapping Instances [ref:apple-wwdc22-10105-instance-overhead]; NVIDIA advises merging BLASes whose world-space boxes overlap significantly [ref:nvidia-2022-rtx-best-practices-shadow-rays]; AMD shows overlapping boxes hurting traversal [ref:amd-rra-instance-overlap]. `town` holds 255,343 Instance slots in use, most of them static and in the static TLAS. This is #404's option B, merging a cell's static Instances into one BLAS, which costs instancing's memory and longer builds; no source prices it per ray. Class (a); fits as written.

**8. Ray flags and the query order.** Terminate-on-first-hit for shadow rays [ref:nvidia-2022-rtx-best-practices-shadow-rays], the DXR fast path for an opaque first-hit query [ref:dxr-spec-inline-shadow-fast-path], one live query in scope [ref:amd-rdna-guide-ray-tracing] and fast-trace builds with compaction [ref:nvidia-2022-rtx-best-practices-as-builds] are Praxis's already. What wgpu 30.0.2 passes: naga's Metal writer honours the opacity cull, forced opacity and first-hit flags with the cull mask and the ray's interval [ref:naga-30.0.2-msl-ray-flags-honoured]; the face-culling bits exist [ref:naga-30.0.2-ray-flag-bits] but set no triangle cull mode on Metal; wgpu-hal maps only refit and fast build into Metal's usage, whose default is the build tuned for tracing [ref:wgpu-hal-30.0.2-metal-as-usage], and maps every build flag on Vulkan [ref:wgpu-hal-30.0.2-vulkan-as-build-flags]. Face culling is a cost in the one source that prices it, "about 10% overhead to tracing" in MegaLights [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-proxy-mismatch-screen-traces]. Praxis's lever is its query order: each shadow ray asks first for the nearest hit, so that a back face near its origin, from the LOD drawn lying inside LOD 0's BLAS or from a terminator, can be passed (`shadowed`), and only then for any hit. Every query asked for any hit instead saves 3,690 µs, 27.5%: a ceiling, measured, on an image that differs, since back faces then shadow. A form that asks for the nearest hit only over the stretch of ray where a back face can still be passed, and for any hit beyond, would keep part of it; how much depends on that stretch, unmeasured. Class (a); fits as written.

**9. Metal's intersector.** naga lowers every ray query to Metal's intersection query [ref:naga-30.0.2-msl-intersection-query], which Apple says "increases the amount of ray trace scratch memory that must be read and written, as well as disables the reorder stage" [ref:apple-techtalk-111375-intersection-query-cost]. The reorder stage groups intersection-function calls [ref:apple-techtalk-111375-hardware-traversal-reorder], which Praxis's opaque triangles never make, so only the scratch traffic would apply. No source prices it. #873 ran hand-written Metal shaders through wgpu's passthrough per entry point and found naga's lowering cost nothing, but every form it tried kept the intersection query, so the intersector is unmeasured; the route exists without a naga change. Class (a); no ADR clause.

**10. One TLAS root, or tighter boxes.** A ray the static TLAS lets through starts a second traversal at the dynamic TLAS's root (ADR 0020). Apple keeps a static and a dynamic structure under one root through instancing levels, "with only minor impact on trace time" [ref:apple-wwdc23-10128-static-dynamic-split] [ref:apple-wwdc23-10128-multi-level-instancing], up to 16 levels on Apple9 [ref:metal-feature-set-tables-instancing-levels]; DXR's and Vulkan's partitioned TLAS rebuild only the changed partitions under one root [ref:dxr-2026-ptlas-overview] [ref:vulkan-1.4.367-partitioned-tlas]. wgpu 30 builds one level, so a root needs a wgpu-hal change (#873). The dynamic TLAS's 2,711,801 queries a frame cost 2,234 µs, 16.7%: the ceiling, measured, for either a root or, inside wgpu 30, boxes tighter than the one box that skips the dynamic TLAS today (#412), which a sun ray from below always crosses. The flocks are 100 groups of 100 birds; a box per flock, or a coarse grid of occupied cells, would let most sun rays skip, at the price of the tests in the ray-query kernel, unmeasured. Class (a); one root falls under ADR 0007's subsystem past wgpu's safe API.

**11. Coherence and dispatch order.** Apple9 traverses in fixed-function hardware, each ray independent of its SIMD group [ref:apple-techtalk-111375-hardware-traversal-reorder]. Reordering rays raises an RTX GPU's trace speed 1.3 to 2.0 times on incoherent bounces, but the sort's overhead is hard to win back in hardware tracing [ref:meister-2020-ray-reordering]; neighbouring pixels' rays toward one light are coherent already. Dispatch order acts through the cache: tiling thread-group IDs gained 47% on a Battlefield V denoising pass on an RTX 2080 at 1440p, "particularly important" for ray queries in a 2D compute shader [ref:bavoil-2020-thread-group-id-swizzling]. Praxis dispatches 8×8 workgroups in row order. Unmeasured; a cheap A/B. Class (a).

**12. A screen-space march before the world ray.** MegaLights' "main method to avoid incorrect self-shadowing, is screen space ray tracing", whose reach offsets the world ray's origin [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-proxy-mismatch-screen-traces], for 0.47 ms of its 5.51 [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-cost]; Bend's screen-space shadows cost 0.19 ms at 1440p on a PS5 and "cannot replace shadow maps, only complement them" [ref:aldridge-2023-bend-screen-space-shadows-distant-trees]. A depth buffer holds no back faces or thickness, so for hard shadows a march can only shorten a world ray, never settle a miss, and under 4x MSAA the edge rays keep their world rays. Class (a); its use here is technique 6's self-shadowing, not fewer rays. Bend's code is Apache-2.0 [ref:bend-2023-sss-code-licence].

## Reuse

**13. Light-space caches of static shadow.** DOOM keeps its lights' maps in one atlas and redraws only those that change, caching the static part [ref:courreges-2016-doom-shadow-atlas-cache]; Unreal's virtual shadow maps cache 128×128 pages across frames, invalidated by a moving light or by casters moving under them, with a static copy that dynamic casters composite over [ref:ue-5.9-vsm-page-caching]; Indiana Jones keeps a static tile per still light and spreads its far sun cascades over up to 30 frames [ref:de-vahl-2025-indiana-jones-shadow-static-cache]. None of these sources gives a GPU time. The history is kept in light space, invalidated by what moves and never reprojected on screen: class (b) in effect. But the cache is a shadow map, so adopting it amends ADR 0020's direct-light clause and its Consequence that cascaded shadow maps are dropped, and the edge rays need a map's equivalent. Six 256×256 16-bit faces for each of 1,024 lamps are 805,306,368 bytes (computed), past the 536,870,912-byte render-target Budget, so only paging fits. Closed, or Epic's EULA.

**14. Precomputed voxelized shadows.** Static-light visibility over static geometry, voxelized into a sparse octree and compressed as a DAG: a sun equal to a 256k×256k shadow map in 100 MB, evaluated in under 1 ms at 1080p [ref:sintorn-2014-compact-precomputed-voxelized-shadows]; 48 MB with faster construction, and 165 spotlights at 0.5 MB and 114 ms of build each on average, all evaluated in 3.2 ms on a GTX 980 at 1080p [ref:kampe-2016-voxelized-shadows-many-lights]. For static lights it is ADR 0020's rejected option B with the dynamic TLAS traced on top, and its edge is a voxel's, not a sample's. At a spotlight's size 1,024 lamps take about 512 MB (computed), and a point light sees the whole sphere. Cooked per Chunk it fits ADR 0018; class (b) where rebuilt as Chunks change. No code found.

**15. An occlusion grid that settles rays.** The light buffer keeps, per cell of a cube around a light, the depth past which everything is shadowed [ref:haines-greenberg-1986-light-buffer-via-woo]; voxel occlusion testing keeps two bits per light per voxel, full, null or complicated, and traces no ray where a voxel is full or null [ref:woo-amanatides-1990-voxel-occlusion-testing], timed in CPU seconds per 512×512 image in 1990. Its authors name the limit: null occlusion "can never be found in a voxel that contains a surface", and every shaded pixel's voxel contains one, so only the full half, deep umbra, settles rays. ReGIR's world-space grid holds light samples, not visibility [ref:rtxdi-fdbd9b3-regir-world-space-grid]. In front of the rays every verdict stays a ray's or a certain cell's, so ADR 0020's hard shadow holds; the static grid is class (b), rebuilt as Chunks change, and the dynamic TLAS is still traced. Two bits for 1,024 lamps over a 32³ grid each are 8,388,608 bytes (computed, the grid size illustrative). No modern GPU form with costs found.

## Hybrid

**16. A raster classifier for the sun's rays.** AMD's classifier rejects pixels facing from the sun and those the cascades' minimum and maximum depth decide, lists the 8×4 tiles left with a mask and a ray interval, and may trace from the sun toward the surface [ref:amd-fidelityfx-shadow-classifier] [ref:amd-fidelityfx-shadow-classifier-ray-interval]. No cost is published. With one unjittered ray it is class (a). A coarse sun depth map used only to classify, never to shade, amends ADR 0020's Consequence "cascaded shadow maps are dropped", not its direct-light clause, and it must be conservative or thin casters vanish. It reaches the sun's base rays, 31.0% (arithmetic, before the map's own cost); the edge rays stay where it is uncertain. MIT.

**17. A rasterized sun.** Irregular z-buffer shadows rasterize casters over the receivers' sample positions, sub-pixel exact: 2.2 to 26.0 ms at one sample, 3.8 to 46.0 at 32, over nine scenes on a Titan X at 1080p [ref:wyman-2016-frustum-traced-izb] [ref:wyman-2016-frustum-traced-izb-timings]; HFTS shipped it in The Division, near contacts, 7.9 to 9.7 ms in three views on a GTX 980 at 1080p [ref:story-2016-hfts-division]. Class (a); it replaces the sun's rays, 54.4%, by a raster pass of its own, and amends ADR 0020's direct-light clause for the sun; its NVIDIA raster features would meet ADR 0021's Capability floor. `docs/research/shadow-edge-antialiasing.md` judged it poor for 1,024 lamps, a raster per light; it is the sun's alone. Closed.

**18. Rays first, maps for the rest.** MegaLights falls back to virtual shadow maps for content rays handle badly [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-foliage-falls-back-to-shadow-maps]; Olsson et al. trace the lights whose maps would be small [ref:olsson-2015-clustered-shadows-hybrid]. For Praxis: rays for the sun and near the lamps, a cached map (13) for the lamps' far reach, whose rays are up to 22.2% (technique 3, arithmetic), amending ADR 0020's clause there.

## One ray budget

Probe rays read the same TLAS as shadow rays (ADR 0020), so a frame's rays are one budget. What it rests on:

- **Price.** A shadow ray on `town` costs 1.93 ns on average in the shadow mask pass (above). A probe ray with its triangle fetch and the sun ray from its hit costs 1.50 ns timed alone: 2 ms bought 1,331,000 of them on `town` with 10,000 Moving Instances (`docs/audit/m5.5/scalability.md`, row M17; computed). In the frame, behind an indirect dispatch as the shadow mask pass's are, that prototype read 50 to 60% over its time alone on `town` and `world`, cause not measured (#1006).
- **Shares.** No document allots the 12 ms; the shares that exist are the gate's 6 ms, the dynamic TLAS's 1 ms and the probes' 2 ms (`docs/audit/strategy/2026-11.md`, S3-04), which leave 3 ms for the shadow mask pass and the rest.
- **Practice.** Published probe updates trace 66,000 to 524,288 rays a frame: Assassin's Creed Shadows about 66,000 [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-probe-timings], GIBS 100,000 [ref:apers-2024-gibs-probe-budget], id Tech 8 at most 326,144 [ref:sousa-2025-idtech8-gi-timings] and DDGI's paper 524,288 [ref:majercik-2019-ddgi-timings], each computed in `docs/research/global-illumination-and-lighting.md`. Beside ray-traced probes, Indiana Jones draws its lights' shadows into a shadow atlas [ref:de-vahl-2025-indiana-jones-shadow-static-cache] and Metro Exodus Enhanced gives each of up to 256 lights in its ray-traced GI a shadow map [ref:4a-2021-metro-exodus-enhanced-light-clusters]; the titles that trace direct shadows denoise them (Call of Duty [ref:olejnik-2026-cod-light-load-balancer], MegaLights [ref:narkowicz-costa-2025-megalights-cost]).

Three ways to split them:

- **B1. Fixed shares.** Probes get a fixed ray count set from their price on the reference machine: 2 ms buys about 1.33 million on `town`, 8,192 probes at 162 rays or 5,200 at 256. Shadow rays get what the gate leaves: at `town`'s Baseline gate, 6,732.5 µs, and with the probes' 2 ms, the shadow mask pass may take 3,267.5 µs, 27% of its Baseline's 12,292.2 (computed). Its floor with no ray, 2,299 µs here, leaves room for about half a million rays at 1.93 ns, about a tenth of today's (computed, mixing a quiet Baseline with this note's slower runs). Simple and predictable: the probes lag no worse than their fixed count, and the shadows carry the whole cut.
- **B2. Shadow rays first, probes the rest, decided on the GPU in the same frame.** Direct light is class (a) and shows at once; probes are class (b) and only lag. The shadow mask pass knows its rays once `build_shadow_mask` and the edge list have run, so a small dispatch can turn the frame's remaining budget into the probe pass's indirect workgroup count, between a floor and the 2 ms cap: probe rays = (budget − shadow rays × 1.93 ns − the pass's floor) / 1.50 ns, at least the floor's. No CPU feedback, so a capture repeats to the bit; a frame heavy in shadow rays slows the probes, not the frame. The floor is the probe rate that meets ADR 0020's "90% of its value within 0.5 s", which M6 measures. The prices are `town`'s on the M4 Pro and would be read per adapter.
- **B3. One cut for both.** A probe ray's hit traces a sun ray and, to shade lamps there, about 1.04 lamp rays (`docs/audit/m5.5/scalability.md`, row B22): about 1.33 million lamp rays beside 1.28 million sun rays at 2.1 million probe rays (computed there, unmeasured). Those are shadow rays, and the deterministic cuts above (techniques 1, 3, 6, 10) apply to them as to a pixel's. A hit's error is averaged into a probe's irradiance over many rays, so its bound can be looser than a pixel's; how much looser no source says.

## Fit to Praxis

- **ADR 0013.** Every measurement here is the M4 Pro's at one source. No RTX 2070 SUPER figure was taken; its `town` Baselines (`shadow_mask_pass` 3,703.1 µs on Vulkan and 2,783.3 on DX12, at `92927566`) come from another source, so no ratio is quoted and no cost here rejects a technique.
- **ADR 0007 and ADR 0021.** One TLAS root (10) and Metal's intersector outside passthrough (9) need wgpu-hal or naga changes; passthrough itself is a wgpu feature, which would join the Capability floor or keep a path without it.
- **ADR 0020.** Techniques 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 amend its one ray per light or its BLAS clause; 13, 14, 17 and 18 its direct-light clause; 16 its Consequence on shadow maps; 7 to 12 and 15 fit as written.
- **Code in the ray-query kernel costs.** Technique 1 measured it again: work added to `build_shadow_mask` costs occupancy whether or not it saves rays (#404, #417). A cut decided outside that kernel (in `list_shadow_edges`, which holds no ray query, or a pass before it) is the form #407 and #440 point to.

## Options

None is chosen. Each says what it could take from `town`'s shadow mask pass on the M4 Pro.

- **O1. Inside the ADRs and wgpu 30.** The runtime checks (#873), 1.30 ms, 10.8% of the pass as #873 measured it; a query order that asks for the nearest hit only where a back face can be passed (8), under a measured ceiling of 27.5%; boxes tighter than one per frame for the dynamic TLAS (10), under 16.7%; a tiled dispatch order (11), unmeasured, with no bound below the rays' 82.9%. The ceilings overlap and none is a saving; each is a prototype's question.
- **O2. Fewer rays, amending ADR 0020's one ray per light.** A relative bound (1), 2.9 to 3.3% measured, more if computed outside the ray-query kernel; a shadow range shorter than the light's (3), up to 22.2% at half the range (arithmetic) for missing lamp shadows past it; the sun's base rays from a sparse lattice with a history-free fill (5), 5.8 to 15.2% (arithmetic) for missed occluders thinner than the lattice.
- **O3. Cheaper geometry.** Merged static Instances (7) and coarser shadow BLASes (6), unmeasured, with no bound below the rays' 82.9%; a prototype that merges `town`'s static Instances per Chunk would price both against BLAS memory.
- **O4. Reuse in world or light space, class (b).** An occlusion grid in front of the lamps' rays (15), which keeps ADR 0020 and settles deep umbra, a fraction of the lamps' 28.5%, unmeasured; precomputed voxel shadows (14) or light-space caches (13) for the lamps, up to their 28.5%, amending ADR 0020 and past the render-target Budget at `town`'s 1,024 lamps unless paged.
- **O5. A raster sun.** A classifier (16) for the sun's base rays, up to 31.0%, amending a Consequence; a rasterized sun (17), its 54.4% for a raster pass, amending the direct-light clause.
- **O6. A wgpu-hal of Praxis's own** (ADR 0007): one TLAS root (10), under the same 16.7%, and concurrent dispatch, whose ceiling #873 put at 1.7 ms.

Closing `town`'s GPU frame on the M4 Pro asks 6,851 µs of the shadow rays if nothing else moves (its Baseline's 18,851.4 µs against 12,000), 56% of the pass's Baseline. O1's ceilings and the runtime checks sum to about that, so they would reach it only together, each keeping nearly all of its ceiling, and they overlap; with O2 the image changes, and O4 and O5 change what decides a shadow. On the RTX 2070 SUPER `town` already meets its Budget.
