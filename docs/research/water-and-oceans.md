# Water and oceans: state of the art

Researched 2026-11-21 for ticket #950 (parent #944). It surveys open ocean, shores, lakes and rivers, from the surface's motion to its shading and what the simulation reads of it, against ADRs 0008, 0014, 0019, 0020 and 0022. It ends in options and chooses none.

**Summary.** The fixed 64 Hz schedule reads a wave height reproducibly only by computing it on the CPU at the Tick's own time. A height read back from the GPU arrives frames late, when the main thread polls, so which Tick sees it depends on frame timing; no source keys one to a simulation step. Per surface model:

- **Summed waves and FFT spectra** are functions of position and time. A Tick evaluates the same sum, runs a small FFT or a direct sum of the low band (War Thunder, by a vendor deck: 128×128 at a fixed 48 ticks a second, within 5 cm of the drawn sea at 3 m amplitude), or looks up **a baked looping table** (Crest). The Tick's time is its counter modulo the loop period, exact in integers. This is reproducible on one binary, as ADR 0022 asks, if the sine is the engine's own: Rust leaves `sin`'s precision unspecified even between two calls. The table needs no sine at run time.
- **Flow on authored bodies** is a cooked plane and flow field: reproducible. **Stateful models** (wave particles as a set, ripples, wakes, shallow water) are reproducible only if the fixed schedule steps them on the CPU, and otherwise are for the eye alone. For **baked offline simulation** (Horizon Forbidden West's shore waves), no source says how gameplay reads it.
- **What ships.** Uncharted 3 queried its waves on the CPU for 0.1 ms on a PlayStation 3. Five of the seven open FFT oceans read back from the GPU; Wicked, Spartan and Crest by default run their waves on the frame clock.
- **Surface and mesh.** FFT cascades in the open engines and in four shipped seas, two of them by vendor decks; summed waves, wave particles or baked data in Unreal, Uncharted and Horizon. Rings or a quadtree whose vertices morph with the camera need no tessellation stage and no history.
- **Reflection.** Four forms read no screen-space history: sky with probes or cube maps (class a or b), one frame's screen-space reflection (a), a planar second render (a), and ADR 0020's mirror ray (a). For scale, one primary ray per pixel costs 4.1 to 5.7 ms at 1440p on the M4 Pro's synthetic open scene. The one shipped screen-space reflection that names its colour source (Frostbite's) reads the previous frame: class (c), ruled out.
- **Refraction.** Every engine read that refracts samples a copy of the same frame's opaque colour and depth, class (a). Under ADR 0019 the lit pass then resolves before water: the 4x colour is stored and reloaded, as in Wicked, or water draws without MSAA.
- **Without TAA.** Far wave normals glitter unless filtered into roughness. Slope-variance filtering does that with no history. Naughty Dog wrote that "Temporal AA and water has lots of issues".
- **Cost of delay.** Nothing M5 took is rebuilt. One M6 decision binds: what a ray hit is shaded with decides what a mirror ray shows.

Sources: SIGGRAPH and GDC talks (2010 to 2025), papers from 1990 to 2025 and one master's thesis, the documentation of Unreal Engine 5.9, Unity HDRP 17.0 and Godot 4, and the source of the open entries the engines register lists for this survey, each at its pinned commit (`docs/research/open-engines-register.md`). No engine source under a restrictive licence was read. Every quote in `docs/references.toml` was matched by script against a saved copy of its source on 2026-11-21. Nothing was measured for this note; the ray costs are `docs/research/global-illumination-and-lighting.md`'s. Rain and wet surfaces are #951's, fog, clouds and sky `docs/research/volumetrics-without-screen-space-history.md`'s, the terrain under the water #948's, the physics engine #952's.

Terms: the classes are `CONTEXT.md`'s Temporal classes, applied to what the shading reads; a surface that is a function of time has no history and is marked "none". A **read-back** is a copy of GPU results to the CPU. Licence classes are #944's: **A** adaptable with citation, **E** evidence only.

## The surface

**1. Summed trochoidal waves.** The height is a sum of sinusoids, "a continuous function describing the height and surface orientation of the water at all points", sampled per vertex; the GPU Gems chapter that shipped in Uru keeps four geometric waves [ref:finch-2004-summed-waves]. Bruneton and others sum 60 trochoids for 19.2 ms a frame at 1024×768 on an NVIDIA 8800 GTS [ref:bruneton-2010-ocean-geometry-to-brdf]. Crest's 2019 deck sums Gerstner waves into 8 cascades of 512×512 for 1.99 ms, and draws 170k triangles in 0.98 ms, at 1080p on a GTX 1070 [ref:bowles-zimmermann-2019-crest-readback-and-cost]. Unreal builds in this model alone [ref:ue-5.9-water-waves], as Torque3D does [ref:torque3d-9521d43-water]. Uncharted 3 chose a function that can "Evaluate at any point in R space and time", needed "for cutscenes and multiplayer": "we cannot "advance" the state. So our system is stateless" [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2012-uncharted-stateless-waves]. With horizontal displacement, the height over a point needs an inverse, found by a few iterations (technique 9).

**2. An FFT spectrum in cascades.** Tessendorf's patch is an FFT of a wave spectrum that "can be tiled seamlessly"; "the wave field at any chosen time can be computed without computing the field at any other time", and with frequencies rounded to one base frequency it "loops after a time T" [ref:tessendorf-2004-fft-ocean]. Horvath replaces his Phillips spectrum, which "doesn’t adjust wave amplitudes to account for shallow ocean depths", with JONSWAP and a depth term [ref:horvath-2015-wave-spectra]. Tiling is hidden by patches of different sizes: Crest's cascades centred on the viewer [ref:crest-ff0602f-system-notes], six of 256 samples in web-ocean [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-readme], four bands in Atlas [ref:mihelich-tcheblokov-2019-atlas-waves] and GarrettGunnell/Water [ref:acerola-water-1673a12-readme]; Wicked keeps one 512×512 patch [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ocean-patch]. A 2024 paper hides the period by blending one tile in the vertex shader, and prices the tile on an RTX 2070 SUPER, frame resolution not stated: 0.78, 0.98 and 2.16 ms for 128, 256 and 512 samples a side, about 1.5 ms to render [ref:lutz-2024-aperiodic-ocean]. Atlas gives 0.5 ms for its wind waves on a Radeon VII and an RTX 2080, a vendor's figure [ref:mihelich-tcheblokov-2019-atlas-waves]; World of Warships' whole sea took 4.2 ms at 1920×1080 on an AMD HD 7970 [ref:kryachko-2016-world-of-warships-cost]. Sea of Thieves is "an implementation of" Tessendorf's technique [ref:ang-2018-sea-of-thieves-ocean], and Doom Eternal builds its displacement map from the same notes [ref:geffroy-2020-doom-eternal-water]. GodotOceanWaves updates one cascade a frame, which its readme says still stutters [ref:godot-ocean-waves-a171446-updates]. Foam comes from where the surface folds (the Jacobian of the horizontal displacement), in a form mipmapping can filter [ref:dupuy-bruneton-2012-whitecaps], and persists by decaying in a texture per cascade or in world-space clipmaps [ref:crest-ff0602f-foam-and-shadow-history] [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-shading]: history in wave or world space, never on screen.

**3. Wave particles.** Each particle carries a small wave whose position "is derived from initial position, velocity and time" [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2012-uncharted-stateless-waves]; the original paper times a boat in a tank at 5.87 ms and a thousand boxes at 1.07 s on a GeForce 7900, and copies force textures "to main memory" for the objects [ref:yuksel-2007-wave-particles]. Uncharted 3 ran them on a PlayStation 3's SPUs for 0.9 ms [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2012-uncharted-queries]; Uncharted 4 used them "Instead of using the Tessendorf technique" [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2016-uncharted4-water].

**4. Height-field simulation.** Kass and Miller's shallow-water grid of 1990 [ref:kass-miller-1990-shallow-water] and Tessendorf's ripples, which keep "the height grid from the previous time step" [ref:tessendorf-2004-iwave], carry each step into the next; Chentanez and Müller's ocean scene, a 256×256 grid with particles for spray, costs 13.13 ms on a GTX 480 at a fixed 16.66 ms step, with "all the steps" on the GPU "except for the solid" coupling [ref:chentanez-mueller-2010-height-field-water]. Shipped use is local: Unreal's "ripples caused by footsteps or the wake behind a boat" are "height fields that are rendered as displacements" [ref:ue-5.9-water-rivers-and-ripples], and its 5.9 Preview adds shallow-water rivers [ref:unreal-5.9-preview-release-notes]; Atlas times its interactive waves at 0.6 to 0.8 ms, a vendor's figure, and labels its wakes and explosions a prototype [ref:mihelich-tcheblokov-2019-atlas-waves]. Crest says of its own that it "must progress frame by frame and can not be set to use a specific time" [ref:crest-ff0602f-deterministic-waves].

**5. Flow on authored bodies.** Valve's flow maps move a normal map along an authored vector field, "Pixel shader flow, not geometric flow"; using the same map for physics was future work [ref:vlachos-2010-water-flow]. Unreal's rivers "do not drive surface motion using waves" and write spline velocity to a flow map [ref:ue-5.9-water-rivers-and-ripples]. Torque3D's rivers are spline volumes whose flow the CPU reads from the segment that holds a point [ref:torque3d-9521d43-water-queries]. Proland's rivers follow Yu and others, whose wave sprites ride particles, the new ones read back to the CPU each frame [ref:yu-2009-rivers], and its header lists a screen flicker [ref:proland-e59a245-ocean].

**6. Baked offline simulation.** Horizon Forbidden West's breaking waves "should be driven by an baked offline sim", played back on a vertex buffer "generated by a compute shader"; its deck gives no cost and does not cover physics, and names Killzone 3's streamed mesh and COD WWII's looping simulation as earlier baked seas [ref:malan-2022-forbidden-west-water]. Unreal 5.8's roadmap lists a baked river workflow [ref:unreal-roadmap-5.8-chaos-fluids]. Crest's notes call realistic shoreline waves "a challenging open problem", and what it does at a shore is attenuate waves by a depth cache and add foam [ref:crest-ff0602f-shorelines]; Unity lists "Breaking waves on shorelines" as unsupported [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-caveats].

**7. Research since 2018.** Water Surface Wavelets advects wave amplitude on a coarse grid so waves reflect off islands, at 8.54 ms for the amplitudes and 4.16 ms for heights on a GTX 1070 laptop; its GPU version "uses hardware tesselation" [ref:jeschke-2018-water-surface-wavelets], and its code is MIT [ref:water-surface-wavelets-69c88a7-licence]. Its boundary-aware successor claims waves "interacting with beaches and rocks"; only its abstract could be opened [ref:jeschke-2020-boundary-aware-waves]. Fundamental solutions give closed-form ripples and an obstacle solver at "2 or 3 seconds per frame" [ref:schreck-2019-fundamental-solutions]. Jeschke and Wojtan's 2023 solver joins shallow-water flow and dispersive waves on a 512×512 grid at about 100 steps a second on an RTX 2080 Max-Q [ref:jeschke-wojtan-2023-dispersive-shallow-water]. None is described as shipped, and all but the closed-form ripples carry state.

## How a Tick reads the height

ADR 0022 asks that "the same binary on the same machine, fed the same per-Tick inputs, reaches a bit-identical simulation state whatever the frame timing", with up to four Ticks a frame.

**8. Read-back.** Wicked copies its displacement map into a ring of read-back textures each frame, a latency of the ring's length in frames; buoyancy samples it once a frame, before a fixed-step loop, and the waves' phase follows frame time [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ocean-readback] [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ocean-readback-latency] [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-buoyancy-step]. Spartan's fixed 200 Hz step applies buoyancy "per step so the force integrates consistently", from a cache the displacement reaches through a four-slot read-back, with the waves on the render clock [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-ocean-readback] [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-buoyancy-path]. Crest's default uploads query points and reads the answers back asynchronously, and its boat script asks from the fixed update [ref:crest-ff0602f-height-queries] [ref:crest-ff0602f-collision-async] [ref:crest-ff0602f-query-requests] [ref:crest-ff0602f-buoyancy]; web-ocean reads one height under the camera with "a few frames of latency" [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-height-probe], GarrettGunnell/Water one texel of its first band [ref:acerola-water-1673a12-buoyancy], and Crest's 2019 deck "a subset of the displacement textures" [ref:bowles-zimmermann-2019-crest-readback-and-cost]. Unity documents a read-back mode and a CPU mode; its API page and manual disagree on which has "a few frames latency" [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-script-modes] [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-cpu]. So does NVIDIA's WaveWorks [ref:nvidia-waveworks-host-readback]. A synchronous read costs 5 to 15 ms a frame for a 256×256 texture in Unreal, by a thesis's own measurement [ref:eriksson-wingard-2022-readback-cost]; War Thunder's deck says a read-back stalls on some drivers [ref:tcheblokov-war-thunder-physics-on-cpu]. Under ADR 0022 an asynchronous read fails for a reason no source weighs: wgpu runs the mapping's callback when the device is polled or a queue submitted [ref:wgpu-29-map-async], which ADR 0016 leaves to the main thread at its point in the frame, so which Tick first sees a height depends on frame timing. That is a reading of the ADRs.

**9. The CPU evaluates the same waves.** Uncharted 3 queried its stateless waves in SPU jobs, 0.1 ms for "water query (point and ray)" by "a search method to the wave field", and floated boats on hull points with high frequencies left out [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2012-uncharted-queries]. War Thunder, in a vendor deck read from a transcript, runs the "Simulation for physics" on the CPU, "Server + each client", at a "Fixed timestep, 48 ticks/second" and 128×128, beside a graphics simulation of up to 512×512 each frame, with "< 5 cm discrepancy @ 3m amplitude" between them and "900 results in <1msec"; it avoids read-backs "by performing simulation on CPU" [ref:tcheblokov-war-thunder-physics-on-cpu]. Atlas notes that the FFT "Only depends on absolute time and spectrum parameters" and "Can be simulated independently on server and clients" [ref:mihelich-tcheblokov-2019-atlas-waves]. Crest's first deck says "Use low freq waves for physics/gameplay" [ref:bowles-2017-crest-low-frequency-physics], and its deprecated CPU option "sums over all waves" at "the CPU performance cost of evaluating the waves" [ref:crest-ff0602f-gerstner-cpu]. Unreal lists a wave-height function among its water's CPU statistics and warns that many queries from "gameplay systems, physics" add "significant time" [ref:ue-5.9-water-cpu-queries]. Tessendorf's notes give the FFT on a grid or "a discrete fourier transform" at other points [ref:tessendorf-2004-fft-ocean]. The thesis states the objections: CPU cycles, and "a mismatch between the logical ocean on the CPU and the visual ocean on the GPU" [ref:eriksson-wingard-2022-readback-cost]. The evaluation is a function of position and Tick, reproducible on one binary only with a sine of the engine's own: Rust leaves `sin`'s precision unspecified, varying "within the same execution" [ref:rust-1.96-f64-sin-precision], a point ADR 0022 makes only across platforms.

**10. A baked looping table.** Crest bakes its FFT at a set number of frames a second over a loop length, with frequencies rounded so the loop closes, and samples it by position and time on the CPU [ref:crest-ff0602f-baked-fft] [ref:crest-ff0602f-baked-sampler]. Its limits: one set of waves, no shoreline attenuation, one sea level. Crest calls its animated waves "deterministic" [ref:crest-ff0602f-deterministic-waves]. A press interview says Assassin's Creed III precomputed cycling displacements; no primary source was found.

**Boats.** Every source floats a body on a few sampled points: Uncharted 3 along the hull, Atlas by a fitted plane, Unreal on sphere pontoons [ref:ue-5.9-water-rivers-and-ripples], Crest on force points in scripts it calls rudimentary [ref:crest-ff0602f-buoyancy], Unity by a script that sets a transform [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-script-modes]. Jolt's buoyancy impulse takes one surface plane from its caller [ref:jolt-5.7.0-buoyancy], so whichever physics engine #952 weighs, the wave height is the caller's to supply, by technique 8, 9 or 10. Arc Blanc, a 2025 preprint, sums buoyancy on the GPU and "transfers the data to the CPU", on an RTX 2070 SUPER [ref:algis-2025-arc-blanc].

## The mesh

**11. Rings or a quadtree with a vertex morph.** Geometry clipmaps morph each ring's edge to the coarser ring, and the transition, not timed, "tracks the continuous viewer position" [ref:losasso-hoppe-2004-clipmap-transition]; CDLOD morphs each vertex in the vertex shader [ref:strugar-2010-cdlod-morph]. Crest snaps vertices to a grid and morphs across each ring [ref:crest-ff0602f-lod-morph]; web-ocean morphs over the last fifth of a quadtree level's range, in WGSL [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-shading]; Unreal's tiles morph four quads into one [ref:ue-5.9-water-mesh]; Uncharted 4 uses CDLOD, a "quadtree like structure (GPU friendly)" [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2016-uncharted4-water]. Crest's first deck gives under 1 ms for shape and mesh on a GTX 980 [ref:bowles-2017-crest-low-frequency-physics]. GarrettGunnell/Water tessellates in a hull stage [ref:acerola-water-1673a12-buoyancy], which wgpu's pipelines do not have.

**12. A screen-space grid.** Wicked builds its grid in screen space each frame and has no LOD [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ocean-patch]; Proland clamps its grid at an analytic horizon [ref:proland-e59a245-ocean]. Its author's thesis names the cost: "the artefact known as swimming", since when the camera moves the grid's points move over the height data, less visible on an animated surface [ref:johanson-2004-projected-grid-swimming]. Doom Eternal and World of Warships ship one; Doom Eternal's deck shows its grid's image with TAA disabled and enabled [ref:geffroy-2020-doom-eternal-water] [ref:kryachko-2016-world-of-warships-cost].

## Reflection

**13. Sky, probes and cube maps.** web-ocean reflects only a sky cube map, its mip chosen from the slope variance [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-shading]; Wicked falls back to its environment map; Unreal composites "reflection captures, sky, and newly computed screen space reflections" [ref:ue-5.9-single-layer-water-inputs]. Red Dead Redemption 2 renders a low-resolution reflection cube map of its sky in 0.15 ms on a PS4 [ref:bauer-2019-rdr2-sky-probes-and-reflection]. ADR 0020 keeps runtime ray-traced cube maps for glossy specular (class b), without shipped precedent (`docs/research/global-illumination-and-lighting.md`); Assassin's Creed Shadows keeps a 128×128 fallback cube map of terrain, sky, clouds and far entities, one face a frame [ref:leblanc-conte-2025-acs-far-cubemap].

**14. One frame's screen-space reflection.** A ray marched through the depth buffer returns the colour at its hit: 1.2 ms on a GeForce Titan and 6.4 ms on a GeForce 650M at 1920×1080 for 25 steps a pixel, with the limit that the depth buffer "fails to represent areas of high depth complexity and those far outside the viewport" [ref:mcguire-mara-2014-screen-space-ray-tracing]. For a flat reflector each pixel can instead be projected to where it reflects: 0.95 ms at 3840×2160 on a GTX 1070, against 3.2 ms for a hierarchical march, for planar, non-glossy surfaces without normal maps, in its author's private research [ref:cichocki-2017-pixel-projected-reflections]. Both read the copy technique 18 makes and leave holes a fallback fills. Lumix marches with a per-frame jitter in a frame that ends in TAA [ref:lumix-8ce5ab5-water-ssr]; without a filter over frames only the unjittered, mirror form is clean.

**15. With history.** In deferred engines "the most popular" source for the hit's colour "is re-projecting the previous frame’s final lighting", and the stochastic form adds a temporal filter that "Can’t kill all the lag anyway", for 2.19 to 4.41 ms at 1600×900 on a PS4 [ref:stachowiak-2015-ssr-previous-frame]. ADR 0008 rules this out. Far Cry 5 reused its engine's screen-space reflection through a water pre-pass into the G-buffer, without saying whether it keeps history [ref:mcauley-2018-far-cry-5-water]; Bevy's water example is deferred only [ref:bevy-0.20.1-water-example].

**16. Planar reflection.** Unreal's manual: "your entire scene to be rendered twice" [ref:ue-5.9-water-reflections]. Torque3D renders the scene again and may skip a frame's update [ref:torque3d-9521d43-planar-reflection]; Wicked samples a planar texture where one exists [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ocean-shading]. Far Cry 5 dropped it: one water height was "always a pain", and sloped rivers cannot use it [ref:mcauley-2018-far-cry-5-water]. Crest's planar reflection script is class E and was not opened [ref:crest-ff0602f-planar-reflection-origin].

**17. A mirror ray.** ADR 0020's form: one ray, class (a). This repo measured one primary ray per pixel to 2 km at 4.1 to 5.7 ms on the M4 Pro's open scene and 8.4 to 9.4 ms on its dense one, at 2560×1440, each hit fetching a normal and no Material; capping rays at 100 m saved at most 17% (`docs/research/global-illumination-and-lighting.md`, Measured on the M4 Pro). No figure exists on the RTX 2070 SUPER. The cost follows how many pixels trace more than how far. Lumen traces water "with reflections forced to mirror" [ref:ue-5.9-water-reflections]; Spartan's traced reflections go through a spatiotemporal denoiser [ref:spartan-engine-cb258a1-water-reflection]; Unity's water receives ray-traced reflections and cannot appear in them [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-caveats].

## Refraction, depth and order

**18. A copy of the same frame.** GPU Gems 2 uses "the current back buffer as a refraction map", masked so objects in front do not leak in [ref:sousa-2005-generic-refraction]. Far Cry 5: "You render your water before your transparent objects, having resolved the frame buffer beforehand", and "Water writes depth, so transparent objects don’t appear underwater" [ref:mcauley-2018-far-cry-5-water]. Unreal's water pass takes "The fully lit scene and depth" as input, optionally downsampled [ref:ue-5.9-single-layer-water-inputs], after lighting and before other translucency [ref:ue-5.9-single-layer-water]. Godot copies the screen "after the opaque geometry pass, but before the transparent geometry pass" [ref:godot-docs-screen-texture-copy]; Doom Eternal, forward rendered, splits its colour into before-water and after-water [ref:geffroy-2020-doom-eternal-water]. Absorption comes from the depth behind the surface [ref:lumix-8ce5ab5-water]. Wicked is the one Forward+ engine with MSAA read here: its ocean pass loads the multisampled colour the opaque pass stored and resolves again, after the resolved colour is downsampled for refraction [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ocean-order]. Unity's water is "entirely incompatible" with MSAA [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-limits].

**19. No refraction.** web-ocean and Proland shade open water with a body colour under the Fresnel term and read nothing of the frame [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-shading] [ref:proland-e59a245-ocean]. Between the two lies a blend whose strength comes from the depth behind the surface and that copies no colour; no source read does it.

**Order.** Each source that states an order draws water first among the transparent draws. Crest, Wicked, Far Cry 5 and Unreal have it write depth, so "transparent objects" are "not being visible behind" it [ref:crest-ff0602f-rendering-notes]; Lumix draws it with depth read-only (`data/scripts/core/render/transparent.evox`). Unity copies the depth buffer before water so that transparents behind it sort per pixel [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-depth-order].

## Light

**20. Slope-variance filtering.** Bruneton's model moves each wave train "from geometry to normal to BRDF" as its wavelength falls under the grid's resolution, which "eliminates geometric aliasing as well as popping" [ref:bruneton-2010-ocean-geometry-to-brdf]; Proland precomputes the filtered slope variance from the spectrum [ref:proland-e59a245-ocean], web-ocean derives it per pixel from mip-mapped moments [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-shading], and whitecaps filter the same way, in under 10 ms a frame at 1280×720 on a GeForce 560 Ti [ref:dupuy-bruneton-2012-whitecaps]. It is the idea the cooker already applies to normal maps [ref:toksvig-2004-variance]. The alternative among the sources is the frame's TAA, which Uncharted 4 found troublesome for water [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2016-uncharted4-water].

**21. Caustics.** Three forms: a texture projected onto the bed [ref:guardado-2004-water-caustics] [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-caustics], as Crest does; the surface's own focusing, the ratio of a refracted triangle's area to its area at rest, a few lines under MIT [ref:wallace-2011-webgl-water], which Heckel's article rebuilds from a normal map rendered from the light [ref:heckel-2024-caustics]; and rays traced from a caustics map drawn "from the light view", "without any denoising involved" for a directional light and with a temporal filter for an area light, timed only in a bar chart [ref:yang-ouyang-2021-water-caustics]. Doom Eternal's caustics image "is used next frame" [ref:geffroy-2020-doom-eternal-water].

**22. Underwater and the waterline.** A full-screen pass after the transparent draws, with a line where the lens meets the surface: Crest [ref:crest-ff0602f-underwater] and Unity [ref:unity-hdrp-17-water-caveats]. Wicked's pass traces the displaced surface again, since "The ocean is not rendered into the lineardepth" [ref:wicked-engine-fc8385b-ocean-order].

**Shadows.** Crest copies shadow maps into its cascades and accumulates them over frames in world space [ref:crest-ff0602f-foam-and-shadow-history]; Far Cry 5 shades and shadows water in forward [ref:mcauley-2018-far-cry-5-water].

## What the register's entries do

Each read at its pinned commit, source only, nothing run.

- **Crest** (`ff0602f`, MIT: A; its planar reflection and example folders E, not opened). Techniques 2, 8, 10, 11, 18 and 22. Fit: nearest in structure; its default height read is one ADR 0022 cannot take, its bake one it can.
- **Wicked Engine** (`fc8385b`, MIT: A). One tiled FFT patch on a screen-space grid, a read-back sampled once a frame, planar or environment reflection, refraction from a quarter-resolution copy; its shore wet map (`WickedEngine/shaders/wetmap_updateCS.hlsl`) is #951's. Fit: evidence for pass order under Forward+ with MSAA; its patch, grid and read-back are not what Praxis needs.
- **web-ocean** (`a07c7fd`, MIT: A). Six cascades in WGSL compute, a CDLOD quadtree, one forward pass at 4x MSAA that reads nothing of the frame, slope-variance filtering, no refraction and no shoreline. Fit: nearest to the stack, open ocean only; by its readme built with an AI coding tool [ref:web-ocean-a07c7fd-readme], so evidence to check line by line.
- **Spartan Engine** (`cb258a1` and nothing later, MIT there: A). Four cascades on a clipmap; a fixed 200 Hz step on read-back heights; deferred, with denoised traced reflections. Fit: the clearest read-back plumbing, on a renderer of another kind.
- **Torque3D** (`9521d43`, MIT: A). Summed waves, planar or cube map reflection, spline rivers with a CPU flow query; its CPU surface leaves the waves out. Fit: its river query is technique 5's shape.
- **Jolt Physics** (`cf16769`, MIT: A). A buoyancy impulse against one surface plane per call (`Jolt/Physics/Body/Body.h`). Fit: whatever supplies the plane decides reproducibility, not Jolt.
- **GodotOceanWaves** (`a171446`, MIT: A): cascade updates spread over frames. **GarrettGunnell/Water** (`1673a12`, MIT: A): needs a hull stage. **Proland** (`e59a245`, BSD-3-Clause: A): technique 20 and the horizon. **Bevy** (`a0577b5`, MIT OR Apache-2.0: A): deferred only. **Lumix** (`8ce5ab5`, MIT: A): techniques 14 and 18 on a flat surface.
- **Unity HDRP 17** and **Unreal 5.9** (E, documentation only): Unity's two script modes and its MSAA limit; Unreal's built-in summed waves, CPU queries and G-buffer water pass.

## Fit to Praxis

- **ADR 0022.** The drawn surface can be evaluated at the blend's time between the two newest Ticks, so a floating body and the water under it are drawn at one instant; a reading of the ADR that no source describes.
- **ADR 0019, the shared pass.** The 4x colour is a transient attachment the lit pass resolves and discards (`src/render/targets.rs`), so ADR 0019 counts no memory for it "when the lit and transparent passes share one render pass and resolve in it". Technique 18 resolves before water. Stored, as Wicked does, the 4x colour is 117,964,800 bytes at 1440p (2560 × 1440 × 4 samples × 8 bytes, computed); otherwise water draws without MSAA over the resolved image, or takes technique 19. The opaque depth is already stored.
- **ADR 0019, order.** Sorting per Instance cannot place a surface kilometres wide among particles and glass. The rule the sources state is water first among the transparents, mostly writing depth, so what lies behind it is lost or needs a second depth buffer, as Unity copies.
- **ADR 0020, shadows.** The shadow mask is traced from the opaque depth, so it holds the bed's verdict and none for the surface. For scale, a sun shadow ray from every visible pixel cost 1.8 to 2.0 ms on the M4 Pro's synthetic open scene (same note); no source prices one for water.
- **ADR 0020, the TLAS and probes.** No engine read puts the animated surface in an acceleration structure; a displaced BLAS would rebuild each frame, 0.6 to 0.75 ms of GPU for one of 5,120 triangles (same note). A flat proxy behind an instance mask, which the shadow mask's rays already carry (`src/render/shaders/shadow_mask.wgsl`), would let probe and mirror rays see a water body while shadow rays pass; without one, probes light the bed as dry ground. No source read does either.
- **ADR 0014.** A tiled patch's coordinate is a World position modulo its period, derived from the Anchor as ADR 0014 asks. web-ocean and Spartan keep f32 positions (`js/main.js`, `source/world/components/Water.cpp`); Proland accumulates an f64 offset (`ocean/sources/proland/ocean/DrawOceanTask.cpp`); Crest shifts its origin by multiples of its largest wavelength [ref:crest-ff0602f-open-worlds]. Time is the same: with frequencies rounded to a loop period [ref:tessendorf-2004-fft-ocean], phase is the Tick counter modulo that period.
- **Chunks.** A lake or river as a Placement would be merged into its Chunk's Far proxy with a baked, opaque Material (ADR 0026) unless the cook leaves it out. An ocean is one surface outside Chunks, drawn past the View distance to the horizon, as ADR 0026 draws flat ground there.
- **ADR 0013.** No published figure was measured at 1440p, and only technique 2's tile on one of Praxis's adapters (the RTX 2070 SUPER). The GPU frame's existing shares sum to 9 of its 12 ms (`docs/audit/strategy/2026-11.md`, S3-04).
- **One queue.** Uncharted 4 times its water "mixed with render and async compute jobs" [ref:gonzalez-ochoa-2016-uncharted4-water]; wgpu exposes one queue [ref:wgpu-29-request-device-one-queue], so a simulation's time lands in the frame.

## Cost of delay

- **The surface and the Tick's read (1, 2, 9, 10).** They add beside everything. The fixed schedule exists (ADR 0022, M1); a baked table or a flow map is a new kind of Blob beside ADR 0018's. Nothing M5 took or M6 will take is rebuilt.
- **The mesh (11).** Adds beside: a pipeline of its own in the transparent draws. It does not use M5's LOD chain or its Far proxies.
- **Water bodies in Chunks.** Binds one M5 mechanism lightly: the Far proxy's cook merges every static Placement, so a water Placement needs a rule there. The rule is a cook change, not a redesign.
- **A mirror ray (17).** Reads what M5 built (Far proxies in the static TLAS past the Load radius) and what M6 is about to decide: what a ray hit is shaded with (`docs/research/global-illumination-and-lighting.md`, options H1 to H3). An average colour per Mesh (H2) would give a mirror flat colours; a decision taken with probes alone in mind means a second hit-shading path when water arrives. This is the one decision of M6's that the survey found bound.
- **Sky, probes and cube maps (13).** Add beside, and reuse M6's reflections decision (R1 or R2 in the same note) and the sky-space cloud map decided on 2026-11-15.
- **The colour copy (18).** Rebuilds a decision M3 took, the shared pass, whenever it is built; nothing in M6 makes it dearer. Glass and particles that refract meet the same question (#17, *Transparency, masked and double-sided Materials*).
- **A shadow verdict on the surface, and a proxy in the TLAS.** Add beside the shadow mask and the static TLAS.

Nothing here argues for moving water ahead of M6. One thing argues for naming it at M6's charting: mirror rays as a reader of the hit-shading decision.

## Options

The surface and the Tick's read:

- **W1. Summed waves, evaluated by the CPU from the same list** (techniques 1 and 9). Reproducible with the engine's own sine, and the simplest; Unreal's and Uncharted 3's shape. Open: how many waves a sea needs before it repeats, and its CPU time per Tick.
- **W2. FFT cascades on the GPU, with the CPU evaluating the low band at each Tick** (2 and 9). War Thunder's shape, by a vendor deck. Reproducible with the engine's own sine; follows a changing spectrum. Open: the CPU time of a small FFT or of a direct sum at the query points, and the gap between the read height and the drawn surface, claimed under 5 cm at 3 m.
- **W3. FFT cascades with a baked looping table for the CPU** (2 and 10). Crest's bake. Reproducible with no sine at run time, and no FFT on the CPU. Open: its bytes, one table per sea state, no shoreline attenuation in Crest's form.
- **W4. A read-back** (8). What five of seven open oceans do. It needs ADR 0022 amended, or a read keyed to a Tick that no source describes.
- Beside any: flow on cooked bodies for rivers and lakes (5), baked simulation where a shore must break (6), and ripples or wakes as detail no Tick reads (3, 4).

Reflection:

- **X1. Sky, probes and cube maps alone** (13), with slope-variance filtering (20). Class (a) for the sky, (b) for probes and cube maps; reads nothing of the frame. Fits as written.
- **X2. X1 with one frame's screen-space reflection** (14). Class (a); reads the same frame's opaque colour and depth, the copy T2 makes.
- **X3. X1 with mirror rays where the surface is near and smooth** (17). Class (a); reads nothing of the frame, only the TLAS and what hit shading M6 decides. ADR 0020's form.
- **X4. A planar render for flat bodies** (16). Class (a); reads a second render of the scene, not the frame. Flat bodies at one height only.

Refraction:

- **T1. None, or a blend by depth alone** (19). Class (a); reads nothing, or only the opaque depth the pre-pass already stores. Keeps the shared pass.
- **T2. A copy of the frame's opaque colour and depth** (18). Class (a); reads the same frame's resolved opaque colour and depth. Ends the lit pass before water: a stored 4x colour, or water without MSAA.

Prototype questions, each a cost no source publishes, to be measured on the M4 Pro and the RTX 2070 SUPER (ADR 0013). No Reference scene holds water; each names the one it would start from.

1. **The Tick's read.** CPU time per Tick of W1, W2 and W3 for a boat's worth of query points, at up to four Ticks a frame, against the 6 ms CPU Budget; W3's bytes; and the height gap between what a Tick reads and what is drawn. On `orrery`, which shows the fixed schedule, with a water surface and floating bodies.
2. **Reflection.** One mirror ray per water pixel with its hit shaded, against one frame's screen-space reflection with a sky fallback, at 1440p on `world` with a sea-level surface, whose rays meet Far proxies; judged at 4x MSAA with no history against a supersampled capture.
3. **The split pass.** GPU time and bytes of resolving before water: a stored 4x colour against a water pass without MSAA, on `town` with a water body.
4. **Glitter.** Whether slope-variance filtering holds a far sea still from frame to frame at 4x MSAA with a moving camera, on the scene of question 2.
5. **Shadow on and under water.** The cost of a second verdict for the surface, on the scene of question 3.
